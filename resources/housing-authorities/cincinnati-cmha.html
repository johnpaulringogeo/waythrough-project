The Cincinnati Metropolitan Housing Authority (CMHA) runs Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers and public housing for Cincinnati and the rest of Hamilton County. HUD lists it as PHA OH004 with a program type of Combined. It is a different agency from the Cuyahoga Metropolitan Housing Authority in Cleveland and the Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority, which also go by CMHA. Its main office is at 1627 Western Ave, Cincinnati, OH 45214, (513) 721-4580, and its voucher customer service office is next door at 1635 Western Ave, (513) 977-5800. The short version today: CMHA says its Housing Choice Voucher waiting list is closed, and people picked in its 2026 lottery wait behind the current list. Public housing is the open door: CMHA says you can submit a preliminary application at any time through its online portal. This page covers CMHA's exact words, how its lists work, the phone numbers, and the other housing authorities around Cincinnati.

Waiting-List Status

Waiting lists open and close frequently, so treat the status below as a starting point and confirm on the official site before you rely on it.

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8): Closed — Checked November 5, 2026: CMHA's waiting-list page says "The waiting list for CMHA's Housing Choice Voucher Program is currently closed." On its 2026 lottery it says: "You will receive a notice indicating that you were selected to be placed on the 2026 HCV Wait List via email. CMHA will begin to pull from the 2026 HCV Wait List after the current wait list is exhausted." If you were not picked, CMHA says "you may reapply when our wait list is open in the future." Its estimate: "Our best estimate for the wait can be 6 months to 3 years." CMHA says "A placement number is assigned by a random-draw system and preferences" and that all contact with applicants is by email.
  • Public housing: Taking preliminary applications at any time — CMHA's Apply for Housing page says: "You can submit a preliminary application at any time. Once you apply, your application will stay on the waiting list until you are selected." Register on CMHA's portal at myinfo.cintimha.com. CMHA says that after you log in and choose "Apply to Waitlist," "You will only be able to open the waitlists that are featured or highlighted." To check your status, CMHA says to click "Wait List Status"; you are active if the status says "On List" (checked November 5, 2026).

Programs CMHA Runs

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8); the waiting list is closed
  • Public housing (CMHA calls it Asset Management): "over 3,700 units varying between efficiency and six-bedroom, located within Hamilton County"
  • Project-Based Vouchers at partner apartment communities, with lists joined through Rent Cafe
  • Family Self-Sufficiency: "a voluntary, five-year employment incentive program" for voucher holders and public housing residents
  • HCV Homeownership vouchers through the Family Self-Sufficiency department

How to Apply

Apply directly through the authority's official application page: cintimha.com/housing/asset_management/how_to_apply/apply_for_housing.php. If you are already on the voucher list, call 513-977-5800; CMHA says staff "will not be able to provide you with a position number but will confirm if you're active on the list." For public housing, log in to the CMHA portal and click "Wait List Status." Tell CMHA in writing if your address changes and keep the email you applied with, since CMHA contacts applicants by email.

New to the process? Our step-by-step guide to applying for Section 8 walks through the whole thing, and how to find your PHA helps you locate the right authority.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Cincinnati Section 8 waiting list open?

No. As of November 5, 2026, CMHA says the waiting list for its Housing Choice Voucher Program is currently closed. People picked in its 2026 lottery were told by email, and CMHA says it will start pulling from the 2026 list after the current list is used up. If you were not picked, you can reapply the next time the list opens. Public housing is different: CMHA takes preliminary applications at any time.

How do I apply for CMHA public housing?

Register on CMHA's applicant portal at myinfo.cintimha.com, choose Apply to Waitlist, and apply to the lists that are featured or highlighted. CMHA says your application stays on the waiting list until you are selected. Keep your email address working, answer CMHA's requests by the deadline, and tell CMHA in writing if your mailing address changes.

How long is the wait for CMHA?

For vouchers, CMHA says its best estimate is 6 months to 3 years. It explains that it gets no new vouchers from HUD, so it can only offer help when someone leaves the program, and that your place is set by a random draw and preferences. None of these estimates is a promise, and Section 8 is not built for emergencies.

How do I check my status with CMHA?

For the voucher list, call 513-977-5800; staff will tell you whether you are active but not your position number. For public housing, log in to the CMHA portal and click Wait List Status; you are active if it says On List. The voucher office is at 1635 Western Ave, open Monday to Friday, 8:00 am to 4:30 pm, and its email is hcvhelp@cintimha.com.

I live in Northern Kentucky or outside Hamilton County. Where do I apply?

Apply to your own county's agency. In Ohio, the Butler, Clermont and Warren metropolitan housing authorities run separate lists. In Kentucky, the Housing Authority of Covington and the Housing Authority of Newport serve their cities. You can be on more than one list at a time, so apply to every agency where you would be willing to live.

What does a Section 8 tenant pay in Cincinnati?

Under HUD rules, a family with a voucher or in public housing usually pays about 30% of its adjusted monthly income toward rent and utilities, and the housing authority pays the rest of an approved rent. Adjusted income is your income after HUD's deductions, such as those for children, elderly or disabled family members, and some medical and child care costs. CMHA lists the income limit for vouchers as 50% of the area median income.

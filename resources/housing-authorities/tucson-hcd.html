City of Tucson Housing and Community Development (HCD)

Quick Facts

Phone: (520) 791-4171

Apply: HCD application page
Status last checked: November 3, 2026

The City of Tucson Housing and Community Development Department (HCD) is Tucson's public housing authority, and it also runs Pima County's Section 8 program. HUD lists two agencies at HCD's office, 310 N. Commerce Park Loop: Housing and Community Development Tucson, PHA AZ004, program type Combined, and the Pima County Housing Authority, PHA AZ033, program type Section 8, both with the phone number (520) 791-4171. HCD's 2026 Annual Plan for AZ004 lists 1,098 public housing units and 4,278 Housing Choice Vouchers, and the City says the voucher program has about 6,000 vouchers for City of Tucson and Pima County residents. The short version today: on January 1, 2026, HCD closed the City of Tucson Section 8 list, the Pima County Section 8 list and the City's public housing lists, and no reopening date has been set. This page covers HCD's exact words, how the monthly lottery works for people who applied before the closure, HCD's preferences, the specialty programs that still take referrals, and the separate South Tucson Housing Authority.

Waiting-List Status

Waiting lists open and close frequently, so treat the status below as a starting point and confirm on the official site before you rely on it.

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8), City of Tucson and Pima County: Closed. Closed (checked November 3, 2026). HCD's Section 8 page reads: "The Housing Waitlist is currently closed and is not accepting new applications at this time." HCD's legal notice says that effective January 1, 2026 it closed the City of Tucson HCV (Section 8) and Pima County HCV (Section 8) waitlists, and that "No date has been set for the waitlists to reopen." HCD's applicant portal, tucsonaz.myhousing.com, says: "There are currently no waiting lists open."
  • Public Housing: Closed. HCD's Public Housing page reads: "The Public Housing Waitlists are closed." The January 1, 2026 legal notice names MLK Apartments, Silverbell Homes, Southpark, Posadas Sentinel, Lander Gardens, and AMPs 3, 4, 5 and 6 (checked November 3, 2026). When a name comes up, HCD says you get an email and a letter and have 10 business days to respond, and that you cannot choose your own unit.
  • El Portal (City-owned affordable rentals): Closed. HCD's El Portal page reads: "El Portal is not currently accepting applications to be placed on our waitlist" (checked November 3, 2026). El Portal staff: (520) 791-5353.
  • HUD-VASH, FUP and other specialty programs: By referral. In its December 23, 2025 announcement, the City said HCD "will continue to provide vouchers, when capacity allows, for specialty programs" such as HUD-VASH and the Family Unification Program (FUP). HCD's Specialty Programs page says FUP families and youth "must currently be on the Section 8 waiting list and referred" by the Arizona Department of Child Safety, (520) 751-3097, Arizona's Children Association, (520) 622-7611, or the Arizona Department of Economic Security, (520) 628-6810. The same page still says "Referrals and applications are now being accepted" for La Frontera's Rally Point Apartments, 30 project-based studios for homeless veterans, but gives no date, so call the leasing office at (520) 838-0034 to confirm (checked November 3, 2026).

Programs HCD Runs

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) for the City of Tucson (AZ004) and Pima County (AZ033), about 6,000 vouchers in all
  • Public Housing, 1,098 units in HCD's 2026 Annual Plan, including MLK Apartments, Silverbell Homes, Southpark, Lander Gardens, Craycroft Towers and Tucson House
  • City-owned affordable rentals at Posadas Sentinel and El Portal
  • HUD-VASH vouchers for homeless veterans, by VA referral
  • Family Unification Program (FUP), by referral from partner agencies
  • Moderate Rehabilitation, Shelter Plus Care, HOPWA rent subsidy and other specialty programs
  • Family Self-Sufficiency (FSS)

How to Apply

Apply directly through the authority's official application page: www.tucsonaz.gov/Departments/Housing-and-Community-Development/Section-8/Application-Status. If you applied before the January 1, 2026 closure, HCD says staff cannot give you your place on the waitlist, but you can email tucsonwaitinglist@tucsonaz.gov or call (520) 791-5840 to have staff check your waitlist status and confirm your mailing address, because HCD mails a letter when you are selected.

New to the process? Our step-by-step guide to applying for Section 8 walks through the whole thing, and how to find your PHA helps you locate the right authority.

Other Authorities Serving This Area

  • South Tucson Housing Authority is a separate agency for the City of South Tucson, HUD code AZ025, program type Combined, at 1713 S. 3rd Ave., 520-623-8481. Its own page says it runs 172 public housing apartments and 132 Housing Choice Vouchers, all inside South Tucson's 1.2 square miles, and read on November 3, 2026: "THE WAIT LIST IS CURRENTLY CLOSED."
  • Pima County does not run a separate voucher office. Pima County's rental resources page says "The Pima County Section 8 program is administered by City of Tucson," and HUD lists the Pima County Housing Authority (AZ033) at HCD's address and phone. HCD ran both lists, and both closed on January 1, 2026.
  • Elsewhere in Arizona, the City of Mesa and the City of Phoenix run their own lists. See our Mesa Housing Authority page and our Phoenix Housing Department page.
  • HCD is not an emergency rent fund. If you are behind on rent, start with the Pima County Community Action Agency at (520) 724-2667, and see our Tucson emergency rent help guide: Pima County's Emergency Services Network, Interfaith Community Services, Primavera, utility help, free eviction lawyers through EELS and Southern Arizona Legal Aid, and Arizona's 5-day notice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Tucson Section 8 waiting list open?

No. As of November 3, 2026, HCD's Section 8 page says: "The Housing Waitlist is currently closed and is not accepting new applications at this time." HCD closed the City of Tucson and Pima County voucher lists and the City's public housing lists on January 1, 2026, and its legal notice says "No date has been set for the waitlists to reopen." Treat any reopening date you see elsewhere as unconfirmed unless HCD's own site shows it.

Why did Tucson close the lists?

In its December 23, 2025 announcement, the City said more than 40,000 Tucsonans were waiting for the two programs, which it expected to keep enrolling people for several more years without new applicants. It said the voucher program has about 6,000 vouchers for City of Tucson and Pima County residents, and that with about 11 percent attrition, roughly 660 vouchers come free each year. HCD's director said, "Keeping the list open creates false expectations and erodes public trust."

I applied before January 1, 2026. Where am I on the list?

HCD says that each month it runs an electronic lottery from the pool of pre-applications, that the timing of your application does not help or hurt your odds, and that "Staff cannot provide you with your place on the waitlist." People picked in the lottery go onto the waitlist and get a letter. HCD warns that there are 15,000+ applications to join the list and it could be a year or more before you are chosen. Keep your mailing address current with HCD's online update form, by email to tucsonwaitinglist@tucsonaz.gov, or by leaving a message at (520) 791-5840.

Does HCD give any preferences?

HCD's Housing Choice Voucher Administrative Plan, effective July 1, 2026, lists three local preferences: families whose HCD voucher was ended because of insufficient program funding, then victims of domestic violence, dating violence, sexual assault, stalking or human trafficking who are referred by a partner agency or need a VAWA emergency transfer, and families moving off an expiring Emergency Housing Voucher. Within each group, families are picked by the date and time their complete application came in.

Is Pima County Section 8 a different agency?

No. Pima County says its Section 8 program is administered by the City of Tucson, and HUD lists the Pima County Housing Authority (AZ033) at HCD's office and phone. Both the City and the County voucher lists closed on January 1, 2026. The South Tucson Housing Authority is a separate agency for the City of South Tucson, at 520-623-8481, and its own page also says its wait list is closed.

Can a Tucson landlord refuse my voucher?

Inside Tucson city limits, generally not. Arizona has no statewide source-of-income protection, but HCD's housing discrimination page says source of income "is now protected under local fair housing laws in the cities of Tucson, Phoenix, and Tempe," and that covers Housing Choice Vouchers. The City says the Arizona Attorney General found in March 2023 that its ordinance does not violate state law. Outside those cities, a landlord can still turn down a voucher.

What number should I call?

(520) 791-4171 is HCD's main number. It is on every HCD page and HUD lists it for both AZ004 and AZ033. For waitlist questions, HCD's Application Status page and its legal notice give (520) 791-5840 and tucsonwaitinglist@tucsonaz.gov, and Pima County's rental resources page gives the same number. HCD's office is at 310 N. Commerce Park Loop, open 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday through Friday.

Quick Facts

Official site: www.ebrpha.org/
Apply: EBRPHA application page
Status last checked: November 12, 2026

The East Baton Rouge Parish Housing Authority (EBRPHA) runs the Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher program and the public housing communities it owns in East Baton Rouge Parish. HUD lists it as PHA LA003, under the name Housing Authority of East Baton Rouge, with a program type of Combined. Its office is at 4731 North Blvd., Baton Rouge, LA 70806, and its main line is (225) 923-8100, Monday through Friday, 7:30 am to 4:30 pm. The short version today: EBRPHA says both of its lists are closed, the Housing Choice Voucher list and the list for its own apartment communities, which it calls affordable housing. It takes applications only during open enrollment, and it announces those with a newspaper notice and a post on ebrpha.org. This page covers EBRPHA's exact words, how an opening works, how to check a pending application, and where to go if you need help with rent now.

Waiting-List Status

Waiting lists open and close often. The status below is what we found the last time we checked; confirm it on the official site before you count on it.

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8): Closed. Checked November 12, 2026: EBRPHA's Applicants page says "The Housing Choice Voucher and Affordable Housing waitlists are currently closed." It adds: "EBRPHA only accepts HCV applications when the waitlist is open. Check the EBRPHA website, EBRPHA social media, and your local paper to know when the waitlist is open." EBRPHA's FAQ says an opening is announced with "a public notice in the local newspaper, and a posting on our website," and that the notice gives the exact dates and how to apply. No reopening date is posted. EBRPHA says vouchers are for households at or below 50% of the area median income and that it gives priority to extremely low-income families, about 30% of the area median income.
  • Affordable housing communities (public housing): Closed. EBRPHA's homepage says "Our Affordable Housing and Housing Choice Voucher waitlists are currently closed. Please check back for updates on future waitlist openings" (checked November 12, 2026). When applications open, EBRPHA says you can apply online or in person, and that you may apply to more than one community at a time. Each community keeps its own list, separate from the voucher list.

Programs EBRPHA Runs

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8): rent a private home and EBRPHA pays part of the rent to the landlord; the waiting list is closed
  • Affordable housing communities (public housing) that EBRPHA owns and manages, including Ardenwood Village, Capital Square, Clarksdale, Duane Street, Kelly Terrace, Monte Sano Village, Scotland Villa, Sharlo Terrace, Turner Plaza, Wood Plaza and Zion Terrace; this list is closed too
  • Portability: EBRPHA says a voucher can move with you to another area under program rules; talk with your caseworker before you move

How to Apply

Apply on the authority's own application page: www.ebrpha.org/applicants. If you have already applied, check your place on EBRPHA's applicant portal, which EBRPHA says shows "your current position and any updates," or call (225) 923-8100. Keep your mailing address, phone and email current. If EBRPHA cannot reach you when your name comes up, you can lose your place.

If this is your first time applying, our step-by-step guide to applying for Section 8 covers each stage, and how to find your PHA shows which authority covers where you live.

Other Authorities Serving This Area

  • EBRPHA is not an emergency rent fund. Its own advice: "If you need housing today, call 211 or contact a local partner like Catholic Charities of Baton Rouge or the Capital Area United Way." If you are behind on rent, see our Baton Rouge emergency rent help guide: the City-Parish Office of Social Services, Southeast Louisiana Legal Services' eviction desk at City Court, 211, LIHEAP and Louisiana's eviction deadlines.
  • Already have EBRPHA housing or a voucher and fell behind? Call (225) 923-8100 right away, and call Southeast Louisiana Legal Services at (225) 448-0080. An eviction can cost you your assistance, and in Louisiana the court date can come within days.
  • If you live outside East Baton Rouge Parish, a different housing authority serves you. Use HUD's PHA directory to find it. HUD also lists the Louisiana Housing Authority (PHA LA903, Section 8 only) at 2415 Quail Dr., Baton Rouge, (225) 763-8800.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Baton Rouge Section 8 waiting list open?

No. As of November 12, 2026, EBRPHA says its Housing Choice Voucher and affordable housing waitlists are both closed. No reopening date is posted. EBRPHA takes applications only during open enrollment and announces it with a public notice in the local newspaper and a post on ebrpha.org, so check there and watch the paper.

How will I know when EBRPHA opens its list?

EBRPHA says it announces an opening two ways: a public notice in the local newspaper and a posting on ebrpha.org. The notice gives the exact dates, locations and what you need to apply. EBRPHA also says to follow its social media. Applications are date and time stamped, so apply early in the window.

How do I check my place on an EBRPHA list?

Use the applicant portal at portals.ebrpha.org, which EBRPHA links from its Check my status button, or call (225) 923-8100. Keep your address, phone and email up to date, because EBRPHA has to be able to reach you when your name comes up.

Can I apply for Section 8 and public housing at the same time?

Yes, when the lists are open. EBRPHA says each program has its own application and waitlist and that being on one list does not affect the other. For its apartment communities, you may apply to more than one community at a time.

Who qualifies for an EBRPHA voucher?

EBRPHA says most households at or below 50% of the area median income may qualify, and that it gives priority to extremely low-income families, about 30% of the area median income. Eligibility also depends on family size and citizenship or eligible immigration status. With a voucher you usually pay about 30% of your income toward rent.

What should I do if I need housing help now?

EBRPHA's lists are closed, so it cannot help with emergency rent. Call 211 for shelter and rent referrals. For rent or utility help, call the City-Parish Office of Social Services at 225-358-4561. If you have an eviction notice, call Southeast Louisiana Legal Services at (225) 448-0080 the same day.

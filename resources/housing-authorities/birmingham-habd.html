Housing Authority of the Birmingham District (HABD)

Serves Birmingham, with Housing Choice Vouchers usable in Birmingham and Jefferson County, Alabama · Last checked November 5, 2026

Waythrough is an independent resource, not HABD. We summarize public information to help you get oriented. For applications and the most current waiting-list status, always use the official links below.

Quick Facts

Serves: Birmingham, with Housing Choice Vouchers usable in Birmingham and Jefferson County, Alabama
Phone: (205) 521-0774
Official site: www.habd.org/
Apply: HABD application page
Status last checked: November 5, 2026

The Housing Authority of the Birmingham District is the public housing agency for the City of Birmingham. HUD lists it as PHA AL001 with a program type of Combined, so it runs both Housing Choice Vouchers (Section 8) and public housing. HABD says it "owns and manages 14 developments consisting of more than 4,000 public housing units in the Birmingham Metropolitan Area," and its Department of Rental Assistance Programs runs the voucher program "to eligible families within Jefferson County." The short answer to the question most people bring here is that HABD does not post whether its voucher list is open or closed. Its Section 8 page only says what to do "When a Housing Choice Voucher program waiting list opens." This page covers HABD's exact words, the two different ways to apply (an online portal for vouchers, a paper form for public housing), the special programs that take referrals, and the other housing authorities in Jefferson County.

Waiting-List Status

Waiting lists open and close frequently, so treat the status below as a starting point and confirm on the official site before you rely on it.

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8): No status published. HABD's Section 8 page gives no open or closed status (checked November 5, 2026). Its own words are: "When a Housing Choice Voucher program waiting list opens, interested participants can get started by visiting HABD.org/portals to register and join the waiting list." Its FAQ says: "Individuals interested in a Housing Choice voucher can apply online at habdportals.org." The portal's applicant login page asks you to register with an email address and does not say whether a list is open. For a straight answer, call the HCV call center at (205) 974-4440.
  • Public housing (HABD's 14 developments): Paper application; no status published. HABD's public housing page says: "Interested public housing residents can apply using this form," and links a downloadable HABD Public Housing Application (checked November 5, 2026). Its FAQ says to "complete and drop off an application at our George W. McCoy Building (1301 25th Ave. N) or any of our Community Centers during normal business hours." HABD does not publish an open or closed status or a wait time for any development.
  • Site-based lists: Tuxedo Terrace tax credit units and Freedom Manor (PBRA): Apply through the site's portal; no status published. The top of HABD's portal at habdportals.org carries two separate links labeled "TAX CREDIT WAITING LIST - TUXEDO" and "PBRA WAITING LIST - FREEDOM MANOR" (checked November 5, 2026). Each opens its own applicant login. Neither page says whether the list is open, so call the site before you count on it: Tuxedo Terrace, 2025 Avenue Q, 205-781-5356; Freedom Manor, 1617 5th Avenue North, 205-521-7707.

Programs HABD Runs

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8), usable with private landlords in Birmingham and Jefferson County
  • Public housing at 14 HABD developments in the Birmingham area
  • Project-Based Vouchers (PBV) at partner properties, including the MIMR program at Metropolitan Gardens/Park Place for referred applicants
  • HUD-VASH vouchers for homeless veterans, the Moderate Rehabilitation SRO program with Alabama Regional Medical Center, and HCV Homeownership

How to Apply

Apply directly through the authority's official application page: www.habdportals.org/. If you have already applied, call HABD's Housing Choice Voucher call center, (205) 974-4440, which HABD says handles "questions or concerns on applications, waitlist, inspections, etc.", and keep your mailing address, phone and email current, because a housing authority that cannot reach you when your name comes up can drop you from the list.

New to the process? Our step-by-step guide to applying for Section 8 walks through the whole thing, and how to find your PHA helps you locate the right authority.

Other Authorities Serving This Area

  • Jefferson County Housing Authority serves much of Jefferson County outside Birmingham. HUD lists it as AL086, program type Combined, at 3700 Industrial Parkway, Birmingham, AL 35217, (205) 849-0137. Confirm the number with the authority, because Jefferson County's own housing page gives (205) 849-0123.
  • Other authorities in Jefferson County, as HUD's PHA contact report lists them: the Housing Authority of Bessemer (AL125, Combined, (205) 481-4484), Fairfield Housing Authority (AL010, public housing only, (205) 923-5574), Tarrant Housing Authority (AL013, public housing only, (205) 841-2226) and the Housing Authority of Leeds (AL069, Combined, (205) 699-2208). Each keeps its own list, so you can apply to more than one.
  • HABD is not an emergency rent fund. If you are behind on rent, see our Birmingham emergency rent help guide: 211 Connects Alabama, Legal Services Alabama, LIHEAP through the Community Action Agency of Northeast Alabama, Alabama Power programs, and Alabama's 7-business-day notice. If you already have HABD housing or a voucher and fall behind, call HABD at (205) 521-0774 or the HCV call center at (205) 974-4440 right away.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Birmingham Section 8 waiting list open?

HABD does not say. As of November 5, 2026, its Section 8 page gives no open or closed status and only explains what to do "When a Housing Choice Voucher program waiting list opens": register on its portal and join the list. Its FAQ points voucher applicants to habdportals.org. Call the HCV call center at (205) 974-4440 before you assume a list is open, and ignore websites that claim a status HABD itself does not post.

How do I apply for public housing in Birmingham?

With a paper form. HABD's public housing page links a downloadable HABD Public Housing Application, and its FAQ says to complete it and drop it off at the George W. McCoy Building, 1301 25th Ave. N, or at any HABD community center during normal business hours. HABD does not publish whether any development's list is open or how long the wait is, so ask when you drop off the form. HABD has 14 developments with more than 4,000 units.

Which HABD phone number should I call?

(205) 974-4440 is the Housing Choice Voucher call center, which HABD says is for "questions or concerns on applications, waitlist, inspections, etc." (205) 521-0774 is the main office at 1826 3rd Ave. S, Birmingham, AL 35233. Landlords with Section 8 questions call the Department of Rental Assistance Programs at (205) 521-7460. Emergency maintenance for public housing residents is (205) 623-5449. HUD's directory lists a different main number, (205) 449-0070, so use the numbers on HABD's own site.

How much rent will I pay with a voucher?

Under HUD's rules, a family with a voucher or in public housing generally pays about 30% of its adjusted monthly income toward rent and utilities. Adjusted income is your gross income minus allowances such as those for dependents, and for elderly or disabled households, some medical costs. With a voucher you may pay more than that if you pick a unit that rents above HABD's payment standard. HABD posts its payment standards and utility allowances on its Section 8 page.

Are there special programs for veterans or people with disabilities?

Yes, by referral. For HUD-VASH, HABD says veterans must be registered for VA services and can then call Cortney Rankins at (205) 936-1365 for a screening. The Moderate Rehabilitation SRO program for homeless individuals is run with the Alabama Regional Medical Center, (205) 370-1422. The MIMR project-based program at Metropolitan Gardens/Park Place serves households whose head has a mental illness, and applicants must be referred by a mental health agency or doctor.

Can a Birmingham landlord refuse my voucher?

Yes. Alabama has no law that stops a landlord from turning down a tenant because of a Housing Choice Voucher, and we found no Birmingham ordinance that does. Refusals based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, disability or family status are still illegal under the federal Fair Housing Act, and you can report them to HUD at 1-800-669-9777. HABD says voucher holders find their own landlord using listings on AffordableHousing.com, a site it partners with.

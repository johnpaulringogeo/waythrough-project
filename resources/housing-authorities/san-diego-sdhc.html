San Diego Housing Commission (SDHC)

Waythrough is an independent resource, not SDHC. This is a summary of public information. To apply, or to check the current waiting-list status, use the official links below.

Quick Facts

Serves: the City of San Diego, California
Phone: (619) 231-9400
Official site: sdhc.org/
Apply: SDHC application page
Status last checked: November 3, 2026

The San Diego Housing Commission (SDHC) is the public housing agency for the City of San Diego. HUD lists it as PHA CA063 with a program type of Combined, at 1122 Broadway, Suite 300. SDHC says its Section 8 program serves more than 16,000 households, and that with its nonprofit affiliate, Housing Development Partners, it owns or manages more than 4,100 affordable rental homes. The short version today: SDHC closed its Section 8, public housing and project-based voucher waiting lists on February 1, 2026, is not taking new applications, and is not selecting new families from the Section 8 list. If you need help with rent now, SDHC's Housing Instability Prevention Program (HIPP) is a separate program that works by referral. This page covers SDHC's exact words, what to do if you are already on the list, HIPP, and the County of San Diego's separate housing authority, which serves most of the rest of the county.

Waiting-List Status

Waiting lists open and close often. The status below is what we found the last time we checked; confirm it on the official site before you count on it.

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8): Closed. Closed (checked November 3, 2026). SDHC's Wait List Portal page says SDHC "closed its Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher and Public Housing waiting lists February 1, 2026, at 11:59 p.m." and "SDHC is not accepting new waiting list applications at this time." It adds: "SDHC is not selecting new families from the HCV waiting list to begin receiving rental assistance at this time. We do not expect to be able to pull families from the HCV waiting list for several more years." No reopening date is given.
  • Public Housing: Closed. Closed since the same date, February 1, 2026, at 11:59 p.m. (checked November 3, 2026).
  • Project-Based Vouchers: Closed. SDHC says the February 1, 2026 closure "includes the waiting list for Project-Based Vouchers" (checked November 3, 2026).
  • HUD referral programs: Open only to agency referrals. SDHC keeps an open list only for referral programs HUD requires, and says these lists "are not open to the general public and are only accessible by Community-Based Organization (CBO) referral."
  • Housing Instability Prevention Program (HIPP, rent help, not Section 8): By referral; funding limited. HIPP takes referrals from 2-1-1 San Diego and SDHC's Homelessness Initiatives hotline, 619-578-7768. SDHC says "Funding is limited. HIPP currently can assist up to 300 households at any given time" (checked November 3, 2026), and it does not say whether a slot is free right now, so ask when you call.

Programs SDHC Runs

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8), for more than 16,000 City of San Diego households
  • Public Housing and Project-Based Vouchers
  • More than 4,100 affordable rental homes owned or managed by SDHC and Housing Development Partners, each with its own application
  • Housing Instability Prevention Program (HIPP): $250 to $1,000 a month toward rent for up to 24 months, by referral
  • City of San Diego Eviction Prevention Program, run by the Legal Aid Society of San Diego under contract with SDHC

How to Apply

Apply on the authority's own application page: sdhc.org/housing-opportunities/help-with-your-rent/wait-list-portal/. If you already applied, call (619) 231-9400 to check your place on the list, and give them your new phone number or address every time it changes. If they can't reach you when your name comes up, they can drop you from the list.

If this is your first time applying, our step-by-step guide to applying for Section 8 covers each stage, and how to find your PHA shows which authority covers where you live.

Other Authorities Serving This Area

  • Housing Authority of the County of San Diego (HACSD), run by the County's Housing and Community Development Services (HCDS), is a separate agency: HUD code CA108, program type Combined, 3989 Ruffin Road, San Diego, CA 92123, (858) 694-4801 (toll free (877) 478-5478). For Section 8 it serves the unincorporated county and 13 cities: Chula Vista, Coronado, Del Mar, El Cajon, Escondido, Imperial Beach, La Mesa, Lemon Grove, Poway, San Marcos, Santee, Solana Beach and Vista. Its Section 8 page says "the Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher waitlist closed on February 20, 2026, at 11:59 p.m." (checked November 3, 2026), and that names are picked by date and time of application, with a possible wait of "10 or more years."
  • Four more cities run their own programs, per HUD's California PHA list: Carlsbad Housing and Homeless Services (CA077), (442) 339-2810; Housing Authority of the City of Encinitas (CA155), (760) 633-2723; City of Oceanside (CA132), (760) 435-3360; and the CDC of National City (CA116), (619) 336-4254. We did not check their waiting lists for this page, so call or check their sites.
  • Behind on rent in the City of San Diego? Section 8 is not emergency help. Call 2-1-1 or SDHC's hotline at 619-578-7768 to be referred to HIPP, and see our San Diego emergency rent help guide for CalWORKs back-rent help, the City's free Eviction Prevention Program, and the City's Tenant Protection Ordinance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the San Diego Section 8 waiting list open?

No. SDHC closed its Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher and Public Housing waiting lists on February 1, 2026, at 11:59 p.m., including the Project-Based Voucher list, and says "SDHC is not accepting new waiting list applications at this time" (checked November 3, 2026). SDHC gives no reopening date. The County of San Diego's Section 8 list, which covers most of the rest of the county, closed on February 20, 2026.

I am already on the SDHC list. What happens now?

Your application stays active. SDHC says: "If you have already submitted an application, this change does not affect you." You do not need to do anything, but you can update your information through the SDHC Wait List Portal. If you registered before December 5, 2022, you must create a new portal account, which SDHC says will not affect your position. For questions, call SDHC's Wait List Call Center at 619-578-7640.

How long is the wait for Section 8 in San Diego?

SDHC will not estimate it. SDHC says "We do not expect to be able to pull families from the HCV waiting list for several more years," and that until more federal funding arrives, "we regret that we cannot assist new families from the waiting list nor provide an estimated waiting period." SDHC publishes no waiting-list size, so treat any number of applicants you see online as unconfirmed.

What is the difference between SDHC and the County of San Diego housing authority?

SDHC serves the City of San Diego. The Housing Authority of the County of San Diego, run by the County's Housing and Community Development Services, serves the unincorporated county and 13 cities, including Chula Vista, El Cajon, Escondido, La Mesa, Santee and Vista. Carlsbad, Encinitas, Oceanside and National City run their own programs. Each keeps its own list, and both SDHC's and the County's Section 8 lists are closed.

Can SDHC help me pay rent right now?

Possibly, through HIPP, not Section 8. SDHC's Housing Instability Prevention Program pays $250, $500, $750 or $1,000 a month toward rent for up to 24 months, plus past-due rent and utilities, for City of San Diego households at or below 80% of the area median who spend more than 40% of their income on housing and face a crisis such as an eviction. Immigration status does not affect eligibility. Call 2-1-1 or 619-578-7768 to be referred. SDHC says HIPP can assist up to 300 households at a time.

Can a San Diego landlord refuse my Section 8 voucher?

No. SDHC says the San Diego City Council added Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher rental assistance as a protected source of income, enforceable since August 1, 2019, and California law protects voucher holders statewide. Landlords can still screen tenants on valid rental criteria. The source-of-income hotline SDHC lists, run by the Legal Aid Society of San Diego, is (833) 801-4420.

Sacramento Housing and Redevelopment Agency (SHRA)

Serves The City and County of Sacramento, California, including all incorporated cities in the County · Last checked October 28, 2026

Waythrough is an independent resource, not SHRA. We summarize public information to help you get oriented. For applications and the most current waiting-list status, always use the official links below.

The Sacramento Housing and Redevelopment Agency (SHRA) is the housing authority for both the City and the County of Sacramento. Its mission statement says it serves "as the Housing Authority for the City and County of Sacramento," and HUD lists two housing authorities under it: the City of Sacramento Housing Authority (CA005) and the County of Sacramento Housing Authority (CA007), both with a program type of Combined. SHRA says the voucher program is operated by SHRA "acting as the Housing Authority of the County of Sacramento" for the city and county, "including all incorporated cities in the County," and it also runs public housing. The short version today: the tenant-based Section 8 list is closed, but on sacwaitlist.com, the only place SHRA posts its waiting lists, several project-based voucher and public housing lists show "Bedroom(s) Open," almost all of them for 3, 4 or 5 bedrooms. This page lists each one as of October 28, 2026, how SHRA's preference points work, the scam warning SHRA has posted, and how to reach SHRA, which sees people by appointment only.

Waiting-List Status

Waiting lists open and close frequently, so treat the status below as a starting point and confirm on the official site before you rely on it.

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8), tenant-based: Closed — Closed (checked October 28, 2026). SHRA's waiting list site, sacwaitlist.com, reads: "Tenant-Based Housing Choice Voucher Program waiting list (formerly known as the Section 8 program) is currently closed." No SHRA page gives a reopening date. SHRA says openings are posted on its website, Facebook and Twitter and in local media.
  • Project-based voucher lists: Some bedroom sizes open, as of October 28, 2026 — On sacwaitlist.com, checked October 28, 2026, these project-based lists show "Bedroom(s) Open": Project-Based County, 3, 4 and 5 bedrooms (preferences: Residency, Veteran); Project-Based City, 3, 4 and 5 bedrooms (Homeless, Residency, Veteran); Phoenix Park, 4400 Shining Star Drive, 4 bedrooms; and Mirasol Village, 3, 4 and 5 bedrooms. Phoenix Park and Mirasol Village list the preferences Rent Burdened or Homeless, Residency, Disabled, Veteran, and Displaced by Government. A project-based voucher stays with the unit, so you live at that property.
  • Foster Youth to Independence (FYI) vouchers: "Open for Referral Only" — sacwaitlist.com lists a tenant-based "HOUSING CHOICE VOUCHER - FYI REFERRAL" list as "Open for Referral Only" (checked October 28, 2026), for young people 18 to 24 who left foster care, or will within 90 days, and are homeless or at risk of homelessness. You cannot sign up for it on your own; a referring agency has to send you.
  • Public Housing: Some sites and bedroom sizes open, as of October 28, 2026 — SHRA's public housing page says "Applications for public housing are being accepted for selected sites and bedroom sizes." On sacwaitlist.com, checked October 28, 2026, these lists show "Bedroom(s) Open": Walnut Grove, 14215 Grove Street, Walnut Grove, 3 bedrooms (you must get an access code from a sign posted at the site, facing Grove Street); County Housing, 4 and 5 bedrooms; and City Housing, 4 bedrooms. All three list the preferences Veteran, Homeless, Homeless with Services, and Involuntary Displaced. SHRA says public housing applicants are added "on a first come, first served basis."

Programs SHRA Runs

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8): Tenant Based Vouchers, and Project Based Vouchers at specific privately owned affordable communities
  • Public Housing: apartments, duplexes and single-family homes in the City and County
  • Foster Youth to Independence (FYI) vouchers, by referral only
  • Homeless housing programs and navigation centers, and financing for new affordable housing

How to Apply

Apply directly through the authority's official application page: www.sacwaitlist.com/waitingList. If you have already applied, call (916) 440-1390 to verify your position and keep your contact information current — if the authority cannot reach you when your name comes up, you can be dropped from the list.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the SHRA Section 8 waiting list open?

No. As of October 28, 2026, sacwaitlist.com says the "Tenant-Based Housing Choice Voucher Program waiting list (formerly known as the Section 8 program) is currently closed." SHRA gives no reopening date. Some project-based voucher and public housing lists are open, mostly for 3 to 5 bedrooms, so if your household fits one of those sizes, look at those lists now rather than waiting for the tenant-based list.

Which SHRA lists are open right now?

On October 28, 2026, sacwaitlist.com showed "Bedroom(s) Open" for: Project-Based County, 3, 4 and 5 bedrooms; Project-Based City, 3, 4 and 5 bedrooms; Phoenix Park, 4 bedrooms; Mirasol Village, 3, 4 and 5 bedrooms; and public housing at Walnut Grove, 3 bedrooms, County Housing, 4 and 5 bedrooms, and City Housing, 4 bedrooms. The FYI voucher list is open for referral only. SHRA says it manages 12 different wait lists that open and close at different times, so check sacwaitlist.com before you apply.

How do SHRA's preference points work?

SHRA's HCV and public housing FAQs say your wait depends on when you applied and on your preference points, which are based on factors such as living or working in Sacramento County, having a household member with a disability, being rent burdened, being a veteran or having a veteran in the family, and being homeless. Each list on sacwaitlist.com names its own preferences. SHRA also says it cannot give you your exact position on a list, and that the best way to be picked sooner is to sign up for every list you qualify for.

How do I apply?

Online at sacwaitlist.com, from any device with internet access and an email address. SHRA says "There is no charge to apply," and the site can be translated into many languages. You can also ask SHRA for a paper application. For a reasonable accommodation, call 916-440-1390, TTY 711 or 1 (800) 855-7100. SHRA asks you to apply only to lists whose criteria you meet.

Is that Section 8 flyer I saw real?

Be careful. SHRA's homepage warns that flyers listing the website www.govassistance.org "are NOT LEGITIMATE and should be ignored," and that "All Sacramento Housing and Redevelopment Agency waiting lists are ONLY posted on Sacwaitlist.com." SHRA also says paying someone for a credit check is not part of its process: "There is never a fee to apply." Do not give money or personal information to anyone who contacts you about a list.

How do I keep my place on an SHRA list?

SHRA says you must notify it in writing within 30 days of any change, such as a new address, phone number or email, a household member moving in or out, or a change in income, and you can update your information on sacwaitlist.com. If you are removed for not responding, there is no right to an informal review, but SHRA says you may ask to be reinstated because of extenuating circumstances. If your application is denied for cause, you can request an informal review.

What number should I call, and where is SHRA's office?

(916) 440-1390 is SHRA's communication center. It appears on SHRA's Contact Us, Housing Choice Voucher, Public Housing and Find Housing pages and in the sacwaitlist.com footer; TTY is 711 or 1 (800) 855-7100. SHRA says "You must have an appointment to receive assistance at the office" at 630 I Street, Sacramento, CA 95814, where there is a document drop box; its fax is 916-449-1285. All SHRA offices are closed every other Friday. HUD's directory lists 801 12th Street and (916) 440-1319 for both housing authorities; SHRA's own Contact page lists 801 12th Street for its Development department, so use 630 I Street and (916) 440-1390 for housing questions.

What are the income limits for SHRA public housing?

SHRA's public housing FAQ lists these maximum incomes: $46,850 for 1 person, $53,550 for 2, $60,250 for 3, $66,900 for 4, $72,300 for 5, $77,650 for 6, $83,000 for 7 and $88,350 for 8. At least one household member must be a U.S. citizen or have eligible immigration status. SHRA runs a background check on adults, and drug, fraud or violent criminal activity in the past three years can lead to an individualized review rather than an automatic denial.

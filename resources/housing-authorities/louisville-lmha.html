Housing Authority

Louisville Metro Housing Authority (LMHA)

Serves Louisville Metro and Jefferson County, Kentucky · Last checked October 15, 2026

Waythrough is an independent resource, not LMHA. We summarize public information to help you get oriented. For applications and the most current waiting-list status, always use the official links below.

The Louisville Metro Housing Authority is the public housing agency for Louisville Metro and Jefferson County. HUD lists it as PHA KY001 with a program type of Combined, so it runs both Housing Choice Vouchers and public housing, and LMHA says it serves "more than 11,000 households" and owns and manages "nearly 3,000 traditional Public Housing (PH) units." The short answer to the question most people bring here is that LMHA is not taking new applications for either program. Its Section 8 page says so, its rental process page dates the pause to July 1, 2024, and its applicant portal says the same for public housing. This page covers those exact words, the three phone numbers that do different jobs, the six privately managed communities that keep their own lists, the points system LMHA uses when the voucher list does reopen, and the 20-day rule that removes people who miss a letter.

Waiting-List Status

Waiting lists open and close frequently, so treat the status below as a starting point and confirm on the official site before you rely on it.

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8): Closed — Closed (checked October 15, 2026). LMHA's Section 8 page and its HCV Self-Service page both read: "Please be advised that we are not accepting Housing Choice Voucher applications at this time." Its Rental Process and Eligibility page adds: "As of July 1, 2024 the application process for Metro Louisville has been put on hold." No reopening date is published on any LMHA page or in its news list. LMHA's Administrative Plan says a reopening is announced "at least 10 business days" ahead in local media.
  • Public Housing (LMHA's central list): Closed — LMHA's public housing application page gives no status and sends applicants to its online portal. The portal's welcome page, checked October 15, 2026, reads: "New applications are NOT being taken at this time for Section 8 or Public Housing. Applications are closed." The central list covers Parkway Place, Scattered Sites, Avenue Plaza, Will E. Seay Plaza, Lourdes Hall and St. Catherine Court.
  • Privately managed public housing sites and project-based (PBV) sites: Apply at each site; no status published — LMHA's Admissions and Continued Occupancy Policy names six privately managed communities with their own site-based lists, each taken at that site's office: Liberty Green (502) 582-8500, Sheppard Square (502) 561-0078, Villages of Park DuValle (502) 778-7277, The Oaks (502) 778-8333, Wilart Arms (502) 772-2249, and Family Scholar House (502) 584-8090. Project-based voucher sites also keep separate lists, mostly run by the owners, and LMHA's PBV site list says: "Availability is not guaranteed. Please contact the site for unit information." LMHA publishes no open or closed status for any of these, so call the site.

Programs LMHA Runs

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8)
  • Public Housing at LMHA sites, including senior and disabled buildings for ages 55 and over
  • Project-Based Vouchers (PBV) and RAD project-based sites with their own waiting lists
  • Special programs including Family Self-Sufficiency

How to Apply

Apply directly through the authority's official application page: portal.lmha1.org/. If you have already applied, call (502) 569-3400 to verify your position and keep your contact information current — if the authority cannot reach you when your name comes up, you can be dropped from the list.

New to the process? Our step-by-step guide to applying for Section 8 walks through the whole thing, and how to find your PHA helps you locate the right authority.

Other Authorities Serving This Area

  • Kentucky Housing Corporation runs the statewide Section 8 program for areas outside local housing authorities. HUD lists it as KY901, program type Section 8, in Frankfort, (502) 564-7630. It does not cover Louisville Metro, which is LMHA's jurisdiction.
  • Across the river in Indiana, the Housing Authority of the City of New Albany (IN012, (812) 206-2033) and the Housing Authority of the City of Jeffersonville (IN023, (812) 283-3553) run their own lists. Both numbers are as HUD lists them, so confirm with each authority.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Louisville Section 8 waiting list open?

No. As of October 15, 2026, LMHA's Section 8 page reads: "Please be advised that we are not accepting Housing Choice Voucher applications at this time." Its Rental Process and Eligibility page says: "As of July 1, 2024 the application process for Metro Louisville has been put on hold." LMHA publishes no reopening date. Its Administrative Plan says reopenings are announced at least 10 business days in advance in local media, so watch lmha1.org and local news.

Can I apply for public housing in Louisville?

Not on LMHA's central list right now. LMHA's applicant portal reads: "New applications are NOT being taken at this time for Section 8 or Public Housing. Applications are closed." Six privately managed communities keep their own lists and take applications at their own offices: Liberty Green, Sheppard Square, Villages of Park DuValle, The Oaks, Wilart Arms and Family Scholar House. LMHA does not publish whether those lists are open, so call the community before you go.

I am already on the list. How do I check my place?

Call (502) 584-1704 and follow the prompts. LMHA's Section 8 page describes it as the line for "information regarding your position on the LMHA HCV waiting list" and says it is open 24 hours a day, seven days a week, and the applicant portal gives the same number. Keep your address current in writing: LMHA's Administrative Plan says that if you do not respond to a letter within 20 calendar days, or if mail comes back with no forwarding address, you are removed from the waiting list without further notice.

How will LMHA pick people when the voucher list reopens?

By points, then by date and time, not by lottery. LMHA's Administrative Plan, effective April 21, 2026, assigns preference points: 100 for VAWA, 50 for families who lost assistance because of insufficient funding, 25 for displacement, 5 for residency, 3 for elderly or disabled households and 3 for veterans. Within the same points, families are served first-come, first-served by the date and time the complete application is received. For public housing, LMHA's policy lists families by bedroom size and date and time, with preference holders first, and offers every fourth eligible unit to a family referred from the homeless Continuum of Care.

Which LMHA number should I call?

(502) 569-3400 is LMHA's main office, printed in the footer of every LMHA page, on its public housing application page and on its applicant portal. (502) 569-6060 is the Section 8 number, labeled that way in the footer and given on the Section 8 page as the line to reach a customer service representative. (502) 584-1704 is only for waiting-list position. Office hours are Monday through Friday, 8:00 am to 4:30 pm, and LMHA's Section 8 page says meetings with a specialist are by appointment only.

Where is the LMHA office?

LMHA's own pages, its applicant portal and its May 2026 admissions policy all give 320 South Fifth Street, Louisville, KY 40202. A December 2024 LMHA news post announced a move to the 500W building at 500 W. Jefferson St. starting in May 2025, and HUD's directory gives a third address, 420 S 8th Street, so call (502) 569-3400 to confirm before you visit in person.

What changed for voucher holders in 2026?

LMHA posted three changes on April 28, 2026: a $50 minimum rent starting at each family's next certification, a cut in the standard medical deduction from $2,200 to $500 effective July 1, 2026, and the end of utility reimbursement payments on July 1, 2026. If your share of the rent went up after July, these are the likely reasons, and your specialist can confirm the math.

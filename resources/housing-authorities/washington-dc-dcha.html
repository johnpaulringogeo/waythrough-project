District of Columbia Housing Authority (DCHA)

Quick Facts

Serves: the District of Columbia
Phone: (202) 535-1000
Official site: www.dchousing.org/
Apply: DCHA application page
Status last checked: November 3, 2026

The District of Columbia Housing Authority is the public housing agency for all of Washington, DC. HUD lists it as PHA DC001 with a program type of Combined, so it runs both the Housing Choice Voucher Program (HCVP, or Section 8) and public housing, and it also runs the District-funded Local Rent Supplement Program (LRSP). Its headquarters mailing address is 300 7th Street SW, 10th Floor. The short version today: DCHA says its public housing and voucher waitlists are both closed to new applicants, and it gives no reopening date. This page quotes DCHA's exact words, explains how some households still reach DCHA help through referrals, how to check a waitlist application you already filed, and why DC's emergency rent program, ERAP, belongs to a different agency.

Waiting-List Status

Waiting lists open and close frequently, so treat the status below as a starting point and confirm on the official site before you rely on it.

  • Housing Choice Voucher Program (Section 8): Closed — Closed to new applicants (checked November 3, 2026). DCHA's homepage and its Applicants menu say: "The waitlists for Public Housing and the Housing Choice Voucher Program (HCVP) are currently closed to new applicants. Updates on waitlist status will be posted when available." Its homepage FAQ adds: "DCHA's waitlists are closed." No DCHA page gives a reopening date. DCHA says that if it reopens either list, "it will publicize the opportunity on the DCHA website, and through social media channels and local media outlets."
  • Public Housing: Closed — The same notice covers public housing: closed to new applicants, checked November 3, 2026. DCHA's waiting-list FAQ says: "At this time, DCHA is focused on updating information for those currently on the waitlist." DCHA says households for public housing "may be pulled from a DCHA waitlist or referred from a partner agency."
  • LRSP and project-based vouchers: Referral or site-based list only — DCHA's voucher overview says tenant-based and project-based vouchers are filled from the HCVP waitlist or by referral, that some LRSP project-based and sponsor-based units fill from a property's own site-based waitlist, and that "More than 80% of LRSP Tenant-Based Vouchers go to target populations." DCHA's site does not list any open site-based waitlist, so ask the property directly.

Programs DCHA Runs

  • Housing Choice Voucher Program (HCVP, Section 8), with tenant-based and project-based vouchers
  • Local Rent Supplement Program (LRSP), funded by the District, which includes the Permanent Supportive Housing (PSH) and Targeted Affordable Housing (TAH) programs
  • Public housing, from studios to 6-bedroom units, in mid-rise and walk-up buildings, townhomes and scattered single-family homes
  • Moderate Rehabilitation and special purpose vouchers

How to Apply

Apply directly through the authority's official application page: services.dchousing.org/. If you have already applied, call (202) 535-1000 to verify your position and keep your contact information current — if the authority cannot reach you when your name comes up, you can be dropped from the list.

New to the process? Our step-by-step guide to applying for Section 8 walks through the whole thing, and how to find your PHA helps you locate the right authority.

Other Authorities Serving This Area

  • DC's Emergency Rental Assistance Program (ERAP) is not run by DCHA. ERAP belongs to the DC Department of Human Services (DHS), at erap.dhs.dc.gov, and it books applications by appointment. For ERAP's current appointment opening and the 48-hour rule that can cancel an eviction, see our Washington, DC emergency rent help guide. DCHA is not an emergency rent fund; if you already have DCHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call the DCHA Call Center at (202) 535-1000.
  • If you have nowhere to stay tonight, DCHA's own waiting-list FAQ sends families with children to the Virginia Williams Family Resource Center, 920 Rhode Island Avenue NE (Monday to Thursday 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m., Friday 8:30 a.m. to noon), and everyone to the Shelter Hotline at (202) 399-7093. DCHA says the hotline runs 24 hours a day during hypothermia season, November 1 to April 15, and from 8 a.m. to midnight the rest of the year.
  • Housing Opportunities Commission of Montgomery County (Maryland) is a separate agency with its own lists: HUD code MD004, program type Combined, 10400 Detrick Avenue, Kensington, MD, (240) 627-9400.
  • Housing Authority of Prince George's County (Maryland) is also separate: HUD code MD015, program type Combined, 9200 Basil Court, Largo, MD, (301) 883-5552.
  • HUD's list for the District has one other agency, Community Connections, HUD code DC880, program type Section 8, 801 Pennsylvania Ave. SE, (202) 608-4764. Every other voucher and public housing program in DC runs through DCHA.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the DC Housing Authority waitlist open?

No. As of November 3, 2026, DCHA's homepage says: "The waitlists for Public Housing and the Housing Choice Voucher Program (HCVP) are currently closed to new applicants." No DCHA page gives a reopening date. DCHA says it will announce any reopening on its website, on social media and through local media, so treat any other date you see online as unconfirmed unless dchousing.org shows it.

I applied years ago. How do I check my DCHA waitlist status?

DCHA asks waitlist applicants to create a RentCafe account and manage the application there, and its homepage links to a status check at services.dchousing.org. To confirm your information, DCHA's waiting-list FAQ says to call 202-535-1000, and to email updatewaitlist@dchousing.org to remove your name or ask about an eligibility interview. For help in Spanish, the same FAQ gives 844-306-0531, then press 2. Keep your address and phone current: DCHA says applicants on a waitlist are required to maintain their contact information.

How long is the wait once I am on the DCHA list?

DCHA does not give a number. Its waiting-list FAQ says reaching the top depends on "the available number of units and vouchers, when you applied, and any selection preferences you may have indicated on your application such as homeless." When your name comes up, DCHA says it will contact you to schedule a final eligibility interview. If you became homeless after you applied, DCHA says to update your housing status with its form.

Is DC ERAP part of the DC Housing Authority?

No. ERAP, DC's Emergency Rental Assistance Program, is run by the DC Department of Human Services (DHS), not DCHA, and it takes applications by appointment at erap.dhs.dc.gov. DCHA's closed waitlists have nothing to do with ERAP's schedule. Our Washington, DC emergency rent help guide has ERAP's current appointment opening and who qualifies.

Can I get DCHA housing help without the waitlist?

Sometimes, by referral. DCHA says tenant-based and project-based vouchers are filled from the HCVP waitlist or by referral, and that more than 80 percent of LRSP tenant-based vouchers go to target populations. The Permanent Supportive Housing and Targeted Affordable Housing programs are part of LRSP, and their participants may also get services from DHS. DCHA does not post an application for referrals; if you are homeless, start with the Shelter Hotline at (202) 399-7093 or, for families with children, the Virginia Williams Family Resource Center.

What number should I call?

The DCHA Call Center, (202) 535-1000. It is on DCHA's Contact page, in its homepage FAQ and in its waiting-list FAQ. HUD's PHA directory lists a different number for DC001, (202) 535-1500, but DCHA's own pages send callers to 535-1000. For walk-ins, the HCVP Customer Service Center is at 702 H Street NW, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., with the last walk-in at 3:45 p.m.

The Austin Housing Authority (AHA) is the name the Housing Authority of the City of Austin (HACA) now goes by. Its old hacanet.org address forwards to austinha.org, and its policy documents, including the Housing Choice Voucher Administrative Plan revised May 29, 2025, still use the legal name. HUD lists it as PHA TX001 with a program type of Combined. AHA says it is "an independent public entity, separate from the City of Austin," and that it serves 27,000 residents through vouchers, its Pathways properties and the communities built by its subsidiary, the Austin Affordable Housing Corporation. As of November 3, 2026, every AHA waiting list is closed. Its Housing Choice Voucher page reads "Waitlist Applications Closed," and its How do I apply? page says "All Austin Housing Authority waitlists are currently closed." This page covers AHA's exact words, how its lottery and preferences work, how to check a status you already have, and the Travis County authority people often mix it up with.

Waiting-List Status

Waiting lists open and close frequently, so treat the status below as a starting point and confirm on the official site before you rely on it.

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8): Closed — Checked November 3, 2026. AHA's Housing Choice Voucher page reads "Waitlist Applications Closed" and says "When the Housing Choice Voucher waitlist is open, you can submit an application online to be considered for placement on the waitlist. Applicants selected through the lottery process will be contacted to complete eligibility verification." Its How do I apply? page says "All Austin Housing Authority waitlists are currently closed. When a waitlist reopens, we will make a public announcement and eligible households will have the opportunity to apply and participate in the waitlist lottery process." No opening date was posted. AHA also says: "If no housing waitlist opening is posted on our website, we are not currently accepting applications."
  • Pathways properties (former public housing, now PBRA): Closed — AHA's Our Pathways Properties page lists 18 properties and reads "Waitlist Applications Closed" (checked November 3, 2026). AHA says these properties "are made possible through the Project-based Rental Assistance (PBRA) program" and that "your rent is discounted based on your income level and eligibility." When a Pathways list opens, AHA says the property will appear at the top of that page "under Accepting Waitlist Applications." Each property has its own list, so you apply property by property.
  • Pathways at Goodrich Place: Taking applications at the property — AHA's Find Housing page says "Goodrich is Accepting Applications Now" and links to the property's own site, which calls it "an income-restricted community" at 2126 Goodrich Ave., lists a $15 application fee and asks you to call its leasing team at (512) 912-7838 (checked November 3, 2026). This is an application to one apartment community, not a place on the voucher list. Ask the leasing office how rent is set for the unit you want before you pay the fee.

Programs AHA Runs

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8), usable in Austin, Round Rock, Pflugerville, Cedar Park, Leander, Del Valle and Manor
  • Pathways properties: 18 AHA-owned communities with Project-Based Rental Assistance (PBRA), where rent is based on income
  • Special purpose vouchers named in AHA's Administrative Plan: Mainstream, HUD-VASH for veterans, Family Unification, Non-Elderly Disabled, Foster Youth to Independence, Stability Vouchers and Emergency Housing Vouchers
  • Family Self-Sufficiency (FSS) for voucher holders and Pathways residents
  • Affordable communities developed by its subsidiary, the Austin Affordable Housing Corporation

How to Apply

Apply directly through the authority's official application page: austinha.org/helpful-articles/voucher-clients/how-do-i-apply/. If you have already applied, call (512) 477-4488 to verify your position and keep your contact information current — if the authority cannot reach you when your name comes up, you can be dropped from the list.

New to the process? Our step-by-step guide to applying for Section 8 walks through the whole thing, and how to find your PHA helps you locate the right authority.

Other Authorities Serving This Area

  • The Housing Authority of Travis County (HATC) is a separate agency with its own lists, at 502 East Highland Mall Boulevard, Suite 106-B, Austin, TX 78752, (512) 854-8245. HUD lists it as TX480. Its vouchers can be used "within Travis County." As of November 3, 2026, its Housing Choice Voucher waitlist page reads "CLOSED" and "NOTICE: Our waitlist is not currently open." HATC says its list "is organized by a lottery system" and that applying earlier "does not increase your chances." Its affordable housing properties keep their own lists, linked from hatctx.com/waitlists.
  • The Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs (TDHCA) runs a separate Section 8 program for parts of Texas without a local housing authority. HUD lists it as TX901, at 221 E. 11th Street in Austin.
  • AHA is not an emergency rent fund. AHA says: "We do not offer emergency housing, but there are other organizations that do." If you are behind on rent, see our Austin emergency rent help guide for who still pays rent in Austin, free eviction lawyers, and Texas's 2026 eviction timeline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Austin Housing Authority Section 8 waitlist open?

No. As of November 3, 2026, AHA's Housing Choice Voucher page reads "Waitlist Applications Closed," and its How do I apply? page says "All Austin Housing Authority waitlists are currently closed." AHA says that when a list reopens it will make a public announcement and run a lottery among the people who apply. No opening date was posted. Until one is, AHA says "If no housing waitlist opening is posted on our website, we are not currently accepting applications." The Housing Authority of Travis County's voucher list was also closed on November 3, 2026.

Is HACA the same as the Austin Housing Authority?

Yes. The Housing Authority of the City of Austin (HACA) now goes by Austin Housing Authority (AHA). Its old hacanet.org address forwards to austinha.org, HUD lists PHA TX001 as Austin Housing Authority, and AHA's Administrative Plan still uses the legal name. The phone number did not change: (512) 477-4488, Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., at 1124 S. IH 35 in Austin.

How do I check my AHA waitlist status?

Online. AHA's waitlist status page says "You can check your status at any time by visiting the application portal" and links each list separately. Housing Choice Voucher applicants log in to AHA's Emphasys applicant portal at haca.myhousing.com. Each Pathways property has its own RentCafe status link, and for most properties there are separate links for people who applied before and after January 1, 2021. Keep your phone, email and mailing address current in the portal, because AHA uses them to reach you. For questions, call (512) 477-4488.

Who gets preference on the AHA voucher list?

AHA's Administrative Plan, revised May 29, 2025, gives each family at most one weighted local preference plus a residency preference. The weights include elderly 2, disabled 2, involuntarily displaced 2, families with minor children 2, homeless 3 and residency 1. The residency preference covers people who live in, work in, or have been hired to work in Travis, Hays, Bastrop, Caldwell or Williamson County. The homeless preference requires a referral from a homeless service provider that has an agreement with AHA, and up to 25 percent of vouchers issued each year go to it. AHA verifies preferences only when your name is drawn; if it cannot verify one, you go back on the list without it.

How long is the wait for AHA housing?

Years, in most cases. AHA's application portal says "All of AHA's housing programs have extensive waiting lists" and "Waiting times vary from as little as 6 months to over 3 years." Its Find Housing page says "you may be on the waiting list for a number of years." Apply to every list you qualify for when it opens, including the Housing Authority of Travis County and the Pathways properties, rather than waiting on one.

What is the difference between AHA and the Housing Authority of Travis County?

They are two separate agencies with separate waiting lists. AHA (HUD code TX001) is the City of Austin's housing authority. The Housing Authority of Travis County, or HATC (HUD code TX480), runs its own voucher program usable within Travis County, plus its own affordable properties. Being on one list does not put you on the other. HATC is at 502 East Highland Mall Boulevard, Suite 106-B, (512) 854-8245, and its voucher list was closed on November 3, 2026.

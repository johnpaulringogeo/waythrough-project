Quick Facts

Serves: The City of Pittsburgh
Phone: (412) 456-5000
Official site: hacp.org/
Apply: HACP application page
Status last checked: November 5, 2026

The Housing Authority of the City of Pittsburgh (HACP) runs Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers, public housing and project-based vouchers inside the City of Pittsburgh. HUD lists it as PHA PA001 with a program type of Combined. Its main office and One-Stop Shop are at 412 Boulevard of the Allies, Pittsburgh, PA 15219, and its main line is 412-456-5000. The short version today: HACP says its Housing Choice Voucher waiting list is closed, but it lists open public housing waiting lists at eleven sites and open project-based voucher lists at six properties. Public housing applications go to HACP by email, fax, mail or drop-off; project-based voucher lists use an online pre-application. If you live in Allegheny County outside the city, the Allegheny County Housing Authority runs separate lists, and its voucher list is closed too. This page covers HACP's exact words, how to apply to each kind of list, the phone numbers, and where to turn if you need help with rent now.

Programs HACP Runs

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8); the waiting list is closed
  • Public housing (HACP calls it LIPH) at family communities, high rises and scattered sites, each with its own site-based waiting list
  • Project-Based Vouchers at partner properties, applied for with an online pre-application at hacp.myhousing.com
  • Homeownership Program for public housing residents, voucher holders and some eligible families: up to $7,000 for closing costs and a soft second mortgage of up to $52,000, for a home within Pittsburgh city limits
  • Resident Self-Sufficiency, including Family Self-Sufficiency, at 2305 Bedford Avenue, 412-395-3950

How to Apply

Apply directly through the authority's official application page: hacp.org/housing/apply-for-housing/. If you have already applied, check your place on HACP's Applicant Portal or its automated line, 412-520-3848. HACP says it "will no longer check your applicant status over the phone." Keep your contact information current. If HACP cannot reach you when your name comes up, you can be dropped from the list.

New to the process? Our step-by-step guide to applying for Section 8 walks through the whole thing, and how to find your PHA helps you locate the right authority.

Other Authorities Serving This Area

  • Allegheny County Housing Authority (ACHA), HUD code PA006, 301 Chartiers Avenue, McKees Rocks, PA 15136, (412) 355-8940, TTY 800-833-5833 or 711, runs Section 8 and public housing for Allegheny County; ACHA says it "owns and manages 3,300 units, administers over 5,000 vouchers" in "neighborhoods throughout Allegheny County." If you live in the suburbs, this is your agency. Its voucher page said on November 5, 2026: "THE HCVP WAITING LIST IS NOW CLOSED." ACHA randomly sorted 6,791 voucher applications received February 4 to 9, 2026 and kept the top 2,000 on its list.
  • ACHA's public housing applications are taken online at achsng.myhousing.com, and ACHA says applicants are placed on the list "according to the date and time of application." Its site does not say whether that list is open or closed today, so check the portal or call (412) 355-8940. ACHA notes: "The ACHA does not have any emergency housing."
  • If you are homeless or about to be, call Allegheny County's Allegheny Link at 866-730-2368, Monday through Friday, 8:00 am to 5:00 pm, or go to One Smithfield Street, First Floor. The County says the Link manages openings for family shelters.
  • HACP is not an emergency rent fund. If you are behind on rent, see our Pittsburgh emergency rent help guide: ACTION-Housing's eviction program, Neighborhood Legal Services, 211, LIHEAP and Pennsylvania's eviction deadlines. If you already have HACP housing or a voucher and fall behind, call HACP at 412-456-5000.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Pittsburgh Section 8 waiting list open?

No. As of November 5, 2026, HACP says its Housing Choice Voucher Program waiting list is closed and that it cannot accept applications while it is closed. No reopening date is posted. HACP does have open public housing lists at eleven sites and open project-based voucher lists at six properties, so you can still apply for those. If you live outside the city, the Allegheny County Housing Authority's voucher list is closed too.

Which HACP public housing lists are open?

HACP lists Arlington Heights, Caliguiri High Rise, Carrick Regency, Finello Pavilion, Gualtieri Manor, Mazza Pavilion, Morse Gardens, Northview Heights, PA Bidwell High Rise, Pressley Street High Rise and Scattered Sites, each for certain bedroom sizes. Lists can close on short notice: HACP closed the Christopher Smith Terrace list on October 16, 2026. Check HACP's Apply for Housing page before you send your application.

How do I apply to HACP?

For public housing, fill out HACP's application and email it to occupancy@hacp.org, fax it to 412-456-5182, or mail or drop it off at the One-Stop Shop, 412 Boulevard of the Allies. For a project-based voucher list, submit an online pre-application at hacp.myhousing.com. If you need a disability accommodation to apply, HACP lists 412-456-5282.

How do I check my place on an HACP list?

Use the HACP Applicant Portal or call HACP's automated line at 412-520-3848. HACP says it will no longer check applicant status over the phone with staff, and that only applicants with a current application can see their number on the list. First-time users need to create an account.

I live outside Pittsburgh city limits. Where do I apply?

Apply to the Allegheny County Housing Authority in McKees Rocks, (412) 355-8940. Its Housing Choice Voucher list is closed after a February 2026 lottery that kept the top 2,000 of 6,791 applications. Its public housing applications are taken online through its portal at achsng.myhousing.com; call to ask which lists are taking names.

What does a Section 8 tenant pay in Pittsburgh?

Under HUD rules, a family with a voucher or in public housing usually pays about 30% of its adjusted monthly income toward rent and utilities, and the housing authority pays the rest of an approved rent. Adjusted income is your income after HUD's deductions, such as those for children, elderly or disabled family members, and some medical and child care costs.

Housing Authority

New York City Housing Authority (NYCHA)

Serves New York City (all five boroughs) · Last checked October 16, 2026

Waythrough is an independent resource, not NYCHA. We summarize public information to help you get oriented. For applications and the most current waiting-list status, always use the official links below.

The New York City Housing Authority (NYCHA) runs public housing and the largest Section 8 (Housing Choice Voucher) program in the country for all five boroughs. We checked NYCHA's own site on October 16, 2026: the Section 8 waitlist is closed to new applicants, and voucher issuance to people already on that list is still paused. NYCHA public housing is different: you can apply online right now through ApplyNYCHA on NYCHA's Self-Service Portal. NYCHA's phone number for applicants, residents and voucher holders is the Customer Contact Center, (718) 707-7771.

Waiting-List Status

Waiting lists open and close frequently, so treat the status below as a starting point and confirm on the official site before you rely on it.

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8): Closed to new applicants; voucher issuance paused — NYCHA's Applying for Section 8 page says: "The New York City Housing Authority (NYCHA) Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) waitlist is currently closed to new applicants." (checked October 16, 2026). The last window was online from June 3 to June 9, 2024, the first general opening since December 10, 2009. NYCHA reported 633,808 online applications and said "200,000 applications were randomly selected by lottery" for the list. Then, NYCHA says, "Effective August 1, 2025, NYCHA paused active outreach, including activities related to the application process and voucher issuance for applicants on the general HCV waitlist." Its Section 8 Program Updates page says the pause "remains in effect," that you "will stay on the waitlist and your position will remain unchanged," and that "NYCHA is not currently issuing new tenant-based Section 8 vouchers to those on the HCV waitlist." NYCHA will contact applicants when it resumes.
  • Public Housing: Taking applications online through ApplyNYCHA — NYCHA's Apply page says: "To be considered for an apartment in a public housing development, you must submit an application via ApplyNYCHA." (checked October 16, 2026). You pick a first and second borough, not a specific development. You do not send documents with the application; NYCHA checks them at your Eligibility Interview. There is no minimum income, and applicants who live or work in New York City are prioritized. NYCHA sends a letter within 30 days. Your application is active for two years, and you must renew it every 24 months on the Self-Service Portal ("Update/Renew My Case") to stay on the waitlist; you keep your original filing date. NYCHA warns: "Because of the extremely large size of the waitlist and low vacancy rate, we cannot estimate when you will be selected for an Eligibility Interview." You may be on the public housing and Section 8 lists at the same time.
  • Special Admission referrals (HUD-VASH, Stability Vouchers): By referral only — NYCHA says it "will continue to accept and process referrals" under Veterans Affairs Supportive Housing (VASH) and Stability Vouchers. These come through partner agencies (for veterans, the VA), not public sign-up.

Programs NYCHA Runs

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8): NYCHA says about 107,979 vouchers and over 26,933 owners take part
  • Public Housing (apply online through ApplyNYCHA)
  • Special Admission referrals: Veterans Affairs Supportive Housing (HUD-VASH) and Stability Vouchers
  • Emergency Housing Vouchers (EHV) for current participants only; HUD funding for EHV ends in late 2026

How to Apply

Apply directly through the authority's official application page: www.nyc.gov/site/nycha/eligibility/apply.page. If you have already applied, call (718) 707-7771 to verify your position and keep your contact information current — if the authority cannot reach you when your name comes up, you can be dropped from the list.

Other Authorities Serving This Area

  • NYCHA phone numbers and offices. NYCHA calls its Customer Contact Center (CCC) "NYCHA's single point of contact for public housing residents, Section 8 voucher holders, and Section 8 landlords": (718) 707-7771. For help with a public housing application, NYCHA lists the CCC from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday through Friday. Walk-in centers: 478 East Fordham Road (1 Fordham Plaza), 2nd Floor, Bronx, and 787 Atlantic Avenue, 2nd Floor, Brooklyn, both Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.; without internet you can book an appointment there and apply at a NYCHA kiosk. NYCHA's main switchboard is (212) 306-3000. Full list on NYCHA's contact page. Status checks and updates for both programs are on the NYCHA Self-Service Portal (selfserve.nycha.info). NYCHA says: "Never pay money to apply for public housing or Section 8"; report scams to NYCHA's Inspector General at (212) 306-3355.
  • HPD Section 8 is a second door, but not an open one. The NYC Department of Housing Preservation and Development (HPD) runs its own Section 8 program and says it serves over 39,000 households. HPD gives vouchers to families in buildings developed or renovated under certain HPD programs and to homeless households referred by DHS and HRA providers, and its eligibility page says: "Applications are not accepted by the general public." (checked October 16, 2026). If you live in an HPD-assisted building or a shelter, ask your building management or caseworker.
  • NYC Housing Connect for affordable lotteries. HPD calls NYC Housing Connect "New Yorkers' portal to find and apply for affordable rental and homeownership opportunities across the five boroughs." You register once and apply to each lottery that fits your income and household size. These are income-restricted apartments, not Section 8. Details on HPD's Housing Connect page.
  • Behind on rent or facing eviction. HRA's Emergency Assistance, which HRA also calls a "One Shot Deal," can help if you will lose your housing without help; you must have an interview, and you may have to pay some or all of it back. HRA says you can apply "online using ACCESSHRA" (ACCESS HRA) or at a Benefit Access Center; DSS OneNumber (718) 557-1399 (HRA cash assistance page). If you have an eviction case, NYC's Right-to-Counsel program gives free legal help "in every ZIP code, regardless of immigration status," in Housing Court and in NYCHA termination cases: call 311 and ask for the Tenant Helpline, or Housing Court Answers at (718) 557-1379. It is illegal for a landlord to refuse a voucher; report it through 311 to the NYC Commission on Human Rights. Our New York City emergency rent help guide walks through the One Shot Deal application, the documents and repayment rules, Homebase, CityFHEPS and Housing Court (checked October 28, 2026), and our New York City housing resources page has shelter and tenant-rights details.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the NYCHA Section 8 waiting list open?

No. NYCHA says its Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) waitlist "is currently closed to new applicants" (checked October 16, 2026). The last window was June 3 to June 9, 2024, when NYCHA received 633,808 online applications and picked 200,000 by lottery. Since August 1, 2025, NYCHA has paused voucher issuance to people on that list, and NYCHA says the pause remains in effect. NYCHA public housing is a separate list that is taking applications online.

How do I apply for NYCHA public housing?

Online through ApplyNYCHA on NYCHA's Self-Service Portal, selfserve.nycha.info. NYCHA says you "must submit an application via ApplyNYCHA." You choose a first and second borough, you do not need to send documents, and NYCHA mails a letter within 30 days. Without internet, book an appointment at a NYCHA Walk-in Center and apply at a kiosk. For help, call the Customer Contact Center at (718) 707-7771, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday through Friday. Renew your application every two years to stay on the list.

What is the NYCHA phone number?

The NYCHA Customer Contact Center is (718) 707-7771. NYCHA says it serves public housing residents, Section 8 voucher holders, Section 8 landlords and public housing applicants. NYCHA's main switchboard is (212) 306-3000. To report a scam or fraud, call NYCHA's Inspector General hotline at (212) 306-3355.

I am already on the NYCHA Section 8 waitlist. What should I do?

Keep your contact information current on the NYCHA Self-Service Portal at selfserve.nycha.info. NYCHA says applicants were not removed during the pause and "will stay on the waitlist and your position will remain unchanged," and that it will contact applicants in order of preference and placement when voucher issuance restarts. If NYCHA sends you a notice asking you to update your application, answer it by the deadline. For help updating your information, call (718) 707-7771.

What is the difference between NYCHA and HPD Section 8?

They are separate programs. NYCHA runs the largest Section 8 program in the country, and its waitlist is closed. The NYC Department of Housing Preservation and Development (HPD) runs its own Section 8 program for households in certain HPD-assisted buildings and homeless households referred by DHS and HRA providers; HPD says "Applications are not accepted by the general public." NYC Housing Connect is a third, separate door: lotteries for income-restricted apartments, not Section 8.

Where can I get affordable housing or rent help in NYC right now?

Apply for NYCHA public housing through ApplyNYCHA, and apply to affordable lotteries on NYC Housing Connect. If you are behind on rent, HRA's Emergency Assistance (One Shot Deal) can be requested online through ACCESS HRA or at a Benefit Access Center. If you have an eviction case, call 311 and ask for the Tenant Helpline about free Right-to-Counsel legal help.

The Housing Authority of the County of Kern is an independent local government agency that runs Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers, project-based vouchers, public housing (now converted to HUD's RAD program), farmworker housing and housing for people who are homeless across Kern County, including Bakersfield. It says it meets the housing needs of nearly 20,000 low-income Kern County residents and is a Moving to Work agency. Its central office is at 601 24th Street, Bakersfield, CA 93301, phone (661) 631-8500. The short version today: the Section 8 list is closed and the public housing list is closed, but the project-based voucher lists have been open since May 1, 2026, and you apply for each apartment complex separately at kernhousing.com. Some Housing Authority properties also list units ready to move in. This page covers the Housing Authority's exact words, each list, the phone numbers, and where to turn if you need rent help now.

Waiting-List Status

Waiting lists open and close often. The status below is what we found the last time we checked; confirm it on the official site before you count on it.

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8): Closed. Checked November 25, 2026: the Housing Authority's Housing Choice Voucher page says "The Section 8 Waiting List is closed and applications are not available for this program." No reopening date is posted. Its Wait List Information page says applicants "are randomly selected from the waiting list" and are contacted by email.
  • Project-Based Voucher (PBV) lists: Open. The Housing Authority's PBV page says "The waiting list for the Project-Based Voucher (PBV) Program is now open as of May 1, 2026, at 10:00 AM (PST). Applications are being accepted online." It says "Applicants will need to apply for each location individually during the time frame that the waiting list is open," and posts no closing date. Listed sites include 4th Street Senior Apartments, 22nd Street Lofts, Adelante Vista, Benton Park Cottages, Park Place Senior Apartments, Park Real Apartments, Pioneer Cottages, Residences at Old Town Kern, Residences at West Columbus, Rio Vista, South Baker Apartments, Village Park Senior Apartments and Villa San Dimas in Bakersfield, and sites in Delano, Arvin and McFarland. You need Social Security numbers and dates of birth for everyone, a phone number and an email address (checked November 25, 2026).
  • Low Income Public Housing (LIPH): Closed. The Housing Authority's public housing page says "The waiting list for the Low-Income Public Housing (LIPH) program is currently closed, and applications are not being accepted at this time." Its Wait List Information page says the public housing list "transitioned to the RAD program waiting lists" and that applicants "did not lose their position" (checked November 25, 2026).

Programs Housing Authority of the County of Kern Runs

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8): the waiting list is closed. The subsidy is the difference between 30% of the tenant's income and the Kern County payment standard or the gross rent, whichever is lower. Payment standards effective July 1, 2026 run from $1,132 for a studio to $2,861 for five bedrooms
  • Project-Based Vouchers (PBV): income-based assistance tied to specific apartments in Bakersfield, Delano, Arvin and McFarland; you lose it if you move out. The lists are open, one complex at a time
  • Public housing: rent is 30% of monthly adjusted income or a ceiling rent, whichever is less, with a $50 minimum. The old list moved to site-based RAD waiting lists, and applicants kept their original date and time
  • Affordable and senior housing managed by the Housing Authority, such as Pinewood Glen Retirement Community (55+), Westchester Senior Village and Park Place Senior Apartments (62+), with move-in-ready units listed on its Available Housing page
  • Farmworker housing year round, and Migrant Farm Labor Centers open about six months a year for seasonal farm workers whose permanent home is at least 50 miles away
  • Housing for people who are homeless, such as 6th Street Apartments and 889 Oak Street, reached through 2-1-1, and Building Blocks transitional housing for former foster youth ages 18 to 21

How to Apply

Apply on the authority's own application page: www.kernhousing.com/. If you are already on the Section 8 list, the Housing Authority says applicants "are randomly selected from the waiting list and therefore we are unable to determine how long you will have to wait," and that you "will be contacted by email when their application reaches near the top of the waiting list." Keep your email address current. If your family size or address changes while you wait, update your Section 8 or public housing application by logging in at the applicant portal; for help, call (661) 631-8500.

If this is your first time applying, our step-by-step guide to applying for Section 8 covers each stage, and how to find your PHA shows which authority covers where you live.

Other Authorities Serving This Area

  • The Housing Authority is not an emergency rent fund. If you are behind on rent inside Bakersfield city limits, call The Open Door Network Housing Department at 661-716-1020 or dial 2-1-1 about the City's Rental Assistance Program, and see our Bakersfield emergency rent help guide for CalWORKs help, Greater Bakersfield Legal Assistance, CAPK utility help and California's eviction deadlines.
  • Already have a voucher or Housing Authority housing and fell behind? Call the Housing Authority at (661) 631-8500 right away and call Greater Bakersfield Legal Assistance at (661) 325-5943. An eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • Disability accommodations and language help: the Housing Authority says you can request a reasonable accommodation for the online application at its office, 601 24th Street, or by calling 661-631-8500, during the whole application window. For free translation, call 661-631-8500 ext. 4101. Relay: TTY/Voice 1-800-855-7100, Spanish 1-800-855-7200.
  • Application kiosks: if you do not have a computer, the Housing Authority lists kiosks at its office (601 24th Street), the Kern County Child Support office (3701 N. Sillect Ave.) and the court building at 1215 Truxtun, Room 300.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Kern County Section 8 waiting list open?

No. As of November 25, 2026, the Housing Authority of the County of Kern says its Section 8 waiting list is closed and applications are not available, and no reopening date is posted. Its project-based voucher lists are open, though: apply online for each apartment complex you want.

How do I apply for a project-based voucher in Bakersfield?

Online at kernhousing.com. Choose Applicant Login, use the link to register, pick I want to Apply and I do not have a registration code, then fill in every page and submit. You get one email when the application is received and another when it is approved or rejected. Apply separately for each complex.

Is the Kern public housing list open?

No. The Housing Authority says the Low-Income Public Housing waiting list is currently closed. The old public housing list moved to site-based lists under HUD's RAD program, and people already on it kept their original date and time.

How long is the wait for Section 8 in Kern County?

The Housing Authority does not give an estimate. It says applicants are randomly selected from the waiting list, so it cannot say how long you will wait, and it contacts people by email when they near the top. Keep your email and address current in the applicant portal.

What does a Kern Section 8 voucher pay?

The Housing Authority pays the difference between 30% of your income and the payment standard or the gross rent, whichever is lower. Payment standards effective July 1, 2026 are $1,132 for a studio, $1,140 for one bedroom, $1,483 for two, $2,062 for three, $2,488 for four and $2,861 for five.

Can a Bakersfield landlord refuse my voucher?

Not because it is a voucher. California Government Code 12955 bans housing discrimination based on source of income, and it names Section 8 vouchers and HUD-VASH vouchers. If a landlord turns you away for that reason, file a complaint with the California Civil Rights Department.

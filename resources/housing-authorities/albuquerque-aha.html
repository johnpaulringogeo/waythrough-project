Albuquerque Housing Authority (AHA)

Serves Albuquerque, New Mexico, with Section 8 vouchers also used in Rio Rancho and Bernalillo County · Last checked October 15, 2026

Waythrough is an independent resource, not AHA. We summarize public information to help you get oriented. For applications and the most current waiting-list status, always use the official links below.

The Albuquerque Housing Authority is the public housing agency for the City of Albuquerque. HUD lists it as PHA NM001, under the name City of Albuquerque Housing Authority, with a program type of Combined, so it runs both Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers and public housing, from one office at 1840 University Blvd SE. This month is unusual. AHA's How to Apply page, updated October 15, 2026, lists a Section 8 pre-application window of October 16 through November 15, 2026, and a public housing window that is already running, October 5 through October 31, 2026. Both are online only. This page covers what each window means, the annual Section 8 lottery that wipes the pool every year, the points system that orders the public housing list, and the two project-based properties taking applications today.

Waiting-List Status

Waiting lists open and close frequently, so treat the status below as a starting point and confirm on the official site before you rely on it.

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8): Pre-application window listed for October 16 to November 15, 2026 — Checked October 15, 2026. AHA's How to Apply page reads: "Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) OPEN October 16, 2026 through November 15, 2026." On the day we checked, the window had not started yet and the page carried apply buttons only for public housing and project-based vouchers, so look for the Section 8 link on that page from October 16. AHA says "Pre-applications will be taken ONLINE ONLY" and "Walk-ins will not be accepted." This is a lottery, not a line: AHA's Section 8 Administrative Plan says "Applicants will be placed on the waiting list using a lottery system," and that "On an annual basis, the Housing Choice Voucher program's application pool and waiting list are purged." The earlier windows AHA's document library records ran in June 2021, September 2022, June 2023, and July 15 to August 15, 2024.
  • Public Housing: Open October 5 to October 31, 2026 — Checked October 15, 2026. The How to Apply page reads: "Public Housing Units OPEN October 5, 2026 through October 31, 2026," with the apply button pointing to AHA's online portal at myhousing.abqha.org. AHA keeps one community-wide public housing list, not separate lists by site, and orders it by preference points and then by the date and time your complete application is received.
  • Project-Based Vouchers (PBV): Accepting pre-applications at two properties — AHA's How to Apply page lists "Project Based Voucher (PBV) Program OPEN Until Further Notice." As of October 15, 2026, its PBV page named two properties taking applications: Felician 2, 1325 Stephanie Rd SE in Rio Rancho, one and two bedrooms, for households with at least one member age 55 or older, with a preference for active military and veterans; and Casitas de Camino, 221 60th St NW, two to four bedrooms, with a preference for veterans and households with children. The same page listed 18 other PBV properties that are "not currently taking applications." A PBV is tied to the unit and does not move with you.
  • Affordable Housing Units: Closed — The How to Apply page reads: "Affordable Housing Units CLOSED Until Further Notice."

Programs AHA Runs

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8), which AHA says helps over 4,000 Albuquerque households
  • Public Housing: about 950 AHA-owned units, including 373 designated for elderly and disabled residents
  • Project-Based Vouchers (PBV) tied to units at specific apartment communities
  • Rental Assistance Demonstration (RAD) and tax credit communities such as Broadway/McKnight, Harper, La Plata and Veranda
  • Veterans housing (HUD-VASH) and Family Self-Sufficiency

How to Apply

Apply directly through the authority's official application page: abqha.org/how-to-apply/. If you have already applied, call (505) 764-3920 to verify your position and keep your contact information current — if the authority cannot reach you when your name comes up, you can be dropped from the list.

New to the process? Our step-by-step guide to applying for Section 8 walks through the whole thing, and how to find your PHA helps you locate the right authority.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Albuquerque Section 8 waiting list open?

A window is scheduled. As of October 15, 2026, AHA's How to Apply page lists Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher pre-applications as "OPEN October 16, 2026 through November 15, 2026." On the day we checked, the window had not started and there was no Section 8 apply button yet, only buttons for public housing and project-based vouchers. Pre-applications are online only, there is no paper form, and AHA says walk-ins will not be accepted. If the dates move, AHA's page is the source to trust, not this one.

How does the AHA Section 8 lottery work?

Applying early does not help. AHA's Section 8 Administrative Plan for fiscal year 2027 says "Applicants will be placed on the waiting list using a lottery system," with each application randomly assigned a number, and that "random drawings will occur from the application pool to create the waitlist," with the number drawn based on need and funding. The plan also says the pool and waiting list are purged every year and "All applicants who are not selected will have to re-apply to the HCV program during the next application period." So if you applied in an earlier year and were never selected, you must apply again in this window.

Can I apply for public housing in Albuquerque right now?

Yes, until October 31, 2026. AHA's How to Apply page reads "Public Housing Units OPEN October 5, 2026 through October 31, 2026," and the apply button goes to AHA's online portal, myhousing.abqha.org. AHA keeps a single community-wide public housing list. Within it, families are ranked by preference points and then by the date and time AHA receives the complete application, so finishing your application earlier in the window does matter here, unlike the Section 8 lottery.

Which preferences does AHA use?

They differ by program. For public housing, AHA's Admissions and Continued Occupancy Policy gives first preference to families involuntarily displaced by government action, a presidentially declared disaster, or fire, explosion or condemnation, to families displaced by reprisals or hate crimes, to domestic violence victims referred by the Bernalillo County District Attorney's Victims Advocate Office or the U.S. Attorney's Office, and to families referred by the state's Keeping Families Together project. A second preference worth two points goes to working families, part-time or full-time students, and households headed by someone 62 or older or with a disability, and a family can get only one set of those points. For Section 8, the Administrative Plan names one local preference: families terminated from AHA's program because of insufficient funding. Federal rules also require that at least 75 percent of new voucher families be extremely low income.

What are the income limits?

AHA publishes 2026 limits on its Eligibility page. For Section 8, income cannot exceed 50 percent of area median income: $35,150 for one person, $40,200 for two, $45,200 for three and $50,200 for four. For public housing the cap is 80 percent: $56,250 for one person, $64,250 for two, $72,300 for three and $80,300 for four. The page lists limits up to eight people.

What is the wait list notification form?

It is a contact sign-up, not an application. AHA's Waiting List Notification page collects your name, address, phone and email so it can tell you when a list opens, and the form itself says it "is NOT an application for any AHA program and this did NOT put me on a WAITLIST." Use it to hear about future windows, but apply through the How to Apply page when a window is open.

What number should I call?

(505) 764-3920 is AHA's main number. It appears on AHA's homepage, its Contact Us page, its Waiting List Notification page and its applicant portal, and HUD's directory lists the same number for NM001. If you need a reasonable accommodation because of a disability, AHA's waiting list page gives (505) 764-3953 or update@abqha.org. The office at 1840 University Blvd SE is open 8:00 am to 4:00 pm, Monday through Friday, but pre-applications are online only. For an after-hours maintenance emergency in public housing, call (505) 857-3718.

Does AHA charge a fee to apply?

No. AHA's How to Apply page says "AHA DOES NOT CHARGE TO APPLY FOR SERVICES" and "If someone asks for a payment to apply for services, they do not represent AHA." Its homepage adds that AHA will not call tenants or landlords asking for a credit card payment over the phone. Report fraud to AHA at (877) 778-5463.

Housing Authority

Omaha Housing Authority (OHA)

Serves Omaha, Ralston, Papillion, La Vista, Millard and Douglas County, Nebraska · Last checked October 15, 2026

Waythrough is an independent resource, not OHA. We summarize public information to help you get oriented. For applications and the most current waiting-list status, always use the official links below.

The Omaha Housing Authority, legally the Housing Authority of the City of Omaha, is the public housing agency for Omaha. HUD lists it as PHA NE001 with a program type of Combined, so it runs both Housing Choice Vouchers and public housing, and OHA says it administers about 4,300 vouchers and about 2,760 public housing homes. Its central office, with the Public Housing Intake and Section 8 departments, is at 1823 Harney Street. The short version today: the Section 8 list is closed, and public housing is open only for one-bedroom apartments in OHA's senior towers, for people 62 and older. This page covers OHA's exact words on both, how OHA announces a voucher opening and runs its lottery, the preference points on the public housing list, and the two other housing agencies in the metro that people often confuse with OHA.

Waiting-List Status

Waiting lists open and close frequently, so treat the status below as a starting point and confirm on the official site before you rely on it.

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8): Closed — Closed (checked October 15, 2026). OHA's homepage and its Contact page both read: "The Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) wait list is currently closed." OHA's applicant portal says: "Currently, OHA's Section 8 wait list is closed." No OHA page gives a closing date or a reopening date. The most recent opening OHA documents ran from 8:00 a.m. on Tuesday, August 8, 2023 to noon on Monday, August 14, 2023, followed by a lottery of "no less than 1,500 names."
  • Public Housing: Open only for one-bedroom senior tower apartments, ages 62 and older — OHA's Public Housing page reads: "Effective January 1, 2025, the Public Housing waiting list is only open for one bedroom apartments in our Senior Towers to applicants ages 62 and older." The homepage and the applicant portal repeat it, checked October 15, 2026. Applications go in through OHA's online portal at myportal.ohauthority.org, or with help in person at 1823 Harney Street, 8 am to 4 pm, Monday through Friday. The list runs in date and time order, and preference points can move an application up.

Programs OHA Runs

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8), about 4,300 vouchers
  • Public Housing, about 2,760 homes including senior towers, apartments, town homes, duplexes and single-family houses
  • HUD-VASH vouchers for veterans, by referral from the VA
  • Affordable and market-rate housing at OHA properties, with their own applications

How to Apply

Apply directly through the authority's official application page: myportal.ohauthority.org/. If you have already applied, call (402) 444-6900 to verify your position and keep your contact information current — if the authority cannot reach you when your name comes up, you can be dropped from the list.

New to the process? Our step-by-step guide to applying for Section 8 walks through the whole thing, and how to find your PHA helps you locate the right authority.

Other Authorities Serving This Area

  • Douglas County Housing Authority is a separate agency, HUD code NE153, program type Combined, at 3211 N 90th Street, Omaha, NE 68134, (402) 444-6203. It describes its area as "the subdivisions and small community jurisdictions in the greater Omaha metropolitan area," and its own site said on October 15, 2026: "We are NOT accepting Section 8 Applications."
  • Bellevue Housing Authority, HUD code NE174, program type Combined, serves Bellevue in Sarpy County from 8214 Armstrong Circle, (402) 734-5448. HUD's Nebraska list has no separate Sarpy County housing authority.
  • Municipal Housing Agency of Council Bluffs, across the river in Iowa, HUD code IA023, program type Combined, 505 S Sixth St., (712) 322-1491.
  • OHA is not an emergency rent fund. The City of Omaha's HOME Tenant-Based Rental Assistance, which OHA runs with City funds, "can provide up to 24 months of assistance," but the City says "All applicants for this program are to be referred to OHA by an existing transitional housing or support service program" (checked October 16, 2026), so you cannot apply to it directly. If you are behind on rent, dial 211 and see our Omaha emergency rent help guide: Douglas County General Assistance, Together's walk-in Housing Stability Clinic, Heartland Family Service, free eviction help at the courthouse, and Nebraska's 7-day notice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Omaha Section 8 waiting list open?

No. As of October 15, 2026, OHA's homepage and Contact page say: "The Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) wait list is currently closed." OHA's applicant portal says the same thing in different words. OHA does not publish a closing date or a reopening date. The most recent opening it documents ran from August 8 to August 14, 2023, so treat any other date you see online as unconfirmed unless OHA's own site shows it.

How will I know when OHA opens the voucher list?

OHA says that when the list opens, it will be published in the Omaha World-Herald Sunday paper, the Omaha Star and El Perico, and on OHA's website, at least seven business days before applications are accepted. You then fill out a pre-application on OHA's portal and select the Section 8 wait list. After the window closes, OHA says "a computerized lottery system will randomly choose a predetermined number of pre-applicants to fill out the full application." So applying on the first day does not put you ahead.

Can I apply for public housing in Omaha right now?

Only for one bedroom in a senior tower, and only if you are 62 or older. OHA's Public Housing page says that effective January 1, 2025, the waiting list "is only open for one bedroom apartments in our Senior Towers to applicants ages 62 and older." You can apply online at myportal.ohauthority.org, or get help in person at 1823 Harney Street from 8 am to 4 pm on weekdays. For help by phone, OHA gives (402) 444-6900, extension 2285 or 2215.

How does OHA order the public housing list?

By date and time, adjusted by preference points. OHA's public housing application page says "Applications are placed on the waiting list in date/time order. Preference points can move an application higher on the waiting list," and that preferences are available for Mobility, Displaced, and Senior (62 and older), if you are eligible and provide documentation. OHA also lists as ineligible anyone who "withdrawn from the waiting list within the past six months," so think twice before withdrawing.

How long is the wait once I am on the voucher list?

OHA's own pages disagree. Its Wait List Information page says "Currently the estimated wait for assistance is six months to two years," while its Section 8 FAQ says OHA tries to keep the list at a number where the wait is normally two to six months. Neither is a promise. OHA also says plainly that "The Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) Program is not an emergency housing program," so if you need housing now, look at shelter and rental assistance first.

What number should I call?

(402) 444-6900 is OHA's main number. It is in the footer of every OHA page, in the body of its Contact, Housing Vouchers and Landlords pages, and on its applicant portal, and HUD's directory lists the same number for NE001. OHA publishes no separate Section 8 phone line; for voucher questions it gives the email contactsect8@ohauthority.org. The office is at 1823 Harney Street, and OHA notes that the entrance to the Intake Department is in the back of the building.

Does OHA cover Douglas County, or is that a different agency?

Both, which confuses people. OHA's statement of policies says "The jurisdiction of the OHA is the city of Omaha, Ralston, Papillion, LaVista, Millard, and the County of Douglas." The Douglas County Housing Authority, HUD code NE153, is a separate agency that serves smaller communities around the metro, and its own site said on October 15, 2026 that it is not accepting Section 8 applications either. If you live in Bellevue, the Bellevue Housing Authority runs its own program.

What should I bring to a Section 8 appointment?

OHA's application process page lists a picture ID, Social Security cards or proof of Social Security numbers for everyone in the household, birth certificates for members under 18, documentation of all income and assets, proof of citizenship or eligible immigration status, and proof of custody of children, plus disability verification, a marriage certificate or a divorce decree if they apply. Everyone must pass a criminal background check. If you are missing a document, OHA says you must provide it within the time it gives you.

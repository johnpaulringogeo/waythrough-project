Quick Facts

Serves: the city of Seattle, Washington
Phone: (206) 239-1674
Official site: www.seattlehousing.org/
Apply: SHA application page
Status last checked: November 3, 2026

The Seattle Housing Authority (SHA) is the public housing agency for the city of Seattle. HUD lists it as PHA WA001 with a program type of Combined, so it runs both Housing Choice Vouchers and public housing. SHA says it provides housing or rental assistance to 37,774 people in 18,138 households, owns and operates 8,777 apartments and homes at 376 sites, and administers 8,144 Housing Choice Vouchers. Its central office is at 101 Elliott Ave W, Suite 100. Seattle works differently from most cities: there is no voucher waiting list to wait out. SHA says "The opportunity to apply for a chance to receive a voucher is ongoing. Families and individuals may apply at any time," and it draws names at random whenever vouchers are available (checked November 3, 2026). The catch is that you must check in once a month with Save My Spot, or SHA removes you. SHA's own apartments, through the Low-Income Public Housing and Seattle Senior Housing programs, also take applications, but SHA says the wait is "usually" no shorter than two years. This page covers SHA's exact words on both, its preferences, how to apply, and the King County Housing Authority, the separate agency for the suburbs, whose lists are all closed.

Waiting-List Status

Waiting lists open and close frequently, so treat the status below as a starting point and confirm on the official site before you rely on it.

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8): Taking applications at any time; names are drawn at random — Checked November 3, 2026. SHA's How to Apply page reads: "The opportunity to apply for a chance to receive a voucher is ongoing. Families and individuals may apply at any time. Applicants will be randomly selected from the voucher list whenever vouchers are available." It adds: "The chance of being drawn is the same no matter when households apply." SHA's FAQ says "You can apply to be on the SHA voucher list anytime" and "There is no ranking on the SHA voucher list and no limit to when applications are accepted or the number of applicants on the list," so "there is no way to estimate if and when you will be drawn." To stay on the list, SHA says "you must also check in once every month using Save My Spot"; "If you do not do this or we are unable to contact you, we will remove you from the list." You apply online through the SHA Portal, and the application is free.
  • Low-Income Public Housing and Seattle Senior Housing: Taking applications for up to two properties in each program; every property has a wait — SHA's housing page says "All properties have a wait time" and "Usually wait time is no shorter than two years, and often longer" (checked November 3, 2026). You may apply for up to two Low-Income Public Housing properties and, if you are eligible, up to two Seattle Senior Housing Program properties. SHA's property list shows an average wait for each building and bedroom size; on November 3, 2026 it ranged from one to two years at some buildings to five to six years at others, and some sizes were marked "Rarely available" or "Undetermined." These lists also require the monthly Save My Spot check-in. Questions go to SHA Housing admissions at (206) 239-1737.
  • Special Portfolio and Tax Credit apartments: Separate applications, property by property — SHA says its standard housing application is not for the Special Portfolio or Tax Credit programs, which "have a different application process." On November 3, 2026, SHA's homepage said it "is now leasing tax-credit homes at Yesler," with one-, two- and three-bedroom homes and Housing Choice Vouchers accepted. Tax Credit rent is not subsidized, but SHA says there is no citizenship requirement for that housing.

Programs SHA Runs

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8), 8,144 vouchers, with applicants drawn at random
  • Low-Income Public Housing, in apartment buildings, smaller buildings, town homes and a few single-family homes across Seattle
  • Seattle Senior Housing Program, for people 62 and older and adults with disabilities
  • Special Portfolio and Tax Credit apartments, with their own applications
  • Special purpose vouchers, such as HUD-VASH for veterans, by referral
  • Collaborative Housing with partner housing providers

How to Apply

Apply directly through the authority's official application page: www.seattlehousing.org/housing/housing-choice-vouchers/how-apply-housing-choice-voucher. If you have already applied, there is no place in line to check, because SHA says "There is no ranking on the SHA voucher list"; to stay on it you must check in once every month with Save My Spot and keep your contact information current in the SHA Portal, or SHA will remove you from the list.

New to the process? Our step-by-step guide to applying for Section 8 walks through the whole thing, and how to find your PHA helps you locate the right authority.

Other Authorities Serving This Area

  • Preferences on the voucher list. SHA checks eligibility only when your name is drawn. It serves first households with income at or below 30 percent of Area Median Income, or that are homeless, at the time of selection or in the 12 months before. SHA says applicants at 31 through 50 percent "will be returned to the list until all eligible applicants who meet the preferences have been served," and applicants above 50 percent "are not eligible for a voucher and will be removed from the list." You do not have to live in Seattle to apply, but a new voucher must be used inside Seattle for at least one year.
  • King County Housing Authority (KCHA) is a separate agency, HUD code WA002, at 600 Andover Park W., Tukwila, WA 98188, main line (206) 574-1100 (on its Contact page and in HUD's directory); its Contact page also lists a Section 8 office at 700 Andover Park W., (206) 214-1300. KCHA says it serves King County "not including Seattle or Renton," which takes in cities such as Bellevue, Kent, Federal Way and Shoreline. Its waitlist page read on November 3, 2026: "All waitlists are currently closed and we are not accepting any new applications." For vouchers, KCHA says "We do not know when the waiting list will re-open in the future," and that when it does open, you "can only apply during a specified two-week period prior to a random lottery drawing."
  • Renton Housing Authority serves Renton, which neither SHA nor KCHA covers. HUD lists it as WA011, 2900 NE 10th Street, Renton, (425) 226-1850 ext. 220. Farther out, Tacoma (WA005) and Everett (WA006) have their own housing authorities and their own lists.
  • SHA is not an emergency rent fund. SHA says "Since the wait for a voucher is uncertain, it is advisable to seek other option for housing assistance if you are in need." If you are behind on rent, see our Seattle emergency rent help guide: Keep King County Housed through United Way of King County, free eviction lawyers, and the 14-day notice rules.
  • Never pay to apply. SHA warns: "Application is free. If any website asks for money to complete an application, it is not the correct site." Type seattlehousing.org into your browser yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Seattle Section 8 waiting list open?

SHA does not open and close its voucher list. Its How to Apply page says "The opportunity to apply for a chance to receive a voucher is ongoing. Families and individuals may apply at any time" (checked November 3, 2026). Anyone 18 or older, or an emancipated minor, can apply online through the SHA Portal at seattlehousing.org. Being on the list is not the same as getting a voucher: SHA draws names at random only when vouchers become available, and it says "there is no way to estimate if and when you will be drawn from the list."

Does applying early move me up the SHA list?

No. SHA says "The chance of being drawn is the same no matter when households apply" and "There is no ranking on the SHA voucher list." Every household on the list has an equal chance in each drawing. What does matter is your income and housing situation when you are drawn: households at or below 30 percent of Area Median Income, or homeless now or in the past 12 months, are served first.

What is Save My Spot, and what happens if I miss a month?

Save My Spot is SHA's monthly check-in. SHA says applicants on the voucher list and on SHA property waitlists "are required to check in once every month," and "Applicants who do not check in once every month will be removed from the lists." You can check in online at savemyspot.seattlehousing.org with the head of household's Social Security number, or by phone at (206) 256-7000 or toll-free (833) 366-5925. SHA notes that leaving a phone message or using its contact form does not count as a check-in.

Do I have to live in Seattle to apply?

No. SHA's FAQ says you do not have to live in Seattle to apply. But if you are drawn and get a voucher, you must first use it inside the city of Seattle for at least one year. If you live in the suburbs and want to stay there, the King County Housing Authority covers King County outside Seattle and Renton, but all of its waitlists were closed on November 3, 2026.

How long is the wait for SHA public housing?

Years, in most cases. SHA says every one of its properties has a wait and that it is "usually" no shorter than two years, "and often longer." Its online property list shows the average wait by building and bedroom size; on November 3, 2026 those ranged from one to two years at some buildings to five to six years at others. You can apply to up to two Low-Income Public Housing properties and up to two Seattle Senior Housing properties, and you must check in monthly with Save My Spot. For admissions questions, call (206) 239-1737.

What phone number should I call at SHA?

For the voucher list, call (206) 239-1674 or email voucherlist@seattlehousing.org. That number appears on SHA's How to Apply page, its voucher FAQ and its Contact page, which says applicants can also ask there for a paper application, help in person, a disability accommodation or an interpreter. For SHA apartments, admissions is (206) 239-1737. SHA's central office is (206) 615-3300, the number HUD lists for WA001, at 101 Elliott Ave W, Suite 100, open Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.

If you are looking for Section 8 in Salt Lake City, there are two housing authorities to know, and they are separate agencies. The Housing Authority of Salt Lake City (HASLC), HUD code UT004, serves the city from 1776 S. West Temple. Housing Connect, HUD code UT003, was formerly the Housing Authority of the County of Salt Lake and serves the county. They have not merged, but they share one list for regular Section 8 vouchers: HASLC says "HASLC and Housing Connect (Salt Lake County Housing Authority) have a combined tenant-based HCV program waitlist," so one application puts you on the list for both. One more thing: HASLC's website is now haslcutah.org. The old haslc.com address now sends you to a housing authority in Missouri, so do not use it. This page explains what HASLC runs, the status of each list in its own words, and where to turn while the voucher list is closed.

Waiting-List Status

Waiting lists open and close frequently, so treat the status below as a starting point and confirm on the official site before you rely on it.

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8), Salt Lake County Consolidated list, shared with Housing Connect: Closed. Closed (checked November 5, 2026). The list is called the "Section 8 Waiting List – Salt Lake County Consolidated" and is hosted on AffordableHousing.com. HASLC's waitlists page says: "All waiting lists managed on AffordableHousing.com are currently closed to new applicants." Its programs page adds that "The waiting list for this program is often closed and only opens every several years because of funding limits." No reopening date is posted.
  • Mainstream and Non-Elderly Disabled vouchers: Closed. Both are on AffordableHousing.com, so the same statement applies: "All waiting lists managed on AffordableHousing.com are currently closed to new applicants" (checked November 5, 2026). HASLC says these vouchers are drawn from the regular tenant-based list.
  • Project-based vouchers at Pamela's Place, The Magnolia, Palmer Court and Wendell Apartments: Closed. These four project-based lists are also on AffordableHousing.com, which HASLC says are "currently closed to new applicants" (checked November 5, 2026).
  • Project-based vouchers on the HASLC Applicant Portal (The Aster, Atkinson Stacks/Sunrise Metro, Capitol Homes, Lake Street, Rendon Terrace, SPARK, Taylor Springs, Wasatch Manor): No open-or-closed status posted. HASLC's Applicant Portal says "Register here if you would like to be added to the project-based voucher waiting list for:" these buildings, but neither the portal nor the waitlists page says whether these lists are open or closed (checked November 5, 2026). HASLC's apply page also says Capitol Homes and Pamela's Place "require contacting the property directly." Call 801-487-2161 before you count on a list being open. Some permanent supportive housing units have "a homeless or chronic homeless preference."
  • Public housing: Phillips Plaza and Romney Plaza: No open-or-closed status posted. Both buildings are listed under "Public Housing Waiting Lists" on the HASLC Applicant Portal, and HASLC says "Both properties are for senior and/or disabled households only." No open-or-closed status is posted (checked November 5, 2026).
  • Housing Connect lists (Salt Lake County): Closed. Housing Connect lists its Housing Choice Voucher, Project-Based Voucher, RAD Project-Based Voucher and Public Housing programs and says: "All of these programs' waiting lists are currently closed and are not accepting applications" (checked November 5, 2026).

Programs HASLC Runs

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8), tenant-based, on a waiting list shared with Housing Connect
  • Mainstream and Non-Elderly Disabled vouchers, drawn from the same tenant-based list
  • Project-based vouchers: "almost 650 PBVs in Salt Lake County," including permanent supportive housing, VASH, senior and family units
  • Public housing at two buildings, Phillips Plaza and Romney Plaza, for senior and/or disabled households only
  • HUD-VASH (by VA referral) and Family Unification Program vouchers (by DCFS referral)
  • TANF Family Housing Program (short-term rental help for families with children)
  • About 1,000 HASLC-owned apartments with no subsidy

How to Apply

Apply directly through the authority's official application page: haslcutah.org/apply-for-housing/. If you are already on a tenant-based list, HASLC says to "check your status and, if applicable, update your contact information on AffordableHousing.com"; project-based and public housing applicants use the HASLC Applicant Portal. Keep your contact information current: HASLC warns that "Failure to keep your information current may result in being removed from the waiting list."

Other Authorities Serving This Area

  • Housing Connect (formerly the Housing Authority of the County of Salt Lake) serves Salt Lake County from 3595 South Main Street in South Salt Lake, (801) 284-4400, TDD (801) 284-4407, Monday to Friday 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. It owns and manages 184 public housing units across the county. All of its waiting lists are closed (checked November 5, 2026). It is a separate agency from HASLC; the two share only the tenant-based voucher list.
  • If you need a place to sleep, not a place on a list, Salt Lake City's Homeless Hotline is 801-990-9999. The City says to call it "If you or someone you know is experiencing or is at risk of experiencing homelessness." You can also dial 211 (or 888-826-9790).
  • Behind on rent? No City, County or state rent fund is open, but Utah Community Action can help with rent or a deposit after a short-term crisis: (801) 359-2444. Our Salt Lake City emergency rent help guide explains every door, free legal help, and Utah's 3-business-day eviction clock.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Housing Authority of Salt Lake City the same as Housing Connect?

No. They are two separate housing authorities. HASLC (HUD code UT004) serves Salt Lake City; Housing Connect (UT003), formerly the Housing Authority of the County of Salt Lake, serves the county. What they share is one waiting list for tenant-based Section 8 vouchers: HASLC says "HASLC and Housing Connect (Salt Lake County Housing Authority) have a combined tenant-based HCV program waitlist," and that applicants are "automatically added to the waitlist for both Housing Authorities." Project-based and public housing lists are still separate.

Is the Salt Lake City Section 8 waiting list open?

No. As of November 5, 2026, HASLC says "All waiting lists managed on AffordableHousing.com are currently closed to new applicants," and that includes the shared Salt Lake County Consolidated Section 8 list. HASLC also says the list "is often closed and only opens every several years because of funding limits." Housing Connect says its own lists are "currently closed and are not accepting applications." When the shared list reopens, one application covers both agencies.

Can I apply for HASLC's project-based or public housing buildings now?

Maybe. HASLC's Applicant Portal invites you to "Register here if you would like to be added to the project-based voucher waiting list" for eight buildings, and it lists Phillips Plaza and Romney Plaza as public housing waiting lists. But HASLC does not post whether these lists are open or closed. Call 801-487-2161 to ask. Phillips Plaza and Romney Plaza are "for senior and/or disabled households only."

What is the HASLC website? I found haslc.com.

HASLC's website is haslcutah.org. The old haslc.com address now redirects to a different agency, a housing authority in St. Louis County, Missouri. Use haslcutah.org, call 801-487-2161, or visit 1776 S. West Temple, Salt Lake City, Monday to Friday from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.

How do I check my place on the list?

If you are on a tenant-based list, HASLC says to "check your status and, if applicable, update your contact information on AffordableHousing.com." For project-based and public housing lists, log in to the HASLC Applicant Portal. Keep your address and phone current: HASLC warns that "Failure to keep your information current may result in being removed from the waiting list." HASLC also notes that "Placement on the waitlist does not mean that you are eligible for assistance."

Can a Salt Lake City landlord refuse my voucher?

Usually not. Utah's Fair Housing Act lists source of income as a protected class (Utah Code 57-21-5) and defines it to include "rental assistance or rent supplements" (57-21-2). Some small owners are exempt, such as an owner who rents out a single-family home without a real estate agent and owns fewer than four (57-21-3). If you are refused, contact the Utah Antidiscrimination and Labor Division at 801-530-6800.

Quick Facts

Serves: the City of Orlando and Orange County applicants, and (under a management agreement) the Sanford Housing Authority
Phone: (407) 895-3300
Official site: www.orlandohousing.org/
Apply: OHA application page
Status last checked: November 5, 2026

The Orlando Housing Authority (OHA) is the public housing agency for the City of Orlando. HUD lists it as PHA FL004 at 390 North Bumby Avenue, Orlando, FL 32803. OHA's phone is 407.895.3300 (TDD 407.894.9891), and its office is open Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. OHA has been a Moving to Work (MTW) agency since 2011, and its homepage says it is "Serving the Florida Cities of Orlando & Sanford," because it also manages the Sanford Housing Authority. Much of the rest of the metro is served by a different agency: Orange County's Housing and Community Development Division (HUD code FL093) runs its own Section 8 program, which we cover on this page too. The short version, checked November 5, 2026: OHA's public housing and Section 8 lists are closed, and so is Orange County's Section 8 list. The only lists open on OHA's portal are three Sanford Housing Authority project-based voucher lists. This page gives each program's status in the agency's own words, how to apply, the preferences, and the other housing authorities around Orlando.

Waiting-List Status

Waiting lists open and close frequently, so treat the status below as a starting point and confirm on the official site before you rely on it.

  • OHA Section 8 / Housing Choice Voucher: Closed — Checked November 5, 2026: OHA's Apply for Housing page says "OHA Section 8/Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) - CLOSED" and "OHA cannot predict when the waiting lists will reopen because it depends upon when current clients leave the programs." Its Public Housing FAQ says the voucher list "closed in June 2021." OHA's MTW Annual Plan counts 14,585 households on the voucher list and says there are no plans to open it during the plan year.
  • OHA Public Housing: Closed — OHA's Apply for Housing page says "OHA Public Housing Waiting Lists - CLOSED," and its Waiting List Information page says OHA's "public housing and section 8 program waiting lists are CLOSED" (checked November 5, 2026). OHA's MTW Annual Plan counts 12,420 households on the community-wide public housing list and 905 on the site-based lists for Carver Park, all closed.
  • Sanford Housing Authority (SHA) Project-Based Vouchers: Open (no closing date posted) — OHA's Apply for Housing page says "SHA Project Based Voucher (PBV) - OPEN." On November 5, 2026, OHA's MyHousing portal listed open waiting lists for Somerset Landings, Georgetown Square and Monroe Landings, each with "Start: 07/20/2026 12:00 AM" and no end date shown. These are project-based units in Sanford: the help stays with the apartment, not with you. OHA posts no closing date, so these lists can close without notice. Apply at oha.myhousing.com; if the lists no longer appear there, they have closed, and the page will say when another list opens.
  • Sanford Housing Authority Public Housing: Closed — OHA's Sanford Housing Authority page says "The Sanford Housing Authority Public Housing Waiting List is CLOSED" (checked November 5, 2026). Sanford's office is at 407.323.3150, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m.
  • Orange County Housing and Community Development Section 8 (a separate agency): Not accepting applications — Orange County's Section 8 page says "The Housing Choice Voucher Program (Section 8) is not accepting applications at this time" and "Current Status: We are not accepting applications at this time" (checked November 5, 2026). Its last opening ran May 9 to May 11, 2023, online only, and the County picked a final list of 1,000 applicants by random lottery. If you applied then and moved, the County says you must send your change of address in writing. Call (407) 836-5150 or email housing@ocfl.net.

Programs OHA Runs

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8): OHA says it administers "approximately 4,600" vouchers, about 3,900 tenant-based and about 600 project-based
  • Public housing: OHA says it "owns and operates over 1,450 public housing units, located in fourteen (14) housing complexes," including the Landings and Villas at Carver Park, Reeves Terrace, Lake Mann, Murchison Terrace, Ivey Lane, Omega, Marden Meadows, Villas at Hampton Park, Johnson Manor, Meadow Lake and Lorna Doone
  • Day-to-day management of the Sanford Housing Authority, including its public housing and its project-based voucher properties
  • Special-purpose vouchers listed in OHA's MTW Annual Plan: HUD-VASH for veterans, Mainstream vouchers, Emergency Housing Vouchers and Stability Vouchers
  • A project-based homeownership program at Carver Park and self-sufficiency services through OHA's MTW Resource Center

How to Apply

Apply directly through the authority's official application page: oha.myhousing.com/. If you are already on an OHA list, check your status in OHA's MyHousing portal at oha.myhousing.com. OHA says any change of address, phone number, income or family size must be reported within 10 business days and that "ALL CHANGES MUST BE IN WRITING. No changes will be taken over the phone." For portal help, call 407-894-1500 — if OHA cannot reach you when your name comes up, you can lose your place.

New to the process? Our step-by-step guide to applying for Section 8 walks through the whole thing, and how to find your PHA helps you locate the right authority.

Other Authorities Serving This Area

  • Orange County Housing and Community Development Division (HUD code FL093) runs a separate Section 8 program from 525 East South Street, Orlando, FL 32801, (407) 836-5150, housing@ocfl.net. Its Section 8 page says it is "not accepting applications at this time" (checked November 5, 2026). The County's 2026-2027 Action Plan says it served about 1,226 households with rental subsidies in fiscal year 2024-2025, plus 641 vouchers from other places and 55 HUD-VASH veterans. Orange County's Fair Housing ordinance also bans refusing a renter because of a "lawful source of income," which the County defines to include Section 8 and VASH vouchers; the County's tenant-rights notice applies it in unincorporated Orange County.
  • Seminole County Housing Authority (HUD code FL033): its site says "At this time our waiting lists for Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) and Public Housing are closed" and that "It will be several years before we will be able to reopen the list" (checked November 5, 2026).
  • Osceola County (HUD code FL201, Kissimmee, (407) 742-8400): its Section 8 page says "The Section 8 waiting list is currently closed, Osceola County is not accepting applications at this time" (checked November 5, 2026).
  • Housing Authority of the City of Winter Park (HUD code FL082, (407) 645-2869) runs public housing only; its site says "WPHA does not administer a Section 8 program" and lists the Meadows and Tranquil Terrace waiting lists as "CLOSED" (checked November 5, 2026).
  • OHA is not an emergency rent fund. Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Orlando Section 8 waiting list open?

No. As of November 5, 2026, the Orlando Housing Authority's Apply for Housing page says "OHA Section 8/Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) - CLOSED" and "OHA Public Housing Waiting Lists - CLOSED." OHA says it cannot predict when they will reopen. Orange County's separate Section 8 program is also "not accepting applications at this time." The only lists open on OHA's portal are three Sanford Housing Authority project-based voucher lists, which opened July 20, 2026 with no closing date posted.

What are the open Sanford project-based voucher lists?

On November 5, 2026, OHA's MyHousing portal at oha.myhousing.com showed open lists for Somerset Landings, Georgetown Square and Monroe Landings, Sanford Housing Authority properties, each starting July 20, 2026. With a project-based voucher, the rent help is tied to that apartment, so you live there to get it. Under standard HUD rules, your share of the rent is about 30% of your adjusted monthly income; ask the property how it figures your rent. No closing date is posted, so apply soon; if the lists are gone from the portal, they have closed.

Does the Orlando Housing Authority cover all of Orange County?

Not by itself. OHA is the City of Orlando's housing authority and also manages Sanford's. Orange County runs its own Section 8 program through its Housing and Community Development Division at (407) 836-5150, and that list is not accepting applications. OHA gives a public housing preference to people who live, work, or have a verified job offer in Orange County. Seminole County, Osceola County and Winter Park have their own housing authorities, and all of their lists were closed on November 5, 2026.

Who gets preference on OHA's lists?

OHA's Public Housing FAQ lists preferences for people with disabilities; people employed full-time for at least six continuous months, at least 28 hours a week; Orange County residents, workers or people with a verified job offer there; honorably discharged veterans; transitional housing graduates; former OHA homeownership participants; and a few others. OHA says wait time depends on your preferences and bedroom size and "cannot be determined." OHA's Administrative Plan and public housing policy have been temporarily removed from its website.

How do I check my place on OHA's list or change my address?

Use OHA's MyHousing portal at oha.myhousing.com. OHA says only people already on its waiting list can register an account, and an inactive pre-application means it was withdrawn. Report any change of address, phone number, income or family size within 10 business days, in writing; OHA says no changes are taken over the phone. For portal help, call 407-894-1500 or email partnerportal@orl-oha.org.

Can an Orlando landlord refuse my Section 8 voucher?

It depends where you live, and it is not settled. Orange County's Fair Housing ordinance (Code section 22-52) bans refusing to rent because of a lawful source of income, including Section 8 and VASH vouchers, and the County's tenant-rights notice applies this in unincorporated Orange County. The City of Orlando's own code has no source-of-income rule. Florida's 2023 preemption law (section 83.425) does not mention source of income, and we found no court ruling on whether it overrides the County's rule. Refusals based on race, disability, family status or another protected trait are illegal everywhere.

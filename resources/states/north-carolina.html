North Carolina Housing Resources & Tenant Rights

Waythrough Project · Updated October 2026

North Carolina sets its rental rules at the state level under Chapter 42 of the General Statutes, and tenant protections are limited. Eviction (called “summary ejectment”) moves quickly through small-claims court. There is no rent control, and G.S. 42-14.1 goes further than most states — it preempts cities from adopting rent control or from barring landlords who refuse federal housing assistance, so no North Carolina city can require a landlord to take a voucher. The North Carolina Housing Finance Agency (NCHFA) is the state housing agency and runs several rental-assistance programs. This page covers the statewide rules, the eviction timeline, and links to every North Carolina city we cover.

Public Housing & Vouchers in North Carolina

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers and public housing are run by local authorities — the Charlotte Housing Authority (INLIVIAN), the Raleigh Housing Authority, the Greensboro Housing Authority, and many more. The NC Housing Finance Agency (NCHFA) adds statewide programs:

  • Vouchers come from local housing authorities, not NCHFA. NCHFA warns that it "does not distribute Housing Choice Vouchers" and that a scam has told people to apply for Section 8 through it. Apply at your local PHA
  • Finding an affordable apartment: NCHFA points renters to NCHousingSearch.org, a free bilingual listing service, plus HUD's and USDA's affordable apartment searches (NCHFA renter resources)
  • Transitions to Community Living vouchers, operated by NC DHHS with the LME/MCOs, for people with behavioral-health disabilities moving out of restrictive settings
  • LIHTC income-restricted apartments — search HUD’s LIHTC database or read how to find LIHTC housing

Apply to several authorities at once. Use HUD’s PHA directory or our how to find your PHA and how to apply for Section 8 guides. City waitlist status is on the city pages below.

Rent Control & Source-of-Income Preemption

North Carolina has no rent control, and G.S. 42-14.1 preempts local governments from adopting it. The same statute also bars cities and counties from prohibiting landlords from refusing federal housing assistance — meaning a North Carolina city cannot pass a source-of-income ordinance requiring landlords to accept Housing Choice Vouchers. Statewide, there is no source-of-income protection either. So a landlord can legally decline a voucher anywhere in the state; the practical step is to find landlords who already participate. Ask your PHA for its list and read our source-of-income protections guide.

Is There an Eviction Moratorium in North Carolina?

No. There is no statewide eviction ban or moratorium in North Carolina in 2026; the pandemic-era orders ended years ago. The NC Judicial Branch is direct about it: asked whether you can be evicted if you could not pay rent for a good reason, its answer is "Yes. Failing to pay rent is grounds for eviction even if it is not your fault" (nccourts.gov, checked October 15, 2026). What protects you is the process:

  • The landlord must go to court. Changing the locks, cutting utilities or removing doors to force you out is not allowed (G.S. 42-25.6).
  • For unpaid rent, the landlord must demand the rent and wait 10 days before filing.
  • A magistrate hears the case in small claims court. Either side has 10 days to appeal to District Court, and the landlord cannot remove you until that period ends.
  • After that, the sheriff must carry out a Writ of Possession within 5 days. You then have 5 days (belongings worth $500 or less) or 7 days (more than $500) to arrange to collect your things.

An eviction is a civil case, not a criminal one, so it does not go on a criminal record. It is a public record, though, and can show up on tenant screening.

Emergency Rental Assistance in North Carolina

The statewide pandemic program (HOPE) has ended. Current help is local and program-based:

  • Dial 211 to reach local emergency rental and prevention funds and your regional Coordinated Entry for shelter and rapid re-housing
  • Community Action Agencies administer crisis help and utility assistance — the Low Income Energy Assistance Program (LIEAP) and the Crisis Intervention Program (CIP) run through your county Department of Social Services. Lowering a utility bill frees up cash for rent; see utility assistance programs
  • NCHFA programs and partner nonprofits (Salvation Army, Catholic Charities) provide one-time help
  • Wake County runs Wake Prevent!. Wake County's eligibility list (checked October 15, 2026): at least 30 days late with a late notice or letter, more than 90 days in the home, no more than 3 months of rent owed, under $5,000 owed, and help only once every 3 years. You apply online through the county's Neighborly portal

North Carolina Tenant Law: Key Protections at a Glance

Quick Reference: North Carolina (NC)

  • Source-of-income protection: none — and the state preempts cities from creating it (G.S. 42-14.1)
  • Rent control: none — preempted statewide (G.S. 42-14.1)
  • Security deposit limit: 1.5 months (month-to-month), 2 months (longer terms), 2 weeks (week-to-week) — G.S. 42-51
  • Deposit return deadline: 30 days, itemized (60-day outer limit if interim accounting given)
  • Eviction demand (nonpayment): 10-day written demand (G.S. 42-3)
  • Notice to end month-to-month: 7 days (G.S. 42-14)
  • Implied warranty of habitability: yes (Residential Rental Agreements Act, G.S. 42-42)
  • Self-help eviction: illegal — only the sheriff can remove a tenant (G.S. 42-25.6)

Security deposits

Under the Tenant Security Deposit Act (G.S. 42-50 to 42-56), deposits are capped at 1.5 months’ rent for month-to-month, 2 months for longer terms, and 2 weeks for week-to-week. The landlord must account for the deposit within 30 days (or up to 60 days if an interim accounting is provided), and the money must be held in a trust account or bonded. A landlord in willful bad faith forfeits the right to keep any of the deposit and owes any actual damages you can prove, plus possible attorney’s fees (G.S. 42-55). See how to recover your security deposit.

Eviction process & how long it takes

Self-help eviction is illegal — only the sheriff can remove a tenant after a court order (G.S. 42-25.6). The summary ejectment sequence is:

  • 10-day written demand for nonpayment (your lease may set different terms for other breaches)
  • Summary ejectment filed in small claims court before a magistrate; a hearing is usually set within about 7 days of the summons
  • Magistrate judgment, then a 10-day window to appeal to district court for a new hearing
  • Writ of possession: if the landlord wins and you do not appeal, the sheriff posts and executes the writ — you typically have a few days to remove your belongings

An uncontested North Carolina eviction commonly runs about three to five weeks from the demand to a sheriff lockout; an appeal extends it. Appear at your hearing and bring your lease, receipts, and photos. Get help from Legal Aid of NC and read how to avoid eviction.

Repairs: What Renters and Homeowners Can Do

Renters: your landlord must keep the home fit and habitable and repair electrical, plumbing, heating and other supplied systems once you ask in writing, except in emergencies (G.S. 42-42). Put every repair request in writing. North Carolina does not let a tenant hold back rent on their own: "The tenant may not unilaterally withhold rent prior to a judicial determination of a right to do so" (G.S. 42-44). If repairs are not made, contact Legal Aid of NC or your city's housing code inspectors. Our repair request letter helps you write it.

Homeowners with low incomes can get repair help:

  • City of Raleigh (checked October 15, 2026): the Limited Repair Program offers a 0% deferred loan of typically up to $20,000, forgiven after five years, for owner-occupants of at least five years in Raleigh with one severely deteriorated system, run by Preserving Home (919-336-5200). The Raleigh Home Revitalization Program (typically up to $30,000) covers targeted areas. The Substantial Rehabilitation Program is "not accepting new applications at this time." Details at raleighnc.gov.
  • Wake County: Elderly and Disabled Homeowner Grants, an Emergency Grant Program for urgent hazards (heating, well or septic, roof, electrical), and a Major Repair Loan Program of up to $90,000, with online applications. Housing.Rehab@wake.gov or 919-856-5906 (wake.gov).
  • Statewide: NCHFA funds local governments and nonprofits that do urgent repairs and accessibility changes for low-income homeowners who are older or have special needs (NCHFA repair programs). Behind on your mortgage? NCHFA's free foreclosure prevention counseling line is 1-888-442-8188 (NCHFA).

Expunction and Housing Applications in North Carolina

A criminal record is the most common reason applications get denied, and North Carolina's expunction laws can clear many records:

  • Dismissed charges and not-guilty verdicts disposed on or after December 1, 2021 are expunged automatically, 180 to 210 days after the case ends (a felony dismissed as part of a plea deal is excluded). Older dismissals can be expunged by petition (G.S. 15A-146).
  • Nonviolent convictions can be expunged by petition in the county of conviction: one nonviolent misdemeanor after 3 years, more than one after 7 years, one nonviolent felony after 10 years, and two or three after 20 years, counted from the conviction or the end of the sentence, whichever is later (G.S. 15A-145.5).
  • After an expunction, state law says you are not making a false statement if you leave the expunged arrest, charge or conviction out "in response to any inquiry made of the person for any purpose," with narrow exceptions such as law-enforcement certification, and a records check will not show it (G.S. 15A-153).
  • Evictions are not criminal records and cannot be expunged under these laws. They are public court records.

Start with your county clerk of court or Legal Aid of NC (1-866-219-5262). Read our expungement and housing guide and criminal records and housing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a landlord refuse my Section 8 voucher in North Carolina?

Yes. North Carolina has no statewide source-of-income protection, and G.S. 42-14.1 bars cities and counties from prohibiting landlords who refuse federal housing assistance - so no NC city can require a landlord to accept a voucher. The practical step is to find landlords who already participate; ask your PHA for its participating-landlord list.

How much can a landlord charge for a security deposit in North Carolina?

Under G.S. 42-51, the cap is 1.5 months' rent for a month-to-month tenancy, 2 months for a longer term, and 2 weeks for week-to-week. The landlord must account for the deposit within 30 days (up to 60 if an interim accounting is given), and willful bad faith forfeits the landlord’s right to keep any of it, plus your actual damages and possible attorney’s fees (G.S. 42-55).

How fast is eviction in North Carolina?

Fairly fast. After a 10-day demand for nonpayment, the landlord files a summary ejectment in small claims court, and a magistrate hearing is usually set within about 7 days. You have 10 days to appeal to district court. An uncontested case commonly runs three to five weeks from demand to a sheriff lockout. Only the sheriff can remove you - self-help eviction is illegal.

Is there rent control in North Carolina?

No. G.S. 42-14.1 preempts local rent control statewide, and there is no state rent cap. Your protection against an increase comes from your lease term and the required notice before a month-to-month change.

How much notice ends a month-to-month tenancy in North Carolina?

Just 7 days (G.S. 42-14) - shorter than most states. Year-to-year tenancies require one month's notice and week-to-week two days. Nonpayment of rent requires a 10-day demand before the landlord can file for summary ejectment.

Is there an eviction moratorium in North Carolina in 2026?

No. North Carolina has no eviction ban or moratorium in 2026. The NC Judicial Branch says failing to pay rent is grounds for eviction even if it is not your fault. The landlord must still go to court: a 10-day written demand for rent, a hearing before a magistrate, and 10 days to appeal before the sheriff can remove you. Get help from Legal Aid of North Carolina at 1-866-219-5262.

Can an expunged record be left off a North Carolina housing application?

Under G.S. 15A-153, a person whose record was expunged is not making a false statement by leaving the expunged arrest, charge or conviction out in response to any inquiry, apart from narrow exceptions such as law-enforcement certification, and a records check will not show it. Dismissals and not-guilty verdicts since December 1, 2021 are expunged automatically. Evictions are civil records and are not covered.

Emergency Rent Help in Minneapolis: Free Eviction Lawyers, City and County Rent Aid, and Minnesota's 14-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Minneapolis and you are behind, here is the honest picture. Minneapolis has more doors than most cities, but each one has a catch. The City's Emergency Rental Assistance is only for households at or below 30% of the area median income who have their landlord's 30-day notice, and you apply through a partner group. RentHelp Hennepin takes applications only with a pre-eviction notice or a court filing. Hennepin County's Emergency Assistance can help with some housing costs in a crisis. And the City says low-income renters have the right to a lawyer in eviction cases. Everything here was checked November 5, 2026.

Here's the short version: Keep every notice your landlord gives you. With the notice in hand, call a City partner such as Tenant Resource Connections at 612-767-9737, or apply at RentHelp Hennepin. Call HOME Line at 612-728-5767 for free legal advice. If you get court papers, call Hennepin County Adult Representation Services at 612-348-7012 or Mid-Minnesota Legal Aid at 1-877-696-6529 the same day. And go to court: in Minnesota the first hearing comes 7 to 14 days after the summons is issued.

Two notices before an eviction for unpaid rent

Minnesota law says that before filing an eviction for unpaid rent, the landlord must give you a written notice with the total amount due, an itemized list of rent and fees, and who can take your payment. The notice must say "Your landlord can file an eviction case if you do not pay the total amount due or move out within 14 days from the date of this notice" (Minn. Stat. 504B.321, subd. 1a). Minneapolis adds its own rule: the City says the property owner "must send the notice at least 30 days before they start the eviction process." If the required notice is not attached to the eviction complaint, the court must dismiss the case and expunge it (504B.321, subd. 1(d)). Use those days to apply for help.

Free eviction lawyers in Minneapolis

The City of Minneapolis says: "Low-income renters in Minneapolis have the right to a lawyer in eviction cases." The City points to two providers (checked November 5, 2026):

  • Hennepin County Adult Representation Services: "Call Adult Representation Services at 612-348-7012 or email contactARS@hennepin.us." The county offers free legal advice and representation to income-eligible residents facing eviction and has an online form to find out if you qualify.
  • Mid-Minnesota Legal Aid: phone intake at 1-877-696-6529 ("Lines open daily, Friday until noon"), or apply online through mylegalaid.org/get-help.
  • HOME Line gives free legal advice to Minnesota renters by phone: 612-728-5767 (Twin Cities), 866-866-3546 (Greater Minnesota), Spanish 612-255-8870, Somali 612-255-8860, Hmong 612-255-7104. Its office is closed to walk-ins, and you can also email an attorney from homelinemn.org.
  • At Housing Court: Hennepin County Housing Court says parties "may be eligible for free legal advice from volunteer attorneys or for no-fee mediation." Ask for both at your first hearing.

Help paying rent in Minneapolis

Each program below has its own rules. Apply to every one you fit, and keep copies of what you send. None of them promises help.

City of Minneapolis Emergency Rental Assistance

The City says: "Minneapolis residents at risk of eviction can get help paying rent and utilities." Its page was last updated June 4, 2026, and does not list an end date (checked November 5, 2026).

  • Who qualifies: your rental must be in Minneapolis; your household income must be at or below 30% of the area median income, and "The program counts income from every adult in your home"; and you must have the 30-day notice from your landlord that "explains what you owe, when you must pay it, and who you can contact to make the payment." The 30% limit is $27,810 a year for one person and $39,720 for four.
  • How to apply: the City cannot take applications itself. Call one of its partners, who check your eligibility and help you apply: Agate Housing & Services 612-887-0670, Community Mediation & Restorative Services 763-561-0173, CLUES 651-243-0688, Isuroon 763-344-9934, Minnesota Indian Women's Resource Center 612-728-2000 (ask for rent assistance), or Tenant Resource Connections 612-767-9737.
  • No 30-day notice? The City says to get legal advice from HOME Line or Mid-Minnesota Legal Aid. If you already have an eviction court summons, start an application through RentHelp Hennepin or Tenant Resource Connections.

RentHelp Hennepin

Hennepin County's rent help portal is at renthelphennepin.hdsallita.com. It says: "Please apply here if you have a Pre-Eviction Filing or a Court Filing. Applications without these documents will not be processed." Everyone must complete a pre-screen first, and "Funds are not guaranteed." For help with the application, call or text 2-1-1. For more help, the county gives the Tenant Resource Connection: 612-767-9737, "Leave a message. We'll call you back." (checked November 5, 2026)

Hennepin County Emergency Assistance

Hennepin County gives emergency help to people in a "financial crisis that poses a direct threat to their physical health or safety." It is called Emergency Assistance for households with children under 18 and Emergency General Assistance for households without children. The county lists help such as emergency temporary shelter, "Housing costs like damage deposits or home repairs," and "Utility shut-off prevention"; for back rent it points to its eviction prevention page. Apply online at MNbenefits (mnbenefits.mn.gov), or mail the combined application to P.O. Box 107, Minneapolis, MN 55440. For a mailed form or help applying, call 612-596-1300, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. You will need a phone interview, and "An emergency general assistance application is only good for 30 days."

United Way 211

Dial 2-1-1, or call 651-291-0211 or toll-free 800-543-7709. You can also text your ZIP code to 898-211. United Way 211 says "We're here 24 hours a day, 7 days a week," with phone menus in English, Spanish and Hmong.

Utility bills: Minnesota's Energy Assistance Program

The Minnesota Department of Commerce says Energy Assistance "provides benefits of up to $1,400, plus additional support to respond to emergencies." Renters and homeowners can qualify; "a family of four could earn up to $73,969 annually." The money goes straight to your utility or fuel company. "The deadline to apply for Energy Assistance for the winter of 2026-2027 is May 31, 2027." In Hennepin County the local provider is the Community Action Partnership of Hennepin County: 952-930-3541, eap@caphennepin.org. It says a household "may be eligible to receive up to $600 in crisis funds" with an active disconnection, low or no delivered fuel, or, if 60 or older, a current or past-due bill. Apply online through Commerce or with a paper application from caphennepin.org.

Minnesota eviction timeline: what happens and when

Minneapolis evictions are heard in Hennepin County Housing Court under Minn. Stat. chapter 504B. These are the steps for unpaid rent:

  • 1. The notices. Minnesota requires a written 14-day notice before filing for unpaid rent (504B.321, subd. 1a). In Minneapolis, the City says the owner must send its notice at least 30 days before starting the eviction process. Apply for help with the notice in hand.
  • 2. The summons. The first hearing "shall be not less than seven nor more than 14 days from the day of issuing the summons" (504B.321, subd. 1). Call a lawyer that day.
  • 3. Court. Go. If you do not show up, you can lose by default. Ask for a volunteer attorney and for mediation. Bring your lease, notices, receipts, texts and photos of repair problems.
  • 4. Paying to stay. In a case for unpaid rent, you may, "at any time before possession has been delivered," pay the landlord or bring to court the rent owed, with interest, the court costs and a $5 attorney fee, and keep your home (504B.291, subd. 1). Ask the court clerk or your lawyer for the exact amount. This does not apply if the landlord also claims a serious lease violation.
  • 5. Judgment. If the landlord wins, the court issues a writ of recovery and order to vacate. In most cases the court "shall stay" the writ "for a reasonable period, not to exceed seven days" (504B.345, subd. 1(d)), but not after a default. If you win, the court must expunge the case (504B.345, subd. 1(c)).
  • 6. The officer. The officer serving the writ demands that you leave "within 24 hours" (504B.365, subd. 1).
  • Lockouts are a crime. A landlord "who unlawfully and intentionally removes or excludes a tenant" is "guilty of a misdemeanor," and so is one who shuts off heat, electric, gas or water to force you out (504B.225). Call 911 and a lawyer.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The Minneapolis Public Housing Authority (MPHA) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its Section 8 page says "The Housing Choice Voucher waitlist is currently closed for new applications." The one-time Bring It Home voucher window ran October 13 to 15, 2026 and is closed; MPHA said applicants would see their status in RentCafe by November 1, 2026, so log in at portal.mphaonline.org to check. MPHA's high-rise lists for people 50 and older and for people with disabilities are open. See our MPHA guide. If you already have MPHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call MPHA right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • Calling the City for an application. The City says it "cannot access the application system or help with applications." Go through a partner group.
  • RentHelp Hennepin without papers. It will not process an application without a pre-eviction filing or a court filing.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent help in Minneapolis right now?

Yes, if you fit the rules. The City of Minneapolis Emergency Rental Assistance helps renters in Minneapolis with household income at or below 30% of the area median income who have their landlord's 30-day notice; apply through a partner such as Tenant Resource Connections at 612-767-9737. RentHelp Hennepin takes applications from renters with a pre-eviction notice or a court filing. Hennepin County Emergency Assistance helps in some crises. Funds are not guaranteed, so apply early.

What notice must my Minneapolis landlord give before filing an eviction for unpaid rent?

Minnesota law requires a written notice at least 14 days before the landlord files for unpaid rent, listing what you owe and who can take your payment. In Minneapolis, the City says the owner must send the notice at least 30 days before starting the eviction process. If the required notice is not attached to the complaint, the court must dismiss the case and expunge it.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Minneapolis?

The City says low-income renters in Minneapolis have the right to a lawyer in eviction cases. Call Hennepin County Adult Representation Services at 612-348-7012 or email contactARS@hennepin.us, or call Mid-Minnesota Legal Aid at 1-877-696-6529. HOME Line gives free legal advice at 612-728-5767. At Housing Court, ask for a volunteer attorney.

Can I stop a Minnesota eviction by paying the rent?

In a case only about unpaid rent, usually yes. Minnesota law lets you pay the landlord, or bring to court, the rent owed with interest, the court costs and a $5 attorney fee at any time before you are actually removed, and keep your home. Ask the clerk or a lawyer for the exact amount, and get a receipt.

Is the Minneapolis Section 8 waiting list open?

No. MPHA says "The Housing Choice Voucher waitlist is currently closed for new applications." The state-funded Bring It Home voucher list took applications only October 13 to 15, 2026, and that window is over; check your status in RentCafe at portal.mphaonline.org. MPHA's high-rise public housing lists for people 50 and older and for people with disabilities are open.

Where can I get help with my heating bill in Minneapolis?

Apply for Minnesota's Energy Assistance Program through the Community Action Partnership of Hennepin County at 952-930-3541, or online through the Department of Commerce. Benefits go up to $1,400, and the deadline for this winter is May 31, 2027. If you have a shutoff notice or no fuel, ask about crisis funds of up to $600.

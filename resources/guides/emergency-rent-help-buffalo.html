Emergency Rent Help in Buffalo: Back-Rent Help, Free Eviction Lawyers, and New York's 14-Day Rent Demand

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Buffalo and you are behind, here is where things stand. New York's pandemic rent program (ERAP) is closed. What is left is Erie County DSS Emergency Services, which can pay back rent if you qualify, a couple of nonprofit back-rent programs with strict rules, and a free eviction lawyer for tenants who are financially eligible through the WNY Eviction Prevention Project. New York also gives you more time than most states: a 14-day rent demand before the landlord can file, and a 14-day notice before a marshal can put you out. Everything here was checked on November 12, 2026.

The short version: If you got a rent demand or court papers, call the WNY Eviction Prevention Project at (844) 230-7376 that day. Call Erie County DSS at (716) 858-8000 and ask Emergency Services about your back rent. Dial 2-1-1 for everything else. And go to every court date: in New York, paying the full rent owed before the warrant is carried out makes the judge cancel it, unless the landlord proves you held rent back in bad faith.

New York's clock: a 14-day demand, court, then a 14-day warning

Before a landlord can sue you for unpaid rent, New York law requires a written rent demand with "at least fourteen days' notice" (RPAPL 711(2)). If rent is not received within five days of the due date, the landlord also has to send you a written notice by certified mail, and if they skip it, "The failure of a lessor ... to provide a lessee with a written notice of the non-payment of rent may be used as an affirmative defense" (RPL 235-e(d)). If you lose in court, the marshal must give you "at least fourteen days' notice, in writing" before the eviction (RPAPL 749(2)). And up until the warrant is carried out, "the court shall vacate a warrant upon tender or deposit with the court of the full rent due," unless the landlord shows you "withheld the rent due in bad faith" (RPAPL 749(3)).

Free eviction lawyers in Buffalo

Buffalo has no right-to-counsel law. But Buffalo City Court itself says eligible tenants "at no cost may be entitled to legal representation," and it names the providers: Neighborhood Legal Services, the Center for Elder Law and Justice, Legal Aid Bureau, the Volunteer Lawyers Project and the Western New York Law Center (checked November 12, 2026).

  • WNY Eviction Prevention Project: one hotline for all of those groups, (844) 230-7376. It gives "FREE eviction defense and advocacy to financially-eligible* persons facing homelessness because of eviction in Erie, Niagara, Wyoming, Orleans, and Genesee counties," and says "Financial eligibility is based on federal poverty guidelines." Services include legal advice, stepping in with your landlord before court, representation in court, and referrals for money help. See nls.org.
  • At your first court date: the Western New York Law Center says "If you live in the city of Buffalo, you can ask for help during your first court appearance at Buffalo City Court." Landlord and tenant cases are heard in Part 17, on the 7th floor of 50 Delaware Avenue. The Volunteer Lawyers Project's lawyers are there Tuesday mornings (9:30 to 11:30) and afternoons (2:00 to 4:00), and other groups cover the other days.
  • Neighborhood Legal Services: 47 Court Street, Monday to Friday 9 am to 4:30 pm, 716-847-0650. NLS says "Walk-ins welcome!"
  • Legal Aid Bureau of Buffalo: 290 Main Street, Suite 400, 716-853-9555. Its Housing and Tenant Rights Unit handles eviction prevention and housing discrimination.
  • Age 60 or older? The Center for Elder Law & Justice takes intake at (716) 853-3087.

Help paying back rent in Buffalo

Erie County DSS Emergency Services

This is the biggest door. Erie County's Emergency Services unit helps with homelessness, eviction (DSS calls back rent "shelter arrears"), utility arrears and security agreements. As of October 19, 2026 it is at 460 Main Street, 2nd floor. Call the main line at (716) 858-8000, leave a message for Emergency Services at 716-858-6265, or email TAEmergencyServices@erie.gov. You can leave papers in the drop boxes at 95 Franklin Street and 460 Main Street. The County says "After 5 PM, Call 2-1-1 for assistance." Bring your rent demand or court papers, your lease, ID and proof of income. See erie.gov.

Gerard Place Preventive Services

Gerard Place "may help with back rent payments up to six months." The rules are tight: you must "have a 14-day eviction notice from Housing Court. Must live in the City of Buffalo," and "have income below 30% AMI." Gerard Place also expects you to have tried other resources first, including DSS. "Prevention assistance is available on a case-by-case basis until funds are exhausted," and clients are seen by appointment only. Call (716) 897-9948, ext. 108. See gerardplace.org.

Buffalo Urban League

The Buffalo Urban League's Homelessness Prevention Program lists "Payment of past-due rent." It is at 15 Genesee Street, (716) 250-2400, Monday to Friday 8 am to 5 pm. Its page does not post who qualifies or whether money is available right now, so ask when you call. See bulny.org.

211 WNY

Dial 2-1-1 or 1.888.696.9211. 211 WNY says it is "here 24 hours a day, 7 days a week." You can also text your ZIP code to 898211 on weekdays, 8:30 am to 3:30 pm. Ask for rent help in your ZIP code and write down every name and number you get. See 211wny.org.

Utility bills: HEAP, National Fuel and National Grid

  • HEAP (New York State OTDA): "The 2026-2027 Regular HEAP benefit opened November 2, 2026." Emergency HEAP, for a shutoff notice or running out of fuel, is "scheduled to open January 4, 2027." The base regular benefit for a household that pays its own electric or gas heat is $400, plus $61 for the lowest-income households and $35 if someone in the home is under 6, 60 or older, or permanently disabled. The income limit is $3,563 a month for one person and $6,852 for four. Apply at mybenefits.ny.gov or drop off an application at 460 Main Street, Monday to Friday 8 am to 4 pm. Erie County's HEAP phone center: (716) 858-7644.
  • National Grid: "You will be automatically enrolled in EAP when a HEAP grant is added to your National Grid account." EAP is a monthly bill credit. Questions: 1-866-305-1915.
  • National Fuel: its Energy Affordability Program gives a discount to eligible customers. Its Neighbor for Neighbor Heat Fund "is now open and accepting applications," with a "one-time grant of up to $300 per program year" for households with someone 55 or older, a disability, a medical emergency, a veteran, or someone on unemployment. Apply through the Salvation Army (716-888-6230) or Catholic Charities (716-856-4494). National Fuel's New York line is 716-686-6123.

New York eviction timeline: what happens and when

In Buffalo, eviction cases are heard in Buffalo City Court, 50 Delaware Avenue (landlord and tenant, Part 17, 7th floor, 716-845-2662). Tenants in Erie County's towns and villages go to the virtual HUB court. These are the steps for a case about unpaid rent:

  • 1. The late notice. If rent is not received within five days of the due date, the landlord must send you a written notice by certified mail (RPL 235-e(d)). No late fee until then, and never more than $50 or 5% of the monthly rent, whichever is less (RPL 238-a).
  • 2. The 14-day rent demand. A written demand giving you at least 14 days to pay or move (RPAPL 711(2)). Call the WNY Eviction Prevention Project and DSS now, while you still have time.
  • 3. The court papers. If you do not pay, the landlord files in Buffalo City Court and you get a notice of petition with your court date. Write the date down.
  • 4. Court. Go. If you do not show up, the judge can rule against you. Ask for a lawyer at your first appearance. Bring your lease, rent receipts, texts, photos of repair problems, and anything showing DSS or a program is working on your rent.
  • 5. Check the City's rental certificate. The City of Buffalo says that if a property "fails to meet the standards for a Certificate of Rental Compliance it will not be permitted to operate as an income property and tenants may not be evicted for non-payment of rent." Tell your lawyer if your building has never been inspected or failed. You can look up a property's certificate in the City's Open Data, or call 311.
  • 6. The warrant and the 14-day notice. If the landlord wins, a city marshal must give you at least 14 days' written notice before the eviction, and can only carry it out "on a business day between the hours of sunrise and sunset" (RPAPL 749(2)). The City Marshals' office is on the 1st floor of 50 Delaware Avenue, (716) 847-8430.
  • 7. Paying to stop it. Before the warrant is carried out, the court must vacate it if you pay or deposit "the full rent due," unless the landlord proves bad faith (RPAPL 749(3)). This is where a DSS or Gerard Place payment can save the apartment. Get proof of every payment.
  • 8. Asking for more time. You can ask the judge to stay the eviction for up to one year if you cannot find a similar place nearby after trying, or if moving would cause extreme hardship. The judge weighs things like your health and your kids' school (RPAPL 753).
  • Only the court process can put you out. Changing the locks, removing your belongings or cutting off your heat, water or power to force you out is an unlawful eviction, a class A misdemeanor with civil penalties of $1,000 to $10,000 per violation (RPAPL 768). Call 911 and a lawyer.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • New York's pandemic ERAP. OTDA says "New applications stopped being accepted on January 20, 2023," and the ERAP portal and call center have closed. Anyone offering to get you ERAP money now is wrong or running a scam.
  • The Buffalo Municipal Housing Authority (BMHA) runs public housing and vouchers, not emergency rent. Its voucher page says "The Waitlist for the Housing Choice Voucher Program is currently closed. We expect to reopen applications sometime in 2026" (checked November 12, 2026). Its public housing application is open. See our BMHA guide. If you already live in BMHA housing or have a voucher and fall behind, call 716-855-6711 right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • Good Cause Eviction. New York's 2024 Good Cause law only applies in places that opt in, and Buffalo is not on the State's list of opted-in cities (DHCR's list as of May 4, 2026).
  • HOME (Housing Opportunities Made Equal) offers "paralegal counseling to help resolve landlord and tenant disputes" and handles discrimination, including a landlord refusing your voucher, at 716-854-1400. It does not pay rent.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight, call DSS at (716) 858-8000 during the day, or call 211 WNY at 1-888-696-9211 after 5 pm.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Buffalo right now?

Some, with rules. Erie County DSS Emergency Services can help with back rent, utility arrears and security agreements if you qualify; call (716) 858-8000 or go to 460 Main Street, 2nd floor. Gerard Place may pay up to six months of back rent for City of Buffalo renters with a 14-day notice and income below 30% of the area median. New York's pandemic ERAP is closed. Dial 211 for other help.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Buffalo?

Call the WNY Eviction Prevention Project at (844) 230-7376. It gives free eviction defense to financially eligible tenants in Erie County. You can also ask for help at your first court date in Buffalo City Court, or walk in to Neighborhood Legal Services at 47 Court Street. Buffalo has no right-to-counsel law, so income rules apply.

How much notice does a landlord have to give in New York?

For unpaid rent, the landlord must give you a written rent demand with at least 14 days' notice before filing in court. If rent is more than five days late, the landlord also has to send a written notice by certified mail; if they did not, you can raise that as a defense. After a judgment, a marshal must give you at least 14 days' written notice before an eviction.

Can I stop a New York eviction by paying the rent?

Usually, yes, up until the warrant is carried out. New York law says the court shall vacate the warrant if you pay or deposit with the court the full rent due, unless the landlord shows you withheld rent in bad faith. Get proof of every payment, and tell the court if DSS or a program is paying.

Can my Buffalo landlord evict me if the apartment failed inspection?

The City of Buffalo says a property that fails to meet the standards for a Certificate of Rental Compliance is not permitted to operate as an income property and tenants may not be evicted for non-payment of rent. Tell your lawyer and the judge, and call 311 to report conditions.

What if I am going to be homeless tonight?

During business hours, go to the Erie County DSS Homeless Team at 158 Pearl Street, 1st floor, or call (716) 858-8000. After 5 pm, on weekends and on holidays, call 211 WNY at 1-888-696-9211, which handles after-hours emergency housing placement for DSS. For domestic violence, call Haven House at (716) 884-6000, 24 hours a day.

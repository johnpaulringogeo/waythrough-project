Emergency Rent Help in Lexington, KY: City Emergency Assistance, Free Legal Aid, and Kentucky's 7-Day Rules

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Lexington and you are behind, here is the honest picture. The pandemic rent money is gone, but unlike a lot of cities, Lexington's government still runs a rent door of its own: emergency financial assistance through the City's Community & Resident Services, by appointment, for people with a 7-day notice or rent 30 days late, while funds last. Free legal help comes from Legal Aid of the Bluegrass. LIHEAP utility help is open now through Community Action Council. And Kentucky gives you two short clocks: 7 days after a notice for unpaid rent, and 7 days to appeal a judgment. This guide covers Lexington and Fayette County. Everything here was checked on November 12, 2026.

Short version: The day you get a 7-day notice, call Community & Resident Services at (859) 300-5300 for an appointment and Legal Aid of the Bluegrass at 859-431-8200. Dial 2-1-1 for everything else. And go to court: after a judgment, you have only 7 days to appeal.

Kentucky's clock in Lexington: 7 days, court, then 7 more

Lexington-Fayette adopted Kentucky's Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA) by Ordinance No. 98-84 in 1984. Under it, "If rent is unpaid when due and the tenant fails to pay rent within seven (7) days after written notice by the landlord of nonpayment and his intention to terminate the rental agreement if the rent is not paid within that period, the landlord may terminate the rental agreement" (KRS 383.660(2)). The Fayette circuit clerk's page puts it plainly: after a 7-day notice, a landlord "can file on the 8th day." The court's warrant must give you "at least three (3) days' notice" of the trial (KRS 383.210). After a judgment, you can appeal only "within seven (7) days next after the finding" (KRS 383.255). Use the first 7 days: that is when the City's rent help and Legal Aid can still keep a case from being filed.

The City's rent door: Community & Resident Services

The City of Lexington's Department of Housing Advocacy and Community Development runs emergency financial assistance through its Community & Resident Services division, 498 Georgetown St., Lexington, KY 40508, (859) 300-5300, Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. (checked November 12, 2026).

  • What it is, in the City's words: "Emergency financial assistance is available to prevent eviction or utility service disconnection at your current residence. Eligible assistance includes rent, mortgage and/or utility payments." And: "Eligible clients will receive the appropriated financial assistance if funds are available."
  • The City's screening questions: Have you lived in Fayette County for one year or more? Has Community & Resident Services helped you with rent or utilities within the last three years (it has to be no)? For utilities, do you have a disconnect notice or a bill 30 days or more past due? For rent or a mortgage, "Do you have a seven-day notice, or eviction notice or is your rent/mortgage 30 days or more past due?"
  • How to apply: "Call (859) 300-5300 to schedule an appointment." The City's utility page adds that no walk-in appointments are accepted.
  • What to bring: a driver's license or picture ID for everyone 18 or older; Social Security cards for everyone in the home; proof of the whole household's income (the three most recent pay stubs, SSI or SSDI, child support or award letters); proof of 12 straight months of Fayette County residency; your lease; and the eviction notice, forcible detainer papers, or a ledger from your landlord showing the past-due amount. See lexingtonky.gov: Emergency financial assistance.

The City's Housing Advocate

If you are not sure what is coming, the City's Housing Advocate, 200 E. Main St., (859) 280-8425, can help. The City says to reach out if you "Want to know what to expect if you're facing eviction," need "help finding a landlord who will accept a Housing Choice Voucher," or are "Looking for a new place to live and having difficulty." You can also send a contact request from the Housing Advocate page.

Free legal help in Lexington

A lawyer cannot pay your rent, but can check the landlord's notice and papers, raise defenses like repairs that never got done, work out a payment deal, and make sure you get every day the law allows.

  • Legal Aid of the Bluegrass (LABG): Lexington office at 300 E. Main Street, Suite 210. Call intake at 859-431-8200, Monday through Thursday, 10:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m., and Friday, 10:00 a.m. to 1:00 p.m.; "Calls are answered in the order they are received." Or apply online at lablaw.org, where LABG says "You can expect a response within 72 hours." If your court date is close, LABG says: "If you have a scheduled court date in three business days or less, call us to be screened for legal help." It cannot see walk-ins.
  • What LABG handles: for evictions, "advice to tenants regarding community resources, negotiations with landlords, and representing clients in court." It also helps people "facing termination of public housing or Section 8 benefits," and people locked out or whose utilities were cut by a landlord. LABG: Housing
  • No Right to Counsel here. Louisville has a Metro law giving low-income tenants a free lawyer in eviction court. Lexington does not, so call LABG early, not the morning of court.

Help paying rent in Lexington: the other doors

United Way of the Bluegrass 211

Dial 2-1-1, text your ZIP code to 898211, or search uwbg211.org. Ask for rent and utility help in Fayette County and write down every name and number you get.

Community Action Council

Community Action Council, 710 W. High St., 859-233-4600, says that "When funds are available, the Council can help ease the burden on families in crisis by providing direct financial assistance to cover basic needs such as food, rent, and/or utility expenses." Ask what is open when you call. Its Emergency Family Housing program shelters two-parent and group families who are homeless in Lexington-Fayette.

Utility bills: LIHEAP, LEXserv and water

  • LIHEAP Fall Subsidy, November 9 to December 18, 2026: Community Action Council runs LIHEAP for Fayette, Bourbon, Harrison and Nicholas counties. It says applications are taken "on a first-come, first-served basis" starting November 9, 2026, and "through December 18, 2026, or until funding is depleted." Book an appointment online through CAC's scheduler or by calling 859-300-6960. The Fall Subsidy "does not require a disconnect notice, a past due bill, or eviction notice," and the one-time benefit is paid straight to your utility. Income must be at or below 150% of the poverty guidelines, for example $1,995 a month for one person or $4,125 for four. Bring proof of Social Security numbers (or a green card) for everyone, last month's income for everyone, and your most current heating bill. After December 18, 2026, the Fall Subsidy is closed; ask CAC about crisis help, which it says requires a shut-off notice.
  • LEXserv (sewer, water quality and landfill fees): Community & Resident Services can help if you have a 10-day shut-off notice, a defaulted payment plan, service cut for nonpayment, or an account in collections, with income at or below 150% of the poverty guidelines. Call (859) 300-5300.
  • Kentucky American Water: CAC's H2O Help to Others Program pays past-due water and wastewater bills once per calendar year, until funds run out, for customers with income up to 225% of the poverty guidelines and a balance of at least $50 who have paid at least $25 in the last 180 days. Apply through CAC's online form or call 859-233-4600.
  • Other utility programs: CAC says it enrolls eligible customers of Columbia Gas, Delta Natural Gas and Kentucky Utilities (KU) in utility-funded energy assistance. Ask when you book your LIHEAP appointment.

Kentucky eviction timeline in Lexington: what happens and when

In Lexington, an eviction is a "forcible detainer" case in Fayette District Court, Robert F. Stephens District Courthouse, 150 N. Limestone. The circuit clerk's Forcible Detainer Division is in Suite D-112, 859-246-4150, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., Monday through Friday. These are the steps:

  • 1. The notice. For unpaid rent, the landlord must give written notice and 7 days to pay before ending the lease (KRS 383.660(2)). For other lease violations, the notice must give at least 14 days, and if you fix the problem in time, the lease does not end (KRS 383.660(1)). A month-to-month tenancy takes 30 days' written notice to end (KRS 383.695). Call Community & Resident Services and LABG now.
  • 2. The court papers. The clerk's page says a landlord can file on the 8th day after a 7-day notice, the 15th day after a 14-day notice, and the 31st day after a 30-day notice. The warrant must give you at least three days' notice of the trial (KRS 383.210). Call LABG the day you get them; if court is three business days away or less, call instead of applying online.
  • 3. Court. Go. If you do not show up, the judge can rule against you. Bring your lease, receipts, texts, photos of repair problems, and anything showing the landlord took or refused your rent. If Community & Resident Services approved help, bring that proof.
  • 4. The 7 days after judgment. You can appeal within 7 days, but you must deposit with the circuit court clerk the rent owed since the case started and keep paying each month's rent into court while the appeal is pending (KRS 383.255). If no appeal is filed by the seventh day, the court "shall, on request," issue a warrant of restitution (KRS 383.245).
  • 5. The warrant and the sheriff. In Fayette County, the clerk's page says the landlord requests the warrant for possession between the 8th and 30th day after the judgment and must "call the sheriff or constable of your choice to schedule an appointment for them to be present when the property is removed." Under local court policy, a warrant cannot be issued more than 30 days after the judgment without a motion and notice to you. Move your things before that appointment if you can.
  • Lockouts and shutoffs are illegal. If a landlord "unlawfully removes or excludes the tenant from the premises or willfully diminishes services" by cutting off heat, running water, hot water, electric, gas or a similar service, you can get back in or end the lease and recover up to three months' rent and attorney's fees (KRS 383.655). Call LABG.
  • Retaliation. A landlord may not raise rent, cut services or bring an eviction because you complained to a code agency or the landlord about repairs, or joined a tenant union. A complaint within one year before the landlord's action creates a presumption of retaliation. That protection does not apply if you are behind on rent (KRS 383.705).

Security deposits: the landlord must keep your deposit in a separate bank account and tell you where it is, give you a written list of existing damage before you move in, and inspect and list damage again at move-out. A landlord who skips the separate account and both lists "shall not be entitled to retain any portion" of it (KRS 383.580). Sign the move-out list or write down exactly what you disagree with, because that is what you can contest later.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The old Housing Stabilization Program. Community Action Council, which ran Lexington's pandemic rent program with the City, says the federal funds "have now been exhausted." Some sites still link to it. It is over.
  • The Team Kentucky Eviction Diversion Program. It covered "All Kentucky counties except Jefferson County and Fayette County," and it "stopped accepting applications on Tuesday, April 1, 2025." Lexington renters were never eligible.
  • The Lexington Housing Authority (LHA) runs Section 8 and public housing, not emergency rent. Its voucher page says "The LHA's HCV waiting list is currently closed," although its senior apartments and Centre Meadows lists are open (checked November 12, 2026). See our LHA guide. If you already have LHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call (859) 281-5060 right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • A local voucher-discrimination rule. Since March 6, 2024, Kentucky law (2024 Ky. Acts ch. 3, House Bill 18) says no city or urban-county government "shall adopt or enforce" a rule stopping a landlord from refusing a tenant whose rent "includes funding from a federal housing assistance program." So a Lexington landlord can legally refuse a voucher. Refusals based on race, disability, family status or another federally protected trait are still illegal; report them to HUD.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight, dial 2-1-1, and call the Hope Center (men) at 859-252-7881 or Greenhouse17 at 800-544-2022 if you are not safe at home.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Lexington right now?

Yes, by appointment and while funds last. The City of Lexington's Community and Resident Services offers emergency financial assistance for rent, mortgage or utilities to prevent eviction or disconnection. You need at least a year in Fayette County, no help from that office with rent or utilities in the last three years, and a seven-day notice, an eviction notice, or rent 30 days past due. Call (859) 300-5300 to book.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Lexington?

Call Legal Aid of the Bluegrass at 859-431-8200, Monday through Thursday 10 a.m. to 3 p.m. or Friday 10 a.m. to 1 p.m., or apply online at lablaw.org and expect a response within 72 hours. If your court date is three business days away or less, call instead. Lexington has no Right to Counsel law, so call as soon as you get a notice.

How much notice does a Lexington landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

Seven days. Lexington-Fayette has adopted Kentucky's Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, so the landlord must give written notice that the lease will end if you do not pay within 7 days. The Fayette circuit clerk says the landlord can file on the 8th day. For other lease violations the notice must give at least 14 days, and a month-to-month tenancy takes 30 days' written notice.

How long do I have after an eviction judgment in Lexington?

Seven days to appeal, and you must deposit the rent owed since the case began with the circuit court clerk and keep paying rent into court. If you do not appeal, the landlord can request a warrant for possession between the 8th and 30th day after the judgment and schedule the sheriff or a constable. Call Legal Aid of the Bluegrass the day of the judgment.

Where do I get help with my utility bill in Lexington?

Community Action Council runs LIHEAP in Fayette County. Its Fall Subsidy takes applications from November 9 through December 18, 2026, or until funds run out, for households at or below 150% of the poverty guidelines, and it does not need a disconnect notice. Book online or call 859-300-6960. For LEXserv bills, call the City's Community and Resident Services at (859) 300-5300.

Can my Lexington landlord lock me out or cut off my utilities?

No. Under Kentucky's landlord-tenant act, which Lexington has adopted, a landlord who locks you out or cuts heat, water, electric, gas or a similar service can be made to let you back in, and you can recover up to three months' rent and attorney's fees. Only the sheriff or a constable with a court warrant can remove you. Call Legal Aid of the Bluegrass at 859-431-8200.

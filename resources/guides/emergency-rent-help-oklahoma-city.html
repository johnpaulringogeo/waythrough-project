Emergency Rent Help in Oklahoma City: Who Still Pays, Free Eviction Lawyers, and the 5-Day Clock

Matt · Updated October 2026

If you rent in Oklahoma City and you are behind, we could not find a rent program run by the City of Oklahoma City or Oklahoma County that you can apply to yourself. The City sends people to 211 and to local nonprofits instead. The door with the clearest rules is Upward Transitions: every Monday it screens families with children or seniors who have a 5-day notice, and it pays up to two months of rent, capped at $1,000. The Community Action Agency now helps with rent only for families in its Head Start and early childhood programs. For eviction help, Legal Aid Services of Oklahoma takes applications at 1-888-534-5243, and the OCU Law Tenant Rights Clinic helps any Oklahoma County tenant. Everything here was checked on October 15, 2026.

Here's the short version: On Monday morning, apply to Upward Transitions online at 7 a.m. or by phone at 9 a.m. at (405) 232-5507. Dial 2-1-1 for Heartline's list of other rent help. If you live in ZIP code 73111, 73119, or 73120, call Legal Aid's Right to Counsel line at (405) 554-INFO right away; there is no income limit. Everyone else: apply to Legal Aid at 1-888-534-5243 or call OCU's clinic at (405) 208-5207. And if you get a court summons, go to court. Oklahoma eviction hearings can come in as little as 5 days.

Oklahoma moves fast: the 5-day notice and the 5-to-10-day hearing

Under 41 O.S. section 131(B), a landlord can end your lease if you do not pay "within five (5) days after written notice of landlord's demand for payment." The landlord can send that notice before or after filing in court. Once a case is filed, the summons sets your trial "not less than five (5) days nor more than ten (10) days" after it is issued (12 O.S. section 1148.4), and it has to reach you at least 3 days before the hearing (12 O.S. sections 1148.5 and 1148.16). That is why every rent door below asks for your 5-day notice: by the time you have a court date, you may have only days left.

Help paying rent in Oklahoma City: the doors that pay

Apply to more than one. Each runs on its own money, and none of them promises help.

Upward Transitions: Monday rent screening

Upward Transitions is a United Way of Central Oklahoma partner agency. Its rent and utility help opens once a week: "Our weekly rent and utility assistance screening opens each Monday online via our website at 7:00 a.m. for families with children under the age of 18 or seniors age 55 or older living in Oklahoma County or Oklahoma City limits. Individuals without internet access may apply to be screened by phone at 9:00 a.m. Mondays." It takes requests "until the number of qualifying requests received exceeds our funding capacity for the week," and it warns that "We reach our weekly funding capacity very quickly" (checked October 15, 2026).

  • Who qualifies, in its words: you live in Oklahoma County or the Oklahoma City limits; you have "a 5-day notice to quit, eviction notice, or cutoff notice in the client's name for the current address"; a child under 18 or a senior 55 or older lives in the home; you have proof of the hardship that made the bill hard to pay; and you have "proof of income or pending income."
  • How much: "Assistance is limited to one time, up to two months' rent not to exceed $1,000."
  • What to have ready: photo ID and Social Security cards for the adults, Social Security numbers for the children, proof of income, your lease, and the eviction notice or 5-day notice. You will also sign releases so the agency can talk to your landlord.
  • When not to try: "We do not accept requests during weeks with a holiday." It also says it may take up to 48 hours before someone contacts you.
  • One catch: an older notice on the same page says that because of "technical difficulties," requests had to be made by phone. If the online form does not open at 7 a.m., call (405) 232-5507 at 9 a.m. If you get a busy message, the agency says to hang up and call again.

Office hours are 8 a.m. to noon and 1 to 4:30 p.m. on weekdays. The agency says to call ahead to make sure a program is taking clients. Start at its online assistance screening page.

211 Heartline: the switchboard

The City of Oklahoma City's own advice is: "Call 211 or Text 211OK to 898211 to get help with food, rent, utilities, and more." United Way of Central Oklahoma calls 2-1-1 "a free, 24-hour helpline" and lists rental assistance among the help it connects people to. 211 is run by Heartline, and you can also search Heartline's 211 directory online. Ask for rent help in Oklahoma County and write down every name and number you get.

Community Action Agency of Oklahoma City: only for its Head Start families now

Many older lists still name the Community Action Agency (CAA) as OKC's main rent help. That changed. CAA says its board "approved a refined mission and strategic direction that centers our programs on families with children under five," and that some programs "will gradually transition or sunset over time." Its rent page now reads: "We offer limited emergency assistance for past-due rent, mortgage or utility bills for eligible families enrolled in our Head Start and early childhood programs," funded "through partners such as FEMA and United Way" and "available as funding allows." It cannot help with deposits or hotel costs.

If your child is enrolled in CAA's Head Start or early childhood programs, call its Family Empowerment team at (405) 232-0199, weekdays 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. Applications are not online. CAA notes that families on SNAP automatically qualify for its Head Start.

The Salvation Army and NSO's Stay Housed center

  • The Salvation Army, Central Oklahoma Area Command: the City of Oklahoma City's community resources page lists it for "Rent/Mortgage Assistance." Its own page does not post rules, so call (405) 246-1100 and ask what is open this week.
  • Neighborhood Services Organization (NSO) Stay Housed Tenant Advocacy Center: lists "Personalized Support · Advocacy · Rental Assistance" and helps tenants understand the eviction process and find resources. It also offers free rides to eviction court. Call Stay Housed at 405-984-4202.

Eviction help in Oklahoma City: free lawyers

If you searched for eviction help in Oklahoma City, these are the answers with names. None of them pays rent, but a lawyer can catch mistakes in the landlord's papers, ask for more time, and work out a payment plan.

Legal Aid Services of Oklahoma (LASO)

  • Right to Counsel in three ZIP codes: "Legal Aid Services of Oklahoma, Inc may be able to provide you with free assistance if you qualify and live in Zip Codes 73111, 73119, or 73120." Call (405) 554-4636, which LASO spells "(405) 554-INFO." It says "there is not an income requirement for this program," and "Do not wait for your eviction hearing. Call as soon as you have problems with your landlord." It is "not a financial assistance program."
  • Everywhere else in Oklahoma: apply through LASO's general intake. Call 1-888-534-5243 or 918-428-4357; LASO says "Calls answered Mon – Thurs, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m." Or apply online, then check your email and call the priority number it gives you to finish. LASO's HUD Eviction Prevention program "provides legal assistance to anyone facing eviction" through the same number.
  • At the courthouse: NSO's Stay Housed guide says that "If you want to connect with a free Legal Aid attorney at the courthouse, arrive 30 minutes early."
  • The Oklahoma City office serves Oklahoma and Canadian counties at 2915 North Classen Boulevard, Suite 200. Phone: 405-521-1302. LASO offices are open Monday through Thursday, 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., and some close at noon, so call before you go.

OCU Law Tenant Rights Clinic

Oklahoma City University School of Law's Tenant Rights Clinic (it used to be called HELP) gives free legal help to "all tenants in Oklahoma County facing lease disputes or eviction." It can advise any tenant in Oklahoma, but it can represent tenants in court only for evictions in Oklahoma County. Call (405) 208-5207, use the form on its clinic page, or drop in from 2:30 to 4:30 p.m. on the first or third Monday of the month at NSO's Stay Housed Tenant Advocacy Center, 1318 Linwood Boulevard.

Oklahoma eviction timeline: what happens and when

Oklahoma's rules are in the Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (Title 41) and the forcible entry and detainer law (Title 12). These are the steps for a rent case, in order:

  • 1. The 5-day notice. The landlord gives a written demand for the rent. If you do not pay within 5 days, the landlord can end the lease (41 O.S. section 131(B)). That demand also counts as a demand to move out, so no other notice is needed.
  • 2. The court case. The landlord files an eviction affidavit with the court clerk (12 O.S. section 1148.15). Many of these cases go on the small claims docket (12 O.S. section 1148.14).
  • 3. The summons and hearing. The hearing is set 5 to 10 days after the summons is issued, and you must get the summons at least 3 days before it (12 O.S. sections 1148.4, 1148.5, and 1148.16). NSO's Stay Housed guide says Oklahoma County evictions are heard on the 6th floor of the Oklahoma County District Court, 320 S. Robert S. Kerr Avenue. Check your summons for the exact courtroom and time.
  • 4. Go. If you do not show up, the landlord usually wins by default. At court you can point out mistakes in what you owe, ask for more time, or work out a payment plan.
  • 5. After a judgment. A motion for a new trial must be filed within 3 days and does not stop the eviction (12 O.S. section 1148.10). The landlord or officer must give notice that they will return in 48 hours to put the landlord back in possession (12 O.S. section 1148.10A).
  • Lockouts are illegal. If a landlord wrongfully removes or locks you out, you can go to court to get back in or end the lease, and recover up to twice the average monthly rent or twice your actual damages, whichever is more (41 O.S. section 123).

You can look up your case on the Oklahoma State Courts Network. Our step-by-step guide to avoiding eviction covers the whole process, and understanding landlord notices explains the paper you got.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The Oklahoma City Housing Authority (OCHA) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its site says "HCV waiting list is closed. Not accepting applications" (checked October 15, 2026). Public housing applications are online only, for site waiting lists that are open. Main line: (405) 239-7551. Our OCHA page has the details.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Oklahoma City?

Yes, but it is small and it goes fast. Upward Transitions screens families with children under 18 or seniors 55 and older every Monday, online at 7 a.m. or by phone at 9 a.m. at (405) 232-5507, and pays up to two months of rent, capped at $1,000. Dial 2-1-1 for Heartline's list of other rent help. We could not find a rent program run by the City or Oklahoma County that takes applications directly.

Does the Community Action Agency of Oklahoma City help with rent?

Only for some families now. The Community Action Agency says its limited rent, mortgage, and utility help is for eligible families enrolled in its Head Start and early childhood programs, as funding allows. It does not pay deposits or hotel costs. If your child is enrolled, call (405) 232-0199, weekdays 8 a.m. to 4 p.m.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Oklahoma City?

If you live in ZIP code 73111, 73119, or 73120, call Legal Aid Services of Oklahoma's Right to Counsel line at (405) 554-4636; there is no income limit. Anywhere else, apply to Legal Aid at 1-888-534-5243, Monday through Thursday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., or online. The OCU Law Tenant Rights Clinic also helps Oklahoma County tenants at (405) 208-5207.

How fast is an eviction in Oklahoma?

Very fast. The landlord can end your lease if you do not pay within 5 days of a written demand. After the case is filed, the hearing is set 5 to 10 days after the summons is issued, and you must get the summons at least 3 days before. After a judgment, the landlord must give 48 hours' notice before you are removed.

Can my landlord lock me out in Oklahoma?

No. Only a court can order you out. If a landlord wrongfully removes you or locks you out, Oklahoma law lets you go to court to get back in, and you can recover up to twice the average monthly rent or twice your actual damages, whichever is more.

Does OCHA give emergency rent help?

No. The Oklahoma City Housing Authority runs Section 8 and public housing, and its Housing Choice Voucher waiting list is closed and not accepting applications. If you already live in OCHA housing or have a voucher and fall behind, call OCHA at (405) 239-7551 right away.

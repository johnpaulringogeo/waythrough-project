Emergency Rent Help in Charlotte: Crisis Assistance Ministry, Free Eviction Lawyers, and the 10-Day Demand

Matt · Updated October 2026

If you rent in Charlotte or anywhere in Mecklenburg County and you are behind, the City and the County both point you to the same door: Crisis Assistance Ministry on Spratt Street. The County's Department of Social Services contracts with it to hand out the community's local, state, and federal emergency money, so there is no separate City or County rent application to fill out. The pandemic-era RAMP CLT program is not a door anymore; its old web address no longer leads to a working site. For eviction help, Legal Aid of North Carolina takes applications at 1-866-219-5262, and the County funds eviction prevention through Legal Aid and the Charlotte Center for Legal Advocacy. Everything here was checked on October 21, 2026.

Here's the short version: Go to Crisis Assistance Ministry at 500-A Spratt Street any weekday from 9 a.m. with your ID, Social Security documents, 30 days of income proof, your lease, and your rent notice or court papers. Its number is (704) 371-3001. If you are homeless or will be within 14 days, call Coordinated Entry at (704) 284-9665 or dial 2-1-1. If your landlord has already filed in court, apply to Legal Aid at 1-866-219-5262 the same day. And if you get a summons, go to court. In North Carolina the hearing can come within 7 business days.

North Carolina moves fast: the 10-day demand and the 7-day summons

Under N.C. General Statute 42-3, a lease is forfeited "upon failure to pay the rent within 10 days after a demand is made by the lessor or his agent on said lessee for all past-due rent." Once the landlord files a Complaint in Summary Ejectment, the clerk issues a summons for a court date "not to exceed seven days from the issuance of the summons, excluding weekends and legal holidays" (G.S. 42-28). That is why Crisis Assistance Ministry puts people with court papers near the front of its line, and why you should call Legal Aid the day you are served. One piece of good news: in a case for unpaid rent, if you pay the rent due and the court costs before the judgment, "all further proceedings in such action shall cease" (G.S. 42-33).

Help paying rent in Charlotte: the doors that pay

Start with Crisis Assistance Ministry, and keep looking while you wait. None of these programs promises help.

Crisis Assistance Ministry: the main door

Crisis Assistance Ministry (CAM) is a nonprofit, and it is the place the City and County send renters. In its own words: "Rent and utility assistance for short-term financial emergencies is available in-person for low-income Mecklenburg County residents at 500-A Spratt St, Monday - Friday, beginning at 9:00 a.m." (checked October 21, 2026). Its lobby is open Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., except holidays and scheduled closures.

  • Who qualifies, in its words: "You must live in Mecklenburg County to apply," and "To qualify for financial assistance, you need a regular source of household income." That can be wages, tips or self-employment income; Social Security, disability, veterans' pensions or unemployment; or regular child support, alimony, or pension payments.
  • What to bring (legible copies): a picture ID for the person applying; Social Security documentation for everyone in the household (a card, a letter from the Social Security Administration, a W-2, a paystub with the full number, or a W-7; if you are not registered with Social Security, some form of ID); proof of all household income for the past 30 days; your past-due rent notice or court documents; and your lease. CAM says "Any missing items will require follow-up before your request can be considered."
  • How it works: you fill out an application and consent form, and staff review it with you. Then you can go home and wait for a caseworker's call. CAM warns that "On some days, more people may be seeking assistance than can be served that day," and you may be asked to come back another day. It also says "it may be a week before you get a call."
  • Who goes first: CAM works like an emergency room. It says "those facing court-ordered eviction are addressed before those with a late notice for rent who have not been to court." The most urgent cases are people already locked out after an eviction order or already disconnected.
  • Where the money goes: "If assistance is awarded, a check is issued directly to your utility vendor, landlord, or clerk of court."
  • What it does not cover: CAM sends you to 2-1-1 for first month's rent or a security deposit on a new home, hotel stays, utility bills from a past address, and mortgage payments. And it says: "If you have multiple months past due rent or your landlord has already filed court papers, contact Legal Aid for assistance."
  • If you cannot get there: Mecklenburg County says homebound callers should call CAM at (704) 371-3001, press 0 for an operator, and ask for the Crisis Homebound Line.

Before you go, you can take CAM's short eligibility quiz on its Emergency Financial Assistance page. CAM also says you can apply directly to other local agencies, such as Good Friends, while you wait, without going through CAM first.

The City of Charlotte and Mecklenburg County: they send you to CAM

The City's Emergency Housing Assistance page says the City helps "by partnering with non-profit community partners," and its instruction for renters is plain: "For assistance with rent or utility payments, contact Crisis Assistance Ministry at 704-371-3001." Mecklenburg County's Emergency Assistance Program says the same thing in more detail: the Department of Social Services "contracts with Crisis Assistance Ministry (CAM) to serve as the community's central location for distribution of local, state and federal emergency funds," and CAM "is the first portal of entry for financial services in the community" (both checked October 21, 2026).

The County's own Emergency Program Aid "is offered through churches and community agency referrals to eligible residents who are facing a hardship," and the County notes "There is no guarantee that financial assistance will be provided." Its office is the Valerie C. Woodard Community Resource Center, 3205 Freedom Drive; phone 704-336-3000. You cannot walk in and apply for it the way you can at CAM, so start at CAM.

Coordinated Entry and NC 211: if you could lose your home soon

  • Coordinated Entry (Mecklenburg County): the County calls it "your first step to connect to homeless services and homelessness prevention resources in Mecklenburg County" if you are homeless or think you will be soon. "Call 704-284-9665 and leave a message. A staff person will call you back Monday through Friday between the hours of 8 a.m. and 5 p.m." The City's advice matches: "If you are homeless or will become homeless in the next 14 days, contact NC 2-1-1 (or 1-888-892-1162)."
  • NC 211: run by United Way of North Carolina. Dial 2-1-1 or 1-888-892-1162 "to receive free and confidential information on health and human services within their community," or search nc211.org. Ask for rent help in Mecklenburg County and write down every name and number you get.

Veterans: Catholic Charities' SSVF program

Catholic Charities Diocese of Charlotte runs Supportive Services for Veteran Families (SSVF) for "Veteran families in Mecklenburg County who are homeless or at risk of becoming homeless." Its funds can cover "short-term rent and utilities, rental and utility arrears," moving costs, and application fees. You qualify if you (or your spouse, as head of household) are a veteran with a discharge other than dishonorable, have "very low income of less than 80% of the area median," and are homeless or in danger of becoming homeless. Call 704-370-3257.

Utility bills: the Crisis Intervention Program

If a power or heating bill is what is pushing your rent out of reach, CAM points to the Crisis Intervention Program (CIP), "if available," which you apply for online through North Carolina ePASS or in person at Mecklenburg County's Department of Community Resources.

Eviction help in Charlotte: free lawyers and mediation

If you searched for Charlotte eviction help, these are the answers with names. None of them pays rent, but a lawyer can catch mistakes in the landlord's papers, raise defenses, handle an appeal, and work out a payment plan. Mecklenburg County says it provides eviction prevention "In partnership with Legal Aid of North Carolina and Charlotte Center for Legal Advocacy," with the goal to "Prevent evictions from reaching the courts when possible."

Legal Aid of North Carolina

  • What it does: "We can help with evictions, fair housing, foreclosures, housing discrimination, maintenance disputes and more." It serves low-income North Carolinians.
  • How to apply: call the Legal Aid NC Helpline at 1-866-219-LANC (5262), "9:00 AM – 3:00 PM, Monday – Friday," or apply online through JusticeHub, its online intake portal, any time.
  • The Charlotte office serves Mecklenburg, Cabarrus, Cleveland, Gaston, Lincoln, Stanly, and Union counties at 5525 Albemarle Road, Suite 100. Phone: 704-594-8662. Hours: Monday through Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m.
  • Free guides: Legal Aid publishes an eviction guide and a guide to eviction appeals.

Charlotte Center for Legal Advocacy

The Charlotte Center for Legal Advocacy says it "serves individuals and families facing a crisis of safety, shelter, health or income." Call its client helpline at 704-376-1600, Monday through Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., or use its online intake. Its Spanish-language helpline is 800-247-1931. The office is at 5535 Albemarle Road. Its own list of services did not name tenant eviction defense when we checked, so when you call, say you have an eviction case and ask whether it or Legal Aid should take it.

City of Charlotte Dispute Settlement Program: free mediation

If you can still talk with your landlord, the City's Dispute Settlement Program offers "free mediation and conciliation services," including for landlord-tenant disputes. It says "we've helped landlords and tenants negotiate rental payment plans, ensuring stable housing while preventing evictions." To schedule a session, call 704-336-2903. A mediator does not decide the case; both sides have to agree.

North Carolina eviction timeline: what happens and when

In North Carolina an eviction case is called "summary ejectment." These are the steps for a rent case, in order:

  • 1. The 10-day demand. The landlord demands the past-due rent. If you do not pay within 10 days, the lease is forfeited (G.S. 42-3). The state courts put it this way: "The tenant did not pay rent, the landlord made a demand for rent and waited 10 days, but the tenant still has not paid the rent."
  • 2. The court case. The landlord files a Complaint in Summary Ejectment with the clerk. The summons sets your court date no more than 7 days after it is issued, not counting weekends and legal holidays (G.S. 42-28). The papers are mailed to you, and the sheriff tries to deliver them in person; if that fails, the sheriff can post them on your door (G.S. 42-29). The courts say that if you were served only by posting and do not show up, the court cannot order you to pay money, but it can still order you out.
  • 3. Small claims court. Go. A magistrate hears the case. If you do not come, the magistrate hears only the landlord's side. The courts say magistrates "may not give a continuance of more than five days unless the parties agree," so bring your receipts, texts, photos, and lease the first time.
  • 4. Paying before judgment. In a case for unpaid rent, if you pay or offer the rent due plus the court costs before judgment, the case stops (G.S. 42-33). This is where a CAM check to the landlord or the clerk of court can save the home.
  • 5. Appeal within 10 days. Either side can appeal to District Court within 10 days of the judgment, and the appeal costs must be paid within 10 days unless you are approved as indigent (G.S. 7A-228). The courts say "The landlord cannot remove the tenant from the home until the appeal period has ended, whether or not the tenant appeals the case." To stay in the home during the appeal, you pay the undisputed back rent the magistrate found (unless you are indigent) and sign a bond to pay rent as it comes due (G.S. 42-34). People who get SNAP, TANF, or SSI are considered unable to pay court costs. The courts' free Guide & File service can help you prepare an appeal.
  • 6. The writ. After the 10 days, the landlord can ask the clerk for a writ of possession. The sheriff must give you notice of about when it will be carried out and has no more than 5 days to do it (G.S. 42-36.2). Ask for your belongings within 7 days after the landlord is put back in possession; the landlord must let you get them during that time (G.S. 42-36.2).
  • Lockouts are illegal. State law says a residential tenant can be removed "only in accordance with the procedure prescribed" by the eviction statutes (G.S. 42-25.6). The courts add that landlords cannot force you out "by changing the locks, turning off utilities or removing the doors." If a landlord does, you can sue to get back in or end the lease, plus actual damages (G.S. 42-25.9).

The North Carolina courts' landlord and tenant help page explains each step. Our step-by-step guide to avoiding eviction covers the whole process, and understanding landlord notices explains the paper you got.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • RAMP CLT, the pandemic-era rent program, is not a current door. Its old web address now leads to a parked domain page, and we found no City or County page offering it (checked October 21, 2026). Ignore old links and flyers that send you there.
  • Catholic Charities Diocese of Charlotte (outside its veterans program above) says: "FOR ASSISTANCE WITH HOUSING, RENT, MORTGAGE, OR UTILITIES, PLEASE CALL 211."
  • INLIVIAN, the former Charlotte Housing Authority, runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. It says "The tenant-based Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) waiting list is currently closed to new applicants. There are currently more than 6,000 households on this list" (checked October 21, 2026). If you already live in INLIVIAN housing or have a voucher and fall behind, call (704) 336-5183 right away and get legal help: the state courts note that an eviction "could affect your right to receive further housing assistance." Our INLIVIAN guide covers its waiting lists, phone numbers, and how to apply.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight, call Coordinated Entry, and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Charlotte?

Yes, and almost all of it runs through one place. The City of Charlotte and Mecklenburg County both send renters who are behind to Crisis Assistance Ministry, 500-A Spratt Street. It takes applications in person, Monday through Friday, starting at 9 a.m., for low-income Mecklenburg County residents with a regular source of income. Call (704) 371-3001. If you are homeless or will be within 14 days, call Coordinated Entry at (704) 284-9665 or dial 2-1-1.

What do I need to bring to Crisis Assistance Ministry?

Legible copies of: a picture ID for the person applying; Social Security documentation for everyone in the household; proof of all household income for the past 30 days; your past-due rent notice or court papers; and your lease. For a utility bill, bring the past-due bill or disconnection notice. Missing items mean a follow-up before your request can be considered.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Charlotte?

Apply to Legal Aid of North Carolina at 1-866-219-5262, Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 3 p.m., or online through its JusticeHub portal. Its Charlotte office serves Mecklenburg County. Mecklenburg County also funds eviction prevention through Legal Aid and the Charlotte Center for Legal Advocacy, whose helpline is (704) 376-1600. Call as soon as you get court papers; the hearing can be about a week away.

How fast is an eviction in North Carolina?

Fast. For unpaid rent, the landlord demands the rent and waits 10 days. After the landlord files, the court date can be no more than 7 days after the summons is issued, not counting weekends and legal holidays. If the magistrate rules against you, you have 10 days to appeal, and the landlord cannot have you removed until those 10 days pass. After that, the sheriff has up to 5 days to carry out the writ.

Can I stop a North Carolina eviction by paying the rent?

In a case for unpaid rent, yes, if you pay before the judgment. North Carolina law says that if the tenant pays or offers the rent due and the court costs before judgment, all further proceedings in the case stop. After a judgment, you can stay in the home during an appeal only if you pay the undisputed back rent the magistrate found, unless you are indigent, and sign a bond to pay rent as it comes due.

Does INLIVIAN give emergency rent help?

No. INLIVIAN, the former Charlotte Housing Authority, runs Section 8 and public housing, and it says its Housing Choice Voucher waiting list is closed to new applicants, with more than 6,000 households already on it. If you already have INLIVIAN housing or a voucher and fall behind, call INLIVIAN at (704) 336-5183 and get legal help, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.

Emergency Rent Help in Birmingham, Alabama: 211, Free Legal Aid, and Alabama's Seven-Business-Day Clock

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Birmingham and you are behind, here is the honest picture. The big pandemic rent programs are over: Jefferson County says its Emergency Rental Assistance Program “has ended,” and we found no open City of Birmingham rent fund on birminghamal.gov. Alabama has no right to counsel, so there is no guaranteed free lawyer. What you do have: 211 Connects Alabama to find money that is left, free legal aid, utility help, and a short window of seven business days after a notice to pay. This guide covers Birmingham and the rest of Jefferson County. Everything here was checked on November 5, 2026.

Here's the short version: Count seven business days from the day you got the notice. Dial 2-1-1 (or 888-421-1266) and ask for rent help in Jefferson County. Call Legal Services Alabama at 866-456-4995 the same day. If you can pay everything the notice asks for within those seven business days, the lease does not end. If you get court papers, go to court, and know that you have only seven days to appeal.

Alabama's clock: seven business days

For unpaid rent, Alabama law says the landlord's written notice must give the amount of rent and late fees owed and a date “not less than seven business days after receipt of the notice.” Then: “If the breach is not remedied within the seven business days, the rental agreement shall terminate” (Ala. Code § 35-9A-421(b)). Paying in full inside that window keeps your lease. There is a limit, though: no breach “may be cured by a tenant more than two times in any 12-month period except by the express written consent of the landlord” (§ 35-9A-421(d)).

Free legal help in Birmingham

None of these pays rent. But a lawyer can check the notice and court papers for mistakes, raise defenses such as bad repairs, help you work out a payment plan, and make sure you get every day the law allows.

  • Legal Services Alabama gives “free, client-centered civil legal advocacy to low-income Alabamians,” and lists “Eviction” among the housing problems its staff can help with. Call the statewide intake line at 866-456-4995 (English) or 888-835-3505 (Spanish), Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., or apply online at legalservicesalabama.org. The Birmingham office is at 1820 7th Ave North, Suite 200, 205-328-3540.
  • Volunteer Lawyers Birmingham serves Jefferson County residents whose family income is at or below 125% of the federal poverty level. Call 205-250-5198. AlabamaLegalHelp.org notes that it does not serve undocumented non-citizens.
  • No right to counsel. Birmingham and Jefferson County do not guarantee a lawyer in eviction cases, so call early. Legal aid has limited staff, and Alabama's deadlines are short.

Help paying rent in Birmingham: the doors that are left

There is no single rent office to walk into. Use 211 to find who has money this month, and keep calling. None of these programs promises help.

211 Connects Alabama

United Way of Central Alabama runs 211. Dial 2-1-1 or 888-421-1266 (toll-free), or text your ZIP code to 898-211. United Way says housing help through 211 may include “Rental and mortgage assistance,” “Utility assistance” and “Emergency shelter,” and that “Callers do not need to know which program to ask for.” Write down every name and number you get. If you are hearing-impaired, call 7-1-1 and ask for 211 Connects Alabama.

Community Action Agency of Northeast Alabama (Jefferson County)

ADECA named this agency in 2022 to run LIHEAP and other community action programs in Jefferson County. Its Birmingham office is at 900 38th Street North (205-502-2108); for an appointment, call (256) 999-1166. Its Jefferson County page says families may apply for “rental assistance, food cards, fuel gift cards” as funding allows, and that “a full calendar of available resources will be listed on our agency's website starting November 1.” Check caaneal.org/jefferson-county for what is open this month.

The City's Community Service Hub and findhelp

The City of Birmingham does not run an open rent fund that we could find. In June 2026 it opened an in-person Community Service Hub at The Salvation Army in North Birmingham, 2015 26th Ave. North, open “Monday, Wednesday, and Friday from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m.,” with “Connections to housing, food assistance, healthcare, transportation, employment, utility support, and more.” Its online search, bham.findhelp.com, works any time.

If you could lose your home this week

One Roof runs the homeless response system and coordinated entry for Jefferson, St. Clair and Shelby counties. Call 205-254-8833. Veterans can ask 211 for United Way's Priority Veteran program, which helps veterans “at immediate risk of losing their housing.”

Utility bills: LIHEAP and Alabama Power

ADECA runs LIHEAP for Alabama but says it “does not schedule appointments and does not accept or process applications.” In Jefferson County you apply through the Community Action Agency of Northeast Alabama: call center 855-287-1730, or book a case manager on its website. Household income must not exceed 150% of the federal poverty level. Alabama Power says LIHEAP “helps low-income households pay for heating and cooling costs from November through May.” Alabama Power also lists:

  • Project SHARE, for “low-income Alabamians who are age 60 or older and/or disabled.” Apply through The Salvation Army or call 205-328-2420.
  • A monthly discount for customers who get SSI or Medicaid for Low Income Families. Alabama Power says they “may save $14.50 monthly.” Call 1-800-257-2726 or visit a business office for an application.

Alabama eviction timeline: what happens and when

Most Birmingham rentals are covered by Alabama's Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act. These are the steps for an eviction for unpaid rent:

  • 1. The notice. A written notice with the rent and late fees owed and at least seven business days to pay (Ala. Code § 35-9A-421(b)). Pay in full in time and the lease does not end. Remember the two-cures-in-12-months limit in § 35-9A-421(d).
  • 2. The court case. If the breach is not fixed, the landlord can file an eviction case in district court in the county where the home is. Alabama law says eviction cases get “precedence in scheduling over all other civil cases,” so they move fast. If no one is home, papers can be posted on your door and mailed (§ 35-9A-461(b), (c)). Read the summons the day you get it: it tells you how long you have to answer, and that time is short.
  • 3. Court. Go. If you do not respond or show up, you can lose by default. Bring your lease, receipts, texts, photos of repair problems, and any letter showing the landlord refused your rent.
  • 4. After a judgment: seven days. “The automatic stay on the issuance of the writ of possession or restitution shall be for a period of seven days” (§ 35-9A-461(e)). You can also appeal to circuit court “at any time within seven days after the entry” of the judgment (§ 35-9A-461(d)).
  • 5. Staying during an appeal. An appeal does not stop the writ unless you pay the circuit court clerk “all rents properly payable under the terms of the lease since the date of the filing of the action” and keep paying rent as it comes due. Miss a payment and the court shall issue the writ (§ 35-9A-461(d)).
  • Lockouts are illegal. If a landlord locks you out or cuts off heat, running water, hot water, electric or gas, you can get back in or end the lease and recover up to three months' rent or your actual damages, whichever is more, plus attorney's fees (§ 35-9A-407).

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • Jefferson County's ERAP. The County's Community Services page says: “The ERAP programs provided assistance to eligible applicants for emergency rental and utility assistance. This program has ended.” Its old application site no longer loads. Ignore old flyers and news stories about pandemic rent relief.
  • A City of Birmingham rent fund. We found no open City rent program on birminghamal.gov on November 5, 2026. The City's Community Development department, (205) 254-2309, runs housing repair and homeownership programs, not emergency rent.
  • The Housing Authority of the Birmingham District (HABD) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund, and its Section 8 page does not say whether the voucher list is open. See our HABD guide. If you already have HABD housing or a voucher and fall behind, call (205) 521-0774 or the voucher call center at (205) 974-4440 right away, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • A voucher discrimination complaint. Alabama has no law against refusing Section 8, and we found no Birmingham ordinance that bars it. Refusals based on race, disability, family status or another federally protected trait are still illegal; report them to HUD at 1-800-669-9777.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Birmingham right now?

Not from a big public fund. Jefferson County says its Emergency Rental Assistance Program "has ended," and we found no open City of Birmingham rent program on November 5, 2026. Start with 211 Connects Alabama: dial 211 or 888-421-1266, or text your ZIP code to 898-211. The Community Action Agency of Northeast Alabama serves Jefferson County and says it may offer rental assistance as funding allows; its Birmingham office is at 900 38th Street North, 205-502-2108. If you have court papers, call Legal Services Alabama at 866-456-4995 the same day.

How long do I have after a 7-day notice in Alabama?

Seven business days, not seven calendar days. Weekends and holidays do not count. Under Ala. Code 35-9A-421, the notice must say how much rent and late fees you owe, and the lease ends only if you do not pay within seven business days after you receive it. Count the days on a calendar the day the notice arrives, and start calling 211 and Legal Services Alabama right away.

Can I stop a Birmingham eviction by paying the rent?

Yes, if you pay everything the notice asks for within the seven business days. Then the lease does not end. After that window, Alabama law does not make the landlord take the money and drop the case, so get any deal in writing. Also know this limit: the law says a breach of the lease cannot be cured more than two times in any 12-month period without the landlord's written consent.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Birmingham?

Birmingham has no right to counsel, so there is no guaranteed lawyer. Free help comes from legal aid. Call Legal Services Alabama at 866-456-4995, or 888-835-3505 for Spanish, Monday to Friday from 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., or apply online. Its Birmingham office is at 1820 7th Ave North, Suite 200, 205-328-3540. Volunteer Lawyers Birmingham serves Jefferson County residents with income at or below 125% of the federal poverty level at 205-250-5198.

What happens if the judge rules for my landlord?

Alabama gives you seven days. The writ of possession is automatically stayed for seven days after the judgment, and you can appeal to circuit court within seven days. An appeal does not stop the eviction unless you pay the circuit court clerk all rent owed since the case was filed and keep paying rent as it comes due. If you miss a payment, the court can issue the writ. Call a lawyer the same day you lose.

Is the Birmingham Section 8 list open, and can a landlord refuse my voucher?

The Housing Authority of the Birmingham District does not say whether its voucher list is open; its page only explains what to do when a list opens. Call its voucher call center at (205) 974-4440. And yes, a landlord can refuse a voucher: Alabama has no law against it, and we found no Birmingham ordinance that bars it. Refusals based on race, disability, family status or another federally protected trait are still illegal; report them to HUD at 1-800-669-9777.

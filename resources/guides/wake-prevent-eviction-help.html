Wake Prevent!: Wake County's Eviction Prevention Program

Matt · Updated October 2026

If you rent in Wake County, North Carolina, you are behind on rent, and your landlord has sent you a notice, Wake Prevent! is the county program built for exactly that moment. It pays emergency help toward rent and utilities and connects you to housing support services, with the goal of keeping you in the home you already have.

Here's the short version: You apply online at the Wake County Neighborly portal. To qualify you must be at least 30 days late with a notice from your landlord, have lived in the unit more than 90 days, owe no more than 3 months of rent and under $5,000, and not have used the program in the last 3 years. There is no phone number for Wake Prevent! on its own — the department that runs it answers at (919) 856-5689. Apply, then keep working other options in parallel while you wait.

What Wake Prevent! actually is

Wake Prevent! is run by Wake County's Housing Affordability & Community Revitalization department. Wake County describes it as "an emergency resource for Wake County residents who are in a housing crisis and are at risk of experiencing literal homelessness," delivering "a full array of housing support services" so fewer residents lose their housing in the first place.

In plain language, that means two things bundled together:

  • Money toward the crisis. Emergency help with the rent you owe and, where it applies, utilities — paid to keep the tenancy alive rather than handed out as a loan you repay.
  • Housing support services. Staff work on what caused the shortfall and on matching residents to affordable units in Wake County, so the same crisis is less likely to repeat in six months.

It is a one-time-in-three-years emergency resource, not an ongoing subsidy. If what you actually need is a long-term rent subsidy, that is Section 8 — and in Raleigh that waitlist has been closed since October 31, 2023. See our Raleigh Housing Authority page for where that stands.

Scale, so you know what you're applying to: in 2025, Wake Prevent! helped 639 people from 346 households stay in their homes. That is a real program with real money, and also a finite one — which is the argument for applying early rather than waiting until the court date.

Heads up: the program is being redesigned in 2026

Wake County has said it plans to redesign Wake Prevent! during 2026 to incorporate legal services for residents facing eviction, alongside longer-term housing retention support. That is good news — a lawyer at the hearing changes outcomes — but it also means the specifics on this page can shift. Everything below is what Wake County publishes as of October 2026 (checked October 21, 2026). Details may change during 2026; confirm by phone at (919) 856-5689 before you count on any single rule.

Who qualifies: the five rules

Wake County publishes five requirements. You have to meet all five, not most of them. Read them as a checklist against your own situation:

  • 1. You are at least 30 days late paying rent, and you received a late notice or letter. Both halves matter. Being behind is not enough on its own — there has to be something in writing from your landlord. If your landlord has only called or texted, ask for it in writing, and keep the text either way.
  • 2. You have lived in your home for more than 90 days. If you moved in less than three months ago, this program is not the one. Dial 2-1-1 and ask what else is available in Wake County.
  • 3. You owe no more than 3 months of rent. Count what is actually past due, not what you will owe by the end of the year.
  • 4. The amount owed is under $5,000. This is a hard ceiling on the published rules. If you are close to it, apply now rather than after another month accrues and pushes you over.
  • 5. You can only get this help once every 3 years. If Wake Prevent! already paid on your behalf inside that window, you are not eligible again yet.

Two of these — the $5,000 ceiling and the 3-month limit — get harder to meet the longer you wait. That is the single most useful thing to know about this program: the eligibility window closes as your debt grows. Applying at month two is far more likely to work than applying at month four.

If you fail one of the five, do not stop reading. The other Wake County options below are real, and some of them have no such limits.

How to apply

Step 1: Gather your documents before you open the portal

Wake County posts an official Wake Prevent! Required Documents list (checked October 21, 2026). It asks you to "carefully review the entire list below and send everything that applies to your household," and it warns that an incomplete set can get you denied. Have these ready before you open the portal:

  • ID for everyone in the household — a state ID or passport. Children under 18 only need a Social Security card
  • Your eviction paperwork — the late notice or demand letter from your landlord, all pages of any court summons, and anything else the court or your landlord has given you. The late notice is part of the eligibility rule, so it is not optional
  • Your current lease — it must be active, with all pages including the signature page
  • An updated rent ledger with your balance — showing all rent paid and unpaid and any fees, with a running balance. Your landlord can provide it
  • Your 3 most recent months of full bank statements, every page, with balances — including prepaid and app accounts such as Venmo, Cash App, Zelle, Chime, PayPal, Apple Cash, Google Pay and Netspend
  • Proof of income — 3 months of pay stubs showing gross pay (12 stubs if paid weekly, 6 if every two weeks, 3 if monthly); an SSI/SSDI letter dated within the past 30 days, plus 3 months of Direct Express transactions if your benefit is deposited there; or an offer letter on company letterhead if you were just hired
  • Other income — for example a SNAP letter, TANF/Work First, child support (the court order plus 3 months of payment history if it is court-ordered), alimony or unemployment
  • Your filed tax return — the refund page only. The county's list says "Copy of Filed Tax Return for 2026"; if you are not sure which year it means for you, ask at (919) 856-5689

Also have your landlord's contact information and payment address ready, since assistance is generally paid to the landlord rather than to you, and any utility bills you are behind on.

Photograph documents with your phone if you do not have a scanner. A clear, readable phone photo is fine. See our housing document checklist for the version of this list you can print.

Step 2: Apply on the Wake County Neighborly portal

Applications go through Wake County's Neighborly portal. The county's page says "Wake Prevent! receives referrals through the portal" and shows no closure or pause notice for the program (checked October 21, 2026):

Apply: portal.neighborlysoftware.com/WAKECOUNTYNC/Participant

Questions: Wake County Housing Affordability & Community Revitalization — (919) 856-5689, Monday–Friday, 8:30 a.m.–5:15 p.m. · housing.info@wake.gov

  • Create an account using an email address you actually check daily. Decisions and requests for missing documents come through it, and a missed email is the most common way an application dies.
  • Start the Wake Prevent! application from the participant dashboard once you are signed in.
  • Upload everything in one sitting if you can. Partial applications sit in the queue without moving.
  • Write down your application or case number as soon as you have one. You will need it for every follow-up call.
  • Tell your landlord you applied, in writing, the same day. Some landlords will pause a filing if they know money is coming; all of them will need to cooperate with paperwork later.

If you cannot use the online portal — no device, no internet, a disability that makes it inaccessible — call (919) 856-5689 and say so directly. Ask what accommodation or in-person option exists.

Step 3: A note on the phone number

People search for a "Wake Prevent phone number" constantly, so to be clear: there is no dedicated program line. (919) 856-5689 is the main line for the Housing Affordability & Community Revitalization department, which runs Wake Prevent! along with the county's other housing programs. Say "I'm calling about Wake Prevent!" when someone picks up. Other numbers floating around online belong to different Wake County programs — home repair, for instance — and calling those will only cost you a day.

What to do while you wait

An application is not a shield. In North Carolina an eviction case (a "summary ejectment") moves fast — a hearing is commonly set about 7 to 10 days after the landlord files, and an uncontested case can run start to finish in roughly three to five weeks. Assume the clock is running and work these in parallel:

  • Do not move out because of a letter. A notice is not an eviction. Only a court order, carried out by the sheriff, actually removes you. Leaving early forfeits defenses you may have and can still leave you owing the money.
  • Pay something and get a receipt. Partial payment does not automatically stop a case in North Carolina, but a documented good-faith payment history helps you with the landlord, with the magistrate, and with the assistance program.
  • Dial 2-1-1. NC 211 screens you for every other rent, utility, and prevention fund in Wake County and routes you to Coordinated Entry if it comes to shelter. Free, 24/7. You can call it while your Wake Prevent! application is pending — there is no rule against having more than one iron in the fire.
  • If you have been served with court papers, call Legal Aid of North Carolina at 1-866-219-5262 the same day. Not next week. Free eviction defense for people who qualify, and North Carolina's timeline gives them very little room to work with if you wait.
  • Go to your hearing. Not showing up is how most tenants lose. Bring the lease, your payment receipts, your Wake Prevent! confirmation, and photos of any conditions problems.
  • Check your email and portal messages daily. A request for one missing document can idle an application for weeks.

If you are denied, or you don't meet the five rules

Being turned down by Wake Prevent! is not the end of the road, and it is common — the rules are narrow by design. Work down this list:

  • Ask why, and ask whether you can fix it. Call (919) 856-5689 with your application number. If the problem is a missing document or a wrong figure on a ledger, that is fixable. If it is a rule you cannot meet, ask directly: "What else does the county have, and who should I call?"
  • Dial 2-1-1 for the screening that covers everything else in the county.
  • Legal Aid of North Carolina — 1-866-219-5262 · legalaidnc.org. Eviction defense, habitability, discrimination.

The named Wake County nonprofits that run their own emergency assistance:

  • Catholic Charities of the Diocese of Raleigh — emergency financial assistance, food, case management. (919) 821-2050 · catholiccharitiesraleigh.org
  • The Salvation Army of Wake County — eviction prevention and utility assistance. (919) 834-7198
  • Urban Ministries of Wake County — emergency assistance, food pantry, Helen Wright Center for Women. (919) 828-0803
  • St. Vincent de Paul Society of Raleigh — one-time emergency rental and utility help through local parish conferences
  • CASA — long-term affordable housing for low-income people with disabilities and veterans. casanc.org
  • NC LIEAP and the Crisis Intervention Program (CIP) — energy and utility crisis help through Wake County Health and Human Services. Lowering a utility bill frees cash for rent; see utility assistance programs

One caution: the pandemic-era NC HOPE Program has ended and is no longer accepting applications, and NC HAF is for homeowners, not renters. Both still rank in search results. Don't spend a day on either.

Frequently asked questions

What is Wake Prevent!?

Wake County's eviction prevention program: emergency help with rent and utilities plus housing support services, for residents in a housing crisis who are at risk of becoming homeless. It is administered by the county's Housing Affordability & Community Revitalization department, and you apply through the Wake County Neighborly portal.

Who qualifies for Wake Prevent!?

All five must be true: at least 30 days late on rent and you received a late notice or letter; lived in the home more than 90 days; owe no more than 3 months of rent; owe under $5,000; and you have not used the program in the last 3 years.

Is there a phone number for Wake Prevent!?

Not for the program by itself. Call the department that runs it: (919) 856-5689, Monday–Friday, 8:30 a.m.–5:15 p.m., or email housing.info@wake.gov. Applications are submitted online, not by phone.

How do I apply for Wake Prevent!?

Online at portal.neighborlysoftware.com/WAKECOUNTYNC/Participant. Create an account, start the application, and upload what Wake County's Required Documents list asks for: ID for everyone in the household, the late notice or demand letter and any court papers, your active lease and an updated rent ledger, 3 months of full bank statements, proof of income, and the refund page of your filed tax return.

How much money can Wake Prevent! pay?

The published ceiling matches the eligibility rules: no more than 3 months of rent, total under $5,000. Over that and you fall outside the posted rules — but call anyway and ask what else the county has.

How often can I get Wake Prevent! help?

Once every 3 years.

What should I do while I wait to hear back?

Pay what you can and keep receipts, don't move out on the strength of a letter, dial 2-1-1 to be screened for other funds, and if you have been served with court papers call Legal Aid of NC at 1-866-219-5262 the same day. NC hearings are commonly set 7–10 days after filing.

Is Wake Prevent! changing in 2026?

Yes — Wake County has said it plans to redesign the program during 2026 to add legal services for residents facing eviction, plus longer-term housing retention support.

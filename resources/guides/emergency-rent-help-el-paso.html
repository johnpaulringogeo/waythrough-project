Emergency Rent Help in El Paso: Who Still Pays, Free Eviction Help, and the 3-Day Notice

Matt · Updated October 2026

If you rent in El Paso and you are behind, there are two public doors that still pay rent. El Paso County General Assistance "meets the emergency rent or mortgage payment needs of El Paso County residents," and you start with a short online interest form. El Paso United, run by United Way of El Paso County, pays back rent with federal HOME-ARP money: "One-time rent payments for up to three months to prevent eviction." Veterans have a third door through Project Amistad. Project BRAVO, which many older lists name for rent, helps with gas and electric bills, not rent. For eviction help, Texas RioGrande Legal Aid takes calls at (833) 329-8752. Everything here was checked on October 16, 2026.

Here's the short version: Fill out the County's General Assistance interest form today; the County says you should get a call within 48 hours of your confirmation email. Then call El Paso United at (915) 400-7401, weekdays 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. If you served in the military, call Project Amistad's veterans line at 915-298-7307. Dial 2-1-1 for anything else. If you already have court papers, call TRLA right away and go to court. Since January 1, 2026, a Texas landlord can ask the judge to rule without a trial, and you have only 4 days after you are served to answer in writing.

Texas moves fast: a 3-day notice and a 4-day window to answer

Under Texas Property Code section 24.005(a), a landlord must give "at least three days' written notice to vacate" before filing an eviction, "unless the parties have contracted for a shorter or longer notice period" in the lease. If this is the first month you are behind, the notice has to be a notice to pay rent or vacate. A new Texas law, Senate Bill 38, took effect January 1, 2026. Now a landlord can file a sworn motion asking the judge to rule without a trial. The judge can do that unless you file a written response "not later than the fourth day after the date the tenant is served" (section 24.005106). That is why every rent door below asks you to apply the day you get a notice, not the day you get a court date.

Help paying rent in El Paso: the doors that pay

Apply to more than one. Each runs on its own money, and none of them promises help.

El Paso County General Assistance

This is a division of the County's Community Services Department. Its page says it "meets the emergency rent or mortgage payment needs of El Paso County residents" and "provides emergency utility assistance." The online form explains how it works: "The El Paso County General Assistance Program provides emergency rental, mortgage, and utility assistance to income eligible residents. Funding is limited" (checked October 16, 2026).

  • Who qualifies: El Paso County residents under the County's income guidelines. The 2025 guidelines, revised April 10, 2025, use 80% of area median income: $44,450 a year for one person, $50,800 for two, $57,150 for three, and $63,500 for four.
  • How to apply: start with the General Assistance Online Interest Form, in English and Spanish. It asks for your name, address, Social Security number, date of birth, phone, household size, and the one type of help you need. The County says: "You only need to submit one (1) interest form. You should receive a phone call within 48 hours of your confirmation email, excluding weekends and holidays."
  • If you have Section 8: the form asks whether your household gets a rent subsidy such as a Section 8 voucher. Answer honestly; it changes what the County can do.
  • Where: Community Services, 6314 Delta Drive, El Paso, TX 79905. Phone 915-273-3485. General Assistance staff are at 915-273-3496.

El Paso United (United Way's Center for Resiliency)

United Way of El Paso County says its Center for Resiliency "helps individuals and families in the Paso del Norte region access rental assistance during times of financial hardship." The City of El Paso lists the same program as "El Paso United" for rental and utility help. Under the federal HOME-ARP program it offers:

  • Back rent: "One-time rent payments for up to three months to prevent eviction."
  • Ongoing rent: "Short- to medium-term rental payments made directly to landlords."
  • Utilities: "Payments that prevent disconnections and keep homes safe."
  • Who qualifies: HOME-ARP money follows HUD's rules, and United Way screens you for them at intake. It does not post its own income limits, so call and ask.
  • How to apply: call (915) 400-7401, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., or walk in at 6314 Delta Drive (inside the Family Youth Services Center). The City calls this line a "Resilience Navigator" who can also refer you to other help.

Veterans: Project Amistad

Project Amistad's Amistad for Veterans Project, funded by a Texas Veterans Commission grant, lists "Mortgage, Rent, and Rental Deposits," "Utility Bill Assistance," and "Internet Bill Assistance." It does not post its rules online. Call 915-298-7307 and, as its page says, select option 1, then option 1, then enter your ZIP code.

People living with HIV: the City's HOPWA program

The City of El Paso's Housing Opportunities for Persons with AIDS program gives housing and rental help to low-income people living with HIV inside the city limits who are homeless or have been. Call the Department of Public Health at (915) 212-6700 or (915) 212-6665, 701 Montana Avenue.

211 Texas: the switchboard

The City of El Paso sends renters to 2-1-1 Texas for housing options. Dial 2-1-1, say you need rent help in El Paso County, and write down every name and number you get. Ask about church and charity funds, which change month to month.

Project BRAVO: utilities, not rent

Project BRAVO is El Paso's community action agency, and many older lists send renters there. Its own site describes utility help, not rent: "The utility assistance program provides payments for gas, electric, and propane utilities." Paying the light bill can free up money for rent, so it is still worth it, but move fast. Its site says: "Project BRAVO will close applications for the 2026 year on Monday, November 2, or earlier if slots fill up." It takes paper applications only, at its four centers, and says to expect 4 to 6 weeks. Phone: (915) 562-4100.

A note on older lists: some still name pandemic-era funds such as EP Rent Help or Texas Rent Relief. Neither appears on the City's or the County's current resource pages. Don't spend your few days on them.

Eviction help in El Paso: free lawyers and mediation

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can catch mistakes in the landlord's papers, answer a summary-disposition motion in time, and work out a payment plan.

  • Texas RioGrande Legal Aid (TRLA): free civil legal help for low-income people, with an El Paso office at 1331 Texas Avenue. To apply, call (833) 329-8752 (toll-free) or (956) 996-8752. TRLA says its phone line is open "on weekdays from 9:00 a.m. until 5:00 p.m. (Central Time)." El Paso is on Mountain Time, so that is 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. here.
  • Rio Grande Council of Governments mediation: the City of El Paso lists it for landlord-tenant mediation at (915) 533-0998. Mediation only works if your landlord agrees, and Texas law now bars courts from requiring it before an eviction trial.
  • The Justice of the Peace courts: evictions in El Paso County are heard by the Justices of the Peace. Your citation names the precinct, the courtroom, and the trial date.

Texas eviction timeline: what happens and when

Texas eviction law is in Chapter 24 of the Property Code and Rule 510 of the Texas Rules of Civil Procedure. Both were rewritten for cases filed on or after January 1, 2026. These are the steps for a rent case, in order:

  • 1. The notice. At least 3 days' written notice to vacate, unless your lease sets a different period. If you were not behind before this month, it must be a notice to pay rent or vacate (Prop. Code section 24.005(a)). It can be mailed, left inside your home in a conspicuous place, handed to anyone 16 or older who lives there, or sent by email if you agreed to that in writing (section 24.005(f-3)).
  • 2. The petition and citation. The landlord files a sworn petition in justice court (section 24.00505). The citation sets your trial for "not less than 10 days nor more than 21 days after the petition is filed" (Rule 510.8(b)(10)), and no trial can be held until 4 days after you are served (Rule 510.15(b)).
  • 3. Summary disposition: the 4-day deadline. If the landlord asks for judgment without a trial, you must file a written response with your facts and documents no later than the 4th day after you are served. If the judge finds the facts are really in dispute, the case goes to trial 10 to 21 days after filing (section 24.005106).
  • 4. Go to trial. If you do not show up, the landlord usually wins by default. You can file your answer on or before the trial day (Rule 510.11).
  • 5. Appeal. You have 5 days after the judgment is signed to appeal to county court, by bond, cash deposit, or a sworn statement that you cannot afford court costs (section 24.005107). To stay in your home during the appeal, you must pay one rental period's rent into the court registry within 5 days of filing the appeal, and keep paying each period (section 24.0053(a-2)). If you miss a payment, the court can issue a writ of possession without a hearing (section 24.0054).
  • 6. The writ. A writ of possession cannot be issued before the 6th day after judgment. The officer posts a warning on your door at least 24 hours before carrying it out (section 24.0061(b) and (d)).
  • Lockouts. Texas lets a landlord change the locks on a tenant who is behind on rent only if the lease allows it and after written notice, and the landlord "must provide the new key to the tenant at any hour, regardless of whether or not the tenant pays any of the delinquent rent" (section 92.0081). Otherwise, a landlord may not keep you out "except by judicial process."

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • HOME (the Housing Authority of the City of El Paso, formerly HACEP) runs Section 8 and El Paso's converted public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its voucher page says: "Currently, the waitlist for our Housing Choice Voucher Program is closed. We will notify the public when the waitlist is scheduled to reopen" (checked October 16, 2026). The same page says you can still apply online for its Project-Based Voucher and Affordable Housing programs. HOME's website is now ephome.org (it used to be hacep.org). Phone: (915) 849-3742. Our HOME page has the open lists and how to apply.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, call the Welcome Center at the Opportunity Center for the Homeless, (915) 248-5567, see I need housing tonight, and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in El Paso?

Yes. El Paso County General Assistance helps income-eligible county residents with emergency rent, mortgage, and utility bills; start with its online interest form and expect a call within 48 hours. United Way's El Paso United pays up to three months of back rent through HOME-ARP; call (915) 400-7401, weekdays 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Veterans can call Project Amistad at 915-298-7307. Funding is limited at all three.

Does Project BRAVO help with rent?

Not with rent. Project BRAVO's site describes utility help for gas, electric, and propane bills, plus housing counseling and affordable apartments with a long wait. Its 2026 utility applications close Monday, November 2, 2026, or earlier if slots fill up. For rent, apply to El Paso County General Assistance and El Paso United.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in El Paso?

Call Texas RioGrande Legal Aid at (833) 329-8752 or (956) 996-8752. The line is open weekdays 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Central Time, which is 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. in El Paso. TRLA has an El Paso office at 1331 Texas Avenue. Call as soon as you get a notice, not on the day of court.

How fast is an eviction in Texas now?

Very fast. The landlord gives at least 3 days' written notice to vacate, then files in justice court. Trial is set 10 to 21 days after filing. Since January 1, 2026, the landlord can ask for judgment without a trial, and you must file a written response within 4 days after you are served. After a judgment, you have 5 days to appeal, and the writ cannot issue before the 6th day.

Can my landlord lock me out in Texas?

Only in a narrow way. If your lease allows it and the landlord gives written notice first, a landlord can change the locks when you are behind on rent, but must give you a new key at any hour, whether or not you pay. A landlord cannot otherwise keep you out without a court order.

Is the El Paso Section 8 waitlist open?

No. HOME, the Housing Authority of the City of El Paso, says its Housing Choice Voucher waitlist is closed and it will notify the public before it reopens. You can still apply online for its Project-Based Voucher and Affordable Housing programs. HOME does not give emergency rent help; call (915) 849-3742 if you already have a voucher and fall behind.

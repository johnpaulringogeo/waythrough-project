Emergency Rent Help in Oakland: The City's Limited Rent Fund, Rent Board Counselors, and Free Eviction Lawyers

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Oakland and you are behind, here is the honest picture. There is no big, open rent relief fund. The City pays Bay Area Community Services (BACS) to run a homelessness prevention program that can help with past-due rent, but the City says "Funding is extremely limited." What Oakland does have is strong local law: the Rent Adjustment Program (RAP) and the Just Cause for Eviction Ordinance, which, among other things, bar an eviction for a small rent debt. Unlike San Francisco, we found no right-to-counsel program on the City's tenant pages, so free and low-cost lawyers come from the nonprofits the City lists. Oakland is in Alameda County. Everything here was checked on November 5, 2026.

Here's the short version: Apply for Keep People Housed, the BACS program the City funds, at keeppeoplehoused.org, and call the City's Housing Resource Center at (510) 238-6182. Dial 2-1-1 the same day. If you got a notice or court papers, call Centro Legal de la Raza at (510) 437-1554 or the East Bay Community Law Center at (510) 548-4040, and call RAP's housing counselors at (510) 238-3721. If you are sued, file your answer within 10 court days.

California's two clocks, plus Oakland's rent-debt rule

For unpaid rent, a California landlord must first give you a written notice to pay or move out, with "three days' notice, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays" (Code of Civil Procedure 1161; the weekend and holiday rule came from AB 2343 in 2018). If the landlord then sues, your response "shall be filed within 10 days, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays, after the complaint is served" (Code of Civil Procedure 1167, as amended by AB 2347, effective January 1, 2025). In Oakland, for units covered by the Just Cause ordinance, the City says that for an eviction over unpaid rent, "The rent owed must be no less than one month of fair market rent as determined by HUD." You still owe the rent, but a small debt cannot be the reason for an eviction. The City also says "A copy of every Eviction Notice must be filed with the Rent Adjustment Program within 10 days."

Help paying rent in Oakland: the doors that are left

None of these programs promises help. Apply to every one that fits you, and keep your lease, your notice, and proof of your income ready.

Keep People Housed (BACS, funded by the City of Oakland)

The City's Housing Resource Center page says "Bay Area Community Services (BACS) is providing emergency financial assistance and care coordination services" to Oakland residents, and warns: "Funding is extremely limited" (checked November 5, 2026).

  • What it can do, in the program's words: "KPH may be able to help eligible applicants who have past-due rent, or who need help paying the costs of moving into a new unit they have already identified and plan to lease. Applicants are now eligible for one-time assistance."
  • How to apply: go to keeppeoplehoused.org and choose "City Of Oakland." The Oakland application page was online when we checked. BACS's main line is (510) 613-0330.
  • City help with next steps: the City's Housing Resource Center, 250 Frank H. Ogawa Plaza, 6th Floor, takes calls at (510) 238-6182 and email at HousingAssistance@oaklandca.gov.

Alameda County Housing Secure and Season of Sharing

  • Alameda County Housing Secure is, in the City's words, "a collaborative of legal services providers partnering to prevent displacement," with "very limited rental assistance available for Oakland residents contingent upon Alameda County funding availability." Ask about it when you call Centro Legal de la Raza, one of its members.
  • Season of Sharing: the City's resource sheet describes this County program as "a private fund providing one-time crisis-based assistance for housing and critical family needs to Alameda County residents," and says "Assistance is not guaranteed." Call the pre-screening line at (510) 272-3700.
  • CalWORKs families: if you have children and get CalWORKs, or may qualify, ask the Alameda County Social Services Agency about CalWORKs Homeless Assistance. The state says it can cover "up to two months of rent arrearages." You apply with form CW-42.

211 Alameda County

Eden I&R runs 211 for Alameda County. Dial or text 2-1-1; calls are "always free, always confidential, and available 24/7." Ask about rent help, shelter, and legal help in Oakland.

Free and low-cost eviction lawyers in Oakland

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can check whether the landlord followed Oakland's rules, file your answer on time, and work out a payment plan or more time. These groups are on the City's own list of local resources (dated January 7, 2026).

  • Centro Legal de la Raza, 3400 E. 12th Street: "Tenants seeking to apply for free legal services should contact Centro Legal via phone at (510) 437-1554 or via email at tenantsrights@centrolegal.org." It offers bilingual legal help. The City adds: "There are very limited emergency financial assistance funds."
  • East Bay Community Law Center (EBCLC): its Housing Program handles "Eviction/unlawful detainer cases" for people who "Live in Berkeley, Oakland, Emeryville or the City of Alameda" and meet its income limits. Call (510) 548-4040 and leave a message for the Housing team; it says it will call back "within 48-72 hours."
  • Eviction Defense Center: 350 Frank Ogawa Plaza, Suite 703, (510) 452-4541, text and after-hours hotline (510) 693-2775. Its own site says it provides "free legal services to tenants facing eviction" in Alameda County and Richmond, while the City's sheet says its services are on "a sliding scale basis," so ask about cost when you call.
  • Bay Area Legal Aid: Alameda County residents with a housing problem can call 1-888-382-3405, or the Legal Advice Line at 1-800-551-5554 (Monday and Thursday 9:30 a.m. to 3 p.m., Tuesday and Wednesday 9:30 a.m. to 1 p.m.).

Oakland's Rent Adjustment Program: free counseling and your local rights

  • Talk to a housing counselor: call RAP at (510) 238-3721 or email RAP@oaklandca.gov. The City says housing counselors are available "Monday to Thursday, 9:30am to 4:30pm," and its flyer lists drop-in hours at the Housing Resource Center, 250 Frank H. Ogawa Plaza, 6th floor. "RAP also provides housing counseling and mediation."
  • Who is covered: the City's flyer says Oakland's eviction protections cover most apartments, most condos, single-family homes, and Section 8 or subsidized housing, but not new construction "Built within the last 10 years." Rent control covers most apartments but not units "Built on or after January 1, 1983," condos, or single-family homes.
  • Rent increases: "Effective August 1, 2026, the new annual CPI rent increase is 2.3%" for units covered by the Rent Adjustment Ordinance. That rate runs through July 31, 2027; RAP posts the next rate before August 1, 2027.
  • Notices go to RAP: "A copy of every Eviction Notice must be filed with the Rent Adjustment Program within 10 days," and "A property owner's failure to file this notice is a defense to any resulting unlawful detainer action."
  • School-year rule: the City says no-fault evictions are prohibited during the school year when the household includes school-age children or an Oakland Unified educator.

Help with utility bills in Oakland

  • LIHEAP through Spectrum: the City says "Eligible households may receive assistance once per program year with PG&E bills. To determine eligibility or apply, call 510-881-0300 x 245," or apply through Spectrum Community Services' HEAP page. Call (510) 881-0300, extension 245. The state says "Federal funding for LIHEAP is limited."
  • PG&E CARE and FERA: CARE "provides a monthly discount of 20% or more on gas and 35% or more on electricity." Apply online at PG&E; "If you do not qualify for CARE, we will check to see if you qualify for FERA."
  • EBMUD water discount: EBMUD's Customer Assistance Program "provides up to 50% reduction on the water bill for qualified low-income customers in single-family dwellings," and up to 35% off wastewater charges. It helps only if the water account is yours. Call EBMUD at 1-866-403-2683.

California eviction timeline in Oakland: what happens and when

  • 1. The 3-day notice. The notice must state the amount due and who to pay, and you get three days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or court holidays (Code of Civil Procedure 1161). In a covered Oakland unit, the rent owed must be at least one month of HUD's fair market rent for a unit your size, and the landlord must file the notice with RAP within 10 days. Call RAP and a lawyer now.
  • 2. The lawsuit. If you do not pay or move, the landlord can file an unlawful detainer case in Alameda County Superior Court. A notice alone does not end your tenancy.
  • 3. Answer within 10 court days. You have 10 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or court holidays, after you are served (Code of Civil Procedure 1167). If you miss it, the landlord can get a default judgment.
  • 4. Trial or settlement. If you answered, the court sets a trial. A settlement can include a payment plan or a move-out date. A landlord's failure to file the notice with RAP is a defense in Oakland.
  • 5. Just cause. Oakland's Just Cause ordinance covers most rentals. Where it does not apply, California's Tenant Protection Act protects a tenant who "has continuously and lawfully occupied" a home for 12 months (or 24 months in some cases when a new adult joins the lease), and the notice must state the just cause (Civil Code 1946.2). The state law says it will "remain in effect only until January 1, 2030" unless lawmakers extend it.
  • Lockouts are illegal. A landlord cannot change the locks, remove doors or windows, or shut off your utilities to force you out (Civil Code 789.3). Only the sheriff can remove you, after the court case.

The California Courts' eviction help for tenants explains each step and the forms. Our step-by-step guide to avoiding eviction covers the whole process, and understanding landlord notices explains the paper you got.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The Oakland Housing Authority (OHA) runs Section 8 and public housing, not an emergency rent fund. Its Waitlists page shows "Closed" for every list: Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8), OHA Affordable (Public) Housing, the OAHPI Project-Based Voucher list, the OHA Mainstream Voucher Program, and other agencies (checked November 5, 2026). OHA says "Status is not provided by telephone." See our OHA guide. If you already have OHA housing or a voucher and your income dropped, report it to OHA right away and ask about a rent change; your share of rent is generally based on 30% of your adjusted income.
  • The old Keep Oakland Housed rent relief. The pandemic-era rounds are over. Use the current Keep People Housed application instead.
  • RAP does not pay rent. It can stop an illegal rent increase and explain your rights, which can still save your home.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Oakland right now?

Only a little. The City funds Bay Area Community Services to run Keep People Housed, which may help eligible Oakland renters with past-due rent one time, but the City says funding is extremely limited. Apply at keeppeoplehoused.org and call the City's Housing Resource Center at (510) 238-6182. Also dial 211, ask Centro Legal de la Raza about Alameda County Housing Secure, and call Season of Sharing's pre-screening line at (510) 272-3700.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Oakland?

Call Centro Legal de la Raza at (510) 437-1554 or email tenantsrights@centrolegal.org, or call the East Bay Community Law Center at (510) 548-4040 if you meet its income limits. The Eviction Defense Center at (510) 452-4541 and Bay Area Legal Aid at 1-888-382-3405 also help tenants in Alameda County. We found no right-to-counsel program on the City's tenant pages, so call as soon as you get a notice.

Can I be evicted in Oakland for owing a small amount of rent?

Not in a unit covered by Oakland's Just Cause ordinance. The City says that for an eviction over unpaid rent, the rent owed must be no less than one month of fair market rent as determined by HUD for a unit your size. You still owe the rent. The landlord must also file every eviction notice with the Rent Adjustment Program within 10 days. Call RAP at (510) 238-3721 to check a notice.

How long do I have to respond to an eviction lawsuit in California?

10 court days. Since January 1, 2025, California law gives a tenant 10 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, and court holidays, to file a response after the summons and complaint are served. If you do not file on time, the landlord can ask for a default judgment and the judge can decide without you.

How much can my Oakland landlord raise the rent?

For units covered by Oakland's Rent Adjustment Ordinance, the annual increase from August 1, 2026 through July 31, 2027 is 2.3%. The City says rent control does not cover units built on or after January 1, 1983, most condos, or single-family homes. Call RAP at (510) 238-3721 to check your unit.

Is the Oakland Section 8 waiting list open?

No. On November 5, 2026, the Oakland Housing Authority's Waitlists page showed every list as closed, including Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) and public housing, with no reopening date. OHA says status is not provided by telephone; it emails applicants about their pre-application status.

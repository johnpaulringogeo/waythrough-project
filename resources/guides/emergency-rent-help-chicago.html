Emergency Rent Help in Chicago: the City's Rental Assistance Program, Court-Based Help, Free Eviction Lawyers, and the 5-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Chicago and you are behind, the main door that pays right now is the City's Rental Assistance Program (RAP), run by the Department of Family and Support Services (DFSS). Its page says: "The Rental Assistance Program (RAP) application portal is open and accepting new applications." RAP is for households at or below 30% of the area median income who have a 5-day notice or an eviction notice. If your landlord has already taken you to eviction court, the state's Court-Based Rental Assistance Program can pay up to $10,000 of back rent. For a free lawyer, call Cook County's Early Resolution Program at (855) 956-5763; it is open to every Cook County resident, whatever their income or immigration status. The City's older ERAP program is closed. Everything here was checked on November 3, 2026.

Here's the short version: Gather your 5-day notice, your lease, your ID, and proof of the income you lost, then apply for RAP on the City's application portal. If you need help, walk into any of the six DFSS Community Service Centers, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. (listed below). Tell your landlord, because the landlord has forms to fill out too. If you got court papers, call the Early Resolution Program at (855) 956-5763, ask about Court-Based Rental Assistance, and go to every court date.

A 5-day notice is not an eviction

Under Illinois law (735 ILCS 5/9-209), a landlord who wants to end your lease for unpaid rent must first give you a written demand that gives you "not less than 5 days after service" to pay. The notice must say: "Only FULL PAYMENT of the rent demanded in this notice will waive the landlord's right to terminate the lease under this notice, unless the landlord agrees in writing to continue the lease in exchange for receiving partial payment" (checked November 3, 2026). So a partial payment does not cancel the notice unless the landlord agrees in writing. The Cook County Sheriff's tenant guide adds that "Payment of back rent within the five day period could prevent an eviction case from being filed against you." The notice does not mean you have to leave. Only a court order can do that, and the Sheriff says: "The only entity that may evict you from your residence is the Cook County Sheriff."

Emergency rental assistance in Chicago: the doors that pay

RAP is the main one for tenants who are not in court yet. Court-Based Rental Assistance is for tenants who are. The others below are smaller. Funding and rules change, so call before you count on any of them.

Chicago Rental Assistance Program (RAP): the main door

In DFSS's words, RAP "provides funding to Chicagoans who are at risk of becoming homeless. RAP helps Chicagoans who have housing right now, but who may become homeless soon because they are experiencing a temporary loss or reduction in income that makes it difficult to pay rent." It can pay rent, and it can pay security deposits "in cases of fire, flood, foreclosure, housing relocation, domestic violence, or a court order to vacate" (checked November 3, 2026).

  • Who qualifies, in the City's words: you "live in the City of Chicago"; you "are at risk of becoming homeless due to eviction, loss of income, or another emergency"; your "household income is at or below 30% of Area Median Income (AMI)"; you "can demonstrate the ability to sustain housing after assistance is provided"; and your "landlord completes the RAP requirements." The City says "RAP is open to all Chicago residents regardless of legal immigration status."
  • What 30% of AMI means: the Chicago Housing Authority's 2026 table (effective May 1, 2026) lists the Chicago-area 30% limit as $25,550 for one person, $29,200 for two, $32,850 for three, and $36,450 for four.
  • Documents: "A 5-day notice of nonpayment or eviction notice"; proof of the income loss or other emergency; a pay stub, benefits statement, or a statement explaining that you have no income; a photo ID such as a driver's license, State ID, or CityKey; a current lease of at least 6 months; and the landlord agreement forms. You also need proof that you can pay rent going forward, such as pay stubs, Social Security or disability income, a job offer letter, a benefit award letter, or bank statements.
  • How to apply: online through the City's RAP portal, which you also use to check your status. The City has RAP information in Spanish, and in-person and phone translation for other languages at the Community Service Centers.
  • What happens next: "If your application is selected, a case manager will call or email you to make sure your application is complete." Then "An in-person, video, or photo inspection of your rental unit is then required." The word "selected" matters: applying does not guarantee you will be funded.
  • Your landlord's part: the landlord fills out a participation agreement and an IRS Form W-9. A property manager also needs an affidavit, and if more than 6 months of rent is owed, the City requires a payment agreement between you and the owner.

DFSS Community Service Centers help with new applications and status checks. All are open Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.:

Court-Based Rental Assistance Program (CBRAP): if you are already in eviction court

CBRAP is run by the Illinois Housing Development Authority (IHDA). Its page says "The Illinois Court-Based Rental Assistance Program is Open" (checked November 3, 2026). In IHDA's words, eligible applicants "may qualify for up to $10,000 that can be applied to past due rent, up to $700 for court costs, and up to two months' future rent to prevent housing displacement."

  • Who qualifies: you rent in Illinois, you are "in a court eviction proceeding due to nonpayment," and your household income is at or below 80% of the area median income. IHDA says "Proof of citizenship is not required."
  • Documents: a government-issued photo ID; proof of address dated within 60 days; proof of household income; evidence of past-due rent; the eviction complaint, summons, and court case number, with your name on them; your signed lease if you have one; and email addresses for you and your landlord.
  • How it works: "A tenant should apply first. If eligible, their landlord will be sent an email inviting them to complete their section of the application." Apply through IHDA's CBRAP page. IHDA says its "goal is to notify applicants of funding eligibility within 45 days from the start of your application review."
  • Help by phone: IHDA's line is (866) 454-3571. It says the CBRAP call center "has English, Spanish, and Polish speaking staff available."
  • Check that it is still open. CBRAP's status can change. On the same day IHDA's page said it was open, a Cook County partner site, chicookilrenthelp.org, said "The Court-Based Rental Assistance program is not currently open." Ask the Early Resolution Program at (855) 956-5763 which is right for your case.

All Chicago Emergency Financial Assistance

All Chicago, a nonprofit that works to prevent and end homelessness in Chicago, "provides critical, flexible financial support to individuals and families who are unhoused or at risk of losing their housing." It can help with "Rent or mortgage payments," "Security deposits," "Utility bills," and "Other emergency housing costs." Since 2026 it focuses its money "in 15 focus neighborhoods where eviction happens at the highest rates in Chicago" (checked November 3, 2026). To ask for help, All Chicago says: "Call 311 and ask for short-term assistance," or contact a participating agency. Its list includes the Center for Changing Lives ((773) 342-6210), Franciscan Outreach at 10 S. Kedzie ((312) 746-5642), the Law Center for Better Housing ((312) 347-7600), Neighborhood Housing Services ((773) 329-4111), and the Primo Center ((773) 722-0544).

Illinois Homeless Prevention Program

The Illinois Department of Human Services (IDHS) funds local agencies to pay rent, back rent, utilities, and security deposits for households "at immediate risk of eviction, foreclosure, or homelessness." IDHS says the total help "may not exceed the dollar value of six months' worth of the client's rent," and that "The household must document a temporary economic crisis beyond its control and must be able to demonstrate an ability to meet the prospective rental/utility obligations after the assistance has been granted" (checked November 3, 2026). You don't apply to IDHS directly. You go through a provider agency; All Chicago says it blends these state funds into its own program, and 2-1-1 can point you to others.

2-1-1 Metro Chicago: the switchboard

2-1-1 Metro Chicago calls itself "a free, local, 24/7/365 service that connects all Cook County residents to essential health and social service support." Dial 2-1-1 from any phone, or text your ZIP code to 898-211.

The City's ERAP is closed

The Department of Housing's page says: "The City's ERAP program is now closed." That was the pandemic-era Emergency Rental Assistance Program. The same page sends renters to RAP, which it says "is currently accepting applications" (checked November 3, 2026). Old flyers and posts about ERAP are out of date.

Utility help in Chicago: CEDA's LIHEAP

  • LIHEAP through CEDA: CEDA runs the state's Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program in Cook County. CEDA says households with an older adult, a person with a disability, a child age 5 or under, or a shutoff could apply starting October 1, and "Starting November 1st – All income eligible households can apply for LIHEAP." Applications are taken "first come first serve basis through August 13, 2027, or until funds are exhausted," so after August 13, 2027 that season is over, and money can run out sooner (checked November 3, 2026).
  • Income limits: for ComEd, Nicor, Peoples Gas, and North Shore Gas customers, CEDA lists 30-day gross income limits of $3,990 for one person, $5,410 for two, $6,830 for three, and $8,250 for four. Renters whose heat is included in the rent can get cash help.
  • Shutoffs: CEDA's Reconnection Assistance "provides a benefit of up to $2000 to prevent imminent disconnection or to re-connect" heat or electric service. If you are shut off or about to be, CEDA says to apply through a Partner Intake Site so your application is expedited.
  • How to apply: online through CEDA, at a Partner Intake Site, or by calling the CEDA call center at (800) 571-2332. Have Social Security numbers or ITINs if you have them (CEDA says people without them can still apply), 30 days of income proof for everyone in the home, and your latest gas or electric bill, or your lease if heat is included.
  • Peoples Gas Share the Warmth: CEDA says it "provides up to $200 towards the past due balance of your Peoples Gas bill."

Paying the utility bill can free up cash for rent. Our utility assistance guide covers other programs.

Eviction help in Chicago: free lawyers and the Early Resolution Program

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can check the landlord's notice and case, ask for more time, and work out an agreement.

  • Early Resolution Program (ERP), Cook County Legal Aid for Housing and Debt: Cook County's program "provides free legal help for renters facing eviction," and "connects participants with legal aid, mediation services, and referrals to additional programs, such as rental assistance." The County says "All services are available to Cook County residents regardless of income, language, or immigration status." Call (855) 956-5763, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Its partners include Legal Aid Chicago, the Law Center for Better Housing, CARPLS Legal Aid, Chicago Volunteer Legal Services, and the Center for Conflict Resolution.
  • Legal Aid Chicago: free civil legal help for people in Chicago and suburban Cook County. Call 312-341-1070. Its intake line "accepts calls weekdays beginning at 8 a.m. We take a limited number of calls each day so it's best to call early." It offers a callback so you don't have to wait on hold, and "The phone menu is in English and Spanish." You can also apply through its online application, available 24/7.
  • Law Center for Better Housing (LCBH) and Rentervention: LCBH says it "provides free, comprehensive legal representation so that renters have a trusted advocate in court." Its free online tool, Rentervention, helps Chicago renters with common problems.
  • At the courthouse: the Cook County Sheriff says "Eviction legal resources can be found in the Daley Center on Wednesday mornings between 9:00 a.m. to 10:00 a.m. on the 14th floor," and that there is a court advice desk on the Daley Center's Concourse Level.
  • Sheriff's social services: if you are facing eviction, the Sheriff's Social Service department is at 312-603-3337.

Illinois eviction timeline in Chicago: what happens and when

These steps come from Illinois law, Cook County's Early Resolution Program, and the Cook County Sheriff's tenant guide:

  • 1. The notice. For unpaid rent, a written demand that gives you at least 5 days to pay (735 ILCS 5/9-209). Pay the full amount in that time if you can, and keep proof.
  • 2. The court case. After the notice period ends, the landlord can file a complaint in the Circuit Court of Cook County. You will be served a Summons and Complaint. The Sheriff says the Summons gives "the date, time and location that you are required to appear in Court."
  • 3. Go to court and ask for the Early Resolution Program. ERP offers free legal help, mediation, and referrals to rental assistance. This is also when CBRAP can help, because it needs your court case number.
  • 4. The Order for Possession. If the landlord wins, the court can enter an Order for Possession. Once any stay period ends, the landlord files it with the Sheriff. The Sheriff warns: "ENFORCEMENT MAY OCCUR AS SOON AS 24 HOURS AFTER AN ORDER HAS BEEN PLACED WITH THE SHERIFF'S EVICTIONS OFFICE." You get one letter from the Sheriff, and the Sheriff does not give the date or time of the eviction.
  • 5. Asking for more time. You or your lawyer can file a motion with the court after the order. The Sheriff says copies of any motion, and any order that stays the eviction, "MUST be provided to the Sheriff's Office located in Room 701 of the Daley Center."
  • Rental assistance is checked. The Sheriff requires the landlord to submit a "Verification of Non-Rental Assistance form" for every order, and says "If the form is not completed and submitted to our office the eviction will not be scheduled."
  • Your belongings. The Sheriff says it "DOES NOT REMOVE OR SECURE A TENANT'S/DEFENDANT'S PERSONAL PROPERTY." Make a plan for your things early.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The Chicago Housing Authority (CHA) runs vouchers and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. CHA says its "Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) waitlist is closed at this time. When the list is opened, CHA will make public announcements and all households will have the opportunity to participate in a waitlist lottery." It also says "Public Housing, Project-Based Voucher (PBV), and PBRA (Project-Based Rental Assistance) waitlists are open" (checked November 3, 2026). Most waitlists are long; CHA says they are "often estimated at 10+" years. The CHA Client Center is at 60 E. Van Buren Street, 312-742-8500. Our CHA page has the details.
  • The City's ERAP is closed. Use RAP instead.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who still has a home. If you could lose your home tonight, CHA says to call 3-1-1 for emergency shelter. See also I need housing tonight.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Chicago right now?

Yes. The City's Rental Assistance Program (RAP) says its application portal is open and accepting new applications. It is for Chicago residents at or below 30% of the area median income who have a 5-day notice or an eviction notice. You can apply online or get help at any of six DFSS Community Service Centers, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. The City's older ERAP program is closed.

Who qualifies for Chicago's Rental Assistance Program?

You must live in Chicago, be at risk of homelessness because of eviction, lost income, or another emergency, and have household income at or below 30% of the area median income. For 2026, that is about $25,550 for one person or $36,450 for four. You must show you can pay rent after the help ends, and your landlord must complete the RAP forms. Immigration status does not matter.

What if I already have an eviction court case in Chicago?

Call Cook County's Early Resolution Program at (855) 956-5763 for free legal help, mediation, and referrals to rental assistance. The state's Court-Based Rental Assistance Program can pay up to $10,000 of back rent, up to $700 in court costs, and up to two months of future rent for tenants in an eviction case for nonpayment with income at or below 80% of the area median income. IHDA's page says it is open; call to confirm before you count on it.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Chicago?

Call the Early Resolution Program at (855) 956-5763, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. It serves all Cook County residents regardless of income, language, or immigration status. You can also call Legal Aid Chicago at 312-341-1070 on weekdays starting at 8 a.m.; it takes a limited number of calls each day, so call early.

How much notice does an Illinois landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

At least 5 days, in writing, under 735 ILCS 5/9-209. Only full payment of the rent demanded cancels the notice, unless the landlord agrees in writing to take less. The notice does not mean you have to leave. The landlord must win a court case first, and only the Cook County Sheriff can carry out an eviction order.

Does the Chicago Housing Authority give emergency rent help?

No. CHA runs vouchers and public housing, not an emergency rent fund. Its Housing Choice Voucher waitlist is closed, but CHA says its Public Housing, Project-Based Voucher, and PBRA waitlists are open. If you already have CHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call CHA right away at 312-742-8500, or the voucher call center at 312-935-2600.

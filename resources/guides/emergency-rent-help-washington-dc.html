Emergency Rent Help in Washington, DC: ERAP Appointments, the 48-Hour Rule, Free Eviction Lawyers, and the $600 Notice Rule

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Washington, DC and you are behind, the door that actually pays is the District's Emergency Rental Assistance Program (ERAP), run by the Department of Human Services (DHS). It is not taking new appointments today. DHS says: "Scheduling for ERAP appointments will open December 3, 2026 at 12:00 p.m.," and it will stay open only until the appointments that FY27 money can support are filled, so be ready at noon that day. Families who could lose their home now can go to the Virginia Williams Family Resource Center at 202-526-0017. For eviction help, call the Landlord Tenant Legal Assistance Network at 202-780-2575. DC law also has a rule that matters a lot: if your ERAP application is approved for the full amount, your landlord must cancel a scheduled eviction when you tell them at least 48 hours ahead. Everything here was checked on November 3, 2026.

Here's the short version: Gather your lease, photo IDs for every adult, 30 days of income proof, 30 days of bank statements, and proof of what caused your emergency now. On December 3, 2026 at noon, go to the DC ERAP portal to book an appointment, before the slots run out. If you are a family at immediate risk of losing your home, go to Virginia Williams at 64 New York Avenue NE. If you got court papers, call the Landlord Tenant Legal Assistance Network at 202-780-2575 and go to court.

An approved ERAP application can cancel a scheduled eviction

D.C. Code § 42-3505.01(r)(3) says that when an eviction "based solely on a claim for nonpayment of rent has been authorized by the court and the tenant notifies the housing provider, no later than 48 hours prior to the scheduled date and time of the eviction, that the tenant has an approved ERAP application that would pay the full amount necessary to redeem the tenancy, the housing provider shall cancel the eviction." If the landlord reschedules, the new date must be "no sooner than 3 weeks from the earlier-scheduled eviction date" (checked November 3, 2026). An application that is only pending also helps: under § 42-3505.01(r)(1), the court "may stay" a nonpayment case when you show it a pending ERAP application, but only once per case. Tell the landlord in writing, keep a copy, and bring your ERAP approval to court.

Emergency rental assistance in DC: the doors that pay

ERAP is the big one. The others below are for families at risk of homelessness or for people already in crisis. Call before you count on any of them.

ERAP (Emergency Rental Assistance Program): the main door

In DHS's words, ERAP "helps District residents earning less than 40% of the Area Median Income (AMI) who are facing housing emergencies, by providing funding for overdue rent including late fees and court costs if qualified household is facing eviction." It also pays security deposits and first month's rent for people moving into a new apartment (checked November 3, 2026).

  • When you can apply: ERAP now works by appointment. DHS says "Scheduling for ERAP appointments will open December 3, 2026 at 12:00 p.m." Scheduling "will remain open only until we receive the number of appointments that can be supported with available FY27 program funds. Once that point is reached, DHS will stop making appointments." DHS also says it "will pause ERAP appointment scheduling periodically to process applications." Before December 3, you cannot book; after that, slots can be gone fast, so check the ERAP FAQ page for the current status.
  • Who qualifies, in DHS's words: "you need to be a DC resident, you need to be at least 30 days behind in rent for back rental assistance applicants, or have a need for security deposit and first month's rent assistance and your household needs to be under the income limits." For back rent, you must be the leaseholder of the DC unit. You can get ERAP "once within a 12-month period."
  • Income limits: the chart on the ERAP site (labeled FY26) lists 40% of AMI as $45,892 a year for 1 person, $52,448 for 2, $59,004 for 3, $65,560 for 4, $70,804.80 for 5, $76,049.60 for 6, $81,294.40 for 7, and $86,539.20 for 8.
  • How much it pays: for back rent, DHS says ERAP "may provide up to five times the rental amount based on the area zip code and bedroom size." For a move, the first month's rent and the deposit can each be up to one month's unsubsidized rent. Payments always go to your landlord.
  • What to have ready: proof of residency (a lease or a letter from your landlord); photo ID for all adults; proof of income for the last 30 days; bank statements from the last 30 days for all accounts, plus SNAP if you get it; and proof of the emergency, such as a termination letter, a change in pay, medical bills, or a death or change in the household. Your landlord must send an updated rent ledger and a signed W-9; you may send these yourself too.
  • What happens next: a case manager calls you at your appointment time and does the assessment, which "starts the 45-day clock in processing the application." Then you and your landlord are told if it is approved or denied. Submit only one application. Check your status by logging in at erap.dhs.dc.gov, and check your spam folder.
  • If you are denied: you can ask for a Fair Hearing within 90 days by calling the Office of Administrative Hearings at (202) 442-9094 or the DHS Family Services Administration at (202) 698-4170.
  • Who runs ERAP besides DHS: DHS lists two partner agencies. Housing Counseling Services, 2410 17th St NW, 202-667-7006, says it "helps tenants prevent eviction." The Community Partnership for the Prevention of Homelessness lists an ERAP line at 202-543-5298, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. For written questions, DHS's ERAP office is at erap.program@dc.gov.

Virginia Williams Family Resource Center: families at risk tonight

DHS says: "If you are a family experiencing homelessness or at immediate risk of homelessness, please contact the Virginia Williams Family Resource Center (VWFRC), the central intake office for all families in the District seeking assistance with their housing needs." It is at 64 New York Avenue NE and is open Monday to Thursday, 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m., and Friday, 8:30 a.m. to noon. Call 202-526-0017. After hours, families can call the Homeless Services Hotline at 202-399-7093 or 311 (checked November 3, 2026).

Single adults: the Homeless Services Hotline

For an individual "experiencing or at risk of homelessness," DHS points to the Homeless Services Hotline at 202-399-7093 or 311, for "services, resources, shelter availability, and shelter transport."

Office of the Tenant Advocate (OTA)

OTA is a DC agency for tenants. It does not run a rent fund, but it gives advice on landlord disputes and helps you find out "what types of legal representation is available to you." It also "provides emergency housing to tenants displaced by fires, floods, or government closures," with hotel stays, help with packing and moving, and coordination with other agencies. Call (202) 719-6560; phone intake is Monday to Friday, 8:45 a.m. to 4:45 p.m. Walk-ins are Monday to Thursday, 9 a.m. to 3 p.m., at 899 North Capitol Street NE, Suite 6200 (checked November 3, 2026).

Utility help in DC: LIHEAP and DOEE

  • LIHEAP: the Department of Energy and Environment (DOEE) says LIHEAP "is now accepting applications for the 2027 fiscal year." It offers "energy bill assistance between $250 and $1,800 as a one-time regular energy assistance benefit." Apply online through DOEE's Utility Affordability Programs page, or call 311 or (202) 737-4404. In-person help is by appointment only: DOEE says scheduling through 311 begins November 10, 2026, and appointments start November 17, 2026, at 1207 Taylor Street NW and 2100 Martin Luther King Jr. Avenue SE.
  • If your power or gas is already off: DOEE treats it as a crisis if you owe $250 or more after the regular benefit and your service has been disconnected. Bring the Pepco or Washington Gas paper that shows the disconnection. There are no emergency walk-ins; schedule through 311.
  • Other DOEE programs: the Utility Discount Program (UDP) and CRIAC relief on DC Water bills are also taking FY2027 applications. DOEE's main line is (202) 535-2600. DOEE warns that it is the only one allowed to take LIHEAP applications, so don't give yours to a utility or anyone else.

Eviction help in DC: free lawyers

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can check the landlord's notice, ask the court to pause the case while ERAP decides, and work out more time.

  • Landlord Tenant Legal Assistance Network (LTLAN): the main phone door for tenants without a lawyer. The DC Bar Pro Bono Center says LTLAN connects you with an attorney who can "provide free legal information, advice, or legal representation." Call 202-780-2575, Monday to Thursday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., or fill out the online intake; an intake specialist calls back within 48 hours. Its services are for households "with income below 400% of the Federal Poverty Line."
  • Landlord Tenant Resource Center (LTRC): free legal information in person at the courthouse, in "Room 223 on the second floor of the D.C. Superior Courthouse, Building B," 510 4th St NW. Hours: "Tuesdays: 10 a.m. – 1 p.m." It may stop taking people before 1 p.m. on busy days, so come early.
  • Legal Aid DC: call 202-628-1161 or apply online at any time. Its staff are at the Landlord and Tenant Court, 510 4th Street NW, Room 221, on Mondays and Wednesdays, 9:30 a.m. to 3 p.m. Legal Aid says it generally helps people "with income below 200% of the Federal Poverty Guidelines," and sometimes up to 300%.
  • Bread for the City: its Legal On-Call line is (202) 386-7616; leave a message and it calls back within 48 hours. For landlord-tenant cases, it sends people to LTLAN at (202) 780-2575. It serves DC residents with low incomes at 1525 7th Street NW and 1700 Marion Barry Ave SE.
  • Think you may be evicted very soon? The DC Bar says you can check if you are on today's eviction schedule by calling the Clerk's Office at 202-879-4879, then call LTLAN right away.

DC eviction rules for unpaid rent: what happens and when

These steps come from D.C. Code §§ 42-3505.01 and 42-3505.01a, on the DC Council's official code site:

  • 1. No case for less than $600. A landlord must give you written notice of plans to file "at least 10 days before filing the claim," and "shall not issue such notice if the amount of rent that the tenant has failed to pay is less than $600." Late fees alone can never be the reason for an eviction.
  • 2. What the notice must say. It must list the total rent owed, include a ledger of charges and payments, and say "You have the right to remain in the rental unit if the total balance of unpaid rent is paid in full." The wording the law requires also tells the tenant the landlord can file if the rent is not paid "within 30 days of this notice," and it must list the Office of the Tenant Advocate (202-719-6560) and LTLAN (202-780-2575). It must come by certified mail or tracked delivery, and also by hand delivery or posting on your door. If you get one, call LTLAN the same week.
  • 3. The court case. The court can dismiss a case if the landlord skipped the notice or filed too early. The court "may stay" a nonpayment case once if you show a pending ERAP application.
  • 4. Notice of the eviction date. If the landlord wins, it must tell you the eviction date "not fewer than 21 days before the date of eviction," by phone or electronic message, by first-class mail, and by posting at your door. The notice must list phone numbers for the U.S. Marshals, the Office of the Tenant Advocate, and the court's Landlord and Tenant Branch.
  • 5. The 48-hour ERAP rule. If ERAP approves the full amount, tell the landlord at least 48 hours before the eviction; the landlord "shall cancel the eviction" (see the box above).
  • 6. No evictions in bad weather. A landlord cannot evict you when the National Weather Service predicts at 8 a.m. that the temperature at National Airport will fall below 32 degrees or rise above 95 degrees, or "When precipitation is falling at the location of the rental unit."
  • 7. A license is required. The landlord has to show the court a current rental housing business license when it asks for the eviction order, unless the court waived it.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The DC Housing Authority (DCHA) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its site says "The waitlists for Public Housing and the Housing Choice Voucher Program (HCVP) are currently closed to new applicants" (checked November 3, 2026). If you already applied, you can check your status online. If you already have DCHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call the DCHA Call Center at (202) 535-1000. Our DCHA guide has both lists' status, how to check an old application and how to apply.
  • Old ERAP flyers and phone numbers. ERAP changed to an appointment system, and the old rules about applying any time no longer hold. Use the ERAP FAQ page for the current status.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and call the Homeless Services Hotline at 202-399-7093.

Frequently asked questions

Is DC ERAP open right now?

Not for new appointments as of November 3, 2026. DHS says scheduling for ERAP appointments will open December 3, 2026 at 12:00 p.m., and will stay open only until the appointments that FY27 funds can support are filled. After that, DHS stops making appointments. Have your documents ready before December 3 and book at erap.dhs.dc.gov.

Who qualifies for ERAP in Washington, DC?

You must be a DC resident, be at least 30 days behind on rent (for back rent), and have household income under 40% of the Area Median Income. The ERAP site lists that limit as $45,892 a year for one person, $59,004 for three, and $65,560 for four. You can get ERAP once in a 12-month period, and the money goes straight to your landlord.

Can ERAP stop my eviction in DC?

It can. If the court has authorized an eviction only for unpaid rent and you have an approved ERAP application that would pay the full amount needed, tell your landlord at least 48 hours before the scheduled eviction. Under D.C. Code section 42-3505.01(r)(3), the landlord must cancel it, and any new date must be at least 3 weeks later. A pending application can also lead the court to pause your case once.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in DC?

Call the Landlord Tenant Legal Assistance Network at 202-780-2575, Monday to Thursday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., for households under 400% of the federal poverty line. You can also call Legal Aid DC at 202-628-1161, or visit the Landlord Tenant Resource Center at the courthouse, 510 4th St NW, on Tuesdays from 10 a.m. to 1 p.m.

How much notice does a DC landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

The landlord must give written notice at least 10 days before filing, and cannot file at all if you owe less than $600. The notice the law requires tells you the landlord can file if the rent is not paid in full within 30 days of the notice. If the landlord wins, it must tell you the eviction date at least 21 days ahead. Only a court can order an eviction.

Does the DC Housing Authority give emergency rent help?

No. DCHA runs Section 8 and public housing, and it says both waitlists are closed to new applicants. If you already have DCHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call the DCHA Call Center at (202) 535-1000 right away.

Emergency Rent Help in Seattle: Keep King County Housed, 211, Free Eviction Lawyers, and the 14-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Seattle and you are behind, the door that actually pays is Keep King County Housed, the county's rent help program run by United Way of King County. It pays up to $10,000 per household for back rent, but it picks households at random from a waitlist, and United Way says "not everyone who applies will be selected." The City of Seattle does not take rent help applications itself right now: its renter help page says to call 2-1-1 for rent assistance referrals. If your landlord has started an eviction, Washington law gives many low-income tenants the right to a free, court-appointed lawyer; call the Eviction Defense Screening Line at 1-855-657-8387. Everything here was checked on November 3, 2026.

Here's the short version: Fill out United Way's Client Intake Form today and save your Record ID. Then call 2-1-1 (or 877-211-9274), Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., for other rent help. If you got a 14-day notice or court papers, call the Eviction Defense Screening Line at 1-855-657-8387, respond to the summons by its deadline, and go to every hearing.

Washington gives many tenants a free eviction lawyer

RCW 59.18.640 says that, "Subject to the availability of amounts appropriated for this specific purpose, the court must appoint an attorney for an indigent tenant in an unlawful detainer proceeding" (an eviction case), and the state pays for that lawyer (checked November 3, 2026). You count as indigent if you receive public assistance such as TANF, SSI, Medicaid, or food stamps, or if your income after taxes is 200% of the federal poverty level or less. The state's Office of Civil Legal Aid runs the program through contracted lawyers. The way in is the Eviction Defense Screening Line, 1-855-657-8387, run by the Northwest Justice Project: weekdays 8:30 a.m. to 7 p.m., with voicemail only from 8:30 to 9:15 a.m. and after 4 p.m. You can also apply online. NJP warns: "Contacting us does not pause a court case or extend a deadline."

Emergency rental assistance in Seattle: the doors that pay

Keep King County Housed is the big one. The others below are narrower. Funding changes, so call before you count on any of them.

Keep King County Housed (United Way of King County): the main door

King County calls Keep King County Housed "a rent assistance and eviction prevention program" for "renters who need help covering past-due rent and/or have an active eviction notice." It is "an ongoing program" funded by the Washington State Department of Commerce, and United Way of King County runs it with community partners (checked November 3, 2026).

  • Who qualifies, in United Way's words: "You live in King County, WA," "You owe back rent at your current address," "Your household income is at or below 80% of Area Median Income (AMI)," and "Your landlord agrees to participate." You must also be at risk of homelessness: you have been told your right to stay will end within 21 days, or you have missed a rent payment and still owe at least part of it.
  • How much: United Way says the program "is never able to provide more than $10,000 of rental assistance per household," that it can include "up to 2 months of future rent, if the renter is eligible," and that you can only be selected "every two years, even if you move." It does not pay move-in costs or rent owed at a previous address.
  • How you are chosen: King County says applicants "are placed on a waitlist" and "randomly selected from the highest tier available on the waitlist." United Way says, "We select households completely at random," and "Because our selection process is random, we are unable to prioritize case." If you are not picked, "No need to reapply; you will remain on the waitlist."
  • How to apply: fill out the Client Intake Form on United Way's rent help page (there is a Spanish version, the "Formulario de Admisión de Clientes"), and save your Record ID. You will need your landlord's name, phone number, and email, plus your address, monthly rent, and household income. You can check your status on the same page.
  • If you are selected: you get emails titled "Selected to Apply: Rental Assistance Program" and "Online Connect Access," and a case manager from a partner agency contacts you by phone, text, or email. Then you upload income documents for every adult, proof of housing instability (such as a rent ledger or a 14-day notice), and proof of residency. United Way says "There are no citizenship requirements to receive assistance," and you do not need a Social Security number or ITIN.
  • Timing and payment: United Way says payments "are typically issued within 2-3 weeks after approval" and go to your landlord. The money is a grant: "They are NOT a loan and do not have to be paid back." Your landlord must agree to the program's terms; if the landlord refuses, United Way cannot help.

The City of Seattle: no application of its own right now

The City's Renting in Seattle "I Need Help" page says: "Trouble Paying Rent - call 2-1-1 for referrals for rent assistance" (checked November 3, 2026). The City is building something new. In June 2026, Seattle's Human Services Department announced about $8 million "in 2027 funding to help residents stay housed," including rental assistance, legal services, and "a new centralized application process," and said the City "prepares to launch an online portal next year." Until that portal opens, the City does not take applications from renters. For questions about your rights, call the Renting in Seattle Helpline at 206-684-5700; the City says to call it for eviction referrals.

211: the switchboard

In Washington, 211 is run by Crisis Connections, which says 211 "connects you to help with food, housing, bills, and many other basic needs emergencies." Live hours are Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., and 211 is closed on holidays. Rent help is phone only: "we are not able to offer Rent (Eviction Prevention), Utilities (Disconnection Protections), or Legal service referrals via chat. For these needs, please call us at 211 or 877-211-9274." Dial 2-1-1 or 877-211-9274. Interpreters are available in over 155 languages.

Utility help in Seattle: discounts and past-due bills

  • Utility Discount Program (City Light and Seattle Public Utilities): the City says the program "provides 60% off electricity bills and 50% off water, sewer, and garbage bills" every month. One application also covers emergency bill help: up to $710 a year toward a past-due City Light bill and up to $537 a year toward a past-due Seattle Public Utilities bill. There is no deadline. Apply through the Utility Discount Program page or call 206-684-0268, Monday to Thursday, 7:30 a.m. to 3 p.m. Interpretation is free.
  • LIHEAP and PSE HELP through Byrd Barr Place: Byrd Barr Place helps Seattle residents pay for heat and fix furnaces. It says its Energy Assistance Program "is officially now open and will remain open until August 2027 or until funding is exhausted," so after August 2027, or sooner if the money runs out, check its page for the next round. It also runs PSE HELP for Puget Sound Energy customers. Apply online, by email or mail, or in person at 722 18th Avenue. Call 206-812-4940.

Paying a utility bill can free up cash for rent. Our utility assistance guide covers other programs.

Eviction help in Seattle: free lawyers and clinics

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can check the landlord's notice, raise defenses, and work out more time.

  • Eviction Defense Screening Line (statewide): 1-855-657-8387, weekdays 8:30 a.m. to 7 p.m. NJP calls it "the statewide entry point for renters seeking eviction-related legal help." Have your notices or court papers and your household income ready, and expect to wait on hold "for up to 30 minutes." This is how you ask for a court-appointed lawyer under RCW 59.18.640.
  • Housing Justice Project (King County Bar Association): HJP "provides free legal assistance to low-income tenants who have received an eviction summons and/or who have an active filed eviction case in King County Superior Court." It helps tenants respond to a summons with a Notice of Appearance, offers attorney consultations, and "may be able to provide attorneys for qualified tenants." If you have a hearing, go to a clinic: the King County Courthouse, 516 Third Avenue, Room W314, Seattle, Monday to Friday, 10 a.m. to noon; or the Regional Justice Center, 401 Fourth Avenue North, Room 1281, Kent, Monday to Thursday, 10 a.m. to noon. You can also request help through its Get Help page. HJP says "we are unable to guarantee assignment of an attorney to every tenant with an eviction case."
  • Renting in Seattle Helpline: 206-684-5700. The City handles maintenance problems and landlord problems, and gives referrals if you face eviction.
  • Solid Ground Tenant Services: tenant counselors explain Washington landlord-tenant law at 206-694-6767, a voice message line. Solid Ground has no attorneys on staff, and it says its Tenant Services "does NOT provide rental assistance."
  • Other legal problems: the state's Office of Civil Legal Aid says King County residents should call 211 to reach the CLEAR legal aid intake system. Outside King County, CLEAR is 1-888-201-1014; seniors 60 and over can call 1-888-387-7111.

Washington eviction rules: what happens and when

These steps come from the Revised Code of Washington, the City of Seattle, and the Housing Justice Project:

  • 1. The 14-day notice. For unpaid rent, the landlord must serve a "FOURTEEN-DAY NOTICE TO PAY RENT OR VACATE THE PREMISES" in the form set by RCW 59.18.057. It must list the rent, utilities, and other recurring charges due, and say: "You must pay the total amount due to your landlord within fourteen (14) days after service of this notice or you must vacate the premises." The form also tells you about your right to a lawyer and the screening line. The notice is not an eviction.
  • 2. Rent help counts. Under RCW 59.18.410, the landlord "shall accept any pledge of emergency rental assistance funds" from a government or nonprofit agency made before the notice runs out, if it covers the full rent owed, and must pause any court case for 14 court days after giving the agency the payment information it needs.
  • 3. The court case. If the notice runs out, the landlord can serve a summons and complaint and file in King County Superior Court. Respond by the deadline on the summons. HJP can help you file a Notice of Appearance.
  • 4. Paying can still stop it. RCW 59.18.410 says that before a judgment, or until five court days after it, you can pay the court or the landlord "the amount of the rent due, any court costs incurred at the time of payment, late fees if such fees are due under the lease and do not exceed $75 in total, and attorneys' fees if awarded," and then "any judgment entered shall be satisfied and the tenant restored to his or her tenancy." With a pledge letter of financial help from a government or nonprofit agency, you have "until the date of eviction."
  • 5. Seattle's extra defenses. Seattle's Just Cause Eviction Ordinance requires a just cause to end your tenancy. The City also lists a winter eviction defense, "December 1 - March 1," for households "at or below 80% of AMI," and a school-year defense, September to June, for households with children in school (daycare through high school) and for school employees. Both have exceptions; for example, the winter defense does not apply to landlords "with ownership interest in less than 4 properties." These are defenses you raise in court, so talk to a lawyer.
  • Lockouts are illegal. United Way puts it plainly: your landlord "CANNOT evict you from your unit without going through a court process (RCW 59.18.290, RCW 59.18.300)."

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The Seattle Housing Authority (SHA) runs Housing Choice Vouchers and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its voucher list is open all the time: SHA says "The opportunity to apply for a chance to receive a voucher is ongoing. Families and individuals may apply at any time," and people are "randomly selected from the voucher list whenever vouchers are available" (checked November 3, 2026). To stay on the list, "you must also check in once every month using Save My Spot," online or by phone at 206-256-7000 (toll-free 833-366-5925). Questions about applying: 206-239-1674. Our Seattle Housing Authority guide explains how the voucher list, Save My Spot and SHA's own apartments work.
  • Old pandemic programs and city funds. The federal pandemic rent money has ended, and the City's new 2027 program has no public application yet. Old flyers and posts still mention past programs.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Seattle right now?

Yes, mainly through Keep King County Housed, which United Way of King County runs for the county. You fill out United Way's Client Intake Form, and households are picked at random from a waitlist; United Way warns that "not everyone who applies will be selected." It pays up to $10,000 per household for back rent at your current address, and your household income must be at or below 80% of the area median income. The City of Seattle does not take rent help applications itself right now. Its Renting in Seattle page says to call 2-1-1 for rent assistance referrals.

How does Keep King County Housed choose who gets help?

King County says households who apply "are placed on a waitlist" and are "randomly selected from the highest tier available on the waitlist." United Way says it cannot prioritize anyone's case, and it will not answer requests to be picked sooner. If you are not selected, you stay on the waitlist and do not need to reapply. If you are selected, you get emails titled "Selected to Apply: Rental Assistance Program" and "Online Connect Access," and a case manager from a partner agency contacts you. Your landlord must agree to take part.

Can I get a free eviction lawyer in Seattle?

Often, yes. Washington law (RCW 59.18.640) says that, subject to the money set aside for it, the court must appoint a lawyer for an indigent tenant in an eviction case. Indigent means you receive benefits such as TANF, SSI, Medicaid, or food stamps, or your income after taxes is 200% of the federal poverty level or less. Call the Eviction Defense Screening Line at 1-855-657-8387, weekdays 8:30 a.m. to 7 p.m. If you have a court date, the Housing Justice Project runs clinics at the King County Courthouse in Seattle and the Regional Justice Center in Kent.

How much notice does a Washington landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

14 days. Under RCW 59.18.057, the landlord must serve a "Fourteen-Day Notice to Pay Rent or Vacate the Premises" in a form set by law. It must list what you owe and tell you about your right to a lawyer and the Eviction Defense Screening Line. The notice is not an eviction. You can pay the total amount due within the 14 days, and only a court order can make you leave.

Can I still stop an eviction after my landlord files in court?

Often, yes. Under RCW 59.18.410, you can pay the landlord or the court the rent due, court costs, late fees of up to $75 in total if your lease allows them, and attorney's fees if the court awarded them, before a judgment or within five court days after it, and your tenancy is restored. If you have a written pledge of emergency rental assistance from a government or nonprofit agency, you have until the date of eviction. In Seattle, eligible tenants also have a winter defense from December 1 to March 1 and a school-year defense for families with children.

Does the Seattle Housing Authority give emergency rent help?

No. SHA runs Housing Choice Vouchers (Section 8) and public housing, not an emergency rent fund. Its voucher list is open all the time: SHA says "Families and individuals may apply at any time," and picks people at random. To stay on the list, you must check in every month with Save My Spot, online or by calling 206-256-7000. Questions about applying go to SHA's Housing Choice Voucher program at 206-239-1674.

Emergency Rent Help in Columbus: Free Eviction Lawyers, PRC, 211, and Ohio's 3-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Columbus or anywhere in Franklin County and you are behind, here is the honest picture. There is no open City or County rent fund for everyone right now. The pandemic money is gone; the Columbus Urban League says "emergency housing funds have been discontinued." What is left is narrower. Families with children can apply to Franklin County's Prevention, Retention and Contingency (PRC) Emergency Assistance. The City's new Resilient Housing Initiative is only for extremely low-income Columbus residents about to become homeless, and its screening line was frozen when we checked. What Columbus does have is legal help at eviction court, a right to legal services for low-income families, and a Pay to Stay law. Everything here was checked on November 4, 2026.

Here's the short version: If you have court papers or a 3-day notice, call Legal Aid of Southeast and Central Ohio at 888-246-4420 (Columbus intake 9:30 a.m. to 1:30 p.m. weekdays). If you have a child at home, apply to Franklin County PRC (614-233-2999). Dial 2-1-1 for whatever rent help has money this month. If your gas or electric is off or about to be, book a Winter Crisis appointment with IMPACT Community Action at 614-252-2799. And if you get a summons, go to court: Legal Aid, Franklin County Job and Family Services and mediators are on the 11th floor of the courthouse to meet tenants before their hearings.

Ohio moves fast: a 3-day notice, then court about a week later

Before filing an eviction, an Ohio landlord must give you notice to leave "three or more days before beginning the action," and the notice must say: "You are being asked to leave the premises. If you do not leave, an eviction action may be initiated against you" (Ohio Revised Code 1923.04). The summons must be served "at least seven days before the day set for trial" (ORC 1923.06). A 3-day notice does not mean you must be out in three days; it means the landlord may file. Use those days to call Legal Aid and apply for rent help, and do not skip the hearing.

Free eviction lawyers in Columbus

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can catch mistakes in the landlord's papers, raise defenses like bad repairs or a refused payment, negotiate time or a payment plan, and connect you to rent money.

Legal Aid of Southeast and Central Ohio (LASCO)

  • Who it is: the legal aid office for Columbus (the court's eviction page still calls it the Legal Aid Society of Columbus). LASCO "offers free noncriminal legal help for people with low incomes, veterans, and seniors in 36 counties." Its Columbus office, 1108 City Park Ave., "handles cases in Franklin and Madison counties."
  • How to apply: call the intake line at 888-246-4420. In LASCO's words: "Currently, our Columbus intake is open from 9:30 a.m. to 1:30 p.m. Monday through Friday." Or use the online application on LASCO's Apply page, which "is open all hours."
  • When LASCO may represent you, in its words: if "You receive a subsidy or voucher and received a notice to leave," "If you have been locked out of your home or your utilities have been shut off," or "If you received court papers."
  • At eviction court: LASCO's Tenant Advocacy Project (TAP) "offers free legal representation to income-eligible tenants, particularly in the City of Columbus." The court says that before the hearing starts, "defendants are given the opportunity to speak with an attorney from the Legal Aid Society of Columbus, a Tenant Advocacy Program, to see if they are eligible to be represented free of charge."

Columbus's right to legal services in eviction cases

In December 2024 the Columbus City Council passed Ordinance 3510-2024, which created an "Access to Legal Services" policy in City Code 4551.08. It says "All Covered Individuals are eligible to receive legal services from a Designated Organization(s) in a Covered Proceeding once an action has been filed." A covered individual is a person living in the City "with at least one (1) child" whose "annual gross income is not in excess of one-hundred percent (100%) of the federal poverty guidelines" (City Code 4501.074), and a covered proceeding is an eviction case "in a Franklin County Court." The law adds that this access "is contingent upon additional Council action authorizing the appropriation and expenditure of funds." On April 22, 2026, Mayor Ginther's housing stability package included "$1.5 million" for eviction court legal representation (Access to Counsel). If you fit the definition, tell Legal Aid when you call or at court. If you do not, apply anyway: Legal Aid serves many low-income tenants outside it.

Franklin County Municipal Court: eviction court and the Self Help Center

  • Where: eviction hearings are at the Franklin County Municipal Court, 375 S. High St., Columbus, Courtroom 11B, Monday through Friday at 8:30 a.m. The court says "Bring a copy of your summons to court with you," and "You will be greeted on the 11th floor by Court Navigators who will give you information and help check you in."
  • Help in the building: Legal Aid's Tenant Advocacy Project; "Representatives from Franklin County Job and Family Services are available onsite at Eviction Court to screen tenants prior to their Court appearance to see if they qualify for financial assistance to pay past due rent"; and free onsite mediation by Community Mediation Services.
  • If you do not show up: "the magistrate will hear the case in their absence and make a decision."
  • Self Help Center: a free walk-in service on the 16th floor, Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 3 p.m., (614) 645-7381. Staff give legal information and court forms, not legal advice, and the court warns that "Due to a budget shortfall, the Center may close on short notice," so call first.

Help paying rent in Columbus: the doors that are left

There is no single rent office. Most of what is left is for families with children or for people about to become homeless, and every program depends on limited money. Apply to more than one.

Franklin County PRC Emergency Assistance

  • What it covers: Franklin County Job and Family Services' PRC Emergency Assistance can help with rent or mortgage, security deposits, and utilities "(except for the home heating source utility during months when the Winter Crisis Program is in operation)," among other costs. The County's PRC plan says rent is eligible "when they are overdue or if there is a notice of eviction," and that "Emergency Assistance cannot exceed $2,000."
  • Who qualifies: you must live in Franklin County, "Have at least one minor child, pregnant person, or non-custodial parent living in your home," have gross household income "at or below 175% of the Federal Poverty Level" ($4,813 a month for a household of four, per the County's table), and complete a "Sustainability Determination." Under the plan, your monthly rent must be 75% or less of your household's gross income, and you must not have received Emergency Assistance in the last 12 months. You need a current lease in your name.
  • How to apply: download the application from the County's PRC and How to Apply page and return it with your documents by email to franklin-cdjfs-agencyprc@jfs.ohio.gov, or by fax, mail or in person. Questions go to the PRC Inbound Center at 614-233-2999. If you are already in eviction court, look for the Job and Family Services screeners there.
  • Your landlord's side: the plan says a landlord who agrees in writing to take a PRC payment is "prohibited from evicting a tenant for nonpayment of rent with respect to the period covered by the assistance."

The City's Resilient Housing Initiative (frozen when we checked)

On April 22, 2026, the City said the Resilient Housing Initiative "equips community agencies to serve residents facing an immediate housing crisis with housing problem-solving support, case management and targeted financial assistance," and that it "is the first time city funds have been used directly for this purpose." The Mayor proposed $965,000 for it, split among Broad Street Presbyterian Church, Jewish Family Services, Columbus Literacy Council, The Homeless Families Foundation, Gladden Community House, St. Stephen's Community House and the YMCA of Central Ohio. Rentful614, the rent-help site run as an initiative of the City, Franklin County, United Way of Central Ohio and partners, says the program is "only for extremely low-income Columbus residents" facing homelessness, that "Not all participants will receive financial assistance," and that you reach it through the RHI Screening Line, 614-278-0080. On November 4, 2026 that page said: "Current RHI Status: FROZEN." Check Rentful614's homelessness page before you call.

LSS 211 Central Ohio

LSS 211 Central Ohio (formerly HandsOn Central Ohio) says that "With just one call, text, web chat or online search, 211 provides immediate access to more than 3,000 local programs," and that "A local network of service providers disburses rent, mortgage and utilities assistance payments" for Franklin County residents. Dial 2-1-1 and ask for rent help, eviction prevention and utility help, and write down every name and number you get. If you are already homeless, the Community Shelter Board's Homeless Hotline, (614) 274-7000, "is open 24 hours a day."

IMPACT Community Action: heating help, not rent right now

  • Rent and housing: IMPACT's Housing Stability and Supportive Services page says: "Due to high demand, our wait list is currently paused. Please check back periodically for updates on availability" (checked November 4, 2026).
  • HEAP and the Winter Crisis Program: IMPACT is Franklin County's energy assistance provider. The state's HEAP Winter Crisis Program "runs from Nov. 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027" for households at or below 175 percent of the federal poverty guidelines that are "facing disconnection, have been disconnected, need to establish new service, need to pay to transfer service, or have less than a 25 percent supply of bulk fuel." IMPACT says "Appointments are posted daily at 10:00 AM for the next business day." Its HEAP hours are Monday through Thursday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., and Friday, 8 a.m. to 3 p.m., at 711 Southwood Avenue; call 614-252-2799 or start on IMPACT's Emergency Assistance page. Keeping the heat on frees money for rent.

Ohio eviction timeline: what happens and when

Ohio evictions are "forcible entry and detainer" cases under Ohio Revised Code chapter 1923. In Columbus and the rest of Franklin County they are heard at the Franklin County Municipal Court. These are the steps:

  • 1. The notice. The landlord must deliver a notice to leave at least three days before filing, by certified mail, in person, or by leaving it at your home (ORC 1923.04). Ending a month-to-month lease for other reasons takes notice at least 30 days before the rent due date (ORC 5321.17).
  • 2. The summons. The clerk mails the summons and complaint, and it must be served "at least seven days before the day set for trial" (ORC 1923.06). The complaint often includes a second claim for back rent or damages; you have 28 days from service to answer that money claim (ORC 1923.06). The summons tells you, "You have the right to seek legal assistance."
  • 3. Court. Go. Check in on the 11th floor and in Courtroom 11B, then talk to Legal Aid, Job and Family Services, and the mediators before your case is called. Bring your lease, receipts, texts, photos of repair problems, and proof of any rent you offered. The law limits a continuance to eight days unless the landlord agrees or you post a bond to cover the rent (ORC 1923.08). If the case is about unpaid rent, you can raise counterclaims, and the court may order you to pay rent into court while it is pending (ORC 1923.061).
  • 4. Paying in Columbus: Pay to Stay. Inside the City of Columbus, City Code 4551.07 (Ordinance 2109-2023) says that if a tenant, "or an agency or individual on the tenant's behalf," tenders "all past due rent with reasonable late fees and court costs" before a judgment and the landlord refuses, that tender "shall be an affirmative defense." The City says the law "would allow residents time to secure rental assistance dollars up until a court judgment." A separate City law (Ordinance 2108-2023) requires landlords to accept a third-party payment for a tenant who is not otherwise in breach of the lease. Neither stops an eviction for reasons other than unpaid rent, and Ohio law gives no statewide right to pay and stay.
  • 5. After the judgment. The court says that if the eviction is granted, "The tenant will receive a red tag on the door of the rental property indicating they have five (5) days to leave the premises," and after that the landlord can file for a set out "supervised by a special court bailiff." The officer must carry out the court's order "within ten days" of receiving it (ORC 1923.14). Move what matters before the set out.
  • Lockouts are illegal. No landlord may shut off utilities, lock you out, or threaten to, to get you out "other than as provided in Chapters 1923., 5303., and 5321." of the Revised Code, and a landlord who does is liable for your damages and attorney's fees (ORC 5321.15). LASCO lists lockouts and landlord utility shutoffs among the cases it takes.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The pandemic-era rent programs are over. The Columbus Urban League's Housing Accelerator page says "emergency housing funds have been discontinued," and the City's Housing and Assistance Programs page lists no rent program (checked November 4, 2026). Ignore old flyers that send you there.
  • The Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority (CMHA) runs Section 8 in Franklin County. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its Housing Choice Voucher page says "Waitlist selections are currently in process. If selected, you will receive a notice via mail (and email if on file) with the next steps" (checked November 4, 2026), and CMHA draws from a lottery pool. See our CMHA Columbus page. If you already have a CMHA voucher or unit and fall behind, call CMHA at (614) 421-6000 right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and call the Homeless Hotline at (614) 274-7000.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Columbus right now?

Only in small pieces. On November 4, 2026 we found no open City of Columbus or Franklin County rent fund for everyone. Families with a minor child, a pregnancy, or a non-custodial parent can apply to Franklin County's Prevention, Retention and Contingency (PRC) Emergency Assistance, a one-time payment that cannot exceed $2,000. The City's Resilient Housing Initiative is only for extremely low-income Columbus residents about to become homeless, and its screening line was listed as frozen. IMPACT Community Action's housing stability wait list is paused. Dial 2-1-1, and if you have court papers, call Legal Aid at 888-246-4420.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Columbus?

Call Legal Aid of Southeast and Central Ohio (LASCO) at 888-246-4420; its Columbus intake is open 9:30 a.m. to 1:30 p.m., Monday through Friday, and its online application is open all hours. If you go to eviction court at the Franklin County Municipal Court, the court says tenants get the chance to speak with a Legal Aid attorney from its Tenant Advocacy Project before the hearing to see if they can be represented free of charge. Columbus law also gives a right to legal services to Columbus tenants with at least one child and income at or below 100% of the federal poverty guidelines.

Who qualifies for Columbus's Access to Legal Services (right to counsel)?

Under Columbus City Code 4501.074 and 4551.08, a covered individual is a tenant living in the City of Columbus, with at least one child, whose annual gross income is not more than 100% of the federal poverty guidelines, in an eviction case filed in a Franklin County court. The law says the help depends on the City Council funding a contract with a legal aid organization. The Mayor's April 22, 2026 housing stability package, sent to City Council, included $1.5 million for eviction court legal representation. Tenants who do not qualify can still apply to Legal Aid.

How fast is an eviction in Ohio?

Fast. The landlord must give a written notice to leave at least three days before filing (Ohio Revised Code 1923.04). The 3-day notice does not mean you must be out in 3 days; it means the landlord may file. The summons must be served at least seven days before the hearing (ORC 1923.06). A continuance longer than eight days needs the landlord's consent or a bond from you (ORC 1923.08). In Franklin County, if the eviction is granted you get a red tag on your door giving you five days to leave, and then the landlord can ask for a set out supervised by a court bailiff.

Can I stop a Columbus eviction by paying the rent?

Often, yes, if you rent inside the City of Columbus and the case is only about unpaid rent. Columbus's Pay to Stay law (Ordinance 2109-2023, City Code 4551.07) makes your tender of all past-due rent with reasonable late fees, plus court costs once a case is filed, an affirmative defense if the landlord refuses it before judgment. The City says it lets residents secure rental assistance dollars up until a court judgment. Keep proof of every payment you offered. It is not a defense to evictions for other reasons, and Ohio state law has no general right to pay and stay.

Is the Columbus Section 8 waiting list open?

The Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority (CMHA) does not say open or closed. Its Housing Choice Voucher page says "Waitlist selections are currently in process. If selected, you will receive a notice via mail (and email if on file) with the next steps." CMHA draws from a lottery pool, so applying early does not help. It is not an emergency rent fund: a voucher will not arrive in time to stop an eviction.

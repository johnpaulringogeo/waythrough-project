Emergency Rent Help in Colorado Springs: CERA's Random Selection, 211, Free Eviction Lawyers, and the 10-Day Notice

Matt · Updated October 2026

If you rent in Colorado Springs and you are behind, the door that actually pays is the state's Colorado Emergency Rental Assistance (CERA) program. It can pay up to $15,000, but it picks households at random, and its monthly window is closed right now: the next one opens Wednesday, November 4, 2026 at 10 a.m. and closes Friday, November 6 at 5 p.m. If you already have eviction court papers, you can call the CARE Center at (303) 838-1200 on any weekday for CERA's Daily Random Selection. Neither the City of Colorado Springs nor El Paso County runs its own rent fund; the City's housing department says it "does not provide direct services." For eviction help, call Colorado Legal Services in Colorado Springs at 719-471-0380. Everything here was checked on October 28, 2026.

Here's the short version: Get your lease, your ID, and the eviction paper you received (a demand for rent counts) ready now, and plan to apply on the Division of Housing's CERA page between November 4 and November 6. If you have a court summons, call the CARE Center at (303) 838-1200 instead, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. Dial 2-1-1 or text your ZIP code to 898-211 for local rent help. Call Colorado Legal Services at 719-471-0380, file an answer if you got court papers, and go to court.

A 10-day demand is not an eviction

To evict you for unpaid rent, a Colorado landlord must first serve a written demand giving you 10 days to pay or move out (C.R.S. § 13-40-104(1)(d); HB19-1118 changed it from three days to ten). Colorado Legal Services calls this paper a "Demand for Rent or Possession" and says: "Your landlord cannot file an eviction in court earlier." Paying still works after a case is filed: under C.R.S. § 13-40-115(4), a landlord who gave you a nonpayment notice "shall accept" your full payment of what the notice says you owe, plus any rent that has come due since, "at any time until a judge issues a judgment for possession," and the court then dismisses the case (checked October 28, 2026). You can pay the landlord or the court. A demand for rent is also one of the papers that lets you enter CERA's Monthly Random Selection.

Help paying rent in Colorado Springs: the doors that pay

CERA is the big one. The local doors below are small and run out fast. Call before you count on any of them.

CERA (Colorado Emergency Rental Assistance): the main door

CERA is run by the Colorado Division of Housing (DOH), part of the Department of Local Affairs. In its words, "The maximum total assistance a household can receive is $15,000," and the money "may be used to cover your rental payments on a short term basis and may also cover associated fees if required to protect you from displacement." DOH is also plain about the odds: "We understand that there is more demand than funds available" (checked October 28, 2026).

  • Who qualifies, in DOH's words: you rent your primary residence and "must have a valid lease within the state of Colorado"; your household "must be at or below 60% of the Area Median Income (AMI) for your county of residence"; you are past due on rent; you have had "a significant life event that has negatively affected your ability to pay rent," such as a loss of income, a health crisis, a death in the family, or a big unexpected expense; and you have not received CERA, ERAP, TRAG, or EMAP lot-rent help "within the last 24 months."
  • You need an eviction paper. For the Monthly Random Selection, DOH accepts "A demand for rent," a court summons or eviction notice, an eviction answer, a stipulation, a writ of restitution, a sheriff's notice, mediation documentation, or HB25-1168 payment plan documentation (that last one is only for survivors of violence). The paper must be "active or recently active" and about unpaid rent.
  • Monthly Random Selection: DOH says "Pre-applications for the Monthly Random Selection are currently closed." The next window "Opens: Wednesday, November 4, 2026 at 10:00 a.m." and "Closes: Friday, November 6, 2026 at 5:00 p.m." Only one application per household per window. DOH says "Applying earlier does not improve your chances of being selected." After the window closes, you get the result by email; if you are picked, "you can expect to have about a week to complete the full application."
  • Daily Random Selection (court papers): if you have a court summons or eviction notice, an eviction answer, a stipulation agreement, a writ of restitution, or a sheriff's notice, apply by calling the CARE Center at (303) 838-1200, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. Mountain Time. A demand for rent alone does not qualify for the daily selection.
  • Help with the process: you can call or text the CARE Center at the same number or use the live chat at carecenter.us. DOH says the CARE Center "can help you understand your options" and "may refer you to in-person assistance."

Local rent help: small, and it runs out

The City of Colorado Springs lists these groups under "Rental, Mortgage, or Utility Assistance." Here is what the ones we checked say about themselves (checked October 28, 2026):

  • Catholic Charities of Central Colorado: runs a Colorado Springs Rent Assistance Program, but says: "Because of limited funding, we can serve only about seven of the 500 rent requests we receive each month." Main line: 719-636-2345.
  • Westside CARES, 2808 W. Colorado Ave., for people who live west of I-25 in its service area: it pauses rent and mortgage help from the 1st to the 7th of each month "Due to extraordinary volume of requests." From the 8th on, rent help is in person, Monday to Thursday, 9:30 a.m. to 12:30 p.m., and it asks you to arrive early. You need a valid government-issued ID, and you cannot have received rent help from Westside CARES before. 719-389-0759.
  • Others on the City's list: Tri-Lakes Cares ("rent, mortgage, and utility assistance," northern El Paso County), Volunteers of America ("homelessness prevention"), Colorado Health Network ("rent assistance for people with HIV/AIDS"), and, for veterans, Mt. Carmel Veterans Service Center and Home Front Military Network. We did not confirm whether they have money this month; call first.

Pikes Peak United Way 211: the switchboard

Pikes Peak United Way calls 211 "the free, multilingual, easy-to-remember phone number" for El Paso County and 11 other counties. Its specialists staff the line "from 8am to 5pm Monday through Friday." Dial 2-1-1, call the alternative number 719-955-0742, or text your ZIP code to 898-211. 211 is also how you get a referral for Project COPE utility help (see below). When the line is closed, Pikes Peak United Way points people to 211colorado.org.

The City and El Paso County don't run a rent fund

The City of Colorado Springs Housing and Homelessness Response Department says it "does not provide direct services, housing, or referrals to resources," and that its resource list "is provided for informational purposes only." El Paso County's housing resources page also points renters to outside groups, such as Colorado Housing Connects and Pikes Peak United Way, not to a county fund. The City's old Emergency Rental Assistance Program page dates from October 2022 and describes federal pandemic money; the City's current housing help list sends renters to CERA instead (checked October 28, 2026).

Utility help: LEAP and Colorado Springs Utilities

  • LEAP (Low-income Energy Assistance Program): the state says "LEAP will start taking applications Nov. 1, 2026," and you can apply "at any time between Nov. 1 through April 30." Apply online through Colorado PEAK or by phone at 1-866-HEAT-HELP (1-866-432-8435). The state says LEAP "usually pays between $200 to $1,000" and usually sends the money straight to your heating company. If your heat might be shut off, call right away: LEAP "might be able to process an emergency application in about 10 days if you qualify."
  • Colorado Springs Utilities payment plan or extension: set one up online in My Account or call (719) 448-4800. Springs Utilities says: "We're happy to work with you on a payment extension or help you find another program if you need help with your bill."
  • Project COPE: Springs Utilities says it "provides utilities payment assistance one time per year to families and individuals who experience an emergency situation regardless of income." Project COPE says you must be a Springs Utilities customer, have a past-due balance, have an emergency, and meet with one of its partner agencies. Between November 1 and April 30 you must apply for LEAP first. "Eligible customers may receive Project COPE once every 12 months." For a referral, call 211; Project COPE warns that "Most agencies do not accept walk-ins."

Paying the utility bill can free up cash for rent. Our utility assistance guide covers other programs.

Eviction help in Colorado Springs: free lawyers

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can check the landlord's notice, raise defenses such as HB24-1098 (below), and work out more time.

  • Colorado Legal Services, Colorado Springs office: 102 South Tejon St., Suite 430. It serves El Paso, Lincoln, Teller, and Fremont counties. New applicants: 9 a.m. to noon and 1 to 3 p.m., Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday. Call 719-471-0380 or fill out the online pre-application. CLS says you "may qualify for free civil legal aid if you meet certain income and other requirements based on federal poverty guidelines, or if you are 60 years old or older," and "If you call or visit us, we can help you in your native language."
  • Colorado Poverty Law Project (CPLP): it "connects low-income Coloradans facing eviction with staff and volunteer attorneys who provide critical legal assistance, at no cost to the client." Fill out its intake form or call (303) 532-2641; it notes that "Income restrictions may apply." Its walk-in courthouse help is in Denver and Larimer County, not El Paso County.
  • The Justice Center (Colorado Springs): offers "Ask-a-Lawyer: free legal advice" and "Find-a-Lawyer: free and reduced cost legal representation." Call (719) 473-6212.
  • Community Economic Defense Project (CEDP): El Paso County lists it for eviction help, but CEDP says "We are not currently accepting new clients to help with defending against evictions," and sends people facing eviction to the CARE Center at (303) 838-1200.
  • Mandatory mediation if you get benefits: Colorado Legal Services says, "If you receive certain benefits, like Social Security or cash assistance through the Colorado Works program, also known as, TANF, you might have the right to Mandatory Mediation before your landlord can file an eviction in court." Ask a lawyer about it as soon as you get a demand.
  • Colorado Housing Connects: the City and the County both list it for rental assistance and eviction help. 844-926-6632.

Who pays for these lawyers: the Eviction Legal Defense Fund

Colorado created the Eviction Legal Defense Fund in 2019 (SB19-180, now C.R.S. § 13-40-127). You don't apply to it yourself: the state court administrator gives grants to legal aid groups that help "indigent" tenants, which the law defines as income up to 200% of the federal poverty guidelines. The Judicial Branch says the money is allocated "based on a geographic, need-driven formula" that counts eviction filings in each county. Its 2026-2027 awards include Colorado Legal Services ($167,331), the Colorado Poverty Law Project ($96,814), the Community Economic Defense Project ($243,237), and The Justice Center ($35,366) (checked October 28, 2026).

Colorado eviction rules: what happens and when

These steps come from the Colorado statutes, the Colorado Judicial Branch's self-help page on residential evictions, and Colorado Legal Services:

  • 1. The demand. For unpaid rent, the landlord must serve a written demand giving you 10 days to pay or move out (C.R.S. § 13-40-104(1)(d)). A landlord who owns five or fewer single-family rental homes can use a 5-day notice if the lease says the 10-day period does not apply. Colorado Legal Services says landlords that get certain federal benefits or have federally backed mortgages "often have to give you 30 days." The Judicial Branch says "The demand must be in the tenant's primary language."
  • 2. Pay, and it stops. If you pay everything the demand asks for in time, Colorado Legal Services says "your landlord can't file the eviction against you." Keep proof of every payment.
  • 3. The court case. After the notice period ends, the landlord can file an eviction complaint and summons in county court. The court date on the summons must be at least 7 and no more than 14 days after the summons is issued (C.R.S. § 13-40-111), and the Judicial Branch says service must be made "at least 7 days before your hearing date."
  • 4. Your answer. File an answer (form JDF 103) by the date on the summons. The Judicial Branch warns: "If you do not file an answer or do not appear in court, the court may rule against you. This is called a Default Judgment." If the home has serious repair problems, you may have a defense, but only if you gave the landlord written notice first and pay the rent due into the court registry.
  • 5. After a judgment. If the landlord wins, the court can issue a writ of restitution, which "will allow the Sheriff to physically remove a tenant." Only the sheriff, with that court order, can remove you.
  • Late fees have limits. Under C.R.S. § 38-12-105, a landlord cannot charge a late fee until rent is at least 7 calendar days late, cannot charge more than the greater of $50 or 5% of the past-due rent, and cannot start an eviction because you did not pay late fees.

HB24-1098: most landlords need a reason

HB24-1098, "Cause Required for Eviction of Residential Tenant," was signed and took effect on April 19, 2024. It added C.R.S. §§ 38-12-1301 to 38-12-1307. A landlord "shall not serve a notice to terminate tenancy or a demand for possession or otherwise proceed with an action for unlawful detainer" unless there is cause. Cause includes unpaid rent, lease violations, and nuisance or property damage, plus "no-fault" reasons: demolition or conversion, substantial repairs, the landlord or a family member moving in, selling the home, a tenant who refuses to sign a new lease with reasonable terms, or a history of not paying rent. A no-fault eviction needs a written notice of at least 90 days; the Judicial Branch's form for it says to use it "if the lease isn't being renewed."

The law does not cover short-term rentals, tenants who have lived there less than 12 months, employer-provided housing, and some homes where the owner lives in the same single-family home, duplex, or triplex (or next door). If a landlord who is covered breaks these rules, you can raise it as a defense, and if the landlord can't show it complied, "the court shall dismiss the eviction proceeding" (C.R.S. § 38-12-1306).

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The Colorado Springs Housing Authority (CSHA) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its site says "None of our waiting lists are currently accepting online only pre-applications" and "The current directive from HUD to pause the issuance of new Housing Choice Vouchers is, unfortunately, still in effect" (checked October 28, 2026). Its 2026 opening announcements are all for public housing lists; the latest Housing Choice Voucher lottery listed is from 2025. CSHA also warns that it "does not send communication via text message." Main line: (719) 387-6700.
  • The City's 2022 Emergency Rental Assistance Program was paid for with federal pandemic money. Old flyers and posts still mention it; apply to CERA instead.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Colorado Springs right now?

Yes, but mostly through the state. Colorado Emergency Rental Assistance (CERA) can pay up to $15,000 and picks households at random. Its monthly pre-application window is closed right now; the next one opens Wednesday, November 4, 2026 at 10 a.m. and closes Friday, November 6 at 5 p.m. Neither the City of Colorado Springs nor El Paso County runs its own rent fund. Local charities such as Catholic Charities and Westside CARES help a small number of households each month; dial 211 to find out who has money.

How does CERA's random selection work?

You submit a pre-application and upload your eviction document during the monthly window. Everyone who applies in the window has the same chance, so applying early does not help. After the window closes, the Colorado Division of Housing holds a random selection and emails the results. If you are picked, you get about a week to finish the full application. Your household must be at or below 60% of the area median income for your county, be behind on rent, and not have received state rental assistance in the last 24 months.

What if I already have eviction court papers?

Call the CARE Center at (303) 838-1200, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., to apply for CERA's Daily Random Selection. It is for households with a court summons, an eviction answer, a stipulation, a writ of restitution, or a sheriff's notice. File your answer with the court by the date on the summons, and call Colorado Legal Services in Colorado Springs at 719-471-0380 the same day.

How much notice does a Colorado landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

Usually 10 days. Under C.R.S. 13-40-104(1)(d), the landlord must serve a written demand giving you 10 days to pay or move out, and cannot file in court before that. A landlord who owns five or fewer single-family rental homes can use 5 days if the lease says so, and some landlords with federal funding or federally backed mortgages must give 30 days. If you pay the full amount, the landlord has to accept it any time until a judge enters a judgment for possession.

Can my landlord refuse to renew my lease in Colorado?

Usually only for a reason the law allows. HB24-1098, in effect since April 19, 2024, says a landlord needs cause to end a tenancy. Allowed reasons include nonpayment, lease violations, and no-fault reasons such as major repairs, the owner or a family member moving in, or selling, which need at least 90 days' written notice. The law does not cover tenants who have lived there less than 12 months, short-term rentals, and some owner-occupied homes with up to three units.

Does the Colorado Springs Housing Authority give emergency rent help?

No. CSHA runs Section 8 and public housing. It says none of its waiting lists are accepting online pre-applications, and that HUD's directive pausing new Housing Choice Vouchers is still in effect. If you already have CSHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call CSHA at (719) 387-6700 right away.

Tampa RMAP: What It Pays Now, Who Qualifies, and How to Apply

Matt · Updated October 2026

RMAP is the City of Tampa's Rental and Move-In Assistance Program. Right now it is in a stage the city calls Phase X, and in this phase it only helps people who are moving into a new rental inside Tampa city limits. It can pay first month's rent, last month's rent, the security deposit, and administrative fees, up to $10,000 per household. You apply online only, and only on Tuesdays from 9 a.m. to 12 p.m. It does not pay back rent in this phase. If you are behind on rent where you live now, skip to what to do instead.

Here's the short version: Get approved for a new place in Tampa first, with a move-in date within 45 days. Then, on a Tuesday morning, apply on the city's RMAP online portal and upload your lease, approval letter, or welcome letter. Questions go to the City of Tampa Housing Information Line at 813-307-5555, Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. If you have never had RMAP before and your income is no more than 140% of the area median, you may qualify.

Is RMAP open right now? (checked October 15, 2026)

The city's RMAP page says, in its own words: "The City of Tampa's Rental Move-In Assistance Program (RMAP) reopened for Phase X on Tuesday, April 21st." It adds that "This phase of the program will exclusively provide assistance with move-in costs for eligible residents." The page was last updated on September 17, 2026, and when we checked it on October 15, 2026, it did not say that Phase X had closed. It still says "Applications will be accepted Tuesdays only from 9:00 a.m. to 12:00 p.m."

RMAP has paused between phases before; the same page thanks people for their "patience while the program was temporarily paused." Before you plan around it, call 813-307-5555 and ask whether Phase X is still taking applications.

What is the RMAP program?

The city says Mayor Jane Castor's administration launched the Rental & Move-In Assistance Program in March 2022 "to help local renters with their rising rent bills." It is run by the City of Tampa's Housing and Community Development division, not by the Tampa Housing Authority and not by Hillsborough County. The program opens in phases. Each phase can cover different costs, and the current one, Phase X, covers move-in costs only.

You may see older descriptions of RMAP, including on the portal's own inquiry screen, that mention monthly rent subsidies or help with past-due rent. The city's program page says this phase will "exclusively" help with move-in costs, so go by that. If you are behind on rent, call the Housing Information Line and ask whether anything else is open.

RMAP eligibility: who qualifies

The city lists four requirements for a new move-in. You have to meet all four:

  • "Must have been approved for a unit (Move-in date within 45 days)"
  • "Unit must be located within the city limits of Tampa." A Tampa mailing address is not always inside city limits. The portal checks your new address before you can go further.
  • "Must have a stable source of income that demonstrates the ability to pay monthly rent (Up to 140% AMI)"
  • "Household has not received RMAP assistance previously." If RMAP helped you in an earlier phase, you cannot get it again.

The income limit is 140% of the area median income (AMI). The city's page uses the "Federal HUD and State Income Limits 2025 as of 4/28/2025." At 140%, the most your household can earn in a year is:

RMAP also wants proof you can keep paying the rent after you move in. That is why it asks for a "stable source of income." If you have no income right now, RMAP is not the right door; see the other options below.

What RMAP pays, and what it does not

  • It can pay: first month's rent, last month's rent, the security deposit, and administrative fees, for a new move-in only.
  • The cap: "Maximum assistance per household: $10,000."
  • It does not pay application fees. The city says, "RMAP does not cover or reimburse application fees under any circumstances."
  • It does not help with a home you already live in. "Existing leases or currently occupied units are not eligible."

How to apply for RMAP (the RMAP application)

Step 1: Get approved for a unit first

You need a place lined up before you apply. Then get one of these documents, because you must upload it as proof of your move-in:

  • A full lease (all pages, with signatures). It must be for 12 months, and it must start on the day you apply or within 45 days after. The city will not accept a lease that started before your application date.
  • A housing unit approval letter or welcome letter. It must show the move-in date, the total move-in costs, the unit address, your name, the number of people in your household, and the landlord's or property manager's contact information.

The city warns that "Applications submitted without acceptable proof of move-in will be considered ineligible." Check your letter against that list before Tuesday.

Step 2: Apply online on a Tuesday, 9 a.m. to 12 p.m.

Apply: City of Tampa RMAP online portal, Tuesdays only, 9:00 a.m. to 12:00 p.m.

Questions: Housing Information Line, 813-307-5555, Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 3 p.m.

  • Online only. "In-person applications will not be accepted." The city also says appointments are "not required or available," and booking one "will not allow early access."
  • Start with your new address. The portal asks for the address of the unit you are moving to and checks that it is inside city limits.
  • If a disability makes the online form hard to use, call the City ADA Coordinator, Raquel Pancho, at (813) 274-3964 to ask for an accommodation.

Step 3: What happens next

The city says "most applicants receive an update regarding their application status within 14 business days." Watch your email and phone. If your move-in date is close, call 813-307-5555 and give them your date.

If you are turned down, be careful before you try again. The city makes you wait after several tries: 30 days after 2 denied, expired, or ineligible applications, 60 days after 3, and 365 days after 4. It also says you "may become permanently ineligible to reapply if the information provided in their application cannot be verified." Get your documents right the first time.

RMAP for Section 8 voucher holders

Yes, you can apply if you have a Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8). For voucher holders, the city says RMAP "may assist with the approved security deposit amount, up to 2 times the rent," matching the amount the Tampa Housing Authority approved on your Request for Tenancy Approval (RTA). RMAP does not pay first or last month's rent for voucher households, and it does not pay cleaning fees or similar landlord charges. Have your RTA paperwork from THA ready when you apply.

Watch out for RMAP scams

The city says RMAP is run "only by City of Tampa staff through the official online application process." Nobody can get you approved faster, and applying is free. If someone asks you for money, offers "special access," or says they can guarantee RMAP, they are not with the city. Apply only through the city's portal.

Behind on rent in Tampa? Where to go instead of RMAP

If you need help with rent you already owe, RMAP's current phase cannot help. These are the other real doors in Tampa and Hillsborough County. Try more than one, because each runs on its own limited money.

Metropolitan Ministries rental assistance

Metropolitan Ministries says it "offers financial assistance on a daily first-come-first-served basis with rent, mortgage, and utilities while funding lasts. Not all inquiries will qualify." Here is what to know:

  • Proof of hardship is required. "All applications require proof of financial hardship, including documented loss of employment, furlough, reduction of hours, or approved unemployment applications."
  • One application per household. Metropolitan Ministries says it verifies addresses.
  • In Hillsborough County, you must go to an in-person orientation first. The orientation covers "eligibility, funding, and the approval process." If you live in ZIP code 33610, 33605, or 33619, a few spots open each business day at about 9 a.m. Everyone else in Hillsborough County books a spot online. Spots open at midnight, up to 7 days ahead, and close when they fill. Both sign-up links are on its Hillsborough financial assistance page.
  • Phone: (813) 209-1000, 2002 N. Florida Ave., Tampa.

If you are about to lose your home, Metropolitan Ministries also runs emergency shelter for families. Apply through its Get Help page, or call its Hillsborough intake team at (813) 209-1222.

Hillsborough County Social Services

The county's Social Services department says it provides "stabilization services to low-income residents of Hillsborough County," including "basic living needs, housing, education and training." It does not post a separate rent program on its website, so call and ask what is open. "You can call any of the Community Resource Centers at our Call Center - (813) 272-5220." The centers are Lee Davis, Plant City, University, and West Tampa.

211 in Hillsborough County

In Hillsborough County, 211 is answered 24 hours a day by the Crisis Center of Tampa Bay. Its staff give referrals to "over 3,000 human services available in the Tampa Bay area," including help with rent, utilities, and shelter. Dial 2-1-1. If you are calling from outside Hillsborough County, use (813) 234-1234.

Tampa Housing Authority (Section 8)

The Tampa Housing Authority (THA) runs Section 8, but it is not an emergency rent fund. On October 15, 2026, its Section 8 page said: "Currently the waitlist to receive Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) is closed and will NOT be accepting applications until further notice." Call THA at 813.341.9101. Our Tampa Housing Authority page explains its other lists and the visiting rules.

Bay Area Legal Services (free lawyers)

Bay Area Legal Services is a nonprofit law firm for the Tampa Bay area. Its services are free if you qualify. Call (800) 625-2257, Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., or apply online any time for matters that are not emergencies. It offers interpreters at no cost.

If you already have eviction papers in Florida

Florida eviction cases move fast. Under Florida law, if you want to fight an eviction for unpaid rent with any defense other than "I paid," you must pay the rent the landlord says you owe into the court registry, or file a motion asking the court to decide the amount. You have 5 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, after you are served. If you miss that deadline, the landlord can get a default judgment without a hearing (Florida Statutes, section 83.60(2)). The court clerk's summons will tell you about this rule.

  • Call Bay Area Legal Services the same day at (800) 625-2257.
  • Keep applying for rent help at Metropolitan Ministries and through 211, and tell your landlord in writing each time you apply.
  • Do not move out because of a notice alone. Only a court order can remove you.

Frequently asked questions

What is the RMAP program in Tampa?

RMAP is the City of Tampa's Rental and Move-In Assistance Program. It started in March 2022 and opens in phases. The current phase, Phase X, only helps with move-in costs for a new rental inside Tampa city limits: first month's rent, last month's rent, the security deposit, and administrative fees, up to $10,000 per household.

Is RMAP open right now?

The city's RMAP page says Phase X reopened on Tuesday, April 21, and takes applications on Tuesdays only, from 9 a.m. to 12 p.m. When we checked on October 15, 2026, the page (updated September 17, 2026) did not say Phase X had closed. Call the Housing Information Line at 813-307-5555 to confirm before you apply.

How do I apply for RMAP?

Online only, through the city's RMAP portal, on a Tuesday between 9 a.m. and 12 p.m. You must already be approved for a unit with a move-in date within 45 days, and you must upload one proof of move-in: a full 12-month lease, a housing approval letter, or a welcome letter. In-person applications are not accepted.

Does RMAP pay back rent?

Not in the current phase. The city says Phase X will exclusively help with move-in costs, and existing leases or occupied units are not eligible. If you are behind on rent, contact Metropolitan Ministries at (813) 209-1000, call Hillsborough County Social Services at (813) 272-5220, or dial 211.

Can I get RMAP if I have a Section 8 voucher?

Yes. For voucher holders, RMAP may pay the security deposit the Tampa Housing Authority approved, up to 2 times the rent. It does not pay first or last month's rent for voucher households. Have your RTA paperwork from THA ready.

How does Metropolitan Ministries rental assistance work?

Metropolitan Ministries helps with rent, mortgage, and utilities on a first-come, first-served basis while funding lasts, and every application needs proof of financial hardship, such as a job loss or cut hours. Hillsborough County residents must attend an in-person orientation first; spots are booked through its website. Call (813) 209-1000.

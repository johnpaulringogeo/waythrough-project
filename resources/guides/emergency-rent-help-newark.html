Emergency Rent Help in Newark: Free Eviction Lawyers, Essex County Rent Help, and New Jersey's Pay-to-Stay Rules

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Newark and you are behind, start with this: Newark is one of the few cities where low-income tenants have their own free eviction lawyers, through the City's Office of Tenant Legal Services. New Jersey's pandemic rent program is closed, and we found no City of Newark rent fund. What is left is the State's eviction-prevention partners in Essex County, County Emergency Assistance for people on Work First New Jersey, and NJ 211. The good news in New Jersey law: in a case for unpaid rent, you can end it by paying everything owed by 4:30 p.m. on the trial date. Everything here was checked on November 12, 2026.

The short version: The day you get a court summons, call Newark's Office of Tenant Legal Services at 973-877-9424 or Essex-Newark Legal Services at (973) 624-4500. Dial 2-1-1 and ask for rent help. And go to court on your trial date: New Jersey does not let you answer in writing, and if you do not show up, you lose by default.

New Jersey's clock: no notice, a trial in about three weeks, then three business days

For unpaid rent, a New Jersey landlord does not have to warn you first. DCA's Truth in Renting guide says a notice to quit "is required for all good cause evictions, except for an eviction for nonpayment of rent" (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.2), so the first paper you see may be a court summons. The courts say your trial date "will be at least 21 days from date of service of the summons." A nonpayment case "may be dismissed if the tenant pays to the court by 4:30 p.m. on the scheduled trial date." And even after a judgment, the "Total amount shown on the Judgment of Possession must be paid any time up to three business days after an eviction."

Free eviction lawyers in Newark

A lawyer cannot pay your rent, but can check whether the landlord followed Newark's rent control rules, raise repair problems, and make sure you get every day the law allows.

  • Newark Office of Tenant Legal Services (OTLS). The City set it up by ordinance on December 19, 2018, and says "OTLS's purpose is to provide free legal services to Newark tenants of low-income who are facing eviction or the threat of eviction." Who qualifies, in the City's words: "Newark, tenant-households that are facing the threat of eviction and that have a gross income that is at or below 200% of the current Federal poverty level (income exception made for applicants who are age 60 or older and infirm)." Intake "occurs every weekday" and starts with an intake form. An OTLS lawyer may represent you in court, advise you on repairs and check that your rent is calculated correctly. 920 Mayor Kenneth A. Gibson Blvd., Room 110, 973-877-9424. See newarknj.gov.
  • Essex-Newark Legal Services, 5 Commerce Street, 2nd Floor, gives "free legal assistance on civil matters to Essex-County low-income residents." Call (973) 624-4500 or email enls@lsnj.org.
  • LSNJLAW hotline (Legal Services of New Jersey): "Call 1-888-LSNJ-LAW | 1-888-576-5529," open "Monday through Friday from 8:00 a.m. to 5:30 p.m." You can also apply online at lsnjlawhotline.org. LSNJ says "applicants must be financially eligible to qualify" and help is available in all languages.
  • Volunteer Lawyers for Justice is one of the State's eviction-prevention partners for Essex County only: (973) 943-4754, housing@vljnj.org.

Help paying rent in Newark

The State's eviction-prevention partners in Essex County

New Jersey's Department of Community Affairs (DCA) runs the Omnibus Eviction and Homelessness Prevention program, and "Through its Omnibus Eviction and Homelessness Prevention (OEHP) program, DCA funds community partners in all 21 counties." DCA's Eviction Guide lists these providers for Essex:

  • Essex-Newark Legal Services: (973) 624-4500, enls@lsnj.org
  • United Community Corporation: (973) 642-0181, info@uccnewark.org
  • Volunteer Lawyers for Justice: (973) 943-4754, housing@vljnj.org

The services the guide lists include free legal representation in court, a resource navigator, help negotiating with your landlord, access to rental assistance and relocation support. It does not promise money to everyone, so ask each one what they have now. You can also check which DCA programs you may qualify for at dcaid.dca.nj.gov, and DCA's Office of Eviction Prevention answers general questions at 609-376-0810 or evictionprevention@dca.nj.gov. See evictionguide.dca.nj.gov.

Essex County Emergency Assistance

Essex County's Division of Family Assistance and Benefits (formerly Welfare) says "WFNJ clients may receive Emergency Assistance (EA) in certain situations." The help it lists includes "temporary rental assistance or back rent or mortgage payments," utility payments and moving costs. If you get Work First New Jersey cash assistance, or think you qualify, call the call center at (973) 395-8000 (Monday to Friday, 7:30 am to 4:00 pm) or go to 18 Rector Street or 465 Martin Luther King Jr. Blvd. in Newark. The offices close to the public every third Thursday. See essexcountynj.org.

NJ 211

Dial 2-1-1 or text 898211. NJ 211 says "We offer services 24/7." Ask for rent help in Newark and write down every name and number you get. See nj211.org.

Utility bills: LIHEAP, USF, PAGE and shutoff protection

  • LIHEAP and USF: the BPU says "The LIHEAP application period runs from October 1, 2026, through June 30, 2027," and "Applications are processed on a first-come, first-served basis while funding remains available." One application covers both the Universal Service Fund and LIHEAP; apply at dcaid.dca.nj.gov or call 1-800-510-3102. In Newark, these agencies take applications: La Casa de Don Pedro, 317 Roseville Avenue, 973-485-0795; United Community Corporation, 106 Ann Street, 973-642-0181; and Catholic Charities of the Archdiocese of Newark, 47 Miller Street, 973-266-7954. Income limits are at energyassistance.nj.gov.
  • Winter Termination Program: eligible customers are protected from gas and electric shutoff "from November 15 through March 15." The BPU says "Customers can call their utility company during the protection period and request to be enrolled." BPU: (800) 624-0241.
  • PAGE (Payment Assistance for Gas and Electric): the BPU awarded the PAGE grant to NJ SHARES in October 2026. PSE&G lists NJ SHARES (PAGE) at (866) 657-4273.
  • PSE&G: customer service 1-800-436-7734; ask about payment arrangements.

New Jersey eviction timeline: what happens and when

Newark eviction cases go to Essex County Landlord/Tenant court, Special Civil Part, at the Historic Courthouse, 470 Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Blvd., 973-776-9300 ext. 56897. These are the steps for a case about unpaid rent:

  • 1. Good cause. Under the Anti-Eviction Act (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.1), most New Jersey landlords can only evict for a reason the law lists. Unpaid rent is one. The landlord can file without a notice to quit (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.2). Call OTLS or Essex-Newark Legal Services as soon as you are behind.
  • 2. The summons. You are served with a summons and complaint with a trial date "at least 21 days from date of service of the summons." Write the date down.
  • 3. Court. Go. The courts say "The defendant must go to court to defend against the landlord's claims. Written responses are not acceptable." If you do not come, the case is marked "Default." On the trial date the judge may send you and your landlord to a trained settlement neutral, who "is not a judge and will try to help the landlord and tenant settle their case." Do not sign an agreement you cannot keep; ask a lawyer first.
  • 4. Paying to end it. A nonpayment case "may be dismissed if the tenant pays to the court by 4:30 p.m. on the scheduled trial date" the rent owed plus court costs and other charges the lease allows. This is where help from a provider above can save the apartment.
  • 5. Judgment and warrant. If the landlord wins a judgment for possession, a warrant of removal "cannot be issued to a Special Civil Part Officer until the expiration of three (3) business days," and the landlord must apply for it within 30 days of the judgment. Then "Residential tenants have three business days to leave the property after they are served with a warrant of removal."
  • 6. Paying even after a lockout. The courts say the "Total amount shown on the Judgment of Possession must be paid any time up to three business days after an eviction," by cashier's check, money order or cash, and "The landlord cannot refuse the timely offer of full payment by the tenant."
  • 7. Asking for more time. You can ask for an Order for Orderly Removal (typically no more than 7 calendar days) or a hardship stay of up to six months, which requires paying everything you owe. The courts say these requests "must be made within 10 days from the date of the eviction." The court forms are on njcourts.gov.
  • Only a court officer can put you out. A landlord who changes the locks, shuts off utilities or holds your belongings without a court order can owe you damages (N.J.S.A. 2A:39-1). Call the police, then a lawyer.

Newark rent control and other protections

  • Rent increases are capped. Newark's rent control ordinance says "In no case shall the allowable rent increase exceed 4%," and no increase is allowed "if the dwelling is not in substantial compliance and/or if the landlord has not met the registration requirements." If your rent went up more than the law allows, or your landlord never registered, tell your lawyer: it can change what you actually owe. Office of Rent Control: 920 Mayor Kenneth A. Gibson Blvd., Room 111, 973-733-3675, 8:30 am to 4:30 pm. The City says "you do not need a lawyer to file a petition at the Rent Control Office."
  • Rental registration. Newark requires landlords to register all rental units, and a unit that passes inspection gets a Certificate of Habitability. Division of Compliance: (973) 733-4641.
  • Security deposits: no more than one and one-half months' rent (N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.2), returned with interest within 30 days, with any deductions itemized; a landlord who wrongly keeps it can owe double (N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.1).
  • Grace period: seniors on Social Security or a government pension, and people on SSDI, SSI or Work First New Jersey, get a five business day grace period with no late charge when rent is due on the first (N.J.S.A. 2A:42-6.1).
  • Retaliation is illegal: a landlord cannot evict you or refuse to renew because you complained to a government agency about code violations or tried to enforce your rights (N.J.S.A. 2A:42-10.10).
  • Vouchers: the Law Against Discrimination bars refusing you because of your source of lawful income, including a Section 8 voucher (N.J.S.A. 10:5-12). NJ Division on Civil Rights: 1-833-653-2748.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • New Jersey's pandemic CVERAP. DCA says Phase II "is currently closed as funding has been fully obligated," and "The Eviction Prevention Program (EPP) is also closed to new pre-applications." Ignore old flyers and anyone offering to get you that money.
  • The Newark Housing Authority (NHA) runs public housing and Section 8, not emergency rent. Its voucher page says "The Housing Choice Voucher Program waiting list is currently closed," although most of its public housing lists are open (checked November 12, 2026). See our NHA guide. If you already have NHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call 973-273-6000 right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • Newark's Office of Rent Control decides rent and registration disputes, but it does not pay rent, and it cannot hear discrimination, harassment or retaliation cases; the City refers those to DCA.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and call 211 or New Jersey's homeless hotline at 877-652-1148.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Newark right now?

Some, through the State and the County. DCA funds eviction-prevention partners in Essex County, including Essex-Newark Legal Services at (973) 624-4500, United Community Corporation at (973) 642-0181 and Volunteer Lawyers for Justice. If you get Work First New Jersey, ask Essex County about Emergency Assistance at (973) 395-8000. We found no City of Newark rent fund, and the pandemic CVERAP is closed. Dial 211 for other help.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Newark?

Call Newark's Office of Tenant Legal Services at 973-877-9424. It gives free legal help to Newark tenant households facing eviction with gross income at or below 200% of the federal poverty level, with an exception for applicants 60 or older and infirm. Essex-Newark Legal Services and the LSNJLAW hotline at 1-888-576-5529 also help low-income tenants.

Does a New Jersey landlord have to give notice before filing for unpaid rent?

No. New Jersey requires a notice to quit for other kinds of evictions, but not for nonpayment of rent, so the first paper you get may be a court summons. Your trial will be at least 21 days after you are served. Go to court on that date; written responses are not accepted.

Can I stop a New Jersey eviction by paying the rent?

Yes. A nonpayment case may be dismissed if you pay the court by 4:30 p.m. on the trial date the rent owed plus court costs and other charges your lease allows. After a judgment, you can still pay the total on the Judgment of Possession up to three business days after an eviction, by cashier's check, money order or cash.

How much can a Newark landlord raise the rent?

Newark's rent control ordinance says the allowable rent increase can never exceed 4%, and no increase is allowed if the unit is not in substantial compliance or the landlord has not registered. Some newer buildings are exempt. Call the Office of Rent Control at 973-733-3675; you do not need a lawyer to file a petition.

What if my landlord locks me out?

In New Jersey only a court officer with a warrant of removal can put you out. A lockout or utility shutoff without a court order is illegal, and the landlord can owe you damages. Call the police, then call Newark's Office of Tenant Legal Services or Essex-Newark Legal Services. The courts have an illegal lockout kit on njcourts.gov.

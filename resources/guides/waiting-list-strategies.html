How to Navigate Housing Waiting Lists

Matt · 8 min read

Housing waiting lists are discouraging. In many areas the average Section 8 wait is 2+ years, some cities run 5-10 years, and a lot of lists only open for a few days every couple of years. You can still improve your odds and shorten the wait, and that's what this guide covers.

If you only remember a few things: apply to as many PHAs as you can (there's no limit), and check which preference points you qualify for (veterans, disabled, elderly, homeless). Look at smaller suburban PHAs, where the waits are shorter. Answer any letter or call right away. And never miss a recertification deadline, because that's how people lose their spot.

Strategy 1: Apply to Multiple PHAs

You Can Apply to Unlimited Housing Authorities

If you do one thing from this guide, do this. There is NO federal limit on the number of Section 8 waiting lists you can join. You can apply to as many Public Housing Authorities as you want, all at the same time, and you don't even need to live in a PHA's jurisdiction to apply.

Wait times vary wildly from one PHA to the next. A large urban PHA might have a 5-10 year wait while a smaller city PHA in the same state has a 1-2 year wait, or shorter. A rural PHA might move faster still. Every extra list you're on is another chance at a voucher sooner.

How to Apply Strategically

Start with your local PHA, but don't stop there. Then apply to:

  • PHAs in smaller cities and suburbs near you, which typically have shorter wait times than major urban centers
  • PHAs in rural areas or regions where you'd be willing to relocate, which often have the shortest waits
  • PHAs in neighboring states if you're willing to move

Cast a wide net, but only over places you could realistically move to if your name comes up.

Important Note: Porting

When you get a voucher from a PHA outside your home area, you may be required to use it in that PHA's jurisdiction for the first 12 months (sometimes longer). After that, you can "port" your voucher and use it to move to a different PHA's jurisdiction. For that first year, though, you live where the PHA that approved you operates.

So keep that in mind when you pick where to apply. Only apply to PHAs in areas you'd be willing to live, at least at first.

How to Find Open Waiting Lists

AffordableHousingOnline.com

Start here. AffordableHousingOnline.com tracks 361+ open waiting lists nationwide and updates regularly, and you can search it by state, city, or ZIP code.

Your Local PHA Website

Check your local PHA's website often, or ask to be told when the list opens. Many PHAs will email or call you when it does, but only if you ask.

HUD's PHA Contact Directory

HUD's PHA Contact Directory lists every public housing authority in the country with contact information. Call them or check their websites to ask whether the waiting list is open.

211

Call or text 211 from any phone and ask about local housing help, including which waiting lists are open.

Follow Local Housing Advocacy Organizations

Many cities have tenant unions, housing coalitions, or advocacy groups. Follow them on social media or get on their email lists. They often hear about a list opening before the general public does.

Beware of Scams

Applying for Section 8 is ALWAYS FREE. No legitimate organization charges a fee to apply for Section 8 or help you apply. If someone is asking for money to help you with your housing application, it's a scam. Report it to your state's attorney general.

Local Preferences Can Move You Up the List

What Are Local Preferences?

Many PHAs put certain kinds of applicants ahead of others on the waiting list. A preference can move you a long way up, sometimes turning years of waiting into months.

Common Preferences

  • Current residents: People already living in the PHA's jurisdiction
  • Working families: Households with employment income
  • Veterans: Military service members and veterans
  • Elderly/Disabled: Households with seniors or people with disabilities
  • Homelessness: People experiencing homelessness
  • Domestic violence survivors: People fleeing domestic violence
  • Involuntary displacement: People displaced by natural disaster, government action, or eviction

How to Use Preferences

First, ask each PHA what preferences it offers. The question is simple: "What local preferences do you have, and which ones do I qualify for?"

Then gather proof for each one you qualify for:

  • Proof of residency (utility bill, lease, mail)
  • DD-214 or military discharge papers (for veterans)
  • Disability documentation (medical records, Supplemental Security Income (SSI) / Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) award letter)
  • Domestic violence documentation (restraining order, agency certification)
  • Proof of homelessness (shelter documentation, case worker letter)
  • Eviction notice or displacement documentation

Last, claim every one of them on the application and attach the documents. Don't assume the PHA will figure it out on its own.

While You Wait

Waiting is hard, but don't just sit on the list. This is the time to line up other housing options.

Apply for LIHTC Apartments

LIHTC (Low-Income Housing Tax Credit) apartments are affordable housing that doesn't require a voucher. Many have no waiting list or much shorter waits than Section 8. See our guide on finding LIHTC housing to search for properties in your area.

Check for Public Housing Openings

Public housing has its own waiting lists, separate from Section 8, and sometimes they move faster. Ask your PHA about them.

Explore Other Programs

Depending on your situation, you may qualify for other housing programs:

  • SSVF (Supportive Services for Veteran Families): For low-income veteran families who are homeless or at imminent risk of becoming homeless
  • FUP (Family Unification Program): For families with child welfare involvement
  • Continuum of Care (CoC) permanent supportive housing: For people with disabilities experiencing homelessness. This used to be called Shelter Plus Care; it was folded into HUD's Continuum of Care program, so ask for CoC housing.
  • HUD-VASH (Veterans Affairs Supportive Housing): For veterans experiencing homelessness

Ask your PHA or case manager if you qualify for any of these.

Consider Relocating

If you're nearing the top of a list somewhere outside your area, think seriously about moving. A 6-month wait in another city beats a 5-year wait where you are, and you can always port your voucher home after 12 months.

Staying on the List: Critical Steps

One of the most common setbacks is getting dropped from a list because the PHA couldn't reach you. It's also one of the easiest to prevent.

Keep Your Contact Information Current

This matters more than anything else in this section. If you move, change your phone number, or get a new email address, update every PHA where you have an active application right away. Don't wait, and don't assume they'll figure it out.

Contact each PHA's application office and update your information:

  • Mailing address
  • Phone number (home, cell, work, or any numbers where you can be reached)
  • Email address

Respond to All Communications Promptly

When the PHA contacts you by mail, email, or phone, answer right away. Once your name comes up, you usually have only 10-15 business days to respond. Miss that window and you may be skipped or taken off the list.

Check your mail and your email often. If you get a voicemail, call back the same day.

Check In Periodically

No news isn't necessarily good news. Every 6 months, call each PHA or check your status online to confirm you're still on the list and your information is current. Some PHAs require periodic updates or re-certifications, and missing one can get you removed.

Keep a Spreadsheet

Create a simple spreadsheet or written record of every PHA you've applied to, including:

  • PHA name and jurisdiction
  • Date you applied
  • Application number or reference number
  • PHA phone number and website
  • Your position on the waiting list (if available)
  • Last contact date and any updates

Keep it up to date, and set calendar reminders to check in with each PHA every 6 months.

If You're Selected

You'll Get a Notification

When your name reaches the top of the list, the PHA will try to reach you by certified mail, phone, email, or some mix of those. If your contact information is old, this is where it costs you.

Respond Within the Window

You typically have 10-15 business days to respond. If you don't, the PHA may skip you and go to the next applicant. You can sometimes get an extension with a good reason, but don't count on it.

Attend Your Eligibility Interview

Next comes an eligibility interview, where the PHA checks everything on your application and confirms your income, household composition, and eligibility. Bring updated documents: recent pay stubs, bank statements, identification, and anything else the PHA asks for.

Get Your Voucher and Start Your Housing Search

Once approved, you'll receive your Housing Choice Voucher and information about your rights and responsibilities. You'll have a set time (usually 60-120 days) to find a unit. See our guide on applying for Section 8 for the next steps.

The Golden Rule

Keep your phone number and mailing address current with every PHA you've applied to. Getting dropped because they couldn't reach you is completely preventable.

Quick Checklist

  • Apply to multiple PHAs. There's no limit. Use our tool to find programs near you. Smaller cities and rural areas often have shorter waits.
  • Use AffordableHousingOnline.com, HUD's directory, and 211 to find open lists.
  • Ask each PHA about local preferences, since they can move you way up the list.
  • While waiting, apply for LIHTC housing and look at other programs.
  • Keep your contact information current with every PHA. Losing touch is the #1 reason people get dropped from lists.
  • Check in every 6 months and respond immediately to all PHA communications.
  • Keep a spreadsheet tracking all your applications and status.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is the Section 8 waiting list?

It depends heavily on where you apply, from a few months at smaller suburban PHAs to 5-10+ years in high-demand cities like New York or Los Angeles. The national average is roughly 2-3 years. Applying to several PHAs, especially smaller ones nearby, can cut your wait a lot.

Can I be on multiple Section 8 waiting lists?

Yes. There is no federal limit on how many waiting lists you can join. You can apply to as many PHAs as you want, anywhere, and you don't even need to live in a PHA's jurisdiction to apply. Nothing shortens a wait more than being on several lists.

What are preference points on housing waiting lists?

Preference points bump you higher on the waiting list based on your circumstances. Common preferences include: veteran status, elderly (62+), disabled, homeless, living in substandard housing, involuntarily displaced, or working/in school. Each PHA chooses which preferences to offer. Ask every PHA you apply to what preferences they have, since you may qualify for several.

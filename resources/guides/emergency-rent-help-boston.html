Emergency Rent Help in Boston: RAFT, Who Runs It, Free Eviction Lawyers, and the 14-Day Notice

Matt · Updated October 2026

If you rent in Boston and you are behind, the door that actually pays is the state's RAFT program (Residential Assistance for Families in Transition). It pays up to $7,000 per 12-month period, and in Boston your application is handled by Metro Housing|Boston. The City of Boston's own Rental Relief Fund is closed; the City says it "closed in 2024." For eviction help, Greater Boston Legal Services takes calls at 617-371-1234, and the City's Office of Housing Stability runs a weekly legal clinic. Massachusetts also has a rule that matters a lot: if your case is only about unpaid rent and you have a RAFT application pending, the court has to wait for a decision on it. Everything here was checked on October 21, 2026.

Here's the short version: Apply for RAFT today on the state's Housing Help Hub, then tell your landlord, because the landlord has to file their own part within 21 days. If you need help applying, go to Metro Housing|Boston at 1411 Tremont Street on a Monday from 9 a.m., or call 617-425-6700. Call the City's Office of Housing Stability at 617-635-4200. If you got court papers, call GBLS at 617-371-1234, tell the court you applied for RAFT, and go to court.

A pending RAFT application slows a nonpayment case down

Massachusetts General Laws chapter 239, section 15 covers eviction cases for nonpayment of rent. If "the tenancy is being terminated solely for non-payment of rent," "the non-payment of rent was due to a financial hardship," and you show the court "a pending application for emergency rental assistance," the court "shall" grant a continuance, and shall "not enter a judgment or issue an execution before the application for emergency rental assistance has been approved or denied" (checked October 21, 2026). RAFT counts. The state's tenant guide puts it simply: "If you have applied for RAFT, be sure to tell the court." Bring your RAFT application number and any emails about it.

Help paying rent in Boston: the doors that pay

RAFT is the big one. The others below are narrower. Funding and rules change, so call before you count on any of them.

RAFT (Residential Assistance for Families in Transition): the main door

RAFT is run by the Massachusetts Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities (EOHLC). In the state's words, "RAFT provides up to $7,000 per 12-month period so your family can stay in your current home or move to a new one. You may use the money for rent, utilities, moving costs, and mortgage payments" (checked October 21, 2026).

  • Who qualifies, in the state's words: you are "at risk of homelessness or losing your housing (for example, you received a Notice to Quit or an eviction notice; you're behind on your mortgage; you received a utility shutoff notice; or you can't stay in your home due to health, safety, or other reasons)," and "Your income is less than 50% of your city/town's Area Median Income (AMI)." The limit is 60% of AMI if you are "at risk of domestic violence."
  • Boston's income limits: the state's regional agency lookup lists these RAFT limits for Boston (Boston-Cambridge-Quincy, MA-NH HMFA):
    • 50% of AMI: 1 person $60,000; 2 people $68,600; 3 people $77,150; 4 people $85,700; 5 people $92,600; 6 people $99,450; 7 people $106,300; 8 people $113,150.
    • 60% of AMI (risk of domestic violence): 1 person $72,000; 2 people $82,320; 3 people $92,580; 4 people $102,840; 5 people $111,120; 6 people $119,340; 7 people $127,560; 8 people $135,780.
  • What to have ready: ID for the head of household; proof of your current housing, such as a lease or tenancy-at-will agreement; proof of the housing crisis, such as a Notice to Quit, an eviction notice, or a utility shutoff notice; and income information, which the state tries to verify automatically when you apply. For back rent, Metro Housing|Boston says "A notice to quit or eviction notice/court summons is now required."
  • Your landlord has a part to do. The state says your landlord "will also need to submit an application," and "If they don't submit within 21 days, your application will time out, and you'll have to reapply." Tell your landlord the day you apply.
  • How long it takes: the state says "It usually takes fewer than 30 days to get a response," and payments "usually go out within 14 business days" of approval. Metro Housing|Boston warns that "it is now taking six to eight weeks for completed applications to be processed," and that complete applications are reviewed first.
  • How to apply: online through the state's Housing Help Hub, which also lets you check your status and upload documents. The state says the application "takes 20-30 minutes to complete." You may be asked to verify your identity through ID.me. If you can't apply online, the state says to contact your regional agency: in Boston, that is Metro Housing|Boston.

Metro Housing|Boston: who runs RAFT for Boston

The state's regional agency lookup lists Metro Housing|Boston, 1411 Tremont Street, Boston, MA 02120, 617-425-6700, as Boston's RAFT agency (checked October 21, 2026). It also runs Boston's Housing Consumer Education Center (HCEC), which helps people find other financial help, legal aid, and housing search resources.

  • Walk-in hours: "Mondays only: Starting at 9:00 AM until daily capacity is reached." It warns that "walk-in time slots fill quickly" and recommends an appointment.
  • Appointments: Tuesday through Thursday, booked ahead. Book through its RAFT page (there is a Spanish version of the form), call 617-425-6700, or email Resourceline@metrohousingboston.org. Appointments cover help applying, status checks, uploading documents, and payment questions.
  • Already applied? For status questions, the state's lookup lists RAFTstatus@metrohousingboston.org. You can also log in to the Housing Help Hub and open "View Case Summary" to see which agency has your case.

HomeBASE: only for families eligible for EA shelter

HomeBASE is a bigger benefit, but it is not open to everyone who is behind on rent. The state says it "helps families who have been found eligible for Emergency Assistance (EA) Family Shelter by providing up to $30,000 over a 2-year period," and "There is no open application for HomeBASE. Eligible families will be invited to apply" (checked October 21, 2026). To be eligible for EA, a family must live in Massachusetts, meet the income rules, have children under 21 or be pregnant, and be homeless for a covered reason, such as a no-fault eviction, fleeing domestic violence, or a fire or other disaster. You apply for EA Family Shelter on the same Housing Help Hub. If you are EA-eligible and nobody has contacted you about HomeBASE, the state's line is 866-584-0653, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Metro Housing|Boston runs HomeBASE for Boston.

City of Boston Office of Housing Stability

The City's Rental Relief Fund is closed. The City's page says: "The Rental Relief Fund closed in 2024. There are limited options for renters in need available through the Office of Housing Stability. Please contact OHS at 617-635-4200 to learn about financial assistance options" (checked October 21, 2026). The City's youth rental fund for ages 16 to 24 is also finished: its page says "Status: COMPLETED" with $0 remaining.

The Office of Housing Stability is still worth a call. Its "Housing Crisis Case Coordinators can help you deal with the eviction process. They may also be able to help you get legal, financial, or other help." Call 617-635-4200 or email housingstability@boston.gov. The office is at 26 Court Street, Boston, Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.

Mass 211: the switchboard

Mass 211 says it "can help find options to pay your rent, mortgage, or utilities bills so you can stay in your home." Dial 2-1-1 or 877-211-6277; it is available 24/7. The state also lists 211 as the place for basic RAFT questions.

Eviction help in Boston: free lawyers and clinics

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can find problems in the landlord's case, ask for more time, and work out an agreement.

  • Greater Boston Legal Services (GBLS): free civil legal aid for people with low incomes in Boston and 31 nearby cities and towns, including eviction cases. Call 617-371-1234 or toll-free 800-323-3205. GBLS says its intake counselors "speak Cantonese, English, Haitian Creole, Mandarin, Portuguese, and Spanish." Its main office is at 197 Friend Street. You can check eligibility on its Do I Qualify? page. GBLS notes that "Not every eligible client will receive help."
  • Office of Housing Stability legal clinic: "Each week, the Office of Housing Stability hosts a virtual legal clinic for small landlords and tenants." Register through the City's eviction help page or call 617-635-4200.
  • Access to Counsel (families with school-age children): the City's program "aims to prevent evictions in eligible households with students enrolled in K-12 schools," with full legal representation through GBLS that can start "at the Notice to Quit or hearing notice stage." Families with a student in Boston Public Schools get in through the school's BPS School-Based Homeless Liaison. Other families should call the Office of Housing Stability.
  • Other free or low-cost help the City lists: Harvard Legal Aid Bureau, 617-495-4408; the Volunteer Lawyers Project, 617-603-1700; and the Massachusetts Bar Association Lawyer Referral Program, 617-654-0400.
  • Without a lawyer: the state says its Court Service Centers "are available to all court users without legal representation," with no income or immigration status requirements, and that Community Mediation Centers offer free mediation before court.

Massachusetts eviction timeline: what happens and when

These steps come from the state's tenant guide to eviction and the City of Boston's eviction help page:

  • 1. The notice to quit. For unpaid rent it is "A 14-day notice to quit," or 30 days "If you live in federally-assisted housing." The state says it "does not mean you have to leave." Landlords also have to include a form with a nonpayment notice that lists rental assistance programs.
  • 2. The court case. After the notice period ends, the landlord can have a constable or sheriff serve you a "Summons and Complaint." It lists your court date, where to go, and when your written answer is due.
  • 3. Your answer. You can file a written answer for free, and in some cases counterclaims, for example about repairs or your security deposit. Boston's page notes that tenants can transfer cases filed in a District Court to the Eastern Housing Court.
  • 4. Go to court. In Housing Court, the state says, "your first court appearance will be to try to resolve your case by mediation." If you applied for RAFT, tell the court (see the box above).
  • 5. After a judgment. You have 10 days to appeal. A sheriff or constable must give you written notice 2 days ahead (not counting weekends or holidays) and can only move you out Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. You can ask the court for a stay of execution: up to 6 months, or up to a year for elderly or disabled tenants.
  • Paying can still stop it. In a nonpayment case, the state says your landlord can't move you out if you pay all the rent due before the landlord uses the execution and the landlord accepts it.
  • Lockouts are illegal. "A landlord cannot lock you out or throw you out of your apartment without a judge's order."

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The Boston Housing Authority (BHA) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its site says "BHA is not currently able to accept new applications for the Tenant-Based Program and the waiting list is closed until further notice," but also that "New applicants may apply to public housing programs, the Project-Based voucher program and the Mod Rehab program" (checked October 21, 2026). Main line: (617) 988-4000. Our BHA page has the details.
  • The City's Rental Relief Fund and its youth rental fund are closed or finished. Old flyers and posts still mention them.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Boston?

Yes. The main door is the state's RAFT program, which pays up to $7,000 per 12-month period. In Boston, RAFT applications are handled by Metro Housing|Boston, 1411 Tremont Street, 617-425-6700. Apply online through the state's Housing Help Hub. The City of Boston's own Rental Relief Fund closed in 2024; the City says there are limited options through its Office of Housing Stability at 617-635-4200.

How much does RAFT pay, and who qualifies in Boston?

RAFT pays up to $7,000 per 12-month period for rent, utilities, moving costs, and mortgage payments. You must be at risk of losing your housing, for example because you got a Notice to Quit, and your income must be less than 50% of the Area Median Income, or less than 60% if you are at risk of domestic violence. For Boston, the state lists the 50% limit as $60,000 for one person, $77,150 for three, and $85,700 for four.

Can I be evicted while my RAFT application is pending?

If the case is only for nonpayment of rent and you fell behind because of a financial hardship, Massachusetts law (G.L. c. 239, section 15) says the court shall grant a continuance when you show a pending application for emergency rental assistance such as RAFT, and shall not enter a judgment or issue an execution until the application is approved or denied. Tell the court you applied, and bring proof.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Boston?

Call Greater Boston Legal Services at 617-371-1234 (toll-free 800-323-3205); its intake counselors speak Spanish, among other languages. The City's Office of Housing Stability (617-635-4200) runs a weekly virtual legal clinic and lists the Harvard Legal Aid Bureau (617-495-4408) and the Volunteer Lawyers Project (617-603-1700). Families with a child in Boston Public Schools can ask the school's homeless liaison about the City's Access to Counsel program.

How much notice does a Massachusetts landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

A 14-day notice to quit for nonpayment of rent, or 30 days if you live in federally assisted housing. The notice to quit does not mean you have to leave. The landlord can only start a court case after the notice period ends, and only a court order can force you out.

Does the Boston Housing Authority give emergency rent help?

No. BHA runs Section 8 and public housing. Its tenant-based Section 8 waiting list is closed until further notice, but new applicants may still apply to public housing, the Project-Based Voucher program, and the Mod Rehab program. If you already have BHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call BHA at (617) 988-4000 right away.

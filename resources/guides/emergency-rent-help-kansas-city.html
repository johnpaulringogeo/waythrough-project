Emergency Rent Help in Kansas City, Missouri: Free Eviction Lawyers, 211, and Missouri's Rent-and-Possession Clock

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Kansas City, Missouri and you are behind, here is the honest picture. We found no open City of Kansas City rent fund on kcmo.gov. What the City does have is a Right to Counsel program: since June 1, 2022, tenants inside Kansas City limits who have been sued for eviction can ask for a free lawyer. Rent money now comes from nonprofits with limited funds, most of them on the City's own list of assistance providers, and the way in is United Way 211. This guide covers the Missouri side of the state line, including the parts of Kansas City in Jackson, Clay, Platte and Cass counties. Everything here was checked on November 4, 2026.

Here's the short version: Dial 2-1-1 (or (816) 474-5112) and ask for rent help. If your landlord has filed in court and you live inside Kansas City limits, apply for a Right to Counsel lawyer at that same number, early in the week and well before your trial date. Mark the 15th of the month: that is when Metro Lutheran Ministry opens its rent application for one hour. And if you get a summons, go to court: in Missouri the court date can come within days, and paying all the rent and court costs can end a rent case.

Missouri moves fast: no notice period for unpaid rent

In Missouri, a landlord can file a rent-and-possession case once rent "has become due and payable, and payment has been demanded" and not made, and the one-month termination notice "is not required prior to filing" (RSMo 535.020). The summons must be served "at least four days before the court date," and the court date "shall not be more than twenty-one business days from the date the summons is issued" (RSMo 535.030). The good news: if you pay the landlord, or bring into court, "all the rent then in arrears, and all the costs" on the date of the money judgment, "further proceedings in the action shall cease and be stayed" (RSMo 535.160). Start calling for rent help and a lawyer the day you fall behind.

Free eviction lawyers in Kansas City

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can catch mistakes in the landlord's papers, raise defenses like bad repairs, negotiate a payment plan, and make sure you get every day the law allows.

Right to Counsel: Kansas City, Missouri renters with an eviction case

The City says its "Right to Counsel" program, which "officially started on June 1, 2022," exists "to prevent evictions and provide free legal assistance and support to individuals and families." The City's Housing and Community Development Department contracts with The Heartland Center for Jobs and Freedom, Legal Aid of Western Missouri, and the UMKC Fellows program to provide the lawyers (checked November 4, 2026).

  • Who qualifies, in Legal Aid's words: "If you are a tenant living within the city limits of Kansas City, Missouri (including those in Jackson, Clay, Platte, and Cass Counties), and your landlord has filed an eviction against you in court, you may be eligible." You "must live in the property from which you are being evicted." Legal Aid's page lists no income limit.
  • How to apply: call (816) 474-5112, the number the City gives "to request legal help for your eviction case," or apply online through the program's Eviction Defense application. United Way of Greater Kansas City says to "call 211 for assistance with intake for the Right to Counsel Program."
  • Apply early, early in the week: Legal Aid says "New applicants are accepted starting at 8:00 a.m. on Monday," "Only a limited number of applications are accepted each week," and "If all spots are filled, your application will be denied. You can apply the following week." Most important: "If you have a trial scheduled within four business days, your application will be rejected." So apply the day you are served, not the week of court.
  • What to know: "You do not get to choose which agency you are assigned to." The program is legal help, not a rent payment.

Legal Aid of Western Missouri and the City's Office of Tenant Advocacy

  • Legal Aid of Western Missouri serves 40 Missouri counties. If you live outside Kansas City limits (for example in Independence, Raytown, Grandview or Lee's Summit), Right to Counsel does not cover you, but you can still apply to Legal Aid online from lawmo.org or call its Kansas City Central Office at 816-474-6750, 4001 Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. Blvd., Suite 300.
  • Office of Tenant Advocacy (City of Kansas City): "For Questions about Tenant Rights and Evictions, contact the Office of Tenant Advocacy at 816-513-8434." The City's provider list calls this its Rental Housing Assistance Unit, a "Tenant/Landlord advisor" that gives information on rent and utility help and legal services for evictions. The City also publishes a free 2026 Tenant-Landlord Handbook in English and Spanish on its Tenant Resources page.

Help paying rent in Kansas City: the doors that are left

There is no single rent office to walk into. Use 211 to find who has money this month, and keep calling while you wait for a lawyer. None of these programs promises help.

United Way 211

United Way of Greater Kansas City runs 211 for "the 23 counties surrounding Kansas City," on both sides of the state line. Dial 2-1-1 or (816) 474-5112; it is "Open 24/7," with "language interpretation services available." Ask for rent and utility help in your county and write down every name and number you get. Its online directory is at 211kc.org.

Nonprofits on the City's list of assistance providers

The City of Kansas City's Assistance Providers for Tenants page lists these rent-help groups, in the City's words (checked November 4, 2026). Call before you go; funding changes month to month.

  • Metro Lutheran Ministry, (816) 931-0027: "Homeless prevention, rental assistance, deposits, utility deposit payments, arrears, application fees and financial assistance." Its own page explains how to apply now: the application is "ONLY available online," opens "Monthly on the 15th" (or the next business day), and stays open "for 1 HOUR, from 9:00am to 10:00am." "The first 30 submissions that are determined to be Eligible will be processed for assistance, or until monthly funds are exhausted," and help is limited to once every 12 months. Its Jackson County program is "Eviction Prevention for RENTER Households in KCMO + limited Independence, MO," and it says funding is "currently NOT available for: Lee's Summit, Blue Springs, Grandview, Raytown, Grain Valley, Pleasant Hill." Northland (Clay and Platte) renters use its Northland office, 816-454-5295. See mlmkc.org.
  • Guadalupe Center, (816) 421-1015: "Rapid Rehousing-Homeless prevention, rental assistance and rent arrears."
  • Greater KC Housing Information Center, (816) 931-0443: "Rapid Rehousing-Homeless Prevention, rental/utility assistance and arrears."
  • Save, Inc., (816) 531-8340, ext. 260: "Rental assistance."
  • Mattie Rhodes Center, (816) 471-2536 and reStart, Inc., (816) 472-5664: homeless prevention (reStart also does rapid rehousing).
  • Community Assistance Council, (816) 763-3277: the City lists it for "Homeless Prevention, rental/utility assistance and arrears," but its own site says "Emergency Assistance Applications Are Currently Closed" and "CAC is not accepting online Emergency Assistance applications at this time" (checked November 4, 2026). Its hours are Monday through Thursday, 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.

If you live in Kansas City, Kansas

KCK and Wyandotte County are in Kansas, with different eviction laws and courts, so most of this guide does not apply. Dial 211, which covers both sides. Metro Lutheran Ministry has a Wyandotte County office for rent, utility and mortgage help at 722 Reynolds Avenue: "Must live in Wyandotte County, KS. Subject to funding availability. Call 913-342-8333 to learn more." The Kansas City, Kansas Housing Authority said on November 3, 2026 that it "is not accepting applications for the HCV/Section 8 Program."

Utility bills: LIHEAP through MAAC

Mid America Assistance Coalition (MAAC) says it "processes all LIHEAP applications for residents in Clay, Jackson and Platte Counties in Missouri." Missouri's Energy Assistance is "a one-time payment for one fuel type for your heating or cooling costs from October-May," and Winter Crisis help (ECIP) "is available November—May based on funding." To apply, download the form from maacliheap.org and mail it to MAAC LIHEAP, 4001 Dr. MLKJ Blvd, Suite 270, Kansas City, MO 64130, email it to emailapps@maacliheap.org, or apply online through the Missouri Department of Social Services. Include your main fuel bill and any shut-off notice. MAAC says its office "is a processing center only," but LIHEAP staff are at Evergy Connect, 1710 Paseo Blvd, on Tuesdays from 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. Questions: 816-768-8900; to have a form mailed, call 855-373-4636.

Missouri eviction timeline: what happens and when

Most Kansas City evictions for unpaid rent are "rent and possession" cases under RSMo chapter 535; a case saying your lease is over is usually "unlawful detainer" under chapter 534. In Jackson County, the 16th Circuit Court "has established a Landlord/Tenant Docket" for these cases at the Jackson County Courthouse, 415 E. 12th Street. Parts of Kansas City in Clay, Platte and Cass counties go to those counties' courts. These are the steps for a rent case:

  • 1. The demand. The landlord demands the rent. No waiting period or written notice is required before filing for unpaid rent (RSMo 535.020). Ending a month-to-month tenancy for other reasons takes at least one month's written notice (RSMo 441.060).
  • 2. The summons. The court date must be no more than 21 business days after the summons is issued, unless the landlord agrees in writing to a later date, and you must be served at least 4 days before it. If the summons is only posted on your door and mailed, that has to happen at least 10 days before court (RSMo 535.030). Apply for Right to Counsel right away: Legal Aid rejects applications when trial is within four business days.
  • 3. Court. Go. If you do not show up, the judge can rule against you. Bring your lease, receipts, texts, photos of repair problems, and any letter showing the landlord refused your rent.
  • 4. Paying at the hearing. If the rent due "with all costs" is tendered before the judge at the hearing, the judge does not award possession (RSMo 535.040). If you pay all the rent and costs on the date of the money judgment, the case stops; if you pay the judgment and costs after the trial but before the judgment is final, the eviction is stayed (RSMo 535.160). This is where 211 money or a nonprofit check can save the home. It does not work if the judgment is only for possession.
  • 5. The 10 days after judgment. An order to put you out "shall be issued no sooner than ten days after the judgment" (RSMo 534.350). To stay in the home during a new trial or appeal, you must post a bond covering the rent, damages and costs within ten days after the judgment and keep paying rent into court as it comes due (RSMo 535.110). The deadline to ask for a new trial can also be 10 days (RSMo 512.190), so call a lawyer right away.
  • 6. The eviction itself. In Jackson County, the court's own FAQ says "The entire process from receipt of the Restitution to the actual eviction takes 5 – 7 working days depending on scheduling," and "The Court Officer II will clear the property and order any tenant or occupant to leave immediately." Move your belongings before that day if you can.
  • Lockouts are illegal. A landlord who removes or shuts you out "without judicial process and court order," removes the doors or locks, or cuts off electric, gas, water or sewer to force you out is "guilty of forcible entry and detainer" (RSMo 441.233). If it happens, call the police and a lawyer.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • A City of Kansas City rent fund. We found no open City rent program on kcmo.gov on November 4, 2026. The City's Tenant Resources page points renters to Right to Counsel, the Office of Tenant Advocacy, and the provider list above. Ignore old flyers and news stories about pandemic-era rent relief.
  • The Housing Authority of Kansas City (HAKC) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund, and its website does not say whether any waiting list is open or closed. Its Apply Online page says "You submit one application and will be automatically added to 19 waitlists" (checked November 4, 2026). See our HAKC guide. If you already have HAKC housing or a voucher and fall behind, call (816) 968-4100 right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • Kansas City's voucher-discrimination ban. Since August 28, 2025, Missouri law (RSMo 441.043, from House Bill 595) bars cities from enforcing rules that stop landlords from refusing tenants whose rent includes "funding from a federal or other housing assistance program." So a Kansas City landlord can legally say no to a voucher. Refusals based on race, disability, family status or another federally protected trait are still illegal; report them to HUD.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Kansas City right now?

Not from a City fund. We found no open City of Kansas City rent program on kcmo.gov on November 4, 2026. Rent help comes from nonprofits with limited money, most of them on the City's Assistance Providers for Tenants list, such as Metro Lutheran Ministry, Guadalupe Center, the Greater KC Housing Information Center and Save, Inc. Metro Lutheran Ministry opens its online application on the 15th of each month from 9 to 10 a.m. Start with United Way 211 (dial 211 or (816) 474-5112). If you have court papers, apply for a Right to Counsel lawyer the same day.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Kansas City?

If you live inside Kansas City, Missouri limits and your landlord has filed an eviction against you in court, apply to the City's Right to Counsel program by calling (816) 474-5112 or online through the program's Eviction Defense application. Legal Aid of Western Missouri says new applications open at 8 a.m. on Monday, only a limited number are accepted each week, and an application is rejected if your trial is within four business days. The lawyers come from The Heartland Center for Jobs and Freedom, Legal Aid of Western Missouri, and UMKC.

Does Right to Counsel cover Independence or the rest of Jackson County?

No. Legal Aid says the program is for tenants "living within the city limits of Kansas City, Missouri," including the parts of the city in Jackson, Clay, Platte and Cass counties. If you live in Independence, Raytown, Grandview, Lee's Summit or another city, apply to Legal Aid of Western Missouri directly online at lawmo.org or call its Central Office at 816-474-6750, and dial 211 for rent help.

How fast is an eviction in Missouri?

Fast. For unpaid rent, the landlord only has to demand the rent; no written notice period is required before filing. The court date in a rent-and-possession case can be no more than 21 business days after the summons is issued, and you must be served at least 4 days before it. If the judge rules for the landlord, the order to put you out cannot issue until 10 days after the judgment. In Jackson County, the court says the eviction itself then takes 5 to 7 working days depending on scheduling.

Can I stop a Missouri eviction by paying the rent?

In a rent-and-possession case, often yes. Missouri law says that if you pay the landlord, or bring into court, all the rent owed and all court costs on the date of the money judgment, the case stops. If you pay the judgment and costs after the trial but before the judgment is final, the eviction is stayed. Bring a money order, cashier's check, or cash for the full amount and proof you paid. A lawyer can tell you the exact amount.

Is the Kansas City Section 8 waiting list open, and can a landlord refuse my voucher?

The Housing Authority of Kansas City does not say whether any of its waiting lists is open or closed; its Apply Online page offers one voucher application that adds you to 19 waitlists. Call HAKC at (816) 968-4100 if you are unsure. And yes, a landlord can refuse a voucher: since August 28, 2025, Missouri law (RSMo 441.043) bars Kansas City from enforcing its voucher-discrimination ban. Refusals based on race, disability, family status or another federally protected trait are still illegal.

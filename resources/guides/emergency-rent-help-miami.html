Emergency Rent Help in Miami: What's Open, the Housing Advocacy Hotline, Free Eviction Lawyers, and Florida's 5-Day Rule

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Miami or anywhere in Miami-Dade County and you are behind, here is the hard news first: the big rent programs are closed or paused. The County says its Emergency Rental Assistance Program "is now closed." The City of Miami's ERA program "permanently closed" in 2023. And the County's own rent help for people facing eviction "has been temporarily put on hold until further funding becomes available." What is still open: the County's Housing Advocacy Hotline at 786-469-4545, the Homeless Helpline for prevention at 1-877-994-4357, 211 Miami around the clock, light bill help, and free eviction lawyers at Legal Services of Greater Miami, (305) 576-0080. If you already have court papers, you have only 5 business days to act. Everything here was checked on November 3, 2026.

Here's the short version: Call the Housing Advocacy Hotline at 786-469-4545 and ask what rent help is open today. Call the Homeless Helpline at 1-877-994-4357, choose your language, and press 1 for prevention. Dial 2-1-1. If you were served with an eviction summons, count 5 days (not weekends or legal holidays), file your answer, pay the rent into the court registry, and call Legal Services of Greater Miami the same day.

Florida moves fast: the 3-day notice and the 5-day answer

Under Florida Statutes section 83.56(3), a landlord can end your lease if rent is still unpaid 3 days after a written demand, "excluding Saturday, Sunday, and legal holidays." Once you are served with an eviction summons, you have 5 days (again not counting weekends and legal holidays) to file a written answer. If you raise any defense other than "I paid," section 83.60(2) says you must also pay the rent owed into the court registry, or file a motion asking the court to decide the amount, in those same 5 days. Missing that step "constitutes an absolute waiver of the tenant's defenses other than payment," and the landlord can get a default judgment. In Miami-Dade, the Clerk says registry payments are by wire transfer only: "ONLY WIRE TRANSFERS will be accepted. ACH payments will be rejected." Do not wait on a rent application to protect your court case.

Emergency rental assistance in Miami: what is open and what is closed

Miami-Dade has very little rent money right now. These are the doors that still answer. Call more than one.

Miami-Dade Housing Advocacy Hotline: the first call

The County's rental assistance page, now marked closed, sends people to this line: "Residents in need of housing assistance can contact the Miami-Dade County Office of Housing Advocacy through the Housing Advocacy Hotline at 786-469-4545 or by emailing housingadvocacy@miamidade.gov" (checked November 3, 2026). Call 786-469-4545 or email housingadvocacy@miamidade.gov. It is not a rent fund. Ask which programs have money this month, where to apply, and what to bring. The office's old web address now forwards to the County's Community Services Department home page.

The County's rent help for people facing eviction: on hold

Miami-Dade's Community Services Department runs a program for people who are homeless or facing eviction. In the County's words, "you can receive help with your first month's rent payment, security deposit, mortgage, utility bills, emergency shelter or food." You can get "one type of assistance per year," and "The maximum amount you can receive is $1,000 for rent or $1,500 in mortgage assistance." But the same page says: "Assistance has been temporarily put on hold until further funding becomes available" (checked November 3, 2026). Ask the Housing Advocacy Hotline or a County Community Resource Center if it has reopened before you count on it. The Community Services Department's main line is 786-469-4600.

Homeless Helpline and Camillus House: prevention

  • Miami-Dade Homeless Trust: for homeless prevention, the Trust says to "Call 1-877-994-4357, select your language of choice and press #1 for more information." It adds that prevention information is also available "by calling or visiting any of Miami-Dade's Community Resource Centers." The same number is the Homeless Helpline if you have already lost your home.
  • Camillus House Homeless Prevention Program: Camillus House says its program "provides rental support for families in danger of being evicted," and that it also helps with "basic bills such as utilities." It says it "operates the Homeless Prevention Hotline for all Miami-Dade County." Its page does not list a separate number, so start with the Homeless Helpline above.

211 Miami: the switchboard

211 Miami calls itself a "24/7 Helpline connecting people in need to community resources." Dial 2-1-1, call the alternative number (305) 631-4211, or text your ZIP code to 898211. Ask for rent help, eviction prevention, and utility help, and write down every name and number you get.

Closed: the County's ERAP and the City of Miami's ERA program

The County's Emergency Rental Assistance Program (ERAP 2.4) page says: "The Emergency Rental Assistance Program (ERAP) is now closed." The City of Miami's page says: "As of July 13, 2023 the City's ERA program permanently closed due to the exhaustion of programmatic funds." Old flyers, posts, and application links for both are out of date.

Utility help in Miami-Dade: LIHEAP and FPL Care to Share

The County's Community Services Department runs light bill help through its Community Resource Centers. Call 786-469-4640 or register online through the County's Light Bill Assistance page.

  • LIHEAP regular help: "one benefit payment once a year for eligible households." You do not need a crisis for this kind of help, and "The maximum amount of assistance is $1,350." The County says that if you are approved, a payment credit is sent to your utility "within 45 days."
  • If your power is off or about to be: "If your electricity has been disconnected or face disconnection you must visit a Community Resource Center. Appointments are encouraged but walk-ins are welcomed."
  • FPL Care to Share: for FPL customers in a crisis who are "in imminent danger of having their services disconnected." It is "one-time assistance within a 12 month period, up to $750," and "Assistance is based on funding availability." You are screened at a Community Resource Center.
  • What to bring: a Florida driver's license or photo ID for every adult, proof of income for the last 30 days for everyone in the home, your electric bill with a final or past-due notice, and, if you have Section 8 or public housing, the form 50059 that shows your utility allowance. You must live in Miami-Dade and be a U.S. citizen or have legal permanent resident status.
  • Age 60 or older: the County also has an energy program for low-income households "with at least one person aged 60 and older, during a home energy emergency."

Paying the light bill can free up cash for rent. Our utility assistance guide covers other programs.

Eviction help in Miami: Legal Services of Greater Miami

If you searched for eviction help in Miami, this is the answer with a name. Legal Services of Greater Miami "provides free civil legal services for the low-income communities of Miami-Dade and Monroe Counties." It does not pay rent, but a lawyer can check the landlord's notice, help you answer the summons, and work out a deal. The City of Miami's own page tells tenants: "If you are presently in eviction proceedings in court, you can call 305-576-0080 to apply for free legal help."

  • By phone: (305) 576-0080, Monday to Thursday, 2 to 4 p.m. Text telephone: (305) 446-7570.
  • In person: 4343 West Flagler Street, Suite 100, Miami, Monday and Wednesday, 9 a.m. to noon.
  • Online: apply through the online application on its contact page. Its site has a Spanish page ("Ayuda en Español") and a Haitian Creole page.
  • Time matters: the phone line is open only a few hours a week, and your answer is due in 5 business days. Do not let the deadline pass while you wait for a call back.

Florida eviction timeline: what happens and when

Florida's eviction rules are in Chapter 83 of the Florida Statutes. These are the steps for a rent case, in order:

  • 1. The 3-day notice. The landlord delivers a written demand for rent or possession. You have 3 days to pay, "excluding Saturday, Sunday, and legal holidays" (section 83.56(3)). The statute says these are "court-observed holidays only." A 3-day notice is not an eviction.
  • 2. The eviction summons. If you do not pay, the landlord can file in county court. Miami-Dade's residential eviction summons says you must act "within 5 days (not including Saturdays, Sundays, or legal holidays) after the date these papers were given to you or to a person who lives with you or were posted at your home."
  • 3. Your 5 days. The summons lists what to do: write down why you should not be forced to move (you may use Florida Supreme Court Form 1.947(b), Answer, Residential Eviction) and give it to the clerk at a Miami-Dade County court location; mail or take a copy to the landlord; and pay the clerk the rent that is due, and keep paying it each time it comes due until the case is over. If you disagree with the amount, file a written motion asking the judge to decide it, together with your answer. If you get Section 8 or other rent help, you deposit only your share of the rent (section 83.60(2)).
  • The court locations on the summons: the Osvaldo N. Soto Justice Center, Central Court (20 NW 1st Ave, Room 6.240); North Dade Justice Center (15555 Biscayne Blvd., Room 100); Hialeah District Court (11 East 6th Street, Room 100); Coral Gables District Court (3100 Ponce De Leon Blvd., Room 100); and South Dade Justice Center (10710 SW 211th Street, Room 1200). The Clerk says cases must be filed in the district where the property is located, so check your summons.
  • Paying the registry in Miami-Dade: the Clerk accepts registry payments by wire transfer only, with a required registry fee, and says "Your payment will be applied within 2 business days of receipt of funds." Use the Clerk's online form to get the wire details and the total.
  • 4. Judgment and the 24-hour notice. If the landlord wins, the Clerk issues a writ of possession. The sheriff posts it and can return after 24 hours, and "Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays do not stay the 24-hour notice period" (section 83.62(1)).
  • Lockouts are illegal. A landlord may not shut off your utilities or change the locks to force you out (section 83.67(1) and (2)). A landlord who does can owe you your "actual and consequential damages or 3 months' rent, whichever is greater," plus court costs and attorney's fees (section 83.67(6)).

Florida law now controls most local tenant rules

Section 83.425, added in 2023, says "The regulation of residential tenancies, the landlord-tenant relationship, and all other matters covered under this part are preempted to the state." It "supersedes any local government regulations" on those matters, including "security deposits," "fees charged by the landlord," and "notice requirements." The same 2023 law set the notice to end a month-to-month tenancy at "not less than 30 days" (section 83.57(3)). What is not a door for emergency rent

  • Miami-Dade Housing and Community Development (HCD), which some County pages still call Public Housing and Community Development, runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its Section 8 page says "The waiting list is closed for registration" (checked November 3, 2026). The Applicant Leasing Center is at 786-469-4206. If you already have a voucher or live in public housing and fall behind, call HCD at 786-469-4100 right away.
  • The County's ERAP and the City of Miami's ERA program are closed.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and call the Homeless Helpline at 1-877-994-4357.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Miami right now?

Very little. Miami-Dade's Emergency Rental Assistance Program is closed, the City of Miami's ERA program permanently closed on July 13, 2023, and the County's rent help for people facing eviction (up to $1,000 for rent) is on hold until more funding comes in. Call the County's Housing Advocacy Hotline at 786-469-4545, call the Homeless Helpline at 1-877-994-4357 and press 1 for prevention, and dial 2-1-1, which 211 Miami answers 24/7.

What is the Miami-Dade Housing Advocacy Hotline?

It is the County's line for residents who need housing help. The County's rental assistance page says residents can contact the Office of Housing Advocacy through the Housing Advocacy Hotline at 786-469-4545 or by emailing housingadvocacy@miamidade.gov. It does not promise money. Ask which programs are open, where to apply, and what to bring.

What should I do if I got eviction papers in Miami-Dade?

Count 5 days, not including Saturdays, Sundays, or legal holidays. In that time, give the clerk a written answer at the court location on your summons, send a copy to the landlord, and pay the rent that is due into the court registry, or file a motion asking the judge to decide the amount. The Miami-Dade Clerk takes registry payments only by wire transfer. Call Legal Services of Greater Miami at (305) 576-0080, Monday to Thursday, 2 to 4 p.m.

How much notice does a Florida landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

3 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, and court-observed holidays (Florida Statutes section 83.56(3)). The notice does not mean you have to leave. If you do not pay, the landlord has to file in county court, and only the sheriff can remove you after the court issues a writ, with 24 hours' notice.

Did Florida end Miami-Dade's tenant protections?

Florida law took over most of them. Section 83.425, added in 2023, says the regulation of residential tenancies and the landlord-tenant relationship is preempted to the state, and it supersedes local government rules on matters such as security deposits, landlord fees, and notice requirements. For those matters, the state rules in Chapter 83 apply in Miami.

Is the Miami-Dade Section 8 waiting list open?

No. Miami-Dade Housing and Community Development says the waiting list is closed for registration. HCD is not an emergency rent fund. If you already have a voucher or live in public housing and fall behind, call HCD at 786-469-4100 right away. The Applicant Leasing Center is at 786-469-4206.

Emergency Rent Help in Fresno: Free Eviction Lawyers, CalWORKs Back Rent, 211, and California's 3-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Fresno and you are behind, here is the honest picture. The City of Fresno's rent fund is gone: the City says its Emergency Rental Assistance Program "has distributed all available funding" and "the program is now closed." We found no open City or County rent fund that anyone can apply to. What Fresno does have is a free lawyer program: the City's Eviction Protection Program helps City of Fresno renters facing an unlawful eviction, whatever their income or immigration status. Families on CalWORKs can ask the County for up to two months of back rent. And 211 and a few charities still help when they have money. Everything here was checked on November 5, 2026.

Here's the short version: Dial 2-1-1 (or 1-866-559-4211) and ask who has rent money this month. If you have children and get CalWORKs, or might qualify, call Fresno County's Housing Assistance line at (559) 600-5315. If you live in the City of Fresno and got a 3-day notice or court papers, call the Eviction Protection Program at (559) 621-8400 and ask to be screened. Anywhere in the county, call Central California Legal Services at (559) 570-1200. And if you are sued, file a response within 10 court days, or the landlord can win without a hearing.

California's two clocks: the 3-day notice and the 10-court-day answer

For unpaid rent, a California landlord must first give a written notice to pay the rent or move out. The law gives you "three days' notice, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays" (Code of Civil Procedure 1161). If the landlord then sues, the case is called an unlawful detainer, and your response "shall be filed within 10 days, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays, after the complaint is served" (Code of Civil Procedure 1167, as amended by AB 2347, effective January 1, 2025). Central California Legal Services puts it plainly: "A Notice IS NOT an eviction." Only a court can order you out.

Help paying rent in Fresno: the doors still open

None of these is a big, open-to-all rent fund, and none promises help. Try every one that fits you, and keep your lease, your notice, and proof of income with you when you call.

Fresno County CalWORKs Homeless Assistance: back rent for families

The Fresno County Department of Social Services (DSS) says "The CalWORKs Homeless Assistance (HA) Program aims to help CalWORKs families meet the reasonable costs of securing housing" (checked November 5, 2026).

  • Who qualifies: it serves "eligible CalWORKs recipients, or apparently eligible CalWORKs applicants, who are homeless or at risk of homelessness." CalWORKs is California's cash aid for families with children, so this is for households with kids.
  • What it can pay: "up to two months of rent arrearages" to stop an eviction, "a security deposit and last month's rent" for a new place, or "payments for temporary shelter for up to 16 cumulative calendar days." The County says you can get it "Every 12 months, but there are exceptions."
  • How to apply: call the Housing Assistance Information Line at (559) 600-5315 or email DSSHousing@fresnocountyca.gov. If you do not have CalWORKs yet, apply at BenefitsCal.com or call DSS at (559) 600-1377. Bring your pay-or-quit notice.
  • CalWORKs Housing Support Program: for families with active CalWORKs who are homeless or at risk, the County's brochure lists "rental assistance, housing navigation, case management, security deposits, move-in costs, utility payments" and legal services. Ask about it at the same number.

211 Fresno and Madera Counties

United Way Fresno & Madera Counties runs 211. Dial 2-1-1 or call toll-free 1-866-559-4211. United Way says "Our operators are available around the clock, 365 days a year," with help "in multiple languages." Ask for rent help and for the Fresno Madera Continuum of Care if you could lose your home. Write down every name and number you get.

Catholic Charities Diocese of Fresno

Catholic Charities offers rent and mortgage help, but it says "all assistance is assessed in person on a case-by-case basis when funding is available," and "meeting the eligibility guidelines above does not guarantee funding." It also says "Section 8 housing is not eligible." Go in person to 149 N Fulton Street, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 3:45 p.m. (closed 12:15 to 1 p.m. for lunch), or call (559) 237-0851. Bring your rental agreement, a rent ledger for the last six months, a photo ID, proof of income from the last 30 days, a short letter about your hardship, your whole PG&E bill, and any eviction notice.

Free eviction help in Fresno

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can find mistakes in a notice or lawsuit, file your answer on time, and work out a payment plan or more time.

City of Fresno Eviction Protection Program (EPP)

The City says "The Eviction Protection Program (EPP) defends tenants renting an apartment or house in the City of Fresno." It is run by the City Attorney's Office (checked November 5, 2026).

  • Who qualifies, in the City's words: "This program is open to any City of Fresno resident who is facing an unlawful eviction," "regardless of financial or documented status." The City first checks whether you may be facing an unlawful eviction. If you qualify, you may get a City-appointed attorney or legal services at no cost. The page lists no income limit.
  • How to apply: fill out the online questionnaire at fresno.gov/epp, call (559) 621-8400 and ask to be screened, or mail or drop off the questionnaire at the City Attorney's Office, Attn: EPP, 2600 Fresno Street, Room 3076, Fresno, CA 93721. Flyers and forms come in English, Spanish, Hmong and Punjabi.
  • Only for the City of Fresno. If you live in Clovis, Sanger, Selma or another part of the county, call Central California Legal Services instead.

Central California Legal Services (CCLS)

  • What it does: free civil legal help, including evictions, for people with low income. CCLS says to act fast: "As soon as you receive a Notice of ANY kind, get legal advice."
  • Who qualifies: CCLS says you may qualify if "your income is less than 125% or 200% of the Federal Poverty Level," depending on the case. Seniors in Fresno County may qualify whatever their income.
  • How to apply: call (559) 570-1200 or toll-free (800) 675-8001. Intake is Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to noon and 1 to 2:30 p.m., except holidays and staff training days. You can also fill out the online application at centralcallegal.org. Office: 2115 Kern Street, Suite 200, Fresno.

Fresno Superior Court Self-Help Center and eviction clinic

  • Self-Help Center: 1130 O Street, first floor, Fresno. Walk in Monday from 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. or Tuesday and Wednesday from 9 a.m. to 1 p.m.; phone and email help Thursday and Friday, 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. Phone: 559-457-2143. Staff can help with forms, including the answer to an eviction and a fee waiver.
  • Unlawful detainer clinic: CCLS and the court run free clinics for "Low-income litigants (125% poverty level or below) representing themselves in unlawful detainer cases." Tenant clinics are Tuesdays 1:30 to 3:30 p.m. and Thursdays 9:30 a.m. to noon in the Self-Help Center at the B.F. Sisk Courthouse; follow-up and after-judgment help is Thursdays 1:30 to 3:30 p.m. More information: (559) 570-1200.

Utility help in Fresno: LIHEAP and PG&E discounts

Lowering your power and gas bill frees up money for rent.

  • LIHEAP through Fresno EOC: Fresno Economic Opportunities Commission's Energy Services says "LIHEAP offers one-time financial assistance for energy bills in Fresno County per calendar year." Apply online at caliheapapply.com; Fresno EOC says it "is no longer accepting paper applications for LIHEAP." Applications are handled "on a first-come, first-served basis." Questions: (559) 263-1320, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to noon and 1 to 5 p.m.
  • PG&E CARE: PG&E says CARE "provides a monthly discount of 20% or more on gas and 35% or more on electricity." A household of 1 or 2 qualifies with total yearly income of $43,280 or less, or 4 people with $66,000 or less (limits valid through May 31, 2027). PG&E says "No additional documentation is needed at the time of application."
  • PG&E FERA: if your income is a little too high for CARE, FERA gives "an 18 percent monthly discount on electric rates." It does not lower gas. FERA limits are $43,281 to $54,100 a year for 1 or 2 people and $66,001 to $82,500 for 4. Apply for both online at energyinsight.pge.com/carefera or by calling 1-866-743-2273. If you do not qualify for CARE, PG&E checks you for FERA.

California eviction timeline in Fresno: what happens and when

These are the steps for an eviction over unpaid rent in Fresno County, in order:

  • 1. The 3-day notice. The notice must state the amount due and who to pay. You get three days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or court holidays (Code of Civil Procedure 1161). Now is the time to call 211, the County if you have CalWORKs, and a lawyer. Paying the full amount the notice demands within that time is the cleanest fix; get a receipt.
  • 2. The lawsuit. If you do not pay or move, the landlord can file an unlawful detainer in Fresno County Superior Court. A notice alone does not end your tenancy.
  • 3. Answer within 10 court days. You have 10 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or court holidays, after you are served to file your response (Code of Civil Procedure 1167). If you miss it, the landlord can ask for a default judgment and win without a hearing. If you cannot afford the filing fee, ask for a fee waiver. The Self-Help Center and the court's eviction clinic can help.
  • 4. Trial and judgment. If you file a response, the court sets a trial. A settlement can include a payment plan or a move-out date you can live with.
  • 5. Just cause and rent limits. Fresno has no local rent control, so state law sets the rules. Under California's Tenant Protection Act, once you have "continuously and lawfully occupied" the home for 12 months, a covered landlord needs a just cause, stated in the notice, to end your tenancy (Civil Code 1946.2), and yearly rent increases are capped at "5 percent plus the percentage change in the cost of living, or 10 percent, whichever is lower" (Civil Code 1947.12). Some homes are exempt, such as many single-family homes and buildings built in the last 15 years. Not paying rent is a just cause, so these rules matter most when the notice is for something else.
  • Lockouts are illegal. A landlord cannot change the locks, remove doors or windows, or shut off your utilities to force you out (Civil Code 789.3). Only the sheriff can remove you, after the court case.

The California Courts' eviction help for tenants explains each step and the forms. Our step-by-step guide to avoiding eviction covers the whole process, and understanding landlord notices explains the paper you got.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The City of Fresno's Emergency Rental Assistance Program is closed. The City says it "has distributed all available funding," "the program is now closed," and "We are not accepting new applications" (checked November 5, 2026).
  • California's COVID-19 Rent Relief is over. The state housing department says its "application portal closed to new applications after March 31," 2022. Ignore old links and flyers.
  • Fresno Housing runs Section 8 and public housing for the City and County. It is not an emergency rent fund, and its application page says the Housing Choice Voucher "Interest list is currently closed." On November 5, 2026, eight of its properties were "Accepting Applications": Blossom Trail Commons (Sanger), Citrus Commons (Orange Cove), Corazón del Valle Commons (Huron), Cueva de Oso at William Shockley Plaza (Selma), Manzanilla Commons (Fresno), Pacific Gardens (Fresno), Paseo 55 (Reedley) and Promesa Commons (Fresno). See our Fresno Housing guide. If you already have a voucher or public housing and your income dropped, call (559) 443-8400 right away and ask about a rent change.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Fresno right now?

Not from a general City or County fund. The City of Fresno says its Emergency Rental Assistance Program has distributed all of its funding and is closed. What is left: families on CalWORKs can ask Fresno County for up to two months of back rent at (559) 600-5315; Catholic Charities Diocese of Fresno helps in person when it has funding; and 211 (or 1-866-559-4211) can tell you who has money this month.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Fresno?

If you live in the City of Fresno and face an unlawful eviction, apply to the City's Eviction Protection Program online at fresno.gov/epp or call (559) 621-8400 and ask to be screened. The City says it is open to any City resident regardless of financial or documented status. Anywhere in Fresno County, call Central California Legal Services at (559) 570-1200, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to noon and 1 to 2:30 p.m.

Can Fresno County pay my back rent if I have children?

Maybe. If your family gets CalWORKs, or looks eligible for it, the County's CalWORKs Homeless Assistance can pay up to two months of back rent to prevent an eviction, usually once every 12 months. Call the Housing Assistance Information Line at (559) 600-5315 or email DSSHousing@fresnocountyca.gov, and have your 3-day notice ready.

How long do I have to respond to an eviction in California?

Two clocks. A 3-day notice to pay rent gives you three days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays or court holidays. If the landlord then sues, you have 10 days, again not counting weekends or court holidays, to file a response after you are served. If you miss it, the landlord can win without a hearing. The Fresno Superior Court Self-Help Center at 1130 O Street can help with the forms.

Can my Fresno landlord evict me without a reason?

Often not. Fresno has no local rent control, but California's Tenant Protection Act says that once you have lived in a covered home for 12 months, the landlord needs a just cause, stated in the notice, to end your tenancy. Unpaid rent is a just cause. Some homes are exempt, such as many single-family homes and newer buildings. If you think an eviction is unlawful, call the City's Eviction Protection Program or Central California Legal Services.

Is the Fresno Section 8 waiting list open?

No. Fresno Housing's application page says the Housing Choice Voucher interest list is currently closed. On November 5, 2026, eight of its properties were accepting applications, including Manzanilla Commons, Pacific Gardens and Promesa Commons in Fresno. Fresno Housing is not an emergency rent fund. Call (559) 443-8400 with questions.

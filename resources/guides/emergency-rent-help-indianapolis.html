Emergency Rent Help in Indianapolis: Township Trustees, Free Eviction Help, and the 10-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Indianapolis and you are behind, the public door that pays rent is your township trustee. Each township in Marion County runs its own township assistance office, and you apply to the one where you live; Center Township, the center of Marion County that includes downtown, is at 317-633-3610. The IndyRent rental assistance program is the name many people remember, but we could not confirm that it is taking applications. For eviction help, the City's Tenant Information Hotline is 317-327-2228, and Indiana Legal Services takes applications at 1-844-243-8570. Indiana law gives you 10 days after a nonpayment notice to pay in full. Everything here was checked on November 3, 2026.

Here's the short version: Find your township with the City's address lookup and call that trustee today, before an eviction is filed. If an electric or gas bill is part of the problem, apply to IndyEAP. If you got eviction papers, call the Tenant Information Hotline at 317-327-2228, apply to Indiana Legal Services, and go to every court hearing.

Apply before the eviction is filed, not after

Township help has rules that get harder once a case moves forward. Indiana Legal Services warns that trustee "Rental assistance may not be available if a resident is already behind in rent or an eviction has been filed." Center Township's 2026 standards say it "will not pay late charges, rent accrued prior to the first day of the month before the month of application, judgments for back rent, or attorney fees," and that it may refuse help when "A judgment for eviction or back rent has been taken against the applicant." So the best day to apply is the day you know you will be short, not the week of your court date (checked November 3, 2026).

Emergency rental assistance in Indianapolis: the doors that pay

Start with your township trustee, then work down the list. Each runs on its own money, and none of them guarantees help.

Township trustee assistance (all of Marion County)

Indiana Legal Services puts it simply: "Each Township Trustee has special funding for residents in emergency circumstances." You must apply in the township where you live. Center Township says: "If your address is outside of Center Township, you will have to apply for assistance in your home township." Use the City's My Government Jurisdictions lookup to find yours.

Every township sets its own written standards. Here is how Center Township's 2026 standards work, as an example of what to expect:

  • Income limit: no help when countable income, assets, and resources "equal or exceed one hundred thirty eight percent (138%) of the Federal Poverty Income Guidelines," except in an emergency.
  • What it pays: "Rental and Mortgage Assistance" is on its list of services, along with utilities, food, and bus passes. It pays rent "for only one month at a time," up to HUD's Fair Market Rent for the unit size, and "No rent assistance will be paid until the landlord signs and returns the landlord agreement."
  • What it won't pay: late charges, rent from before the first day of the month before you apply, judgments for back rent, or attorney fees.
  • What to bring: proof of household income for the past 60 days; paid bill receipts for the past 60 days; your lease; a valid, unexpired photo ID for every adult; Social Security cards or proof of application for everyone in the home; tax returns and W-2s; and a WorkOne printout. Once documents are requested, you may have "up to 72 hours" to provide them.
  • How to apply: "Individuals in need of assistance should walk in during our business hours or call us at 317.633.3610." Julia Carson Government Center, 300 E. Fall Creek Parkway N. Drive. Hours: Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m.; Tuesday, 8 a.m. to 7 p.m.; and the last Saturday of the month, 8 to 11 a.m. by appointment.
  • If you are denied: you can file a written appeal with the Marion County Board of Commissioners within 15 days of the denial.

IndyRent: status not confirmed

IndyRent is the rental assistance program many Indianapolis renters remember. On November 3, 2026, IndyRent's own website blocked our check, so we could not read its status. The City's current eviction prevention page does not mention it, and John Boner Neighborhood Centers' rental assistance page now points renters to the City's eviction program and the state housing agency instead. The Coalition for Homelessness Intervention and Prevention (CHIP) still lists IndyRent's contact: indyrent@jbncenters.org, 317.912.1260. Call before you count on it, and apply to your township trustee either way.

St. Vincent de Paul Indianapolis

St. Vincent de Paul says that along with household goods, "Other assistance, such as car repairs or help with utilities/rent, may be available," through "a Home Visit by a local Conference (parish)." Start with its Helpline: 317-687-0169 in English or 317-687-0167 in Spanish, or the online request form on its Get Help page.

Damien Center (only for its patients)

The Damien Center's Direct Emergency Financial Assistance "provides short-term assistance with rent, utilities, incidentals, or other one-time emergencies." It is part of the Damien Center's HIV care services, so it is for its patients, not the general public. If you get care there, ask your care team.

Indiana 211: the switchboard

Indiana 211 is run by the state's Family and Social Services Administration. Dial 2-1-1 or 866-211-9966, "available 24/7," or text your ZIP code to 898-211, Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. You can also search the Indiana 211 database. Indiana Legal Services says local community centers "often have limited funds available for individuals with an emergency financial need," and that 211 can help you find yours.

Help with utility bills in Indianapolis: IndyEAP

The Energy Assistance Program for Marion County is IndyEAP, run by John Boner Neighborhood Centers. It "provides financial assistance to income-eligible households in Marion County to maintain utility services during the winter." It helps with electricity and heating, but "does not assist with water bills."

  • The application window: IndyEAP opened October 1, 2026, and "the last day to submit an application is 5 p.m. on April 30, 2027." After April 30, 2027, this season's window is closed, so check indyeap.org for the next one.
  • Shutoff protection: IndyEAP lists December 1, 2026, as the start of the moratorium protection period for households that have applied, and March 15, 2027, as the day it ends.
  • Income limits (total household income for the last three months): 1 person $8,658; 2 people $11,323; 3 people $13,987; 4 people $16,651; 5 people $19,315; 6 people $21,980.
  • What to send: a signed application, recent proof of income for everyone 18 and older, and a utility bill. IndyEAP says you do not need to send a photo ID, Social Security card, lease, or birth certificate.
  • Crisis help: "The additional benefit for households in crisis (disconnect) is up to $400 per utility."
  • How to apply: online is fastest. Paper applications are at Door #2 of John Boner Neighborhood Centers, 2236 E. 10th Street. Processing can take up to 55 days, so keep paying what you can on your bill. Questions: 317-808-2378 or indyeap@jbncenters.org.

Eviction help in Indianapolis: free lawyers and the Tenant Information Hotline

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer or tenant navigator can explain your options, help you negotiate, and spot defenses.

  • The City's Eviction Diversion Program: it connects tenants "facing evictions or at risk of eviction" to free legal help and other services, through the City's Office of Public Health and Safety, the Lawrence Township Small Claims Court, the Indiana Bar Foundation, Indiana Legal Services, and the Indianapolis Legal Aid Society. The City says: "If you believe that your landlord is going to file an eviction against you or if you have received an eviction notice, reach out to the Tenant Information Hotline at 317-327-2228 as soon as possible and be sure to attend your eviction hearing." The City's housing office is at 317.327.4291, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.
  • Tenant Advocacy Project (TAP) at the courthouse: the Neighborhood Christian Legal Clinic says the City's TAP "provides attorneys and tenant navigators in eight township small claims courts." The Clinic takes free walk-ins, first come, first served, at the Washington, Decatur, and Perry Township small claims courts. Washington Township hours: Mondays 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m., Tuesdays 8:30 to 11:30 a.m., and Wednesdays 8:30 a.m. to 3 p.m. The Clinic's office: (317) 429-4131, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. on weekdays, 3333 N. Meridian Street, Suite 201. It says it "does not provide financial assistance for housing matters."
  • Indiana Legal Services (ILS): free civil legal help for people with low incomes across Indiana. Apply by phone at 1-844-243-8570, Monday through Thursday, 10 a.m. to 2 p.m., or apply online at its Get Help page. ILS says you "should hear from us within about 14 days," so if your hearing is sooner, also call the Tenant Information Hotline.
  • Indianapolis Legal Aid Society: serves people "with incomes of less than 125% of the federal poverty guideline levels," with a focus on children, families, people with disabilities, and the elderly. It "specializes in helping senior and disabled adults remain in their homes when Health Code violations become an issue." Call (317) 635-9538, 615 N. Alabama Street, Suite 122.

Indiana eviction timeline in Indianapolis: what happens and when

  • 1. The 10-day notice. Indiana Code 32-31-1-6 says: "If a tenant refuses or neglects to pay rent when due, a landlord may terminate the lease with not less than ten (10) days notice to the tenant unless: (1) the parties otherwise agreed; or (2) the tenant pays the rent in full before the notice period expires." Paying in full before the 10 days run out ends that notice. Read your lease too, because it can change the rules.
  • 2. The court case. Evictions in Marion County are heard in the township small claims courts (Center, Decatur, Franklin, Lawrence, Perry, Pike, Warren, Washington, and Wayne) or in Superior Court. A notice from your landlord is not a court order. ILS says: "Only a judge can order you to move out."
  • 3. Go to every hearing. ILS warns: "If you do not go to court, you will be evicted. The court could also order you to pay up to $10,000 to your landlord." You can ask the judge to hear your side, make a written "pay and stay" agreement with your landlord, or agree to move. "You do not have to agree to anything or sign anything you do not understand." The first hearing usually decides whether you can stay; a later one may decide how much you owe.
  • 4. Time to move. If you lose, ILS says "Some judges give 7 days. Others may give 2–3 weeks." If you are not out by the move-out date, the sheriff may remove you.
  • Lockouts and shutoffs are illegal. Indiana Code 32-31-5-6 says that "Except as authorized by judicial order," a landlord may not change the locks or shut off electricity, gas, water, or other essential services to force you out, unless the home has been abandoned.
  • Retaliation is illegal. Since 2020, Indiana Code 32-31-8.5 bars a landlord from filing or threatening an eviction because you complained to a housing code agency, complained in writing about repairs or a lockout, joined a tenant group, or testified against the landlord. It does not protect you if you are behind on rent and did not pay within the notice period.
  • Your record can be sealed. Under Indiana Code 32-31-11-3, if the case is dismissed or you win, the court must seal the records on its own. If you lose and later pay the money judgment, you can ask the court to seal them.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The Indianapolis Housing Agency (IHA) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its voucher FAQ says: "The Indianapolis Housing Agency's Section 8 waiting list is closed. We are now processing the randomly selected applications from our 2016 Lottery opening, which concluded on September 15, 2016" (checked November 3, 2026). HUD took possession of IHA with the City in April 2024, and HUD says it "retains ultimate authority over IHA's recovery"; IHA's board is listed as HUD appointed. Main line: 317.261.7200. Our IHA page has the details.
  • The state's Indiana Housing and Community Development Authority lists energy help, foreclosure help, and vouchers for homeowners and renters, but no rent fund for tenants who are behind.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Indianapolis?

Yes, mainly through your township trustee. Every township in Marion County has a trustee office with emergency funds for its own residents, and rent help is one of the services. You must apply in the township where you live; Center Township's number is 317-633-3610. St. Vincent de Paul may also help with rent through a parish home visit (317-687-0169). We could not confirm that the IndyRent rental assistance program is taking applications, so call before you count on it.

How does township trustee rent help work in Indianapolis?

Each township sets its own standards. In Center Township's 2026 standards, help is for households under 138% of the federal poverty guidelines, rent is paid one month at a time up to HUD's Fair Market Rent, and the landlord must sign an agreement before any payment. Center Township will not pay late fees, attorney fees, judgments for back rent, or rent from before the month before you apply. Bring 60 days of income proof and paid bills, your lease, and IDs. Apply early, because help may not be available once an eviction has been filed.

Is IndyRent still open?

We could not confirm it. On November 3, 2026, IndyRent's website blocked our check, the City's current eviction prevention page does not mention it, and John Boner Neighborhood Centers' rental assistance page now points renters to the City's eviction program instead. CHIP still lists IndyRent's contact at 317.912.1260 and indyrent@jbncenters.org. Call to ask, and apply to your township trustee at the same time.

How do I get free eviction help in Indianapolis?

Call the City's Tenant Information Hotline at 317-327-2228 as soon as you think your landlord will file, or as soon as you get an eviction notice. Apply to Indiana Legal Services at 1-844-243-8570 (Monday to Thursday, 10 a.m. to 2 p.m.) or online; ILS says to expect an answer in about 14 days. The Neighborhood Christian Legal Clinic takes walk-ins at the Washington, Decatur, and Perry Township small claims courts and answers at (317) 429-4131. And go to every court hearing.

How much notice does an Indiana landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

Indiana Code 32-31-1-6 says a landlord may end the lease for unpaid rent with not less than 10 days notice, unless the parties agreed otherwise or the tenant pays the rent in full before the notice period ends. After that, the landlord has to file in court; in Marion County, eviction cases go to the township small claims courts or Superior Court. A lockout or utility shutoff without a court order is illegal under Indiana Code 32-31-5-6.

Does the Indianapolis Housing Agency give emergency rent help?

No. IHA runs Section 8 and public housing, not an emergency fund. Its Section 8 waiting list is closed, and IHA says it is still processing applications from its 2016 lottery. HUD took possession of IHA with the City in April 2024, and IHA's board is HUD appointed. If you already have IHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call IHA at 317.261.7200 right away.

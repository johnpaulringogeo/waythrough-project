Emergency Rent Help in Dallas: What's Open, Free Eviction Lawyers, and Texas's 2026 Eviction Clock

Matt · Updated October 2026

If you rent in Dallas and you are behind, start with the hard news: the City of Dallas rental assistance program is not taking rent applications. Its page says "Rental Assistance-Not accepting applications at this time." Dallas County helps with rent only for residents with a disability. The doors still open are smaller: St. Vincent de Paul North Texas, The Salvation Army, Catholic Charities Dallas for people already in an eviction case, and 2-1-1. For eviction help, the Dallas Eviction Advocacy Center gives free legal help to Dallas County tenants at (469) 436-2704, and Legal Aid of NorthWest Texas takes landlord-tenant cases at 855-548-8457. Texas eviction law also changed on January 1, 2026. Everything here was checked on October 16, 2026.

Here's the short version: Request help from St. Vincent de Paul North Texas online or at 469-607-0909. If you have a disability, call Dallas County's Welfare Assistance program at (214) 819-1800. Dial 2-1-1 for other rent help. Call the Dallas Eviction Advocacy Center at (469) 436-2704 as soon as you get a notice. If your court papers mention "summary disposition," you have only 4 days to respond. And if you get a court date, go.

Texas moves fast, and the rules changed on January 1, 2026

A Texas landlord needs to give only "at least three days' written notice to vacate" before filing, unless your lease says otherwise (Property Code section 24.005). The trial must be held between the 10th and the 21st day after the case is filed (section 24.0051(g)). And under SB 38, if the landlord files a motion for summary disposition, you have until the 4th day after you are served to file a written response, or the judge can rule without a trial (section 24.005106). After a judgment you have 5 days to appeal (section 24.005107). That is why every rent door below asks for your notice: by the time you have a court date, you may have only days left.

Help paying rent in Dallas: what is open and what is not

Apply to more than one. Each runs on its own money, and none of them promises help.

City of Dallas rental and utility assistance: not taking rent applications

The City of Dallas Office of Community Care's social services page says "Rental Assistance-Not accepting applications at this time," and that rental and utility applications "are processed subject to funding availability and client eligibility" (checked October 16, 2026). The page does not say whether utility help is open, so ask.

  • How to reach a caseworker: call the intake line at 214-670-8416, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to noon. Walk-ins are not accepted.
  • Who it serves: it "is not able to assist people who live outside of City of Dallas' city limits."
  • What it asks for: a photo ID, Social Security cards for everyone in the home, your lease, proof of 30 days of income, a utility bill, receipts for bills paid in the last 30 days, and your eviction notice.
  • If you are living with HIV/AIDS: the City's HOPWA Short-Term Rent, Mortgage, and Utility (STRMU) program "Provides emergency assistance to individuals and families living with HIV/AIDS" in a temporary crisis. Call 214-670-6338 or email freshstarthousing@dallas.gov for an appointment; no walk-ins.

Dallas County Health and Human Services: rent help for residents with a disability

Dallas County's Welfare Assistance program "provides temporary financial and supportive services," which its flyer says may include rent or mortgage support and utility bill help. Its basic requirements: you live in Dallas County, you are 18 or older, you are a U.S. citizen or have legal status, you have limited income, you are "Not receiving housing assistance (e.g., Section 8)," and you are disabled with medical documentation.

  • How to apply: schedule an appointment at (214) 819-1800, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. The office is at 2377 N. Stemmons Freeway, Suite 200.
  • What to bring: photo ID, Social Security cards for everyone in the home, a medical statement signed by a licensed provider within 30 days, proof of all income for the last 30 days, bank printouts, current utility bills, and your full signed lease.
  • Utility help frees up rent money: the County says "The Comprehensive Energy Assistance Program (CEAP) is open" for households at or below 150% of the federal poverty guideline. It is by appointment only at (214) 819-1848, and "Applications for assistance are processed in the order received."

St. Vincent de Paul North Texas

St. Vincent de Paul's contact page asks, "Do you need help with rent, utilities, food, or other emergency assistance?" To ask for help, fill out its Need Assistance form or call 469-607-0909; it says to "Leave voicemail and we will return the call within the next 48 hours." Help comes through local parish groups, so it depends on where you live and what each group has.

Catholic Charities Dallas and The Salvation Army

  • Catholic Charities Dallas: its housing program lists "Eviction prevention services for those who are going through an eviction process with the landlord," and says the Metropolitan Dallas Homeless Alliance refers clients to it. Ask 2-1-1 how to be referred, or call its helpline at 866-223-7500.
  • The Salvation Army of North Texas: says "We provide emergency assistance to help with mortgage/rent, utility bills," and its Home Sweet Home program offers case management and "financial assistance (1-6 months)." Its pages do not say how to apply, so call the Carr P. Collins Social Service Center, 5302 Harry Hines Blvd., at (214) 424-7050 and ask what is open.

2-1-1 Texas: the switchboard

Texas Health and Human Services says "Dialing 2-1-1 is a free, easy way to find out about services you can get in your area or through state programs," and "If you can't connect to 2-1-1, call us toll-free at 877-541-7905." Ask for rent help in Dallas County and write down every name and number you get.

Texas Rent Relief and the Texas Eviction Diversion Program are over

The Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs says both programs "launched in February 2021 and closed in the summer of 2023." Some Dallas County justice court pages still describe the Texas Eviction Diversion Program and its rent payments. That program is closed, so do not count on it.

Eviction help in Dallas: free lawyers

If you searched for eviction help in Dallas, these are the answers with names. None of them pays rent, but a lawyer can catch mistakes in the landlord's papers, file your response on time, and work out a deal.

Dallas Eviction Advocacy Center (DEAC)

  • Who it helps: DEAC says it "assists all Dallas County tenants regardless of income, race, immigration status, or native language," and that its help is free.
  • The County program: Dallas County's Wrongful Eviction Prevention Program (WEPP) was "Established by the Dallas County Commissioners Court in partnership with the Dallas Eviction Advocacy Center." For that County-funded help, the County says "Eligible individuals must self-certify that they are at or below 200% of the federal poverty guideline," and you sign a retainer agreement with DEAC. It offers full representation or advice only.
  • How to reach it: call (469) 436-2704, email info@dallaseac.org, or use the DEAC contact form.

Legal Aid of NorthWest Texas

  • Landlord-tenant line: its site says "Landlord-tenant issue? 855-548-8457." The City of Dallas eviction assistance page lists the same number as Legal Aid's "Eviction Defense Line" in English and Spanish: 1-855-548-8457.
  • Legal Aid Line: 888-529-5277, or apply online. If you have been served with a lawsuit, Legal Aid says to call your local office.
  • The Dallas office: 1515 Main St., Dallas, TX 75201, 214-748-1234. For intake it says to call "Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m."

At the courthouse

Dallas County eviction cases are heard in its Justice of the Peace courts, in the precinct where the home is. Each court posts its own forms. For example, the JP Precinct 1, Place 2 court at the South Dallas Government Center, 8301 S. Polk Street, Suite 2200, (972) 228-2272, offers a Writ of Re-Entry "to request entry into a residence if wrongfully locked out" and a Writ of Restoration "to request utilities to be restored if wrongfully disconnected." It also says "ALL APPEALS MUST BE FILED IN-PERSON."

Texas eviction timeline in 2026: what happens and when

Texas rewrote its eviction law with Senate Bill 38, which took effect on January 1, 2026. The rules are in Chapter 24 of the Texas Property Code and in Texas Rule of Civil Procedure 510, which the Supreme Court of Texas rewrote as a stand-alone eviction rule for cases filed on or after that date. These are the steps for a rent case, in order:

  • 1. The notice. Your landlord must give you "at least three days' written notice to vacate" before filing, "unless the parties have contracted for a shorter or longer notice period in a written lease" (Property Code section 24.005(a)). Read your lease. If the case is only about unpaid rent and you were not late before the month of the notice, the notice must be a "notice to pay rent or vacate," which gives you the chance to pay. The notice can come by mail, be left inside your home in a conspicuous place, be handed to anyone in the home 16 or older, or come by email only if you agreed to that in writing (section 24.005(f-3)).
  • 2. The court case. The landlord files a sworn petition in the Justice of the Peace court (section 24.00505). In Dallas County that is the Justice of the Peace court for the precinct where your home is. The officer must make a diligent effort to serve you within 5 business days after filing (section 24.0051(f)).
  • 3. The 4-day answer to a summary disposition motion. This is the new trap. With the petition, a landlord can file a sworn "motion for summary disposition without trial." If you do not file a written response with your supporting facts and documents "not later than the fourth day after the date the tenant is served," the judge can rule for the landlord without a trial (section 24.005106; Rule 510.10(a)). If your papers mention summary disposition, get legal help the same day.
  • 4. The trial. The court must hold the trial "not earlier than the 10th day or later than the 21st day after the date the petition is filed," and never sooner than the 4th day after you are served. A trial can be postponed for no more than 7 days unless both sides agree in writing (section 24.0051(g)). You do not have to file a written answer to an ordinary eviction suit (Rule 510.11(a)), but you must show up. If you do not, the landlord usually wins by default.
  • 5. The appeal: 5 days. To appeal, file a bond, a cash deposit, or a Statement of Inability to Afford Payment of Court Costs with the justice court "not later than the fifth day after the date the judgment is signed," and affirm that you have a good-faith defense (section 24.005107; Rule 510.19(a)). To stay in your home during the appeal, you must pay one rental period into the court registry within 5 days of filing the appeal and keep paying each period (section 24.0053(a-2)). If you miss a payment, the court can issue the writ of possession without a hearing (section 24.0054(a)).
  • 6. The writ of possession. If you lose and do not appeal, the writ cannot issue "before the sixth day after the date on which the judgment for possession is rendered" (section 24.0061(b)). The officer must post a warning on your front door at least 24 hours before carrying it out (section 24.0061(d)).
  • Lockouts. A landlord "may not intentionally prevent a tenant from entering the leased premises except by judicial process," with narrow exceptions. A landlord who changes the locks on a tenant who is behind must post a notice saying where to get the new key at any hour, and must give you the key "regardless of whether or not the tenant pays any of the delinquent rent." If a landlord breaks these rules, you can get back in or end the lease and recover one month's rent plus $1,000, plus damages and fees (Property Code section 92.0081).

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • DHA Housing Solutions for North Texas (the Dallas Housing Authority) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund: its site says "DHA is not an emergency housing provider." It does take Section 8 preliminary applications at any time, "You can submit a preliminary application at any time," and an application not picked in a lottery lasts "for up to 18 months" (checked October 16, 2026). Phone: 214-951-8300. Our DHA page has the details.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Dallas right now?

Very little, and the City's program is not taking rent applications. The City of Dallas rental and utility assistance page says "Rental Assistance-Not accepting applications at this time" (checked October 16, 2026). Dallas County helps with rent only for residents with a disability, by appointment at (214) 819-1800. St. Vincent de Paul North Texas takes rent requests through its online form or at 469-607-0909. Dial 2-1-1 for other agencies that have money this month.

Does Dallas County help with rent?

Only for some people. Dallas County Health and Human Services' Welfare Assistance program can help with rent or mortgage and utility bills for Dallas County residents who are disabled, have limited income, and do not already get housing assistance such as Section 8. Call (214) 819-1800, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., for an appointment. The County's energy program, CEAP, is open for help with electric and gas bills at (214) 819-1848.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Dallas?

Call the Dallas Eviction Advocacy Center at (469) 436-2704. It runs Dallas County's Wrongful Eviction Prevention Program and says it helps all Dallas County tenants regardless of income. Legal Aid of NorthWest Texas also takes landlord-tenant cases at 855-548-8457, or through its Legal Aid Line at 888-529-5277. Call as soon as you get a notice, not the day before court.

How long does an eviction take in Texas in 2026?

It can take less than a month. The landlord must give at least 3 days' written notice to vacate unless the lease says otherwise. After the case is filed, the trial must be held between the 10th and 21st day. If the landlord asks for summary disposition, you must file a written response within 4 days of being served. After a judgment you have 5 days to appeal, and the writ of possession cannot issue before the 6th day.

Is Texas Rent Relief or the Texas Eviction Diversion Program still open?

No. The Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs says both programs "launched in February 2021 and closed in the summer of 2023." Some Dallas County court pages still describe the Texas Eviction Diversion Program, but it is not taking applications.

Can the Dallas Housing Authority help me pay rent?

Not in an emergency. DHA says "DHA is not an emergency housing provider." It does take Section 8 preliminary applications at any time, and picks applicants by lottery. If you already have a DHA voucher or live in DHA housing and fall behind, call DHA at 214-951-8300 right away.

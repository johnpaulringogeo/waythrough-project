Housing Options During and After Divorce

Matt · Updated April 2026

Divorce puts a lot of people's housing at risk. You may have to leave your home, your income changes (especially if child support is involved), and you're dealing with lawyers and court dates while trying to keep a roof over your head. If you have a Section 8 voucher or live in public housing, the divorce changes your lease too. This guide covers how to protect your housing through the divorce, what happens to your voucher when your spouse leaves, emergency options if you're in crisis, and your rights under VAWA if domestic violence is involved.

How Divorce Affects Your Section 8 Voucher or Public Housing

Your housing lease is tied to your household. When your spouse moves out (or you do), the household composition changes, and so do your lease and your rent.

Lease Bifurcation (Section 8)

If you and your spouse have a Section 8 voucher, you probably have a joint lease with the landlord, and the voucher is issued to both of you as one "household." When you divorce, one of you transfers to a separate unit with your own voucher, or the lease gets "bifurcated." That means it is split into two separate leases in the same unit (rare), or one of you becomes a new household.

Who Gets to Stay?

Your divorce settlement or custody arrangement often decides this:

  • Custodial parent with children: Usually stays in the current housing with the voucher (or at least has first right to it)
  • Non-custodial spouse: Needs to find alternative housing, possibly with their own Section 8 application if they're eligible
  • No children: Either party can claim the voucher, but the PHA will decide based on who was the original applicant or other criteria

Public Housing Lease

Public housing (not Section 8) works much the same way. The lease is usually in one person's name, the primary leaseholder. If that's you and you want to stay, your spouse has to move out. If it's your spouse, you may lose the housing when they leave.

Income Recalculation When Your Spouse Leaves

When your spouse's income comes off your household, your rent should go down. That drop can be the difference between staying housed and falling into hardship.

What Happens Immediately

The day your spouse moves out, your household composition has officially changed, and you have to tell your PHA right away. The rent should then be recalculated for the household that's left: you and any children or dependents who stay with you.

Example: Income Decrease

Maria and her spouse both work, and together they bring in $4,000/month. Maria's TTP (30% of adjusted income) is $800/month. When her spouse moves out and takes their $2,500/month with them, the household income drops a lot. Depending on deductions, her TTP might fall to $500/month or less.

Child Support and Alimony

Child support or spousal support you receive counts as income for Section 8, so those payments are added to your income and raise your rent. A few details matter here:

  • If your ex isn't paying, the PHA counts only what you actually receive
  • If support is owed but not coming in, document that. Your PHA might let you report $0 support income if it truly isn't being paid
  • Alimony (spousal support) is treated as income
  • Child support is treated as income too

How to Request Income Recalculation

Contact your PHA as soon as your spouse moves out, and bring:

  1. A copy of your divorce decree or separation agreement showing the split
  2. Documentation that your spouse is no longer part of your household (proof they've moved to another address)
  3. Updated income information for yourself and any children with income
  4. Documentation of any child support or alimony you're receiving

Ask for an interim recertification so your rent is recalculated now instead of at your annual recertification. Many PHAs can process this within 30 days.

Updating Your PHA: Household Changes

Income isn't the only thing the PHA needs to hear about during a divorce.

Household Composition

Tell the PHA about:

  • Your spouse moving out
  • Any child custody changes (who lives with you and how often)
  • New household members if you're now living with family or others
  • Change in family size that affects bedroom requirements

Lease Updates

If you stay in the unit, the lease probably needs to be updated so only your name is on it. The landlord takes your spouse off the lease, and the PHA confirms the lease matches the approved household.

Contact Information

Keep your address, phone number, and email current with the PHA. If you move before your next recertification, the PHA still has to be able to reach you. If they can't, you could lose your voucher.

Child Custody, Bedroom Size, and Your Voucher

Your Section 8 voucher is approved for a unit size based on who is in your household, mostly the number of children in your care.

How Custody Affects Bedroom Size

It mostly comes down to how much time the children spend in your home.

  • Full custody or primary custody: Children live with you full-time. They require a bedroom in your calculation. Your voucher is approved for that bedroom size.
  • 50/50 custody: Children split time equally. In some cases, the PHA counts them as requiring a full bedroom with you. In other cases, if custody is truly 50/50 and simultaneous with the other parent, the PHA might allow a smaller unit. Ask your PHA about their policy.
  • Visitation only: Children stay with you occasionally (weekends, holidays). The PHA typically does not count them as requiring a bedroom, unless you have overnight visitation rights.

Custody Changes and Housing Transitions

If custody changes (say you gain full custody after having only visitation), you might need a larger unit. Report the change to your PHA and ask whether you can transfer to a larger unit. It can go the other way too: if you lose custody, you might move to a smaller unit and get a higher rent if your voucher was sized for the larger unit.

Overnight Visitation Rights

Some PHAs count children in your household only if you have overnight visitation rights, not just daytime visits. Weekend or holiday overnights usually count. Check your PHA's policy.

Emergency Housing Options During Divorce

If the divorce has you facing homelessness, start with these:

Domestic Violence Shelter

If your divorce involves domestic violence, emergency shelter is available. Call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233 for shelter referrals in your area. Shelters are free. Along with a place to stay, they offer counseling, legal services, and help finding long-term housing.

Emergency Rental Assistance

If the divorce puts you at risk of eviction or homelessness, you may qualify for emergency rental assistance. Contact your city or county human services department, or see our guide on emergency rental assistance programs. Some programs are set up for people in exactly this kind of crisis.

Temporary Stay with Family or Friends

Staying with family or friends while you work out permanent housing is often the fastest fix. If you're on Section 8, though, the PHA needs to know where you live. Report any stay longer than a few days, and give the PHA a letter from your host confirming your temporary address.

Accelerated Housing Search

If you need a new place fast:

  • Apply to several units at the same time
  • If it helps, tell prospective landlords what's going on (the divorce is recent and you need housing quickly)
  • Bring proof of your Section 8 voucher so landlords can decide faster
  • Ask your housing counselor to help you find available units

VAWA Protections if Domestic Violence Is Involved

The Violence Against Women Act (VAWA) protects people experiencing domestic violence, sexual assault, dating violence, or stalking. If there was abuse in your marriage, VAWA may protect your housing.

What VAWA Protects

Under VAWA, if you're a victim of domestic violence:

  • Your abuser cannot use housing loss as a threat or control mechanism
  • You cannot be evicted solely because of domestic violence or police calls related to abuse
  • You may be able to bifurcate your Section 8 voucher so your abuser doesn't control it
  • Your address can be kept confidential from your abuser
  • You have the right to change locks if you leave the unit (in some circumstances)

How to Request VAWA Protections

Contact your PHA and ask for VAWA protections. You'll typically need to:

  1. File a police report (or explain why you can't)
  2. Provide documentation from a shelter, counselor, clergy, or doctor attesting to abuse
  3. Complete HUD's VAWA certification form
  4. Explain how the abuse affects your housing (e.g., your abuser controls the lease, threatened to evict you, etc.)

Lease Bifurcation Under VAWA

If you and your abuser are both on the Section 8 lease, you can ask the PHA to bifurcate (split) the lease so only you are on it. Your abuser loses access to the voucher, and your housing stays secure even if they remain in the unit for a while. It also protects you from an eviction your abuser starts.

VAWA Criminal History Exception

Domestic violence survivors are sometimes arrested for something that happened during the abuse. If you were convicted of a crime while being abused, VAWA may let you get housing despite the conviction. Tell the PHA about your VAWA status when you apply.

Finding VAWA Resources

Who to call:

  • National Domestic Violence Hotline: 1-800-799-7233
  • Local domestic violence shelter or program
  • Legal aid in your state: Lawhelp.org
  • Your PHA's civil rights office

Non-Custodial Parent Housing Issues

If you're the non-custodial parent and the divorce is costing you your home, you still have options.

Apply for Your Own Section 8 Voucher

You can apply for a Section 8 voucher in your own name if you meet the income and eligibility rules. You won't get priority, though. There's no preference for you as a custodial parent, and no homelessness preference unless you're actually homeless when you apply.

Stay in Shared Housing

Look at a roommate, shared housing, or a co-tenant arrangement. Our guide on shared housing and roommate strategies walks through the options. Sharing usually costs less and is easier to get than a unit on your own.

Emergency Housing if Homeless

If you become homeless, contact your local emergency shelter or call 211. Homelessness may qualify you for priority on Section 8 waiting lists.

Legal Assistance During Divorce

In a divorce, housing gets tangled up with custody, support, and property division. Get a lawyer or counselor involved early.

Legal Aid for Divorce

Free or low-cost help with a divorce is available through:

  • Legal Aid: Lawhelp.org connects you to free legal services based on income
  • Domestic violence programs: Often provide free legal assistance if abuse is involved
  • Legal clinics: Law schools and bar associations often offer free divorce clinics
  • Mediation services: Many communities offer low-cost mediation to help couples reach agreement without court

Housing Counseling During Divorce

A housing counselor can explain how the divorce affects your voucher, walk you through the income recalculation, and help you get your paperwork ready for the PHA. See our guide on finding a housing counselor.

Timeline: What to Do When

Immediately After Separation or Divorce

  • Notify your PHA that your spouse has moved out
  • Request an interim recertification for income adjustment
  • If applicable, request VAWA protections
  • If you're losing housing, apply for emergency rental assistance
  • Contact legal aid if you need a lawyer for divorce or VAWA issues

During Divorce Proceedings

  • Negotiate custody and support that will determine your household income
  • Make sure your divorce decree covers housing (who keeps the lease, where each of you will live)
  • Document all changes to your household for the PHA
  • Keep your PHA updated on your current address and contact information

After Divorce Finalization

  • Provide a copy of your divorce decree to your PHA
  • Update your lease and household composition
  • Check that your Section 8 rent reflects your new household income and composition
  • Update custody and support information at recertification

Don't Lose Your Housing During Divorce

Call your PHA the day your household changes and ask for an interim recertification so your rent is recalculated. If anyone threatens your housing, call legal aid next.

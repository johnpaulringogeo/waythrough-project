Emergency Rent Help in Baltimore: CAP Centers, Free Eviction Lawyers Through ACE, Pay and Stay, and the 10-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Baltimore City and you are behind, the City's own rent help now runs through its five Community Action Partnership (CAP) Centers, part of the Mayor's Office of Children & Family Success. The City says rent help "may be available to eligible applicants for eviction prevention." Call 410-396-5555 or walk into a CAP Center, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. The City does not post whether money is available this month, so call first. If you have eviction papers, Maryland's Access to Counsel in Evictions (ACE) program can give you a free lawyer if your household income is at or below 50% of the state median: dial 2-1-1 and say you need legal help with an eviction. Maryland also lets most tenants "pay and stay": pay what the court says you owe, plus court costs, before the eviction, and you keep your home. Everything here was checked on November 3, 2026.

Here's the short version: Call the CAP Centers at 410-396-5555 today, or go to the one nearest you with your ID, your lease, and any court papers. If you got a court summons, dial 2-1-1 for a free ACE lawyer, or apply online at legalhelpmd.org. If you have a child in a Baltimore City community school, watch United Way's Community Schools rent program, which says it will post an opening date in early November. And go to court on your trial date.

Pay and stay: your right to redeem

In a Maryland failure-to-pay-rent case, the Maryland Judiciary says "you can avoid eviction if you pay the amount the court decided was due, plus court costs, before the eviction takes place." A landlord can ask the court to deny this right only if you have had three judgments for possession for unpaid rent in the last 12 months; in Baltimore City, it is four (checked November 3, 2026). The Baltimore City Sheriff's Office says that on the eviction day, if you are there and have the right to pay and stay, its deputy "will require the landlord or agent to accept payment and the eviction will be cancelled." It says to pay with cash, a certified check, or a money order. This is why rent help can still save your home even after a judgment.

Baltimore rent help: the doors that pay

There is no big statewide rent fund open right now. The City's CAP Centers are the main local door. The others below are narrower. Call before you count on any of them.

Baltimore City CAP Centers: the City's rent help

The City's housing department says: "Assistance with rent may be available to eligible applicants for eviction prevention through the program now administered by the Community Action Partnership (CAP) office under the Mayor's Office of Children and Family Success." The CAP Centers "administer the city's energy, water and rental assistance programs." For help, the City says to "call 410-396-5555 or visit the locations below" (checked November 3, 2026):

  • Eastern CAP Center: 1731 E. Chase Street, 21213
  • Northern CAP Center: 5225 York Road, 21212
  • Northwest CAP Center: 3939 Reisterstown Road, 21215
  • Southern CAP Center: 606 Cherry Hill Road, 21225
  • Southeast CAP Center: 3411 Bank Street, 21224
  • Hours: Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. The Sheriff's Office says "Walk-ins welcome."
  • Moving instead? The CAP office also runs a Security Deposit Assistance Program, which "provides one-time financial assistance to eligible Baltimore City residents who have found a rental unit but need help covering the security deposit."

The City's pages do not list income limits or say whether the fund has money this month. Ask when you call, and bring your ID, your lease, proof of income, and any notice or court paper you got.

Community Schools Rental Assistance: families with a student in a community school

This state program is run by the Maryland Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD), and in Baltimore City it is handled by United Way of Central Maryland. It can pay past-due rent or utilities (including late fees and eviction court fees), move-in costs, and some future rent. To qualify, your household must include "a student enrolled in a Baltimore City community school," you must rent or be trying to rent, and you must be at risk of losing your home, facing eviction, or behind on rent or utilities. Your landlord's property must have a Baltimore City rental license, and you cannot have gotten this help before.

  • Status: on November 3, 2026, United Way's page said applications were not open yet: "Please check back in early November for an update on our opening date." Check its Baltimore City program page for the date.
  • How it works: you fill out the online tenant application and upload proof of school enrollment, your lease, or bills. United Way says it takes up to 200 applications at a time, then closes for 30 days, and that review "can take 90 days or more." You and your landlord have 30 days to send documents once your application is under review.

Department of Social Services: Emergency Assistance for families with children

Baltimore City DSS offers Emergency Assistance to Families with Children (EAFC) for "an eviction notice, mortgage foreclosure, utility turn-off, or other emergency bills." You need at least one related child under 18 in the home and proof that you can keep paying rent and utilities after. "These funds are available to a household only once every 24 months." Apply online at MarylandBenefits.gov or call 1-800-332-6347 (checked November 3, 2026). The Sheriff's Office adds that you must have a Warrant of Restitution and cannot be in public housing or have a Housing Choice Voucher.

Charities that help with rent

  • Samaritan Center (Catholic Charities): 17 West Franklin Street. It offers "Direct financial assistance with rent" for "Baltimore City residents in financial need." Call 667-600-3700. The Sheriff's Office says you need a court notice, a Warrant of Restitution, or an eviction notice, cannot be in public housing or have a voucher, and can get help once every 12 months.
  • Franciscan Center: 101 W. 23rd Street, 410-467-5340. It lists "eviction prevention" among its services. The Sheriff's Office says you must have a Warrant of Restitution and live in ZIP code 21217 or 21218.
  • 211 Maryland: "a free, confidential service that connects Marylanders to food, housing, utility assistance, help paying bills, and more." Dial 2-1-1; if your phone won't connect, the Central Maryland line is 1-866-406-8156.

Utility help in Baltimore: OHEP and the CAP Centers

  • Office of Home Energy Programs (OHEP): Maryland's energy bill help is run by the state Department of Human Services. Its program year "runs from 7/1-6/30 annually." Apply online through the state benefits portal linked on the OHEP page, or call 1-800-332-6347 for a paper application. In Baltimore City, the CAP Centers (410-396-5555) also take energy and water assistance applications.
  • The City's Energy Stabilization Assistance Program (ESAP) is closed: the City says "Due to high volume ESAP Applications are now closed."

Paying the utility bill can free up cash for rent. Our utility assistance guide covers other programs.

Eviction help in Baltimore: free lawyers

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can check the landlord's notice and rental license, raise defenses, and work out a payment plan or more time.

  • Access to Counsel in Evictions (ACE): Maryland law says "A covered individual shall have access to legal representation" (Real Property § 8-902). A covered individual is a tenant in "a household with an income that is not greater than 50% of the median income, adjusted for household size, in the State" (§ 8-901). The Maryland Judiciary says ACE lawyers can help with failure to pay rent, breach of lease, and tenant holding over cases, and with voucher or other subsidy terminations. To get one, call 2-1-1 and "Tell the operator that you need legal help with an eviction case," or apply online at legalhelpmd.org.
  • Baltimore City's own law: City Code Article 13, Subtitle 6A, "Tenants' Right to Counsel in Eviction Cases," says covered tenants "shall receive legal representation" from a designated organization unless something specific to the person or case makes it infeasible.
  • Maryland Legal Aid: it runs the Tenant Right to Counsel Project. Its main intake number is 888-465-2468; it also lists (410) 951-7750 and online intake at mdlab.org. The Sheriff's Office lists its office at 500 East Lexington Street, with walk-ins on Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays, 8:30 a.m. to 2:30 p.m.
  • Pro Bono Resource Center of Maryland: its Tenant & Consumer Hotline is 443-703-3053, Monday to Friday, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., for "a Maryland tenant facing eviction." It says "All PBRC's legal clinics in Baltimore City are available regardless of income."
  • Maryland Court Help Center: free legal help by phone at 410-260-1392, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 8 p.m. The Sheriff's Office lists the in-person center at 501 East Fayette Street, 3rd floor.

Maryland eviction timeline for unpaid rent: what happens and when

These steps come from the Maryland Judiciary's housing page and the Baltimore City Sheriff's Office:

  • 1. The 10-day notice. Before filing, the landlord must give you a written notice of intent to file. The Judiciary says "The notice must tell the tenant how much rent is due and give them 10 days to pay." Landlords must use court form DC-CV-115. Use those 10 days to call the CAP Centers and 2-1-1.
  • 2. The court case. After the 10 days, the landlord can file a failure-to-pay-rent case in District Court. The Judiciary warns that on the trial date, "if either party fails to appear, the court may dismiss the case, issue a judgment, or postpone the trial." Go, and bring your rent receipts and any repair records.
  • 3. After a judgment. "The eviction process cannot start until 7 business days have passed" after the judgment for possession. Then the landlord asks for a Warrant of Restitution, which must be requested within 60 days.
  • 4. Notice of the eviction date. In Baltimore City, the Sheriff's Office says the landlord must tell you the date two ways: "By First-Class mail with a certificate of mailing, at least 14 days before the scheduled eviction," and "By posting notice on the premises, at least 7 days before the scheduled eviction." The deputy checks that these notices were sent, and you can challenge them.
  • 5. Pay and stay. Until the eviction, you can usually pay what the court ordered and keep your home (see the box above).
  • 6. Your belongings. The Sheriff's Office says "The only way to protect your belongings is to move them out before the eviction date." After an eviction, what is left is treated as abandoned.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The Housing Authority of Baltimore City (HABC) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its site says "The HCVP Tenant-Based and the Public Housing waiting list(s) are CLOSED" (checked November 3, 2026). Its Section 8 office at 1225 W. Pratt Street is "closed to the public due to renovations" for the month of November 2026. If you already have HABC housing or a voucher and fall behind, call HABC at 410-396-3232 or the HCVP Call Center at 443-984-2222.
  • United Way's STEP program (Strategic Targeted Eviction Prevention) has ended. United Way says it "operated from April, 2021 through April 2024."
  • The City's ESAP energy fund is closed for now, and old pandemic-era rent portals are gone. Start with the CAP Centers instead.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Baltimore right now?

Some, but it is limited. Baltimore City's rent help runs through its five Community Action Partnership (CAP) Centers, which the City says may help eligible renters for eviction prevention. Call 410-396-5555 or walk in Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. The City does not post whether money is available, so call first. Families with a student in a community school can also watch United Way's Community Schools rent program, which had not opened yet on November 3, 2026.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Baltimore?

Dial 2-1-1 and tell the operator you need legal help with an eviction case, or apply online at legalhelpmd.org. Through Maryland's Access to Counsel in Evictions program, tenants with household income at or below 50% of the state median can get a free lawyer. You can also call Maryland Legal Aid at 888-465-2468 or the Pro Bono Resource Center's tenant hotline at 443-703-3053.

Can I stop an eviction in Maryland by paying what I owe?

Usually, yes. In a failure-to-pay-rent case, you can pay the amount the court decided was due, plus court costs, any time before the eviction takes place. A landlord can ask the court to take away this right only if you have had three judgments for unpaid rent in the last 12 months, or four in Baltimore City. Pay with cash, a certified check, or a money order.

How much notice does a Maryland landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

Ten days. Before filing a failure-to-pay-rent case, the landlord must give you a written notice that says how much rent is due and gives you 10 days to pay. If the landlord later wins, in Baltimore City it must tell you the eviction date by first-class mail at least 14 days ahead and by posting a notice at least 7 days ahead.

Is the Baltimore Section 8 waitlist open?

No. The Housing Authority of Baltimore City says its tenant-based Section 8 and public housing waiting lists are closed. HABC is not an emergency rent fund. If you already have HABC housing or a voucher and fall behind, call HABC at 410-396-3232 right away.

Does Baltimore City DSS help with rent?

Sometimes, for families with children. Baltimore City DSS offers Emergency Assistance to Families with Children for an eviction notice or other emergency bills, once every 24 months. You need a related child under 18 at home and proof you can keep up with rent after. Apply at MarylandBenefits.gov or call 1-800-332-6347.

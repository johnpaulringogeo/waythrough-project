Emergency Rent Help in Orlando, Florida: Orange County's Crisis Assistance and EPIC, Free Legal Aid, and Florida's 5-Day Clock

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Orlando or Orange County and you are behind, here is the honest picture. The City of Orlando's rent program is over: its page says "There are no funds available." Orange County's pandemic program is closed too. What is left is a small set of real doors: Orange County's Crisis Assistance Program for a one-time emergency, EPIC for working families with children facing eviction, Heart of Florida United Way 211, free legal aid, and help with OUC, Duke Energy and LIHEAP bills. Florida has no right to counsel for tenants, and its eviction clock is short. Everything here was checked on November 5, 2026.

Here's the short version: Call 407-836-6500 for a Crisis Assistance screening, or the EPIC line at 407-836-6514 if you have children at home and are two months behind or already in court. Dial 2-1-1 for anything else. If you get an eviction summons, count 5 days (not weekends or legal holidays), file a written answer, pay the rent into the court registry if you dispute anything but payment, and call legal aid the same day.

Florida moves fast: the 3-day notice and the 5-day answer

Under Florida Statutes section 83.56(3), a landlord can end your lease if rent is still unpaid 3 days after a written demand, "excluding Saturday, Sunday, and legal holidays." Once you are served with an eviction summons, you have 5 days (again not counting weekends and legal holidays) to file an answer. If you raise any defense other than "I paid," section 83.60(2) says you must also pay the rent owed into the court registry, or file a motion asking the court to decide the amount, in those same 5 days. Missing that step is treated as giving up your defenses, and the landlord can get a default judgment. Do not wait on a rent application to protect your court case.

Help paying rent in Orlando: the doors that are open

There is no single rent office. These are the programs that exist now. None of them promises help, and money can run out.

Orange County Crisis Assistance Program (CAP)

Orange County calls CAP "a limited financial assistance program for Orange County residents." It offers "one-time financial assistance for rent, mortgage, and/or utilities (electric, gas, or water only)," plus budget counseling and referrals (checked November 5, 2026).

  • Who qualifies, in the County's words: "U.S. citizen or legal resident status"; "Established Orange County resident for at least 90 days"; "Insufficient resources to pay for housing and/or utility expense(s)"; "Documentation of unforeseen event within past 30-60 days that caused emergency financial need"; "One-time assistance will resolve the situation"; and "Verification of future financial management." There is a "Two (2) year eligibility period," so you can get help once every two years.
  • How to apply: "Call 407-836-6500 to be screened for an appointment." Appointments are "first call, first-serve," Monday through Friday, 8:30 a.m. to noon and 1:30 to 4 p.m., and not on County holidays. Call right at 8:30 a.m.
  • Status: the County's page posts no open or closed notice and no funding level. If you cannot get through, keep calling and dial 211 at the same time. ocfl.net Crisis Assistance Program

EPIC: Eviction Prevention in the Community

Orange County says EPIC "is an eviction and foreclosure prevention program for Orange County families." It pairs help with case management.

  • Who qualifies: Orange County resident for at least 90 days; "Behind two or more months on rent or mortgage or under a court filed legal eviction or foreclosure in Orange County, Florida"; "Minor children living in household"; "Employed with sufficient income to verify future financial management"; and willing to take part in case management. "Eligibility is limited to twice-in-a-lifetime."
  • How to apply: "Call the EPIC message line at 407-836-6514 to be screened." Leave your full name, phone number, and your court case number if you have one. "Program staff will contact you for an eligibility screening within two business days." The Orange County Clerk's eviction page also points families with minor children to EPIC.
  • Status: no open or closed notice is posted (checked November 5, 2026). ocfl.net EPIC

Heart of Florida United Way 211

Heart of Florida United Way runs 211 for Central Florida, "available 24/7/365," with "free multi-language translation services." Dial 2-1-1 or (407) 839-4357, text your ZIP code to 898-211, or chat at hfuw.org. Ask for rent help in Orange County and write down every name and number you get. HFUW does not run its own rent fund; it refers you to programs that have money.

Free legal help in Orlando

Florida and Orlando have no right to counsel in eviction cases. We found none on any official City, County, court or legal aid site (checked November 5, 2026). Legal aid is free if you qualify, but it cannot take every case, so call early.

  • Community Legal Services: serves Orange County and 11 other Central Florida counties. It says "Generally, you will be eligible if your income does not exceed 125% of the Federal Poverty Income Guidelines." Helpline 1-800-405-1417: Monday, Wednesday and Thursday 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., Tuesday 8:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., Friday 8:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. Or start online at legalaccessforall.org (the old clsmf.org address now goes there).
  • Legal Aid Society of the Orange County Bar Association: handles "Landlord/tenant issues, foreclosure defense, and substandard housing conditions." Call 407-841-8310, option 1, or walk in Monday to Thursday, 8:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m., at 100 E. Robinson St., Orlando. It says "even if your case falls in an area of law that we handle, we may not be able to take your case." legalaidocba.org
  • Court mediation: the Ninth Circuit says "The County Judge may decide that a mediation is necessary prior to the trial," and lists the fee for residential evictions as "No charge." The judge may order you to deposit money into the court registry by two days before the mediation. Orange County Dispute Resolution Services: 407.836.2004.
  • Orange County Office of Tenant Services: answers tenant-rights questions at 407-836-RENT (407-836-7368) or TenantServices@ocfl.net. It is not a law firm and does not pay rent.

Utility bills: OUC, Duke Energy and LIHEAP

  • LIHEAP (Orange County Community Action): apply online at ocfl.net/LIHEAP or call (407) 836-7429, 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. on weekdays. The County's 2026 flyer says income "cannot exceed 60% of the State Median Income" ($61,837 a year for a household of four), crisis help can reach "$1,000 in benefits per LIHEAP Fiscal Year (subject to funds availability)," and "Applications will be available Monday through Friday according to funding availability." It lists the heating season as October 1 to March 31, when gas bills are covered. After March 31, 2027, call to ask which help is open. People 60 and older can also ask Catholic Charities about EHEAP at (407) 658-1818, ext. 1022.
  • OUC Project CARE: "one-time utility assistance, up to $500 per household, in a 12-month period," run by Heart of Florida United Way. Apply through HFUW's online portal from hfuw.org. HFUW says "limited applications are accepted each week," so try early in the week.
  • Duke Energy customers in Orange, Osceola and Seminole counties can apply through the same HFUW portal; HFUW says help is "limited to twice a year per household in a 12-month period." Winter Park and Winter Garden utility customers can get up to $650 in 12 months.
  • OUC Medical Alert Program: if your power is medically essential, a doctor's form gets you notice before planned outages, but OUC says it "does not exempt customers from service disconnection if they do not make regular payments."

Florida eviction timeline: what happens and when

Orlando evictions are filed in Orange County Court. The Clerk's Civil Division is at the courthouse, 425 N. Orange Ave., Suite 350, Orlando; its main line is 407-836-2000. These are the steps:

  • 1. The 3-day notice. The landlord delivers a written demand for rent or possession. You have 3 days to pay, "excluding Saturday, Sunday, and legal holidays" (section 83.56(3)). A 3-day notice is not an eviction.
  • 2. The eviction complaint. If you do not pay, the landlord can file in county court. Florida uses a fast "summary procedure" for these cases (sections 83.59(2) and 51.011).
  • 3. Your 5 days. The Orange County Clerk says: "The tenant then has five days (excluding weekends and legal holidays) to respond, in writing, to the court." If you dispute anything other than payment, you must also pay the rent the landlord claims into the court registry, or file a motion asking the court to decide the amount, in those same 5 days (section 83.60(2)). If you get Section 8 or other rent help, you deposit only your share. Keep paying rent into the registry as it comes due while the case is open.
  • 4. Judgment and the 24-hour notice. If the landlord wins, the Clerk issues a writ of possession. The sheriff posts it and can return after 24 hours, and "Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays do not stay the 24-hour notice period" (section 83.62(1)).
  • Lockouts are illegal. A landlord may not shut off your utilities or change the locks to force you out (section 83.67(1) and (2)). A landlord who does can owe you your "actual and consequential damages or 3 months' rent, whichever is greater," plus court costs and attorney's fees (section 83.67(6)).

The Clerk's Self Help Center (Suite 340, 407-836-6300, weekdays 7:30 a.m. to 4 p.m.) sells eviction forms and can explain filing steps, but its attorney consults cost $1.00 a minute, so try legal aid first. Our step-by-step guide to avoiding eviction covers the whole process, and understanding landlord notices explains the paper you got.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The City of Orlando's Emergency Rental Assistance Program. The City's page is titled "All Rental Assistance Program Funds Distributed" and says "applications are no longer being processed. There are no funds available." Ignore old flyers and links.
  • Orange County's ERAP2. The County says "The Orange County Emergency Rental Assistance Program (ERAP2) is closed." Some older County directory pages still describe it; they are out of date.
  • The Office of Tenant Services. It answers rights questions, but its own page lists "Provide emergency rental assistance" under what it does not do.
  • The Orlando Housing Authority and Orange County Section 8. They run long-term vouchers and public housing, not emergency rent, and their main lists were closed on November 5, 2026. See our OHA and Orange County guide. If you already have a voucher or public housing and fall behind, call OHA at 407.895.3300 or the County at (407) 836-5150 right away, because an eviction can cost you your help.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Orlando right now?

Not from the City. Its rental assistance page says "There are no funds available," and Orange County says its ERAP2 program is closed. What exists now is Orange County's Crisis Assistance Program, a one-time payment toward rent or utilities after an unforeseen emergency (call 407-836-6500 to be screened), and EPIC, for working families with minor children who are behind or have a court eviction (call 407-836-6514). Dial 211 for other help. Checked November 5, 2026.

Who can get help from EPIC?

EPIC is Orange County's eviction and foreclosure prevention program. You must have lived in Orange County for at least 90 days, have minor children in the home, be two or more months behind or have an eviction filed in court, be working with enough income to keep up afterward, and agree to case management. It is limited to twice in a lifetime. Leave your name, phone number and court case number on the EPIC line, 407-836-6514; staff call back within two business days.

Can I get a free eviction lawyer in Orlando?

There is no right to counsel for tenants in Orlando or Florida, so a free lawyer is not guaranteed. Ask legal aid right away. Community Legal Services takes calls at 1-800-405-1417, generally for income up to 125% of the federal poverty guidelines. The Legal Aid Society of the Orange County Bar Association handles landlord-tenant cases at 407-841-8310, option 1. Neither can take every case, so call the day you get papers and keep counting your 5 days.

How fast is an eviction in Florida?

Very fast. The notice to pay rent is 3 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or legal holidays. After you are served with an eviction summons, you have 5 days, again not counting weekends or legal holidays, to file a written answer with the court. If the landlord wins, the sheriff posts a writ and can remove you after 24 hours, even over a weekend.

Do I have to pay rent into the court registry?

If you raise any defense other than "I paid," yes. Florida law says you must pay the rent the landlord says you owe into the court registry, or file a motion asking the court to decide the amount, within the same 5 days you have to answer. If you get Section 8 or other rent help, you deposit only your share. If you miss this step, the landlord can get a default judgment without a hearing.

Is the Orlando Section 8 waiting list open?

No. On November 5, 2026, the Orlando Housing Authority listed its Section 8 and public housing lists as closed, and Orange County said its Section 8 program is not accepting applications. The only open lists on OHA's portal were three Sanford project-based voucher lists that opened July 20, 2026, with no closing date posted. Section 8 is not a fast fix for back rent either way.

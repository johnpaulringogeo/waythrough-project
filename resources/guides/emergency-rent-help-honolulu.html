Emergency Rent Help in Honolulu, Hawai‘i: The Hawai‘i Relief Program, Free Mediation, and the 10-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Honolulu or anywhere on O‘ahu and you are behind, here is the honest picture. The pandemic-era O‘ahu rent relief programs are over, and we found no open City and County of Honolulu rent fund on honolulu.gov. But two things work in your favor. First, the State's Hawai‘i Relief Program is taking applications from families with children who fell behind. Second, since February 5, 2026, a landlord must give 10 calendar days' notice and offer free mediation before filing an eviction for unpaid rent. Everything here was checked on November 5, 2026.

Here's the short version: If you got a notice for unpaid rent, call the Mediation Center of the Pacific at (808) 521-6767 within 10 days. If you have a child under 18 at home, call Catholic Charities Hawai‘i at (808) 521-4357 (press 1, then 1) about the Hawai‘i Relief Program. Everyone can dial 2-1-1. Call the Legal Aid Society of Hawai‘i at (808) 536-4302. And you do not have to move out by the date on the notice: only a judge can order an eviction. See the eviction timeline below.

Hawai‘i's new rule: 10 days, then free mediation

Since February 5, 2026, HRS § 521-68 says a landlord may demand the rent and give notice that the lease ends “unless payment is made within a time mentioned in the notice, not less than ten calendar days after receipt thereof.” The landlord “shall provide the ten-calendar-day notice to a state-funded mediation center that offers free mediation.” If mediation is scheduled within the ten days and you take part, the landlord “shall only file a summary possession proceeding after the expiration of twenty calendar days” from when you got the notice. These mediation rules come from Act 278 of 2025 and run through February 4, 2028. After that date the 10-day notice stays, but the mediation steps end unless lawmakers extend them.

Free mediation and free legal help in Honolulu

Neither of these pays rent. But mediation can get you a written payment plan, and a lawyer can check your notice and court papers for mistakes and raise defenses such as bad repairs.

Mediation Center of the Pacific (Act 278)

The Hawai‘i Judiciary's Act 278 FAQ lists the Mediation Center of the Pacific as the O‘ahu provider: (808) 521-6767, Landlordtenant@mediatehawaii.org, 1301 Young Street, 2nd Floor, Honolulu, by appointment Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. In the Judiciary's words:

  • Cost: “The mediation process itself is FREE, paid for by State funds for both landlords and tenants.”
  • Deadline: tenants who want to mediate “should contact the service provider listed on the notice within ten (10) days of receipt of the notice.”
  • Your choice: “The Act does not require tenants to mediate. However, if a tenant schedules a mediation as outlined above, their landlord is required to participate in mediation.” Mediation must happen within 30 days after the center reaches both of you.
  • A warning: “if a tenant schedules mediation and cancels or fails to attend, the landlord may request the tenant's payment of their attorney's fees and costs for mediation preparation.” If you book it, go.

Legal Aid Society of Hawai‘i and other free help

  • Legal Aid Society of Hawai‘i: O‘ahu intake (808) 536-4302; other islands 1-800-499-4302. Phone intake runs Monday to Friday, 9 to 11:30 a.m. and 1 to 3:30 p.m., or apply online at legalaidhawaii.org. It says its “physical office spaces are not open to the public” and it is “not currently accepting walk-in intake.”
  • Volunteer Legal Services Hawai‘i: (808) 528-7046, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., as the Judiciary lists it.
  • Landlord-Tenant Information Center (State Office of Consumer Protection): 1-844-808-DCCA (3222), Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to noon. It gives free information on the landlord-tenant code but says it “cannot provide you with legal advice.”
  • No right to counsel. Honolulu does not guarantee a lawyer in eviction cases, so call early.

Help paying rent in Honolulu: the doors that are open

Hawai‘i Relief Program (families with children)

The State Department of Human Services runs this program, and on O‘ahu Catholic Charities Hawai‘i takes the applications. Catholic Charities says: “Applications are now being accepted.” (checked November 5, 2026). The basics, in its words and numbers:

  • What it pays: “non-recurring short-term housing, utility, and automobile payments, not to exceed four (4) months.” Housing payments can reach $6,000, for up to four months; a one-time housing deposit up to $6,000; utility payments up to $2,000, for up to four months. The money goes straight to the landlord or utility.
  • Who qualifies: a family with an adult relative caring for a child under 18, or a pregnant woman in the third trimester; all members living in Hawai‘i; at least one member a U.S. citizen, qualified non-citizen, or COFA citizen; and an episode of need from a listed cause, such as “Involuntary loss of employment without cause,” a cut in income, an emergency medical bill, a natural disaster, or losing public assistance.
  • Income limit: 300% of the federal poverty level for Hawai‘i, gross income. For a family of four, that is $113,850 a year.
  • How to apply: online through Catholic Charities Hawai‘i, or call (808) 521-4357, press 1 for financial assistance, then 1 again. DHS says not to call or visit its own processing centers about this program. No end date is posted, so apply early.

Aloha United Way 211

Dial 2-1-1 from 7 a.m. to 10 p.m., seven days a week, or text (877) 275-6569. From outside Hawai‘i, call (808) 275-2000. AUW lists “Rental, housing, and utility assistance” among its topics. Ask about rent help on O‘ahu if you do not have children at home, and write down every name and number.

Other programs on O‘ahu

  • Catholic Charities Hawai‘i Housing Placement Program: Hawaiian Electric's October 16, 2026 assistance list describes it as for “Families with minors at risk of homelessness or homeless transitioning to housing,” with income at or below 250% of the federal poverty level, and lists it as “Open and accepting applications.” Call (808) 521-4357, press 1, then 3.
  • Rent-to-Work (City DCS, WorkHawai‘i Division): a “Short-term rental subsidy for 12-24 months” with job help, for people who are homeless or at risk, with income at or below 60% of area median income, who are “willing to work.” Use the interest form on the City's WorkHawai‘i page or call (808) 768-7762. The City posts no open or closed status.

Utility bills: H-HEAP and Hawaiian Electric

  • Energy Crisis Intervention (H-HEAP): for a household with a final disconnection notice. On O‘ahu, apply with the Honolulu Community Action Program (HCAP), which says “Applications are processed year-round, although funding is limited” and that it does not take H-HEAP applications by email. Bring a picture ID, Social Security card, proof of income, and a current electric bill. Hawaiian Electric's list puts the maximum at $700 per household. HCAP: 808-521-4531; Central District Office (808) 488-6834.
  • Energy Credit (H-HEAP): the once-a-year window ran June 1 to 30, 2026, and HCAP says “Applications are closed for 2026.” The next window is in June.
  • Salvation Army Community Assistance Center: Hawaiian Electric's list says it is “Open and accepting applications between the 1st – 10th of each month at sahelp.org,” for up to $1,000, with a disconnection notice and income at or below 50% of area median income. Call (808) 841-5565.
  • Hawaiian Electric payment plans: an interest-free plan over 4 or 6 months, or a due-date extension of up to 15 days, with late fees waived; you must keep paying new bills on time. Residential customers who get an H-HEAP credit are auto-enrolled in the Tier Waiver Provision, which charges the lowest tier rate, for 12 months.

Hawai‘i eviction timeline: what happens and when

An eviction for unpaid rent in Hawai‘i is a “summary possession” case in District Court. These are the steps under the rules in effect now:

  • 1. The 10-day notice. Written notice with at least ten calendar days after you receive it to pay. A posted notice counts as received on the day it is posted; a mailed one, two business days after the postmark (HRS § 521-68(a)). The notice must include the rent owed “after applying all rent paid from all sources” and say a copy went to a mediation center (§ 521-68(b)).
  • 2. Mediation, if you ask for it. Contact the mediation center within the 10 days. If mediation is scheduled in that time and you take part, the landlord cannot file until 20 calendar days after you got the notice (§ 521-68(c)). If mediation ends without an agreement, the landlord can file after those 20 days (§ 521-68(i)).
  • 3. The court case. If you did not pay or schedule mediation in time, the landlord may file for summary possession. The complaint must show proof that the notice went to the mediation center (§ 521-68(d)). Go to every court date. Bring your notice, lease, receipts, texts, and photos of repair problems. A tenant can raise the landlord's failure to keep the home livable as a defense.
  • 4. Only a judge can order you out. The Judiciary says “Eviction can only occur after a judge issues an order permitting the landlord to evict the tenant.” Landlords generally cannot change the locks or shut off utilities.
  • 5. HUD-funded homes. If your home is HUD-funded and federal rules or your lease require 30 days' notice, the Judiciary says the landlord must give both the 30-day notice and the separate 10-day Act 278 notice.
  • 6. Dates to know. Before February 5, 2026, the notice was five business days. A 2021 law, Act 57, once required a 15-day notice and mediation, but it repealed itself by its own terms no later than December 31, 2022, so ignore old flyers about it. Act 278's mediation rules run until February 4, 2028; the 10-day notice stays after that.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • A City and County of Honolulu rent fund. We found no open City rent program on honolulu.gov on November 5, 2026. The pandemic-era O‘ahu rent and utility relief programs have closed. Ignore old flyers.
  • The City's Section 8 list. DCS says “The Waitlist is now closed,” that it is “not expected to reopen for approximately three years,” and that “Rental assistance is not available while you are on the Waiting List.” See our Honolulu Section 8 guide. If you already have a voucher and fall behind, call DCS at (808) 768-7077 right away, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • HPHA public housing and vouchers on O‘ahu. The State's HPHA says its O‘ahu voucher and federal public housing lists are closed. Its Rent Supplement Program for Kūpuna has an open interest list, but joining it “does not guarantee rental assistance.”
  • Ignoring the notice. If you do nothing for 10 days, the landlord may file in court. Calling the mediation center costs nothing.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Honolulu right now?

Yes, for some families. The State's Hawai‘i Relief Program is taking applications, and Catholic Charities Hawai‘i runs it on O‘ahu. It is for families with a child under 18, or a woman in her third trimester, who fell behind because of a listed hardship such as losing a job or income, an emergency medical bill, a disaster, or losing public assistance. It can pay housing costs up to $6,000, for up to four months. Call (808) 521-4357, press 1, then 1 again. Everyone else should dial 211. We found no open City and County of Honolulu rent fund on November 5, 2026.

What is the 10-day notice in Hawai‘i?

Since February 5, 2026, a landlord who wants to evict for unpaid rent must first give a written notice with at least ten calendar days to pay, under HRS 521-68 as changed by Act 278 of 2025. The landlord must also send a copy to a State-funded mediation center. A posted notice counts as received the day it is posted, and a mailed notice two business days after the postmark. If you do not pay or schedule mediation within the 10 days, the landlord may file in court.

How do I get mediation, and does it cost anything?

Contact the mediation center listed on your notice within 10 days of receiving it. On O‘ahu, that is the Mediation Center of the Pacific at (808) 521-6767, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., or Landlordtenant@mediatehawaii.org. The Judiciary says the mediation itself is free. If mediation is scheduled within the 10 days and you take part, the landlord cannot file until 20 days after you got the notice. But if you schedule mediation and then cancel or do not show up, the landlord can ask you to pay its lawyer's fees for mediation.

Do I have to move out by the date on the notice?

No. The Hawai‘i Judiciary says tenants "do not have to move out by the date specified in the written notice" and that eviction "can only occur after a judge issues an order permitting the landlord to evict the tenant." It also says landlords generally cannot remove you, touch your things, change the locks, or shut off utilities. If that happens, call the police and the Legal Aid Society of Hawai‘i.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Honolulu?

Honolulu has no right to counsel, so there is no guaranteed lawyer. Call the Legal Aid Society of Hawai‘i at (808) 536-4302, Monday to Friday, 9 to 11:30 a.m. and 1 to 3:30 p.m., or apply online; its offices do not take walk-ins. Volunteer Legal Services Hawai‘i is at (808) 528-7046, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. The Hawai‘i State Bar Association's Lawyer Referral and Information Service is at (808) 537-9140.

Is the Honolulu Section 8 list open, and can a landlord refuse my voucher?

No, the City and County of Honolulu's Section 8 list is closed. The City says it is not expected to reopen for about three years, and the State's HPHA lists on O‘ahu are closed too. Most landlords cannot refuse a voucher: since May 1, 2023, Act 310 makes refusing a tenant because of Section 8 a discriminatory practice. There are exemptions, including owners of four or fewer units in the State, and a court can fine a landlord up to $2,000 for a first violation.

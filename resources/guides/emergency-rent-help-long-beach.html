Emergency Rent Help in Long Beach: Renter Aid, Free Eviction Lawyers, LA County Prevention Money, and Long Beach's Just Cause Rules

Matt · Updated November 2026

Long Beach is not like the rest of Los Angeles County. The City has its own health department, its own housing authority, and its own homeless services system, so some County programs do not cover you and others do. Here is the honest picture. The City's own rent fund, the Long Beach Renter Aid Program, "has closed its October application period and is not accepting applications at this time," with no reopening date. What is open: free eviction lawyers through the City's Right to Counsel partnership with Stay Housed LA, LA County's new prevention money through the nonprofit PATH, back rent for CalWORKs families through the County, and the City's Multi-Service Center for people about to lose their home. Everything here was checked on November 5, 2026.

Here's the short version: If you got a 3-day notice or court papers, call Stay Housed LA at 1-888-694-0040 today. Email PATH at Pathprevention@epath.org and say you live in Long Beach and are behind on rent. If you have children and get CalWORKs, call the County's DPSS at 1-866-613-3777. If you could lose your home soon, call the City at (562) 570-4500. Dial 2-1-1 for everything else. And if you are sued, file a response within 10 court days, or the landlord can win without a hearing.

California's two clocks, plus Long Beach's Just Cause law

For unpaid rent, a California landlord must first give you a written notice to pay or move out, with "three days' notice, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays" (Code of Civil Procedure 1161). If the landlord then sues, your response "shall be filed within 10 days, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays, after the complaint is served" (Code of Civil Procedure 1167, as amended by AB 2347, effective January 1, 2025). Long Beach adds its own Just Cause for Termination of Tenancies Ordinance (Long Beach Municipal Code Chapter 8.99). It protects "Tenants who have continuously and lawfully lived in the rental unit for 12 months or more," and it requires relocation money for some no-fault evictions. Not paying rent is still a legal reason to evict, so get rent help and a lawyer fast.

Help paying rent in Long Beach: the doors that are left

None of these is a big, open-to-all rent fund, and none promises help. Try every one that fits you, and have your lease, your notice, and proof of income ready.

LA County prevention money through PATH (open)

The Los Angeles County Affordable Housing Solutions Agency (LACAHSA) gives Renter Protection and Homelessness Prevention money to nonprofits, and it says "Emergency rental assistance funds are now available" (checked November 5, 2026). LACAHSA does not take applications itself. Its provider flyer lists Long Beach in the South Bay area, and the South Bay provider is PATH.

  • Who qualifies, per the flyer: you "Must live in Los Angeles County and be in a rental situation," and you "Must have experienced an emergency, crisis, or loss of income within the last 12 months that puts the household at risk of losing housing." Income must be under $84,850 for 1 person, $96,950 for 2, $109,050 for 3, or $121,150 for 4, and "There will be a prioritization for people who are low-income."
  • What it can offer: short-term rental help, case management, and help moving if you have to. It is "temporary and short-term, designed for individuals and families who are currently housed."
  • How to ask: email PATH at Pathprevention@epath.org. Say you live in Long Beach, how much you owe, and whether you have a notice. If PATH says it cannot help at your address, dial 211 and ask which LACAHSA provider serves Long Beach.
  • When it ends: LACAHSA lists this round as running from February 1, 2026, to June 30, 2027. After June 30, 2027, this round is over, and money can run out sooner, so ask now. See LACAHSA's Get Help page for updates.

Long Beach Renter Aid Program (closed for now)

This is the City's own rent program, launched April 23, 2026, as part of its Upstream LB effort to prevent homelessness, with County Measure A money. It pays for overdue rent, short-term rent, past-due utilities and moving costs, and "Approved payments are made directly to landlords or utility providers." The City says "Approximately $2.7 million in rental assistance is available during this funding cycle."

  • Status, in the City's words (checked November 5, 2026): "The Long Beach Renter Aid Program has closed its October application period and is not accepting applications at this time." Also: "We are not accepting new applications or maintaining a waiting list." No reopening date is posted.
  • Who qualifies when it opens: you must "Live in rental housing in Long Beach," have household income "at or below 50% of the Area Median Income (AMI)," and "Have experienced an emergency or unexpected event within the past 12 months that places them at risk of losing housing." The City's tenant page says "Priority goes to people 55 and older who have recently lost income, housing vouchers or other benefits."
  • How to find out when it reopens: check longbeach.gov/renteraid, follow the Long Beach Health Department on social media, or email lbrenteraid@longbeach.gov.

CalWORKs back rent through LA County DPSS

Long Beach is in Los Angeles County, so families on CalWORKs use the County's Department of Public Social Services (DPSS). The state says CalWORKs Homeless Assistance can pay "up to two months of rental arrearages" for "eligible CalWORKs recipients or apparently eligible CalWORKs applicants, who are homeless or at risk of homelessness." DPSS also has other CalWORKs help for families at risk of eviction. Call 1-866-613-3777, Monday to Friday, 7:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., and bring your pay-or-quit notice.

The Multi-Service Center and 211 LA

  • Long Beach Multi-Service Center: the City's homeless services hub at 1301 W. 12th Street. The City says "Experiencing homelessness or at risk and need help now? Call (562)570-4500." Hours: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m.; Thursday, 8 a.m. to 2 p.m.; closed daily from noon to 1 p.m. Call (562) 570-4500.
  • 211 LA: dial 2-1-1 or 800-339-6993, "available 24/7." Ask what rent help is open for a Long Beach address this month.

Free eviction help in Long Beach

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can find mistakes in a notice or lawsuit, file your answer on time, and work out a payment plan or more time.

  • Right to Counsel through Stay Housed LA: the City says it funds "$1.5 million in Right to Counsel eviction defense and education assistance," giving "free legal assistance to low-income residents facing eviction through a partnership with Stay Housed LA." Call Stay Housed LA at 1-888-694-0040 or apply online at stayhousedla.org as soon as you get a notice or court papers.
  • Legal Aid Foundation of Los Angeles (LAFLA): its Long Beach office at 601 Pacific Ave. is "Open to walk-ins," Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Or call the hotline at 800-399-4529, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to noon and 1 to 5 p.m., or apply online at lafla.org.
  • Court self-help: Long Beach eviction cases are heard at the Governor George Deukmejian Courthouse, 275 Magnolia Ave., which has an unlawful detainer courtroom. Its Self-Help Center is in Room 3101. The California Courts' eviction help for tenants has the forms.
  • City questions about Just Cause and relocation: Long Beach Housing & Neighborhood Services, 562-570-6949 or NSB@longbeach.gov. The City notes that it "does not provide legal assistance and does not mediate in tenant and landlord disputes." For housing discrimination, call the Fair Housing Foundation at 562-989-1206.

Utility help in Long Beach

In Long Beach, electricity comes from Southern California Edison (SCE), and gas and water come from the City's own Long Beach Utilities, not SoCalGas.

  • LIHEAP through Long Beach Community Action Partnership (LBCAP): LBCAP runs the energy assistance program called HEAP. Right now it says: "Due to limited funding," "We are scheduling appointments for verified 'service off' customers only." Bring current gas and electric bills (all pages, dated within 45 days), proof of income for every adult for the last 30 days, and a California ID. Call (562) 216-4600 or 1-888-351-4061.
  • SCE CARE and FERA: SCE says "Qualifying households receive 32.5% off their electric bills" with CARE, and "an 18% discount" with FERA. CARE fits a household of 1 or 2 with yearly income up to $43,280, or 4 people up to $66,000; FERA covers $43,281 to $54,100 for 1 or 2 people and $66,001 to $82,500 for 4 (limits from June 1, 2026, to May 31, 2027). Apply online at sce.com or call 1-800-798-5723.
  • Long Beach Utilities: it offers a Low Income Gas Discount Program, a low-income senior or disability discount, a Medical Heating Allowance, and a Flexible Payment Plan. Apply through MyUtilityPortal or call (562) 570-5700.

California eviction timeline in Long Beach: what happens and when

  • 1. The 3-day notice. The notice must state the amount due and who to pay. You get three days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or court holidays (Code of Civil Procedure 1161). Now is the time to email PATH, call Stay Housed LA, and dial 211.
  • 2. The lawsuit. If you do not pay or move, the landlord can file an unlawful detainer case. A notice alone does not end your tenancy.
  • 3. Answer within 10 court days. You have 10 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or court holidays, after you are served (Code of Civil Procedure 1167). The California Courts say "If you didn't file any type of response, the judge can decide without you." If you cannot afford the filing fee, ask for a fee waiver.
  • 4. Trial and judgment. If you answered, the court sets a trial. A settlement can include a payment plan or a move-out date.
  • 5. Just cause. Long Beach's Just Cause ordinance covers tenants who have lived in the unit 12 months or more. California's Tenant Protection Act also applies after a tenant "has continuously and lawfully occupied" a home for 12 months (Civil Code 1946.2), and it caps yearly rent increases at "5 percent plus the percentage change in the cost of living, or 10 percent, whichever is lower" (Civil Code 1947.12). Unpaid rent is an at-fault just cause under both, so these rules matter most when a notice is for something else.
  • 6. Relocation money for no-fault evictions. The City says that for a demolition or substantial remodel, tenants get "either $4,500 or two months of the tenant's rent, whichever is greater," and for other no-fault terminations, "relocation assistance equal to one month of the tenant's rent." It "must be paid within 15 calendar days of issuing the written notice of termination."
  • Lockouts are illegal. A landlord cannot change the locks, remove doors or windows, or shut off your utilities to force you out (Civil Code 789.3). Only the sheriff can remove you, after the court case.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • LAHSA's homelessness prevention programs do not cover Long Beach. LAHSA says its Continuum of Care serves LA County "with the exception of Glendale, Long Beach, and Pasadena." Long Beach residents use the City's Multi-Service Center instead.
  • The LA County Emergency Rent Relief Program is closed. Its site says "Application Period Closed" (checked November 5, 2026). If you applied, call (877) 849-0770 about your status. The pandemic-era Long Beach ERAP is also over.
  • The Housing Authority of the City of Long Beach (HACLB) runs Section 8. It is not an emergency rent fund, and it says its "waiting list is currently closed, and no openings are scheduled at this time" (checked November 5, 2026). LA County's Carmelitos public housing lists in Long Beach took applications from August 17 to September 16, 2026, and are closed again. HACLB also says HUD is ending the Emergency Housing Voucher program, with rental help "estimated to end around October 2026." See our HACLB guide. If you have a voucher and your income dropped, call HACLB at (562) 570-6985 right away.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and call the Multi-Service Center.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Long Beach right now?

Some, but not from the City right now. The Long Beach Renter Aid Program closed its October application period, is not keeping a waiting list, and has not announced a reopening date. LA County's LACAHSA prevention money is open, and its flyer lists Long Beach under PATH: email Pathprevention@epath.org. Families on CalWORKs can call DPSS at 1-866-613-3777 about up to two months of back rent.

When will Long Beach Renter Aid reopen?

The City has not said. Its page says the program "has closed its October application period and is not accepting applications at this time" and that it is not keeping a waiting list. When it opens, it is for Long Beach renters with income at or below 50% of the area median who had an emergency in the last 12 months, with priority for people 55 and older. Check longbeach.gov/renteraid or email lbrenteraid@longbeach.gov.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Long Beach?

Call Stay Housed LA at 1-888-694-0040 as soon as you get a notice or court papers. The City of Long Beach pays for a Right to Counsel program through Stay Housed LA that gives free legal help to low-income residents facing eviction. You can also walk in to the Legal Aid Foundation of Los Angeles office at 601 Pacific Ave., Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., or call 800-399-4529.

Do LA County rent programs cover Long Beach?

Some do and some don't. LACAHSA's prevention money and the County's CalWORKs help through DPSS cover Long Beach. LAHSA's homelessness prevention programs do not, because LAHSA serves the county except Glendale, Long Beach and Pasadena; Long Beach residents use the City's Multi-Service Center at (562) 570-4500 instead. The LA County Emergency Rent Relief Program is closed.

Does Long Beach have its own just cause eviction law?

Yes. Long Beach's Just Cause for Termination of Tenancies Ordinance covers tenants who have lived in the unit for 12 months or more, and California's Tenant Protection Act also applies. Unpaid rent is still a legal reason to evict. For no-fault evictions, the City says landlords must pay relocation money: one month's rent, or for a demolition or major remodel, $4,500 or two months' rent, whichever is more.

Is the Long Beach Section 8 waiting list open?

No. The Housing Authority of the City of Long Beach says its waiting list is currently closed and no openings are scheduled at this time. LA County's Carmelitos public housing lists in Long Beach were open from August 17 to September 16, 2026, and are closed again. HACLB is not an emergency rent fund. Call (562) 570-6985 with questions.

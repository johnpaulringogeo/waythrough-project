What Is Annual Recertification for Section 8 and Public Housing?

Matt · 7 min read

Annual recertification is the yearly review your housing authority does of your household’s income and size to confirm you still qualify for assistance. It happens once a year, usually around the anniversary of your lease, and your PHA normally mails you a notice 60 to 90 days beforehand. If you miss the appointment or the paperwork deadline, your PHA can terminate your voucher or your public housing lease, so treat that notice as time-sensitive mail.

What Is Recertification?

Recertification is a yearly check on your household's income and on who lives with you. Your PHA (or the property management company, if you're in public housing) has to verify that you still qualify for housing assistance and that your income hasn't changed much. Every housing assistance program does it, and it comes around each year near the anniversary of your lease.

During recertification, you report:

  • Current household income from all sources
  • Any changes to household composition (new family members, people who moved out)
  • Changes in employment or benefits
  • Changes in assets or liabilities
  • Other information the PHA needs to verify your continued eligibility

You can't skip it. Recertification is a requirement of your lease and your housing assistance agreement, and missing it can get your voucher or lease terminated.

When Does Recertification Happen?

It's tied to your lease. If your lease started on March 15, 2024, your first annual recertification is due around March 15, 2025. Your PHA will typically notify you 60-90 days before it's due, which is plenty of time to gather documents and schedule an appointment.

Watch for the notice in the mail. A lot of recertification deadlines are missed because the letter didn't get read carefully. When you see a notice from your PHA about recertification, put the deadline on a calendar and set a reminder.

Documents You'll Need

Bring these to your recertification appointment. The exact list varies by PHA, but this covers most situations:

Income Documentation

  • Recent pay stubs: Last 30 days of pay stubs from each employed household member
  • Tax returns: Last year's federal tax return for everyone in the household age 18+
  • Benefits statements: Current statements for Social Security, unemployment, child support, veterans benefits, disability payments, TANF, SNAP, or any other benefits
  • Proof of self-employment income: If applicable, tax returns and quarterly income statements
  • Proof of no income: If a household member is not working, you may need a letter from a job training program, school, or unemployment office

Household and Residency Documents

  • Photo ID: Current driver's license or state ID for each household member (or passport if no state ID)
  • Social Security cards or numbers: Verification for each household member
  • Birth certificates: For children under 18 (sometimes required)
  • Proof of citizenship or eligible status: Passport, green card, or Employment Authorization Document
  • Proof of residency: Lease, utility bill, or other document showing current address

Special Circumstances

  • For new household members: Birth certificate, Social Security number verification, and if employed, current pay stubs
  • For people who moved out: Evidence they no longer live there (letter from new address, or written statement from remaining household members)
  • For dependent students: School enrollment verification
  • For people receiving child support or alimony: Most recent check or court order showing amount
  • For people with assets (savings, investments, property): Bank statements or documentation of the asset value

Keep a Folder All Year

Start a folder now and add copies as documents come in: each new pay stub, each benefits statement. When recertification time comes, everything is already there, and you aren't scrambling to collect it.

What Happens If Your Income Went Up

If your income went up since you last recertified, your rent portion goes up too.

With a voucher or in public housing, your share of the rent is usually about 30% of your monthly adjusted income (your income after HUD's deductions). More income means a bigger 30%.

Example:

  • Last year: $2,000 monthly adjusted income. Your rent portion: $600 (30% of $2,000). PHA pays: $500 (assuming a $1,100 rent within the payment standard).
  • This year: $2,500 monthly adjusted income. Your new rent portion: $750 (30% of $2,500). PHA pays: $350 (depending on payment standard).

You pay more, but your share is still based on about 30% of your adjusted income. The one exception: if your unit's rent is higher than the PHA's payment standard, you pay the difference on top of the 30%.

More income doesn't make you ineligible. You still qualify for housing assistance; you just pay more of the rent yourself. As long as you stay under the income limit for your program (which is usually much higher than the initial qualifying income), you keep your voucher or lease.

What Happens If Your Income Went Down

If your income dropped, your rent portion drops too. You pay less, and the PHA covers more.

Example:

  • Last year: $2,000 monthly adjusted income. Your rent portion: $600. PHA pays: $500.
  • This year: $1,500 monthly adjusted income (you lost a job). Your new rent portion: $450 (30% of $1,500). PHA pays: $650.

This is where housing assistance helps most. After a job loss or another drop in income, recertification should work in your favor. Your rent goes down while you're in crisis, and that gives you some breathing room.

It also means reporting income changes promptly is in your interest. If you lose your job, report it. Your rent will be reduced, and you'll have more money for other expenses while you look for new work.

How the 30% Rule Works

Your share (HUD calls it the Total Tenant Payment) is usually 30% of your monthly adjusted income. A few details decide what that number really is:

  • Start with gross income: The PHA first counts your full income before taxes, not your take-home pay. Wages, overtime, tips, and most benefits count.
  • Then subtract HUD's deductions: amounts for dependents, for an elderly or disabled family, for certain medical and childcare costs. What's left is your adjusted income. Our rent calculation guide shows the current amounts.
  • The 10% floor: Your share is the higher of 30% of adjusted income or 10% of gross income (24 CFR 5.628). The 10% figure only matters when your deductions are very large.
  • Minimum rent: In the voucher and public housing programs, a PHA can set a minimum rent of $0 to $50 a month, and it must waive it if you can't pay because of a financial hardship (24 CFR 5.630).
  • Rent above the payment standard: If your unit's rent is higher than the PHA's payment standard, you pay the difference on top of your share.

Interim Recertifications: Reporting Changes Between Annual Reviews

You don't have to wait for the annual review to report a big change. Most PHAs have a process called "interim recertification" for reporting significant changes as they happen. These include:

  • Job loss or significant income reduction
  • A new household member (birth, custody change, or family member moving in)
  • Someone moving out (a person leaving the household)
  • New employment with a significant income change
  • A change in benefits (Social Security increase, benefit loss, etc.)
  • A major change in assets (inheritance, lottery, significant savings depletion)

Contact your PHA as soon as one of these happens. Most require an interim recertification within 10 days of the change. Reporting fast can work in your favor: if your income drops, your rent adjustment is retroactive to the date you reported it, not the date of annual recertification.

Missing an Interim Recertification

If you don't report a major change and the PHA finds out later, you might owe back rent or face lease violation charges. Report changes promptly, even when it's a hassle.

What Happens If You Miss Your Recertification Appointment

Take this part seriously. If you miss your recertification appointment without contacting the PHA first, this is what typically happens:

  • First notice: The PHA sends a notice saying you missed your appointment and must reschedule
  • Continuing failure: If you continue to miss appointments after multiple notices, the PHA can terminate your voucher or lease
  • Termination: Your housing assistance ends. You lose your voucher or your lease is terminated, and you have to move out or pay full market rent

Of all the ways to lose housing assistance, a missed recertification is one of the easiest to prevent. If you get a notice and can't make the appointment, call your PHA right away and reschedule. If you can't get certain documents, tell them why. Many PHAs will work with you if you stay in touch.

A Missed Recertification Can Cost You the Voucher

Missing recertification can get your voucher or lease terminated and leave you with no housing assistance at all. Never ignore a recertification notice. Put the deadline on a calendar, set reminders on your phone, and do whatever it takes to get to that appointment with the documents.

How the Recertification Appointment Works

Here is what happens once you get there:

  1. Arrive on time with your documents. If it's a virtual appointment, call in on time.
  2. Meet with a recertification specialist. This is usually a PHA employee (or property management employee if you're in public housing).
  3. Review household composition. They'll confirm who lives with you and update any changes.
  4. Review income. You'll go through each income source, and they'll verify amounts using documents you provide.
  5. Complete the recertification form. You'll sign paperwork confirming the information.
  6. Receive your new lease or subsidy amount. You'll get documentation of your new rent portion or any changes to your housing assistance.

The whole appointment usually takes 30-60 minutes. Some PHAs now allow virtual appointments by phone or video, which can save you a trip.

Habits That Make Recertification Easier

Keep Everything Organized

Keep a housing folder all year with copies of:

  • Your lease
  • Your current housing assistance documents
  • Pay stubs as you receive them
  • Benefits statements
  • Any correspondence from your PHA

When recertification time comes, it's all in one place.

Report Changes Promptly

Don't hold income changes or new household members until the annual review. Call your PHA and report them as they happen. That way nothing surprises anyone at recertification, and your rent stays accurate.

Make Copies of Everything

Copy every document before you hand it in, and ask for a copy of the recertification form after your appointment. If there's ever a dispute about what you reported, those copies protect you.

Meet Deadlines Early

Don't wait until the last day to schedule. When the notice comes, call to book the appointment right away. If a document turns out to be hard to get, you'll have time to deal with it.

If You Can't Provide a Document

If you're missing a required document, like a recent pay stub or benefits statement, call your employer or benefits office and explain. Most will provide written verification. Bring whatever documentation you can get, and explain in writing why a specific document is missing. Most PHAs will accept a reasonable explanation and other documentation.

What Happens After Recertification

After your recertification is processed:

  • Your new rent portion becomes effective (usually at the next lease anniversary)
  • Your PHA sends you a new lease or subsidy paperwork confirming the changes
  • If your income increased, you'll pay more. If it decreased, you'll pay less.
  • Your next recertification appointment is scheduled for one year later
  • You'll receive another notice 60-90 days before that date

Missing Documents or Incomplete Information

If the PHA says it needs more information after your appointment, answer quickly. Send the missing documents by mail, email, or in person, and keep track of what you sent and when. The PHA should give you a deadline for the missing information. Meet it.

Making Recertification Routine

Recertification looks complicated, but it's manageable if you stay organized. Treat it like any other important deadline: mark it on your calendar, gather documents ahead of time, and show up prepared.

Your housing assistance depends on getting through recertification every year. When the next notice arrives, call your PHA to book the appointment that day and start pulling your folder together.

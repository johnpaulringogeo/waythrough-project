How-To Guide

Special Purpose Vouchers: HUD-VASH, FUP, FYI, Mainstream, and More

Matt · Updated November 2026

Most people wait years for a regular Section 8 voucher. But Congress also pays for special purpose vouchers: Housing Choice Vouchers set aside for certain groups, like homeless veterans, families at risk of losing their kids to foster care, young people leaving foster care, and adults with disabilities. Once you have one, it works like a regular voucher. You rent from a private landlord and usually pay about 30% of your income. The big difference is how you get in. For several of these programs, a partner agency refers you, and the regular waiting list does not matter.

Here's the short version: Find the program that fits you below. If it uses a partner agency (the VA, child welfare, or your local homeless system), go to that agency and ask for a referral. If it uses the waiting list (Mainstream and NED), get on your housing authority's list and tell them about the disability. Either way, ask your housing authority (PHA) in writing whether it has that program at all, because only PHAs that won funding do.

Homeless or about to be tonight?

A voucher takes weeks at best. Call 2-1-1 or see our guide to finding emergency housing tonight. Veterans can call the National Call Center for Homeless Veterans at 877-424-3838, which the VA says is free and open 24/7.

HUD-VASH: vouchers for homeless veterans

Who qualifies: HUD says HUD-VASH pairs a voucher "for homeless Veterans" with case management and clinical services from the Department of Veterans Affairs. The VA decides who is eligible using its own screening rules.

How you get referred: through the VA, not the housing authority. HUD's rules say VA case managers refer eligible families to the PHA, the PHA must accept those referrals, and it may not use a waiting list or local preferences for HUD-VASH. HUD tells veterans to contact a VA medical center and "mention your interest in HUD-VASH," or call the National Call Center for Homeless Veterans at 877-424-3838.

How to ask: HUD says at least one PHA runs HUD-VASH in every state. Ask the VA homeless program staff which PHA it partners with near you. Our step-by-step guide on how to apply for HUD-VASH covers the VA side in detail.

Family Unification Program (FUP): families and youth

Who qualifies: HUD's FUP page lists two groups.

  • Families for whom the lack of adequate housing is a main factor in a child or children being about to go into out-of-home care, or in a delay in bringing them home. HUD says there is no time limit on FUP family vouchers.
  • Youth ages 18 to 24 who have left foster care, or will leave within 90 days under a transition plan, and who are homeless or at risk of homelessness at age 16 or older. Youth vouchers last 36 months. A youth can get up to 24 more months under the Fostering Stable Housing Opportunities (FSHO) rules if they meet certain requirements.

How you get referred: by the public child welfare agency. HUD says the child welfare agency refers families and youth, then the PHA puts them on its waiting list, checks regular voucher eligibility, and issues the voucher. If you have a caseworker, ask them directly: "Can you refer me for a FUP voucher?"

Deadline for youth: the 36 months end on a set date that your PHA can tell you. At least a few months before then, ask your PHA and caseworker in writing whether you can get the FSHO extension, and what other help you can apply for. If the extension is denied, ask for the reason in writing and whether you can ask for an informal review.

Foster Youth to Independence (FYI)

Who qualifies: HUD's FYI page (which now calls it the Melania Trump Foster Youth to Independence Initiative) describes youth 18 to 24, who "have not reached their 25th birthday," who left foster care or will leave within 180 days, and who are homeless or at risk. Like FUP youth vouchers, FYI help lasts up to 36 months, with the same possible FSHO extension.

How you get referred: the same way as FUP: through the child welfare agency, which must also offer or arrange support services. HUD lets PHAs request FYI vouchers on a rolling basis, so a PHA that does not have them today may be able to get them for you. HUD's FUP page says 90 days and its FYI page says 180 days, so ask your caseworker which window applies.

If you are 25 or older, or you were never in foster care: these two programs won't fit. Look at Mainstream (if you have a disability), Stability Vouchers, and your regular waiting list. Our youth housing page lists other options.

Mainstream vouchers: adults with disabilities

Who qualifies: a household that includes at least one person with a disability who is at least 18 and not yet 62 when the voucher help starts (HUD Notice PIH 2020-01). Families do not "age out" later.

How you get in: this is the one most people miss. HUD says Mainstream vouchers are run "using the same rules as other housing choice vouchers." Many PHAs fill them from their regular waiting list, picking the next family that qualifies. Since 2024, Notice PIH 2024-30 lets PHAs keep a separate Mainstream waiting list and give a preference to people referred by partner agencies, often groups that help people leave nursing homes or other institutions. A PHA that takes direct referrals must post the partner names and contact information.

How to ask: make sure your application lists everyone in the household and says that someone has a disability. Then ask, "Do you have Mainstream vouchers? Do you use a separate list or partner referrals?" HUD also requires a first search time of at least 120 days for a Mainstream voucher. If time is running short, ask for an extension in writing before the voucher expires, and say if you need it because of a disability.

Non-Elderly Disabled (NED) vouchers

Who qualifies: HUD defines NED families as families with a head, co-head, or spouse with a disability who is under age 62. NED is an older, separately funded program. HUD says Mainstream and NED are not the same: the funding and eligibility rules differ.

How you get in: from the regular waiting list. HUD Notice PIH 2013-19 says that when a NED voucher turns over, it must be reissued to the next NED family on the PHA's waiting list. Some NED vouchers ("Category 2") are for people moving out of nursing homes and other health care institutions, with a referral from a partner agency. HUD no longer has a separate NED web page, so its notices are the source.

Emergency Housing Vouchers (EHV): closed to new families

Through EHV, HUD gave PHAs 70,000 vouchers for people who were homeless, at risk, fleeing domestic violence or human trafficking, or recently homeless. As of November 4, 2026, HUD's EHV page says PHAs may not reissue EHVs that turned over after September 30, 2023 (Notice PIH 2023-14), and "very few PHAs have remaining leasing authority." In practice, new families can't count on getting one.

If you already have an EHV: keep following every voucher rule, and ask your PHA in writing what will happen to your help and whether it can move you to another program. Get the answer in writing. If you get a notice ending your help, ask for an informal hearing right away and read our voucher termination guide.

Stability Vouchers

Who qualifies: HUD still lists this program. It serves households that are homeless or at risk, people fleeing or trying to flee domestic violence, dating violence, sexual assault, stalking, or human trafficking, and veterans and families with a veteran who meet one of those tests.

How you get referred: through your local Continuum of Care (CoC), the network that runs homeless services and "coordinated entry." HUD's notice says PHAs must accept Stability Voucher referrals straight from coordinated entry, and those referrals are not added to the regular waiting list. Survivors may also be referred by a victim service provider. Ask a shelter, outreach worker, or 2-1-1 how to get a coordinated entry assessment.

How to ask your PHA whether it has these vouchers

  1. Find your PHA. Use our guide on how to find your housing authority. A county and a city PHA may both serve you, and only one may have a program.
  2. Check HUD's lists. HUD posts award lists by PHA for HUD-VASH, FUP, Mainstream, and Stability Vouchers. They are linked from each program page in the Sources below.
  3. Read the Administrative Plan. Every PHA must have a written Administrative Plan, and it "must be available for public review" (24 CFR 982.54). Many have a chapter on special purpose vouchers that names the partner agencies.
  4. Ask in writing. Email or mail a short letter like the one below. Keep a copy.

Sample request

Dear [Housing Authority],

My name is [name]. I [am on your voucher waiting list, application number ____ / am applying for housing help]. Please tell me in writing:

1. Which special purpose vouchers do you run (HUD-VASH, FUP, FYI, Mainstream, NED, Stability Vouchers)?
2. For each one, who is your referral partner, and are you taking referrals now?
3. Do you have a waiting list preference or a separate list for any of them?
4. [If it applies] A member of my household has a disability and is [age]. Please note this on my application and consider me for Mainstream or NED vouchers.

If I need a reasonable accommodation because of a disability to complete this process, I am asking for one. Thank you. [Name, phone, mailing address, date]

If they say no

  • "We don't have that program." Ask whether a nearby PHA does, and check HUD's award lists. You can apply to more than one PHA's waiting list.
  • "The partner isn't referring right now." Ask the partner agency (VA, child welfare, or CoC) when it expects openings, and ask to be assessed now so you are ready.
  • You were found not eligible. Ask for the reason in writing and for an informal review, and ask for the deadline to request it. Our guide on waiting list preferences explains how preferences and reviews work.
  • Disability-related barriers. You can ask for a reasonable accommodation, such as more time or help with forms. See how to request a reasonable accommodation.
  • Need a lawyer? Legal aid can help with denials. See our guide to free legal help for tenants.

Frequently asked questions

What is a special purpose voucher?

It is a Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher that Congress funded for one group of people, such as homeless veterans (HUD-VASH), families and youth in the child welfare system (FUP and FYI), or adults with disabilities (Mainstream and NED). You still pay about 30% of your income, and the voucher works like a regular one. Only housing authorities that applied for and won these vouchers have them.

Can I apply for HUD-VASH or FUP at the housing authority?

Usually not directly. For HUD-VASH, the VA screens you and refers you to the housing authority. For FUP and FYI, the child welfare agency refers you. For Stability Vouchers, the local Continuum of Care or a victim service provider refers you. These referrals do not go through the regular waiting list.

How do I get a Mainstream voucher?

Mainstream vouchers are for households that include a person with a disability who is 18 to 61. Most housing authorities fill them from their regular voucher waiting list, so get on that list and tell them about the disability. Since 2024, HUD lets housing authorities keep a separate Mainstream list or take referrals from partner agencies, so ask how yours does it.

Can I still get an Emergency Housing Voucher?

Almost certainly not as a new family. HUD says housing authorities may not reissue Emergency Housing Vouchers that turned over after September 30, 2023, and very few have any left to lease. If you already have one, keep following the program rules and ask your housing authority in writing what its plan is for your assistance.

How do I find out if my housing authority has these vouchers?

Ask in writing which special purpose vouchers it runs, who its referral partner is for each one, and whether it is taking referrals now. You can also check HUD's award lists for HUD-VASH, FUP, Mainstream, and Stability Vouchers, and the housing authority's Administrative Plan, which must be available to the public.

Emergency Rent Help in New York City: The One Shot Deal, Homebase, CityFHEPS, and a Free Lawyer

Matt · Updated October 2026

If you rent in New York City and you are behind, the door that pays back rent is the City's One Shot Deal, an Emergency Assistance grant from the Human Resources Administration (HRA). You apply online on ACCESS HRA by starting a Cash Assistance application and choosing "One Shot Deal." You do not need to be on Cash Assistance, and you do not need a court case. Most people have to pay some of it back. If your landlord has taken you to Housing Court, you can get a free lawyer in every ZIP code: call 311 and ask for the Tenant Helpline. Everything here was checked on October 28, 2026.

Here's the short version: Apply for a One Shot Deal today on ACCESS HRA, or at a Benefit Access Center, and have your lease, a rent breakdown from your landlord, proof of income, and ID ready. Call 311 and ask for the nearest Homebase office; Homebase can help with the application and with other prevention help. If you got court papers, ask for a free lawyer and answer the case. Do not skip your court date.

You do not need an eviction notice to apply

HRA's own One Shot Deal FAQ asks "Do I need an eviction notice or court order to apply for a One-Shot Deal?" and answers: "NO – if you are behind in payments, and know you cannot pay your back-rent owed by yourself, you can apply for help today! The sooner you apply, the better." It also says "You do not need to be in Housing Court to get help from HRA" and "You do not have to be a Cash Assistance recipient to receive a one-shot deal" (checked October 28, 2026).

The One Shot Deal (HRA Emergency Assistance): the main door

HRA describes it this way: "Emergency Assistance Grants, also called One Shot Deals, are one-time payments to help people who are experiencing homelessness or at risk of losing their housing, or who have been affected by other emergency situations." Its FAQ adds that a One Shot Deal is "An Emergency Assistance grant available to low-income New Yorkers to help in a crisis," and that One Shot Deals "are regularly used for payment of back-rent owed to prevent an eviction" (checked October 28, 2026).

  • Who can get it: HRA's cash assistance page says you may be able to get help if, among other things, "You are experiencing homelessness or will lose your housing if you do not get help." HRA does not post one income limit for the grant. Its FAQ lists what it looks at: "The need for assistance and related documents (e.g., job loss)," "A plan and income to pay future rent," "Affordability of housing," and "Any additional 'at-risk' factors such as disability."
  • What to have ready (from HRA's FAQ):
    • Your lease in your name, or "confirmation (from landlord/Housing Court) that tenancy will continue with payment of arrears"
    • "Rent bills or letters from the landlord showing monthly rent breakdown and rent owed or court order"
    • "Documents to show current income and resources for all members of the household"
    • Proof of identity for each adult, such as a birth certificate, photo ID, or passport
    • Your Social Security Number, or proof that you applied for one
    • Proof of your citizenship or immigration status
    If a relative or friend will help with future rent, an older HRA flyer still on its site says to bring "Documentation of third-party commitments to help with the rent," and that the person "will need to sign that promise." If you have a court case, bring copies of any stipulations or court orders.
  • How to apply online: HRA's steps on ACCESS HRA are: log in or create an account, select "Benefits," then "Start a New Application," then "Cash Assistance," answer the emergency questions, and select "One Shot Deal" as the type of benefit. Submit, then upload your documents with the ACCESS HRA Mobile App. If you already have an open Cash Assistance case, do not start a new application: link your case with "Find My Case," open "View Case," and choose "Request Special Grant."
  • In person or by phone: HRA says you can also apply at "your nearest Benefit Access Center." For questions, call the DSS OneNumber / HRA InfoLine at 718-557-1399.
  • The interview: HRA says, "You must have an interview to get Emergency Assistance or a One Shot Deal."
  • How long it takes: HRA says it decides "as soon as possible" after it has everything it needs, and "will send you a notice if they need more information or documentation from you." The older HRA flyer says it "can take 30-45 days to receive a decision once all documents have been submitted," but that "a determination will be made in advance of a payment date specified in a court stipulation/order." You get the decision by mail, and you can check status on ACCESS HRA or at 718-557-1399.
  • Who gets the money: "Checks are issued directly to the landlord or Housing Court, as applicable."

Paying it back: the rules in HRA's words

  • "NY State law requires most people to pay HRA back at least some of the money, though people receiving SSI are not required to repay."
  • "In the application process, if rent arrears are identified, you are asked to sign a NY State-required repayment agreement in ACCESS HRA. The determination notice will inform you whether or not the grant must be repaid under State law."
  • HRA's cash assistance page adds: "If your income is more than a certain amount, you may be required to sign an agreement that you will pay back the Emergency Assistance," and "HRA will let you know if you need to pay it back."
  • Had one before? "You may apply for a one-shot deal even if you had one in the past. If you had a one-shot deal that you did not pay back, you may not be approved for another one. If you made payments, you will have a better chance. You will need to show HRA new reasons for falling behind."

Homebase: eviction prevention in your neighborhood

Homebase is the City's network of neighborhood homelessness prevention offices, run by nonprofits for the Department of Social Services. HRA says "Homebase provides New Yorkers experiencing housing instability in the community with various homeless prevention services," including "Services to prevent eviction," "Assistance obtaining public benefits," "Emergency rental assistance," "Financial counseling and money management," "Help relocating," and "Short-term financial assistance" (checked October 28, 2026).

  • Who it is for: people who "Are at imminent risk of entering the New York City shelter system," "Are low-income," and "Want to remain stably housed in your community."
  • How to reach it: HRA's Homebase page has a locator by ZIP code, and says "Anyone seeking Homebase services should call the office in advance to schedule an appointment." You can also call 311.
  • Homebase is also the way into CityFHEPS if you still have housing (see below).

CityFHEPS: ongoing rent help, for some households

CityFHEPS is the City's rental assistance voucher. HRA says it "pays part of eligible tenants monthly rent anywhere in New York State for up to five years." It is not a quick fix for a one-time debt, and most people who are still housed do not qualify. HRA's FAQ for people in the community (dated January 23, 2026) says households "must have a gross income at or below 200% of the federal poverty level" and meet one of four criteria (checked October 28, 2026):

  • The household includes someone who served in the U.S. Armed Forces and is at risk of homelessness;
  • The household gets Pathway Home benefits and would be eligible if it were in DHS or HRA shelter;
  • The household was referred by a CityFHEPS qualifying program, and DSS decided CityFHEPS was needed to avoid shelter entry; or
  • The household "is facing eviction in court (or was evicted in the past year)" and includes someone who has lived in a DHS shelter before, or someone with an active Adult Protective Services case or in a designated community guardianship program, or lives in a rent-controlled apartment and will use CityFHEPS to stay there.

How to apply: "To apply for CityFHEPS, you must contact one of the DSS nonprofit service providers called Homebase." If you are in a DHS shelter, ask your case manager or housing specialist. HRA says "Only DSS can determine your eligibility." The five-year limit "does not apply to households that include someone who is 60 years old or older, or that include an adult who receives federal disability benefits." HRA also says landlords "cannot refuse to rent to you because you are receiving CityFHEPS"; report refusals to the NYC Commission on Human Rights at 212-416-0197.

Right to Counsel: a free lawyer in Housing Court

New York City's Right-to-Counsel program is run by HRA's Office of Civil Justice. In the City's words, "Tenants facing eviction in Housing Court or NYCHA administrative proceedings have access to free legal representation or advice," and the "legal services are free and available in every ZIP code, regardless of immigration status" (checked October 28, 2026). It covers a pending eviction case in Housing Court, a warrant of eviction, a NYCHA termination of tenancy case, and a lockout without a court order or warrant, among other situations.

  • At court: at your first court appearance, say "I would like an attorney," or speak with anyone wearing a teal "Speak with a free lawyer" button.
  • By phone: call 311 and ask for the Tenant Helpline, or call Housing Court Answers at 718-557-1379 or 212-962-4795, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.
  • By email: civiljustice@hra.nyc.gov, with your name, phone number, and your Housing Court index number if you have one.
  • More on our free legal help for tenants page.

Housing Court basics: what happens in a nonpayment case

These steps come from the New York State courts' pages for New York City Housing Court (checked October 28, 2026). The Housing Court "hears and decides disputes between residential landlords and tenants in New York City."

  • 1. The rent demand. Before a nonpayment case can start, the landlord must demand the rent and warn that you can be evicted. "The demand must be in writing and must be delivered to the tenant at least 14 days before the court case is started." Apply for a One Shot Deal as soon as you get it, or sooner.
  • 2. The petition. If the rent is not paid, the landlord can file a nonpayment petition and must have it served on you.
  • 3. Answer within 10 days. "If you are served with a nonpayment petition you must answer within 10 days after you have received the notice of petition." You can answer by talking to the clerk at the courthouse or in writing. "The clerk will set a date for trial between 3 days and 8 days after you answer." This is also when you can ask for a free lawyer.
  • 4. Do not miss a date. The courts warn that if you do not answer and appear, the landlord "may be able to enter a possessory judgment and/or a money judgment against you." Your first courtroom is called the Resolution Part.
  • 5. A marshal's notice. "If you have received a notice from a marshal that you are to be evicted you must come to court as soon as possible." At the courthouse you can ask a judge to stop or delay the eviction with an Order to Show Cause. Bring the notice, your rent receipts, and your One Shot Deal paperwork.
  • Court information line: the Civil Court's landlord-tenant information number is 646-386-5750.

Our step-by-step guide to avoiding eviction covers the whole process, and understanding landlord notices explains the paper you got.

NYCHA and Section 8: not an emergency rent fund

  • NYCHA Section 8 is closed. NYCHA says its Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) waitlist "is currently closed to new applicants," and that on August 1, 2025 it "paused active outreach, including activities related to the application process" for the general waitlist (checked October 28, 2026). It still accepts referrals for HUD-VASH and Stability Vouchers through partner agencies. Our NYCHA page has the details.
  • NYCHA public housing still takes applications online through ApplyNYCHA. It is a long-term housing option, not rent help.
  • Already in NYCHA or on a NYCHA voucher and behind? Call NYCHA's Customer Contact Center at 718-707-7771 (Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.) and apply for a One Shot Deal. If NYCHA starts a termination of tenancy case, Right to Counsel covers it.
  • HPD Section 8 is a separate City program, and HPD says "Applications are not accepted by the general public."

311: the switchboard

Dial 311 from any phone in New York City. It is the way to the Tenant Helpline for a free lawyer, to the Homebase office for your ZIP code, and to the NYC Commission on Human Rights if a landlord refuses your voucher. If you could lose your home tonight, 311 can also give you the shelter intake locations: the City lists the PATH intake center for families with children at 151 East 151st Street in the Bronx, open 24 hours. See I need housing tonight and our New York City housing resources page for the full list.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get emergency rent help in New York City?

Apply for a One Shot Deal, HRA's Emergency Assistance grant for back rent. Apply online on ACCESS HRA by starting a Cash Assistance application and choosing One Shot Deal, or go to a Benefit Access Center. You do not need to be on Cash Assistance or have a court case, but you will need an interview. For help, call the HRA InfoLine at 718-557-1399, or call 311 and ask for your local Homebase office.

Do I have to pay back a One Shot Deal?

Usually some of it. HRA says New York State law requires most people to pay HRA back at least some of the money, but people receiving SSI do not have to repay. If rent arrears are found, you are asked to sign a repayment agreement in ACCESS HRA, and the decision notice tells you whether the grant must be repaid. An unpaid past grant can make it harder to get another one.

What documents do I need for a One Shot Deal?

HRA lists your lease in your name (or confirmation from the landlord or Housing Court that the tenancy will continue once the arrears are paid), rent bills or a landlord letter showing the monthly rent and what you owe, proof of current income and resources for everyone in the household, proof of identity for each adult, your Social Security Number or proof you applied for one, and proof of citizenship or immigration status. Bring any court papers too.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in NYC?

Under the City's Right-to-Counsel program, tenants facing eviction in Housing Court or NYCHA proceedings can get free legal help in every ZIP code, regardless of immigration status. Call 311 and ask for the Tenant Helpline, call Housing Court Answers at 718-557-1379 or 212-962-4795, or say "I would like an attorney" at your first court date.

Who qualifies for CityFHEPS if I am not in a shelter?

HRA says household gross income must be at or below 200% of the federal poverty level, and you must meet one of four criteria: a veteran at risk of homelessness; a household on Pathway Home benefits; a referral from a qualifying program; or facing eviction in court (or evicted in the past year) plus a past DHS shelter stay, an active Adult Protective Services case, or a rent-controlled apartment. You apply through a Homebase office.

Is the NYCHA Section 8 waiting list open?

No. NYCHA says its Section 8 waitlist is currently closed to new applicants, and it paused active outreach for the general waitlist on August 1, 2025. NYCHA public housing still takes applications through ApplyNYCHA. Neither is emergency rent help. If you already live in NYCHA or have a NYCHA voucher and fall behind, call 718-707-7771 and apply for a One Shot Deal.

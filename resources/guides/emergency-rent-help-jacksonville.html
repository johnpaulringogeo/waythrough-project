Emergency Rent Help in Jacksonville: Who Pays, the Eviction Diversion Program, and Free Eviction Help

Matt · Updated October 2026

If you rent in Jacksonville or anywhere in Duval County and you are behind, there are three real doors for rent money. The City of Jacksonville's Emergency Financial Assistance Program takes appointments by phone at (904) 255-2469, starting at 9:30 a.m. on weekdays. The Jacksonville Eviction Diversion Program can pay up to three months of back rent once you have a late notice or an eviction case, but your landlord has to start it. And nonprofit rent help from groups like JFCS and Catholic Charities starts with a referral from United Way 211. If you already have court papers, call Jacksonville Area Legal Aid at (904) 356-8371 the same day. Everything here was checked on October 15, 2026.

Here's the short version: Call the City's appointment line at 9:30 a.m. sharp; appointments are for the next business day and go fast. Ask your landlord, in writing, to send the Eviction Diversion Program's consent form to evictioncoordinator@coj.net. Dial 2-1-1 (or (904) 632-0600) and ask for a rent referral. If you were served with an eviction summons, you have only 5 business days to respond in court, so call Jacksonville Area Legal Aid right away.

Florida moves fast: the 3-day notice and the 5-day answer

Under Florida Statutes section 83.56(3), a landlord can end your lease if rent is still unpaid 3 days after a written demand, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, and court holidays. Once you are served with an eviction summons, you have 5 days (again not counting weekends and legal holidays) to file an answer, and if you raise any defense other than "I paid," section 83.60(2) says you must also pay the rent owed into the court registry in that time. Missing that step is treated as giving up your defenses, and the landlord can get a default judgment. Do not wait on a rent application to protect your court case.

Help paying rent in Jacksonville: the doors that pay

Apply to more than one. Each runs on its own money and its own rules, and none of them promises help.

City of Jacksonville Emergency Financial Assistance Program

This is the City's own door, run by the Social Services Division. In the City's words, it "provides temporary financial assistance to prevent eviction for non-payment of rent/mortgage or interruption of utilities." Payments go straight to the landlord or utility, "in an amount and for a period of time specified by policy." Its page carries no closure or funding notice (checked October 15, 2026).

  • How to get in: by phone only. "The appointment line opens at 9:30 a.m., Monday through Friday (except holidays). Appointments are given on a 'first caller, first-service' basis for the next business day." The City adds that "appointments fill quickly" and that "Individuals with court-ordered eviction notices are given priority." Call (904) 255-2469.
  • Who qualifies, in the City's words: you must be a Duval County resident; "Your household income must not exceed 200 percent of the federal poverty level"; you must be in "a documentable crisis situation" such as job loss, lost income, medical bills, car repairs, a family breakup, or a death in the family; and you must have "a delinquent, eviction, foreclosure or shut-off notice or loss of utility service."
  • Who it is not for: anyone the program helped "within the past 24 months," and anyone who could not meet basic needs before the crisis ("You must have had the capacity to meet basic needs before suffering the current financial hardship").
  • What to bring: a valid Florida driver's license or ID with a Duval County address (the City says no passports), photo ID for every adult, Social Security documentation for everyone in the home, proof of all household income for the past 90 days, your lease, and the eviction notice.
  • One more step: you will take a two-hour budgeting and money management workshop as part of the help.
  • If you are turned down: "all potential clients that were denied and are seeking assistance must wait 90 days before they can obtain a new appointment."

The office is at Midtown Centre, 1809 Art Museum Drive, Suite 100, open 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. on weekdays. The Social Services main line is (904) 255-3333, but appointments come only through (904) 255-2469.

The Jacksonville Eviction Diversion Program (JEDP)

The City set aside money for tenants with "a short-term problem paying rent." The program "is able to assist with up to three (3) months' back rent," and its goal is to "stabilize housing for at least six months" afterward. The Duval County Clerk's eviction page says "Jacksonville currently operates an eviction diversion program" (checked October 15, 2026). Its partners are the City, United Way, the Fourth Judicial Circuit, the Clerk, Jacksonville Area Legal Aid, and JFCS.

Your landlord has to start it

The program's own words: "you must communicate with your landlord to have the landlord sign the consent form ... and email it to: evictioncoordinator@coj.net. TENANTS CANNOT SUBMIT CONSENT FORMS FOR THEMSELVES." A coordinator calls you only after the landlord sends the form. Ask your landlord in writing, and keep a copy. There is a separate "Non-court Consent Form" for landlords who have not filed in court yet.

  • Who qualifies (you must meet all of these): a U.S. citizen or legal resident alien; living in Duval County, including the Beaches and Baldwin; behind on rent; a household that includes "a child under the age of 18 years, OR a senior over the age of 60 years, OR a Veteran, OR a disabled adult"; a one-time hardship, with a ledger showing you paid on time before it; income at or below the ALICE threshold for your household size; an eviction notice served on you or an eviction case filed; and the ability to stay housed after the payment.
  • The three-month cap: "If more than three (3) months of rent is owed, the tenant will be required to pay the ineligible amounts PRIOR to EDP cutting a check."
  • Once in a lifetime: the program calls itself "a ONE-TIME FINANCIAL ASSISTANCE AWARD, meaning that EDP cannot cut more than one check per candidate in their lifetime."
  • Documents you send within 3 business days of your interview: the last 2 months of bank statements; proof of income (pay stubs, a SNAP letter, child support, SSI or SSDI letter, or retirement); photo ID and Social Security cards for every adult; and birth certificates for children 17 and under.
  • What it does for your court case: the filed consent form "begins a 30-day extension of eviction lawsuit." Until the coordinator files it, treat every court deadline as still running.

Is there a phone number? No. The program's site lists only an email address, evictioncoordinator@coj.net, and it says coordinators "cannot accept any documents from the tenant prior to the landlord initiating enrollment." Its site, with the eligibility quiz and forms, is jacksonvilleevictiondiversion.org. If you need to talk to a person about your case, call Jacksonville Area Legal Aid, one of the program's partners.

United Way 211: the referral door for nonprofit rent help

In Jacksonville, two of the biggest nonprofit rent programs will not see you without a 211 referral first. United Way of Northeast Florida's 211 is "Available 24/7" and free. To reach it: "Dial 2-1-1, call 1-904-632-0600 or text 'Hello' to 211904." Ask for a rent or eviction-prevention referral and write down every name and number they give you.

  • LJD Jewish Family & Community Services (JFCS): offers "Rent and mortgage assistance, as available," and says "You must first receive a referral from the United Way before you can access JFCS assistance." It does not take walk-ins for financial help, and it notes that "funding availability fluctuates." JFCS is open to people of all faiths. Questions: (904) 448-1933.
  • Catholic Charities Jacksonville: helps with "rent, mortgage, utilities or deposits," and its page says to "call the United Way for a referral by dialing 2-1-1 or 904-632-0600."

Eviction help in Jacksonville: Jacksonville Area Legal Aid

If you searched for eviction help in Jacksonville, this is the answer with a name. Jacksonville Area Legal Aid (JALA) is a nonprofit law firm that gives free civil legal help to eligible residents. Its Duval County office is at 126 W. Adams St. and its number is (904) 356-8371. It does not pay rent, but a lawyer can help you answer the summons, check the landlord's notice, and work out a deal.

  • If you were served: JALA's apply page says that if it is "a time sensitive situation such as you have been served with an eviction notice," you should "call the office directly and state that this is a legal emergency and you need immediate legal help."
  • Other ways to apply: fill out JALA's online intake form, and staff will reach you by email or phone "within the next 3 to 5 business days." That is too slow if you have a 5-day deadline, so call too.
  • Who qualifies: "Generally, JALA will consider representation of clients that are within 200% of the poverty line." Its fair housing unit and its help for people 60 and older do not depend on income.
  • Eviction Defense Self-Help Project: "Have you been served with an eviction summons? Does your rental home need repairs? Get help preparing your answer or creating your letter." It runs every Wednesday afternoon by phone or video, by appointment only; call (904) 356-8371 to apply. JALA lists no clinic on November 11 or 25, or on December 23 or 30, 2026.

Florida eviction timeline: what happens and when

Florida's eviction rules are in Chapter 83 of the Florida Statutes. These are the steps for a rent case, in order:

  • 1. The 3-day notice. The landlord delivers a written demand for rent or possession. You have 3 days to pay, "excluding Saturday, Sunday, and legal holidays" (section 83.56(3)). The Duval Clerk adds: "do not include the day of delivery."
  • 2. The eviction complaint. If you do not pay, the landlord can file in county court. Florida uses a fast "summary procedure" for these cases (sections 83.59(2) and 51.011).
  • 3. Your 5 days. After you are served, you must file a written answer with the Clerk "within five days of receipt of the summons, not including weekends or legal holidays," in the Clerk's words. If you dispute anything other than payment, you also have to pay the rent the landlord claims into the court registry, or file a motion asking the court to decide the amount, in those same 5 days (section 83.60(2)). If you get Section 8 or other rent help, you deposit only your share of the rent.
  • 4. Judgment and the 24-hour notice. If the landlord wins, the Clerk issues a writ of possession. The sheriff posts it and can return after 24 hours, and "Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays do not stay the 24-hour notice period" (section 83.62(1)).
  • Lockouts are illegal. A landlord may not shut off your utilities or change the locks to force you out (section 83.67(1) and (2)). A landlord who does can owe you your "actual and consequential damages or 3 months' rent, whichever is greater," plus court costs and attorney's fees (section 83.67(6)).

Not sure whether a case was filed? The Duval County Clerk's civil department answers at (904) 255-2000. It cannot give legal advice, but it can tell you about your case. What is not a door for emergency rent

  • Jacksonville Housing (JH), the housing authority for Jacksonville and Duval County, runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund, and its site says "All Waiting Lists(s) are officially closed effective Wednesday, May 31st, 2023" (checked October 15, 2026). If you already have a voucher or live in public housing and fall behind, call JH at 904-630-3810 right away. Our Jacksonville Housing page has the waitlist details.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Jacksonville?

Yes, but not as one program. The City of Jacksonville's Emergency Financial Assistance Program takes appointments at (904) 255-2469 starting at 9:30 a.m. on weekdays. The Jacksonville Eviction Diversion Program can pay up to three months of back rent, but your landlord has to start it. JFCS and Catholic Charities help with rent only after a United Way 211 referral, so dial 2-1-1 or call (904) 632-0600. All of them depend on limited funding, so apply to more than one.

What is the phone number for the Jacksonville Eviction Diversion Program?

It does not publish one. The program's only listed contact is the email evictioncoordinator@coj.net, and your landlord, not you, has to email the signed consent form there. A coordinator then calls you. For questions about your eviction case, call Jacksonville Area Legal Aid at (904) 356-8371.

Who qualifies for the Jacksonville Eviction Diversion Program?

You must meet every rule: live in Duval County, be a U.S. citizen or legal resident alien, and have a child under 18, a senior over 60, a veteran, or a disabled adult in the home. You also need a one-time hardship, income at or below the ALICE threshold, an eviction notice or eviction case, and the ability to pay rent going forward. It pays up to three months of back rent, once in a lifetime.

How do I get an appointment with the City's Emergency Assistance Program?

Call (904) 255-2469 when the line opens at 9:30 a.m., Monday through Friday. Appointments are first come, first served for the next business day, and people with court-ordered eviction notices go first. Your household income must be at or below 200 percent of the federal poverty level, and you cannot have received help from the program in the past 24 months.

Can I get a free lawyer for an eviction in Jacksonville?

Maybe. Jacksonville Area Legal Aid gives free legal help to people who qualify, generally households within 200 percent of the poverty line. If you were served, call (904) 356-8371 and say it is a legal emergency. Its Eviction Defense Self-Help Project helps you prepare your answer by phone on Wednesday afternoons, by appointment.

How fast is an eviction in Florida?

Very fast. The notice to pay is 3 days, not counting weekends and court holidays. After you are served, you have 5 business days to answer and, if you raise any defense other than payment, to pay the rent into the court registry. If the landlord wins, the sheriff can remove you 24 hours after posting the writ, even over a weekend.

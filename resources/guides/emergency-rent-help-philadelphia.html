Emergency Rent Help in Philadelphia: What's Open Now and Who to Call

Matt · Updated October 2026

If you rent in Philadelphia and you are behind, there is no city rent application you can fill out on your own right now. The City's main rent money, called Targeted Financial Assistance (TFA), is paid through the Eviction Diversion Program, and only your landlord can start that application. TFA can pay up to $3,500 you owe, plus two more months of rent, straight to your landlord. Your landlord must go through Eviction Diversion before filing an eviction in court. You also have free help: the Eviction Diversion Tenant Hotline at 215-523-9501 and the Philly Tenant Hotline at 267-443-2500 for free legal help.

Here's the short version: If your landlord sent you a "Notice of Diversion Rights," watch for a text or email from the program and fill out the tenant form within five days. If you hear nothing within seven days, call 215-523-9501. Ask your landlord to apply for Targeted Financial Assistance if you owe $3,500 or less. If you are close to losing your home, call the City's Homelessness Prevention InfoLine at (215) 686-7177. If you have court papers, call 267-443-2500 the same day, and go to your hearing.

What is open right now (checked October 15, 2026)

City rental assistance: the City's rent assistance site says "The City of Philadelphia, and its community development agency PHDC, work together to provide rental assistance to tenants in Philadelphia when funding is available." For back rent, that help is Targeted Financial Assistance through Eviction Diversion. The same site says "only landlords may apply for EDP; tenants cannot apply on their own."

FreshStartPHL (move-in help): closed. The City's site says "This program is now closed, and will reopen when funding is available."

PHA Section 8 waitlist: closed. PHA says "The HCV waitlist is currently closed."

Help paying rent in Philadelphia: Targeted Financial Assistance

The Eviction Diversion Program describes Targeted Financial Assistance this way: "a one-time payment from the City to a landlord to cover a tenant's past liabilities. So long as a tenant will continue living in the unit for at least two more months, the payment also includes two additional months of rent."

  • How much you owe: "Tenant arrears are $3,500 or less, as shown on a ledger or rent roll." If you owe more, your landlord can agree to forgive the amount over $3,500 so the case can still be considered for TFA.
  • Your income: "Tenant income is less than 80% of the Area Median Income (AMI)."
  • No recent filing: your landlord cannot have filed an eviction against you for the same unit within one year of the Eviction Diversion application.
  • Other problems settled: if there are issues besides unpaid rent, like repairs, you and your landlord must sign a written agreement that settles them. Mediation in the program can get you there.
  • Once per tenant: "A landlord can only receive TFA once for the same tenant and unit."
  • What it does not pay: security deposits or moving costs.

What it protects: a landlord who takes TFA agrees that everything you owed on the application date is paid off. The landlord also cannot file an eviction for two months after the rent months TFA covered. The program calls this the "TFA Protection Period."

One warning: "a court filing against the tenant by the landlord will result in the disqualification of a pending or future TFA application." If your landlord is talking about court, ask them to stay in the program while TFA is reviewed.

Philadelphia Eviction Diversion Program: what tenants need to know

Under Philadelphia Code section 9-811, "All landlords of residential properties are required to participate in EDP before filing a Landlord-Tenant complaint in Municipal Court." The landlord must take part in good faith for at least 30 days after the application is approved. The program is free.

How it works, step by step

  • Your landlord sends you a Notice of Diversion Rights and then applies online. You cannot apply yourself. The program says, "Do not fill out this application - your landlord must submit the application."
  • You get a text or email from the program at the contact information your landlord gave. If you got the notice but no message within seven days, call the Eviction Diversion Tenant Hotline at 215-523-9501, Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m.
  • Fill out the Tenant Intake Form within five days. The program says tenants who miss that window "risk ineligibility for program services," including TFA.
  • A housing counselor meets with you before mediation to look at your budget and any other problems, and goes to mediation with you.
  • Mediation happens by phone, on weekdays between 9 a.m. and 5 p.m. Sessions are usually an hour or less. You get the call-in number and code 48 hours before.
  • Check your status any time on the program's status page, using the EDP2 application number in your texts or emails.

If your landlord hasn't used the program, you can ask for a voluntary mediation through CORA Good Shepherd Mediation at 215-843-5413, extension 2. That does not count as your landlord's required Eviction Diversion step.

If you live in PHA public housing, PHA has a separate Eviction Diversion process. That rule does not cover Section 8 voucher holders or other subsidized housing.

Eviction help in Philadelphia: free lawyers and the Right to Counsel

The City says "Philadelphia's Right to Counsel law guarantees free legal representation to eligible low-income renters." It covers eviction cases, lease terminations, and PHA housing subsidy terminations.

  • Who qualifies: your income is "at or below 200% of the federal poverty level," and you live in a covered ZIP code: 19121, 19124, 19131, 19132, 19134, 19139, 19141, 19144, 19153, or 19154. "Your immigration status will not affect your eligibility."
  • How to ask: call the Philly Tenant Hotline at 267-443-2500. It is staffed by the Tenant Union Representative Network, and interpreters are available. If you qualify, staff refer you to a lawyer at Community Legal Services, the Legal Clinic for the Disabled, or SeniorLAW Center.
  • Not in a covered ZIP code? Call anyway. The City says that if you don't qualify, "the staff will still try to connect you with other services or advice."

Community Legal Services (CLS)

For landlord-tenant problems, CLS says to call 267-443-2500, Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. You can also walk in to its Center City office, 1424 Chestnut St., Monday through Thursday, 9 a.m. to 12 p.m., or its North Philadelphia office, 1410 W. Erie Ave., on Tuesdays and Thursdays, 9 a.m. to 12 p.m. CLS provides interpreters at no cost.

If you are about to lose your home: the Homelessness Prevention InfoLine

The City's Office of Homeless Services says that if you are facing homelessness, you may qualify for "Financial help with rent, move-in costs, and utilities," as well as mediation and emergency shelter. "Assistance is based on available funding."

  • Call: the Homelessness Prevention InfoLine at (215) 686-7177. The City notes that the line "plays a recorded message only," so listen to the instructions.
  • Or go in person: Appletree Family Center, 1430 Cherry St., or the Roosevelt Darby Center, 804 N. Broad St., both Monday through Friday, 7 a.m. to 5 p.m.
  • Bring: "Birth certificates and Social Security cards for all household members" and proof of income for everyone in the household, dated within the last 30 days. Copies are fine.

211 in Philadelphia

PA 211 Southeast is free and answers 24 hours a day, every day. Dial 2-1-1 or text your ZIP code to 898-211 to talk with a resource specialist about rent, utilities, and shelter.

Philadelphia Housing Authority (PHA): not an emergency fund

PHA runs Section 8 and public housing, but it does not pay a private landlord's back rent. On October 15, 2026, PHA's Housing Choice Voucher page said "The HCV waitlist is currently closed," and that the public will be told by TV, radio, print ads, and its website when it reopens. It was last opened in January 2023. If you applied then, you can check your status with the HCV Customer Support Center at 215-684-4300. Our Philadelphia Housing Authority page has more.

If you already have a court date

Eviction cases in Philadelphia are heard in Municipal Court. The Eviction Diversion Program is clear: "If you have a court date you MUST attend, regardless of what is happening through EDP. Mediation is NOT a hearing date."

  • Call the Philly Tenant Hotline the same day at 267-443-2500.
  • Ask whether your landlord went through Eviction Diversion before filing. The law requires it for residential landlords.
  • Know your rights at home. The court says landlords "cannot change the locks, cut off utilities or remove a tenant's belongings from the property." Only the court process can evict you.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get help paying rent in Philadelphia?

The City's main rent help for back rent is Targeted Financial Assistance, paid through the Eviction Diversion Program. Only your landlord can apply. It can cover up to $3,500 you owe plus two more months of rent, if your income is under 80% of the area median. For questions, call the Eviction Diversion Tenant Hotline at 215-523-9501. If you are facing homelessness, call the Homelessness Prevention InfoLine at (215) 686-7177.

Can I apply for Philadelphia rental assistance myself?

Not for Targeted Financial Assistance. The City says only landlords may apply to the Eviction Diversion Program, and tenants cannot apply on their own. You can ask your landlord to apply, call the tenant hotline, or contact the agencies on the City's rent help list, such as Catholic Social Services or UESF.

What is the Philadelphia Eviction Diversion Program?

A free City program that landlords must use before filing an eviction in Municipal Court. It offers mediation by phone, a housing counselor for the tenant, and sometimes Targeted Financial Assistance. The landlord must take part in good faith for at least 30 days after the application is approved.

Who qualifies for a free eviction lawyer in Philadelphia?

Under the Right to Counsel law, renters with income at or below 200% of the federal poverty level who live in ZIP codes 19121, 19124, 19131, 19132, 19134, 19139, 19141, 19144, 19153, or 19154. Immigration status does not matter. Call the Philly Tenant Hotline at 267-443-2500. If you don't qualify, staff will still try to connect you with other help.

Is FreshStartPHL open?

No. The City's rent assistance site says FreshStartPHL, its move-in assistance program, "is now closed, and will reopen when funding is available."

Is the PHA Section 8 waitlist open?

No. PHA says the Housing Choice Voucher waitlist is currently closed and was last opened in January 2023. PHA will announce a reopening on TV, radio, in print, and on its website.

Emergency Rent Help in Tucson: Pima County's Emergency Services Network, Local Charities, Free Eviction Lawyers, and the 5-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Tucson and you are behind, there is no single rent fund. The doors that pay are the agencies in Pima County's Emergency Services Network, starting with the Pima County Community Action Agency at 520-724-2667. The County is honest that "Rent, mortgage, and utility assistance is limited and based on grant funding." Interfaith Community Services takes rent help calls at (520) 297-6049 from 9 a.m., Monday to Thursday, when it has funds, and the Primavera Foundation answers at 520-623-5111. The City of Tucson does not run its own rent fund, and its Section 8 and public housing waiting lists have been closed since January 1, 2026. For eviction help, Pima County's Emergency Eviction Legal Services (EELS) gives free lawyers to tenants who qualify: call 520-724-3357. Everything here was checked on November 3, 2026.

Here's the short version: Gather the documents on the County's list (below) first, because the County asks you to have them before you call. Then call Pima County Community Action at 520-724-2667, and on Monday to Thursday call Interfaith Community Services at 9 a.m. sharp. If you got a 5-day notice or court papers, call EELS at 520-724-3357 or Southern Arizona Legal Aid at 520-623-9461 the same day. Dial 2-1-1 (9 a.m. to 7 p.m., every day) for more options. Then go to your court hearing.

A 5-day notice is not an eviction

In Arizona, a landlord who is owed rent must first give you a written notice. Under A.R.S. § 33-1368(B), if you don't pay "within five days after written notice," the landlord may then file a special detainer (eviction) case in court. The law says these are calendar days, so weekends count. You can still save the lease. If you pay "all past due and unpaid periodic rent and a reasonable late fee set forth in a written rental agreement" before the landlord files, the law says the rental agreement "shall be reinstated." After the case is filed, paying still works, but you must also pay the landlord's attorney fees and court costs. After a judgment, letting you stay is up to the landlord (checked November 3, 2026). Keep proof of every payment. A 5-day notice is also one of the papers Tucson rent programs ask for, so keep it.

Emergency rental assistance in Tucson: the doors that pay

Each of these runs on grants and runs out. Call more than one, and call early in the day.

Pima County's Emergency Services Network: start here

Pima County says help "may be available for eviction prevention, mortgage payment, move-in, utility, utility deposit, and utility repair/replacement assistance." It also says: "Callers will be screened for potential eligibility and an appointment may be scheduled based on available funds and/or appointments" (checked November 3, 2026). These are the agencies the County lists for rent, mortgage, or utility help:

  • Pima County Community Action Agency: 520-724-2667
  • Chicanos Por La Causa (Tucson): 520-882-0018
  • Interfaith Community Services: 520-297-6049
  • PPEP (Portable, Practical Educational Preparation): rent help 520-741-4397
  • Primavera Foundation: 520-623-5111
  • Salvation Army: 520-795-9671
  • In Ajo: International Sonoran Desert Alliance, 520-387-3570

Documents the County requires "for all types of assistance applications": your state-issued photo ID; proof of U.S. citizenship or permanent legal residence; Social Security cards for everyone in the home; birth certificates for children 17 and under; proof of all household income for the past 30 days; your eviction or late notice and/or lease; current utility bills (no cable or phone bills); and papers about the crisis that put you behind. The County says: "Once you have all the documents listed here, please call the numbers below to request an appointment."

Interfaith Community Services (ICS)

ICS helps with rent, mortgage, and utilities, plus short-term case management, and it says its help "is open to all Pima County residents." But "Emergency financial assistance is available only when ICS has funds. When funds are available, assistance is provided by phone only." Call (520) 297-6049 "beginning at 9:00 a.m., Monday through Thursday." ICS warns that you may be disconnected because of high call volume, so keep trying. "Walk-ins are not accepted for financial assistance."

Primavera Foundation

For rent or utility help, Primavera says to call its Resource Center at 520-623-5111, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., "to inquire about current funding and program availability." If it has money, you will need an intake appointment and documents. Primavera says you may qualify if you are late on rent or utilities, had a hardship such as a job loss, a big medical bill, or a car repair, and meet its income rules. It may ask for photo ID for all adults, Social Security cards and birth certificates or proof of citizenship for everyone in the home, your "Five-day notice from landlord or past due utility bills," proof of the hardship, and 30 days of income for everyone in the home. Its drop-in center is at 702 S. 6th Ave.

St. Vincent de Paul, and DES help for families with children

  • St. Vincent de Paul of Southern Arizona: St. Vincent de Paul offers "one-time rent, mortgage and utility assistance" through volunteer groups at Catholic churches. In Tucson, call the administrative referral line at 520-628-7837 to find the group that serves your neighborhood.
  • DES Short-Term Crisis Services: the Arizona Department of Economic Security helps with rent, utilities, and deposits for families that have "a child under 18" in the home and income at or below 125% of the federal poverty guidelines (150% if someone is 60 or older or has a disability). You can get it once in 12 months. DES says to "contact the local Community Action Agency (CAA) in your area," which in Tucson is Pima County Community Action at 520-724-2667.

211 Arizona: the switchboard

211 Arizona is run by Solari Crisis & Human Services. It "operates 9 a.m. to 7 p.m. daily," with live operators in English and Spanish and interpreters for other languages. Dial 2-1-1, or call 877-211-8661 from any phone. Its Housing Crisis Hotline helps people "who are at risk of becoming homeless." Ask which rent programs in your ZIP code have money right now.

Utility help in Tucson: LIHEAP, TEP, Southwest Gas, and Tucson Water

  • LIHEAP (Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program): apply through DES's A-to-Z Arizona Portal, with a paper application, or with help from Pima County Community Action. DES says the regular benefit is up to $640 based on income, energy costs, and who lives with you, and crisis help (for a shutoff notice) is up to $500. It is "first-come, first-served" and limited to once in 12 months. LIHEAP service line: 1-866-494-1981, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. The state's separate Power AZ program is closed: DES says "Power AZ funds have been exhausted," and applications sent after September 21, 2026 cannot be processed.
  • Tucson Electric Power (TEP): TEP's Lifeline program "offers an $20 monthly discount for qualifying low-income customers" with household income at or below 200% of the federal poverty guidelines; you reapply every year, and enrollment can take up to two bill cycles. TEP "also works with community partners to provide emergency bill payment assistance." TEP Customer Care: 520-623-7711, Monday to Friday, 7 a.m. to 6 p.m.
  • Southwest Gas and TEP help through CPLC: Chicanos Por La Causa runs utility help for Southwest Gas and TEP customers in Pima County. Call its hotline at (602) 805-1666 and choose option 1. You need an account in your name, photo ID, proof of income, and a current bill. Southwest Gas also has a Low Income Ratepayer Assistance (LIRA) discount you can apply for online.
  • City of Tucson Low Income Assistance: Pima County says Tucson Water "will provide a monthly credit to qualifying customers for the water portion" of the bill, and the City gives a $12 monthly credit on trash (Environmental Services) charges. Call the Low Income Assistance Line at (520) 791-5443.
  • Utility help outside the city: the County also lists Arivaca Coordinating Council (520-398-2771), St. Vincent de Paul Santa Catalina (520-825-8288), Salvation Army in Green Valley (520-625-3888), and PPEP utility help (520-770-2506).

Eviction help in Tucson: free lawyers

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can check the landlord's notice, raise defenses, and work out more time or a payment plan.

  • Pima County Emergency Eviction Legal Services (EELS): navigators who explain the process and connect you to other help are open to all tenants in Pima County eviction cases. A free lawyer is for tenants with income at or below 80% of the area median (for example, $53,850 for one person or $76,900 for four). Call 520-724-3357 (520-724-EELS) or use the contact form on the EELS page. The County says to reach out even if you are not sure you qualify.
  • Southern Arizona Legal Aid (SALA): free civil legal help in southern Arizona. Apply by phone at 520-623-9461 or toll-free 800-248-6789; SALA says "Both lines are open 8:15-11:30 a.m. and 1-4 p.m." You can also apply online at apply.azlawhelp.org. The Pima County office is at 2343 E. Broadway Blvd., Suite 200. SALA says it provides "translators for all languages."
  • Step Up to Justice: "free non-criminal legal help for those who qualify" for low-income people in Pima County, including landlord and tenant cases (but not Section 8 matters). Apply on its Apply for Help page; it says it will call you "within 3-5 business days." Office: 320 N. Commerce Park Loop, Suite 100.
  • Self-help: Pima County points tenants to AZEvictionHelp, run by the Arizona Bar Foundation, which has a live chat and a guide to the eviction process. The Arizona courts' eviction self-service center has forms in English and Spanish.

Arizona eviction rules: what happens and when

These steps come from the Arizona Revised Statutes on azleg.gov and the Pima County Consolidated Justice Court:

  • 1. The notice. For unpaid rent, the landlord must give you written notice and wait 5 calendar days (A.R.S. § 33-1368(B)). For other lease problems, the notice is usually 10 days (5 days for health and safety problems).
  • 2. Pay, and the lease comes back. Paying all past-due rent and a reasonable late fee from a written lease before the case is filed reinstates the lease. After filing, you must also pay attorney fees and court costs.
  • 3. The court case. Most Tucson eviction cases are heard at the Pima County Consolidated Justice Court, 240 N. Stone Ave. Under A.R.S. § 33-1377(B), the hearing date on the summons must be "not more than six nor less than three days from the date of the summons." The judge can postpone it for up to 3 days for good cause.
  • 4. The hearing. The court says "The court date indicated on the summons is a mandatory court hearing" and "Failure to appear may result in a default judgment." Pima County says you can attend virtually; the summons explains how.
  • 5. After a judgment. Under A.R.S. § 12-1178(C), "No writ of restitution shall issue until the expiration of five calendar days after the rendition of judgment." A constable, not your landlord, carries out the writ.
  • Federally backed buildings. The Justice Court says that for certain multifamily properties with federally backed mortgages, "writs issued for those properties will be made enforceable after 30 days from the date the '5-Day Notice' is provided." Ask a lawyer whether your building is one of them.
  • No lockouts. If a landlord locks you out or shuts off your power, gas, or water to force you out, A.R.S. § 33-1367 lets you get back in or end the lease and recover up to two months' rent or twice your damages, whichever is more.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • City of Tucson Housing and Community Development (HCD) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. HCD closed the City of Tucson and Pima County Housing Choice Voucher waiting lists and the City's public housing waiting lists on January 1, 2026, and its Section 8 page says "The Housing Waitlist is currently closed and is not accepting new applications at this time" (checked November 3, 2026). If you already have a voucher or public housing and fall behind, call HCD at (520) 791-4171 right away. Our City of Tucson HCD page has HCD's exact words, the monthly lottery and the preferences.
  • The Arizona Rental Assistance Program (ARAP) has ended. DES says "applications are no longer being accepted"; only applications sent by August 31, 2024 are still being processed. The Justice Court's eviction page still links to ARAP and to the old pandemic-era Tucson and Pima County Eviction Prevention Program, so don't count on those links.
  • United Way of Tucson and Southern Arizona's Emergency Relief Fund focuses on rent and utility help, but it works through partner agencies such as Primavera, not through applications from individuals.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Tucson right now?

Yes, but it is limited and runs on grants. Start with the Pima County Community Action Agency at 520-724-2667, which screens callers and sets appointments when money is available. Interfaith Community Services takes calls at 520-297-6049 from 9 a.m., Monday to Thursday, only when it has funds. The Primavera Foundation answers at 520-623-5111 on weekdays. The City of Tucson does not run its own rent fund.

What documents do I need for rent help in Pima County?

Pima County asks for your state-issued photo ID, proof of U.S. citizenship or permanent legal residence, Social Security cards for everyone in the home, birth certificates for children 17 and under, proof of all household income for the past 30 days, your eviction or late notice and lease, current utility bills, and papers about the crisis that put you behind. The County says to have all of them before you call.

What should I do if I got a 5-day notice or eviction papers in Tucson?

Act the same day. Call Pima County's Emergency Eviction Legal Services at 520-724-3357; it can give you a free lawyer if your income is at or below 80% of the area median. You can also call Southern Arizona Legal Aid at 520-623-9461, open 8:15 to 11:30 a.m. and 1 to 4 p.m. Keep applying for rent help, and go to your court hearing, because missing it can mean a default judgment.

How fast can an eviction happen in Arizona?

Very fast. After a 5-day notice for unpaid rent, the landlord can file in court. The hearing must be 3 to 6 days after the summons is issued, and the judge can postpone it for only up to 3 days for good cause. If the landlord wins, the court cannot issue a writ of restitution until 5 calendar days after the judgment. From notice to lockout can take only a few weeks.

Can I stop an Arizona eviction by paying what I owe?

Often, yes. Under A.R.S. 33-1368, if you pay all past-due rent and a reasonable late fee from a written lease before the landlord files in court, the lease is reinstated. After the case is filed, you must also pay the landlord's attorney fees and court costs, before the judge rules. After a judgment, letting you stay is up to the landlord. Get a receipt for every payment.

Is the Tucson Section 8 waitlist open?

No. City of Tucson Housing and Community Development closed the City of Tucson and Pima County Housing Choice Voucher waiting lists, and the City's public housing waiting lists, on January 1, 2026. Its Section 8 page says the waitlist is currently closed and not accepting new applications. HCD is not an emergency rent fund. If you already have a voucher and fall behind, call HCD at (520) 791-4171.

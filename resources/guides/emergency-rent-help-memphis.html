Emergency Rent Help in Memphis: CEAP, MIFA, Free Eviction Lawyers, and the 14-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Memphis or anywhere in Shelby County and you are behind, two doors actually pay rent. The first is CEAP, the Shelby County Community Services Agency's rent and mortgage program, which works by appointment only; new appointments are released on the first Monday of each month. The second is MIFA, whose online application is open weekdays from 7 a.m. to noon. Neither one promises an amount, and both run out. For eviction help, Memphis Area Legal Services takes calls at 901-523-8822. The pandemic-era rent program is over. Everything here was checked on November 3, 2026.

Here's the short version: Apply to MIFA on a weekday morning, before noon. Put the first Monday of the month on your calendar for CEAP (in December 2026, that is the 7th) and get your documents ready now. If your MLGW bill is part of the problem, apply for MLGW's Plus-1 help in My Account. If you got court papers, call Memphis Area Legal Services at 901-523-8822, keep applying for rent money, and go to court.

Read your lease for the words "waive notice"

In Shelby County, Tennessee's Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA, T.C.A. 66-28) applies. For unpaid rent, a Tennessee legal aid guide for these counties says that if you pay rent monthly, "the landlord must give you a 14 day warning," and "You may NOT have to move if you pay the rent or fix the problem. You have 14 days to do this." But there is a catch. The same guide says that if your lease says you "waive written notice," the landlord "can take you to court without warning if you are behind on rent." Check your lease today. If it has that clause, you may get a court date instead of a warning, so apply for rent help the day you fall behind.

Emergency rental assistance in Memphis: the doors that pay

Apply to more than one. Each runs on its own money, and none of them guarantees help.

Shelby County CEAP (Comprehensive Emergency Assistance Program)

CEAP is run by the Shelby County Community Services Agency (CSA). In its words, CEAP "is designed to support families through difficult times and help prevent the loss of housing due to eviction or foreclosure" (checked November 3, 2026).

  • How appointments work: CEAP only helps people who book an appointment online. Its page says the "Rent & Mortgage Application Portal will be open on November 2, 2026, at 8:30 AM," and that "New appointments are released on the First MONDAY of each month." If you see a "Bad Request" message, the CSA says "This means our appointment scheduler is currently full." It also says: "If you cannot access the portal, that means the program is full at this time." The November 2 release has passed; if the pattern holds, the December release is Monday, December 7, 2026, and after that, the first Monday of each month. Check the CSA's rent page that morning for the exact time.
  • Who qualifies, in the CSA's words: you "Must be a Shelby County resident"; your household must be at or below 200% of the Federal Poverty Guidelines ("Gross income is used regardless of expenses or garnishments"); you need "proof of income for seniors (age 60+) or documentation showing a wage reduction or loss due to termination, separation, or reduction in work hours"; and you need a current lease or mortgage agreement. "Unexpected costs such as repairs, funerals, medical, and household expenses do not qualify under this program term."
  • Income limits (yearly, 200% of poverty, effective February 9, 2026): 1 person $31,920; 2 people $43,280; 3 people $54,640; 4 people $66,000; 5 people $77,360; 6 people $88,720; 7 people $100,080; 8 people $111,440. Add $11,360 for each extra person.
  • What to have ready: a valid state-issued photo ID and Social Security cards for everyone in the household, your income or wage-loss papers, your lease, and your Section 8 voucher paperwork if you have one.
  • How much it pays: the page does not list a dollar amount. Requests are handled "first come first served in order by date received."
  • If you are denied: you can appeal within 30 days of the decision. Ask your caseworker for the complaint form.
  • Beware of scams: "WE DO NOT ACCEPT PAYMENT FOR OUR SERVICES. IF ANY PERSON OR AGENCY REQUESTS PAYMENT FOR OUR GRANT FUNDED SERVICES IT IS A SCAM."

Shelby County CSA: 901-222-4200, 1188 Minna Place, Memphis, TN 38104. Hours: Monday through Thursday, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m.; Friday, 8 a.m. to 12:15 p.m.

MIFA (Metropolitan Inter-Faith Association)

MIFA "provides rent or mortgage assistance to Shelby County residents who have lost income or experienced a recent crisis." In its last fiscal year (July 2025 to June 2026), the program served 1,113 households (checked November 3, 2026).

  • When to apply: online only, and only during open hours. "The application is available from 7:00 a.m. to 12:00 p.m. Monday through Friday, excluding holidays." Outside those hours the page says "The Application is now closed." Start from MIFA's Apply for Assistance Online page.
  • Who qualifies, in MIFA's words: you must "Be a resident of Shelby County"; "Be able to document a recent (within the past 90 days) crisis that has affected your ability to pay your bills," such as a loss of household income, a medical emergency, or a job loss ("must be accompanied by proof of approved unemployment benefits"); and "Have not received financial assistance from MIFA in the past 12 months; preference is given to first-time applicants."
  • What to upload: a valid driver's license or state ID; proof of the crisis from the past 90 days; proof of income; and a lease or mortgage statement that is in your name and shows the past-due balance. "Applications cannot be processed without complete documents."
  • One rule to know: MIFA says its help, "alone or in combination with other available community resources, must be sufficient to stop an eviction or foreclosure," and that the amount "is based on the availability of funds and demand for services." If you owe more than MIFA can cover, tell them what else you have applied for.
  • Questions: MIFA's Help Desk, (901) 529-4538 or helpdesk@mifa.org. MIFA's office is at 910 Vance Avenue, open Monday through Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., (901) 527-0208.

Families with children who have already lost their home can call MIFA's Hotline for Homeless Families at (901) 529-4545. MIFA says the hotline "is open from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. every day except select holidays."

Smaller doors worth a call

  • St. Vincent de Paul (Memphis): its local parish groups "provide assistance yearly to 10,000 plus individuals and families in the form of rent, utility assistance, and food." Help comes through a parish, not one central phone line, so ask at a Catholic parish near you. The District Council of Memphis is at 1306 Monroe.
  • The Salvation Army Kroc Center: it lists "rent & utility assistance" among the help that local donations pay for. It does not publish rules, so call 901-729-8007, 800 East Parkway South.

LINC 2-1-1: the switchboard

In Memphis, 211 is answered by LINC, the Library Information Center at the Memphis Public Libraries, "in conjunction with United Way of the Mid-South." The library says the most common requests include "utilities, food, protective services, legal assistance, child care, social support, housing, and mental health." Dial 2-1-1, or search the LINC 2-1-1 database yourself. LINC also has social workers at five library branches (Central, Whitehaven, East Shelby, Hollywood, and Raleigh), 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. on weekdays; call 211 to make an appointment.

Help with MLGW and other utility bills in Memphis

  • Shelby County CSA (LIHEAP): the CSA's utility page says "Utility Assistance is not offered at this time. Please continue to check our website for updates" (checked November 3, 2026). When it reopens, applications are online only.
  • MLGW Plus-1: MLGW's Power of Giving page says Plus-1 "has helped MLGW customers facing financial hardship" for over 40 years. Apply in MLGW My Account online or in the MLGW app. You need an ID that matches the name on the account, an active account, your most recent cutoff notice, income papers for everyone with income, and a list of everyone in the home. "MLGW customers can only be approved for assistance one time in a calendar year," and "Funds for this program are limited." If you can't apply online, book a Community Office appointment at 901-544-6549.
  • Neighborhood Christian Centers (NCC): NCC runs a City of Memphis program for MLGW bills. Its page says "we have reached our threshold for accepting applications," and that the program "will open on the third Wednesday of each month, while funds are available." In November 2026, the third Wednesday is the 18th. Applications are online only during those openings, and NCC says it can't take phone calls about them. Prepay and PowerPay customers do not qualify.

Eviction help in Memphis: free lawyers and clinics

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can find problems in the landlord's case, ask for more time, and help you work out an agreement.

  • Memphis Area Legal Services (MALS): free civil legal help for people with low incomes in Shelby, Fayette, Tipton, and Lauderdale counties, including landlord-tenant cases and Section 8 or other subsidized housing. Apply by phone: "You may apply for assistance by calling (901) 523-8822." MALS says it cannot process email requests except in extenuating circumstances. It provides "in-house interpreting service for Spanish-speakers." Generally, clients under age 60 must be within 125% of the federal poverty guidelines; people 60 and over do not have to meet an income test. "If you have court dates or other critical dates or deadlines, we will need that information when you call." Office: 200 Jefferson Avenue, Suite 1075.
  • Memphis Fair Housing Center: part of MALS. It helps "renters with landlord/tenant issues" and people facing discrimination or homelessness. Call (901) 432-4663.
  • Free walk-in clinics run by MALS:
    • Courthouse clinic (Attorney of the Day): the first and third Thursday of each month, 1 to 3 p.m., at the courthouse at 140 Adams Avenue. MALS's home page says it "is now held in Room 119."
    • Second Saturday Legal Advice Clinic: the second Saturday of each month, 10 a.m. to noon, at the Benjamin L. Hooks Central Library, 3030 Poplar Avenue. "You must arrive by 11:00 AM." In November 2026, that is Saturday the 14th.
    • The Hub Clinic: the fourth Wednesday of each month, 1 to 3 p.m., at 590 Washington Avenue.
    Clinics give advice; MALS says this "does not mean that the attorney will take your case or represent you in court." MALS's online clinic calendar was not posted when we checked, so call before you go.

Tennessee eviction timeline in Memphis: what happens and when

These steps come from the Tennessee legal aid guide for URLTA counties, which lists Shelby County, and from the Shelby County General Sessions Court:

  • 1. The written notice. For unpaid rent paid monthly, the landlord must give you a 14-day written warning (T.C.A. 66-28-505). If you pay rent weekly, it is 10 days. Paying what you owe within the 14 days can keep your home. If the same problem happens again within 6 months, the guide says you get only a 7-day warning and no chance to fix it. And if your lease says you waive written notice, the landlord can skip this step.
  • 2. The detainer warrant. After the notice ends, the landlord files an eviction case, called a detainer warrant, in the Shelby County General Sessions Civil Court. "A detainer warrant is NOT a warrant for your arrest. It just says when to go to court for the eviction hearing." The guide says you will have at least 6 more days until the hearing. To check your case or court date, call General Sessions Civil at 901-222-3400, 140 Adams Avenue, Room 106, Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.
  • 3. Go to court. If you don't go, the landlord wins automatically. Bring your lease, your rent receipts, and proof of every rent-help application. Call MALS before your court date.
  • 4. After a judgment. If you lose, the guide says "you have at least 10 more days to move out," and that during those 10 days "you can ask a lawyer about filing an appeal." After that, the sheriff can remove you and your belongings. Call MALS the same day you lose, because the appeal window is short.
  • Late fees have a limit. The guide says a landlord can charge a late fee only once rent is more than 5 days late, and "The most the late fee can be is 10% of the rent you owe."
  • Lockouts and shutoffs are illegal. "The landlord can't change the locks to make you leave! He can't change the locks even if you are behind on rent." The landlord also can't shut off lights, water, or gas to make you leave, or set your things on the street before the sheriff comes.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The Memphis Housing Authority (MHA) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its site says "MHA's Housing Choice Voucher Program (HCVP) waiting list is currently closed and is not accepting applications at this time." Its public housing page says "A selected Public Housing Waitlists are OPEN!" and lists five senior properties, for ages 62 and up (Lyons Ridge also takes ages 55 to 61 with a disability) (checked November 3, 2026). Main line: (901) 544-1100. Our MHA page has the details.
  • The pandemic-era Memphis and Shelby County rent and utility program is over. Old posts and flyers still point to it.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Memphis?

Yes, but not as one program. The Shelby County Community Services Agency's CEAP program helps with rent by appointment only, and new appointments are released on the first Monday of each month. MIFA takes online applications Monday through Friday from 7 a.m. to noon for Shelby County residents who can document a crisis from the past 90 days. Neither one lists a set dollar amount, and both depend on limited funding, so apply to both. The pandemic-era rent program is over.

When can I apply for Shelby County CEAP rent help?

CEAP only helps people who book an appointment through its online portal. The CSA says new appointments are released on the first Monday of each month, and that a "Bad Request" message means the scheduler is full. Its page listed November 2, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. as the latest opening; after that, watch for the first Monday of each month. Your household must be at or below 200% of the poverty line, for example $54,640 a year for three people, and you need proof of lost or reduced wages unless you are 60 or older. Questions: 901-222-4200.

How do I apply to MIFA for rent help?

Apply online through MIFA's Apply for Assistance Online page between 7 a.m. and noon, Monday through Friday, excluding holidays. You must live in Shelby County, document a crisis from the past 90 days, such as a loss of income or a medical emergency, and not have received MIFA financial help in the past 12 months. Upload your ID, proof of the crisis, proof of income, and a lease in your name that shows the past-due balance. Questions: MIFA's Help Desk at (901) 529-4538.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Memphis?

Call Memphis Area Legal Services at (901) 523-8822. MALS takes applications by phone, not email, and has in-house Spanish interpreting. Have your court date ready when you call. MALS also runs free walk-in advice clinics, including one at the courthouse at 140 Adams Avenue on the first and third Thursday of each month from 1 to 3 p.m. For landlord-tenant and fair housing problems, you can also call the Memphis Fair Housing Center at (901) 432-4663.

How much notice does a Tennessee landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

In Shelby County, which is covered by Tennessee's Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, a landlord generally must give a 14-day written notice for unpaid rent when you pay monthly (10 days if you pay weekly), and paying what you owe in that time can keep your home. If the same problem happened in the last 6 months, the notice can be 7 days with no chance to fix it. If your lease says you waive written notice, the landlord can go straight to court. Either way, only a court order can force you out.

Does the Memphis Housing Authority give emergency rent help?

No. MHA runs Section 8 and public housing, not an emergency fund. Its Housing Choice Voucher waiting list is closed. Its public housing page says five senior properties have open waiting lists, for people 62 and older (one also takes ages 55 to 61 with a disability). If you already live in MHA housing or have a voucher and fall behind, call MHA at (901) 544-1100 right away.

Emergency Rent Help in Louisville: Metro Rent Programs, Free Eviction Lawyers, and Kentucky's 7-Day Clock

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Louisville or anywhere in Jefferson County and you are behind, here is the honest picture. The pandemic-era rent funds are over, but Louisville Metro still runs two small rent programs through its Office of Social Services and Neighborhood Place: the Housing Stabilization Program for working households and the HOPE Assistance Program for seniors and people with disabilities on fixed incomes. Both work by appointment, first come, first served. Louisville also has a Right to Counsel law for tenants in Eviction Court, with the free lawyers coming from the Legal Aid Society. Everything here was checked on November 4, 2026.

Here's the short version: Call (502) 308-3344 to book a Metro rent appointment (option 1 for Housing Stabilization, option 2 for HOPE), and dial 2-1-1 for everything else. If you have an eviction court date, call the Legal Aid Society at (502) 584-1254 that day and leave a voicemail if no one answers. And go to court, on Zoom or in person: after a judgment, Kentucky gives you only 7 days to appeal.

Kentucky's clock in Louisville: 7 days, then court

Louisville follows Kentucky's Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA), which Louisville Metro adopted as its Landlord and Tenant Code. Under it, "If rent is unpaid when due and the tenant fails to pay rent within seven (7) days after written notice by the landlord of nonpayment and his intention to terminate the rental agreement if the rent is not paid within that period, the landlord may terminate the rental agreement" (KRS 383.660). The landlord then files in Jefferson District Court, and the court's warrant must give you "at least three (3) days' notice" of the trial (KRS 383.210). Use the 7 days: that is when Metro's rent programs, 211 and Legal Aid can still keep the case from being filed.

Free eviction lawyers in Louisville

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can catch mistakes in the landlord's papers, raise defenses like bad repairs, negotiate a payment plan, and make sure you get every day the law allows.

Right to Counsel through the Legal Aid Society

Louisville Metro's Right to Counsel law (Metro Ordinance No. 52-2021, approved April 28, 2021, and amended in 2023) is in the Metro Code at section 151.60. It covers "Any proceeding in Jefferson County District Court, Eviction Court ("Eviction Court") to evict, eject, or terminate the tenancy of a covered individual."

  • Who qualifies, in the Metro Code's words: a tenant "whose annual gross income is not in excess of 125% of the federal poverty guidelines." The Code names the Legal Aid Society as the kind of organization that provides the lawyers, from brief advice up to "Ongoing legal representation, throughout the duration of a covered proceeding."
  • How to ask for help: Stop My Eviction, the site Louisville Metro points renters to, says: "Contact the Legal Aid Society at 502-584-1254 for free legal representation. Please leave a voicemail with your contact information so they can call you back!" The Legal Aid Society's toll-free number is (800) 292-1862. Its "Intake Department is open Monday - Thursday. 10 AM - 4 PM," and you can "Apply online 24 hours a day, 7 days a week" from yourlegalaid.org, or in person at 416 W. Muhammad Ali Boulevard, Suite 300.
  • Interpreters: Stop My Eviction says "To schedule language interpreting services for your court date, call Legal Aid: 502 584 1254."
  • Even if you are over the income limit: the Legal Aid Society says its services are for people "with incomes at or below 125% to 200% of the federal poverty guidelines," depending on the program, so call and ask.

Go to court, on Zoom or in person

Stop My Eviction says to "Attend your court date on Zoom or in person! Failure to attend court will result in a judgment being entered against you." Its Eviction Assistance page posts the Zoom meeting ID and call-in number for Jefferson County eviction court. Check the date and time on your court papers.

Help paying rent in Louisville: the doors that are left

None of these programs promises help, and most book up fast. Call more than one, and keep calling while you wait for a lawyer.

Louisville Metro's Housing Stabilization Program

  • What it is, in Metro's words: "Neighborhood Place's Housing Stabilization Program is designed to provide income eligible households with both long- and short-term rental assistance and case management." The household "must be within income guidelines; be willing to participate in case management; and meet other eligibility criteria, including work history requirements."
  • How much: Stop My Eviction says it "can assist working heads-of-household with up to $800 towards rent (paid directly to their landlord) or Louisville Water Company bills," that help "is limited to heads-of-household with 250 hours of documented employment history in the past 6 months," and that "these funds are available once every 12 months."
  • How to apply: "Appointments can be made on a first-come, first-served basis by scheduling online or calling the automated scheduler at 502-308-3344 and select option 1 for Housing Stabilization." The online scheduler is linked from Metro's Neighborhood Place Assistance Programs page.

HOPE Assistance Program (seniors and people with disabilities)

Metro says "The HOPE Assistance program can assist senior and disabled populations living on a fixed-income to reduce household debt to their leasing agent, LG&E, and Louisville Water. Assistance is limited to households that do not have the ability to grow their income due to age, retirement, and/or disability." Call 502-308-3344 and select option 2, or schedule online. Stop My Eviction adds: "if you have already received funds through this program you are no longer eligible."

United Way 2-1-1, Community Ministries and schools

  • Metro United Way 2-1-1: dial 2-1-1, or 502-515-7717 in Kentucky. Metro United Way says 2-1-1 "serves Metro Louisville and its surrounding counties as well as Southern Indiana," is available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, and works "in over 100 languages." Ask for rent help in Jefferson County.
  • Community Ministries: Stop My Eviction says your local Community Ministry "may have help with rent available," that rent help is "prioritized for neighbors who have a plan for paying their rent moving forward," and that there is "no guarantee." Find yours by address with the map on its Resources page.
  • Families with JCPS students: the school's Family Resource and Youth Services Center (FRYSC) "can help navigate for families of students facing hardship."

Utility bills: LIHEAP Fall Subsidy, November 9 to December 18, 2026

The Louisville Metro Office of Social Services runs LIHEAP for Jefferson County. Metro says: "Enrollment runs Nov. 9-Dec. 18, 2026, or until designated funds are depleted, on a first-come, first-serve basis." Book an appointment by phone at (502) 991-8391, which Metro says is available 24 hours a day, or online from Metro's LIHEAP page. Household income must be at or below Metro's limits, for example $1,995 a month for one person or $4,125 for a family of four. Bring a photo ID or other proof of residence, proof of Social Security numbers (or a green card) for everyone in the home, last month's income for everyone, and your most current electric bill. No application is needed in advance.

Kentucky eviction timeline in Louisville: what happens and when

Kentucky lets local governments adopt the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act "in their entirety and without amendment" (KRS 383.500), and Louisville Metro has done so in Chapter 151 of its Code. The court case itself is a "forcible detainer" under KRS 383.200 to 383.285, heard in Jefferson District Court's Eviction Court. These are the steps:

  • 1. The notice. For unpaid rent, the landlord must give written notice and 7 days to pay before ending the lease (KRS 383.660(2)). For other lease violations, the notice must give at least 14 days, and if you fix the problem before the date in the notice, the lease does not end (KRS 383.660(1)). A month-to-month tenancy takes 30 days' written notice to end (KRS 383.695).
  • 2. The court papers. The landlord files in District Court, and the warrant must give you "at least three (3) days' notice of the time and place" of the trial (KRS 383.210). Call the Legal Aid Society the day you get them.
  • 3. Court. Go. On Zoom or in person. If you do not attend, a judgment will be entered against you. Bring your lease, receipts, texts, photos of repair problems, and anything showing the landlord took or refused your rent.
  • 4. The 7 days after judgment. You can appeal "within seven (7) days next after the finding," but you must deposit with the circuit court clerk the rent owed since the case started and keep paying each month's rent into court while the appeal is pending (KRS 383.255). If no appeal is filed by the seventh day, the court "shall, on request," issue a warrant of restitution that orders the sheriff to put the landlord back in possession (KRS 383.245).
  • 5. The sheriff. The warrant goes to the sheriff or a constable, who puts the landlord back in possession. If you will lose your home, call 502-637-2337 for emergency shelter options, which Stop My Eviction lists.
  • Lockouts and shutoffs are illegal. If a landlord "unlawfully removes or excludes the tenant from the premises or willfully diminishes services" by cutting off heat, water, hot water, electric, gas or another essential service, you can get back in or end the lease and recover up to three months' rent and attorney's fees (KRS 383.655).
  • Retaliation. A landlord may not raise rent, cut services or bring an eviction because you complained to a code agency or the landlord about repairs, or joined a tenant union; a complaint within one year before the landlord's action creates a presumption of retaliation. That protection does not apply if you are behind on rent (KRS 383.705).

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • Old pandemic pages. Louisville Metro's old Utility and Rental Assistance page still talks about the state's Healthy at Home Eviction Relief Fund "As of May 9, 2022" and a 2022 LIHEAP season (checked November 4, 2026). It is years out of date. Use the Neighborhood Place programs above instead.
  • The Salvation Army's rent help. Stop My Eviction lists it as "(ON HOLD UNTIL JANUARY 2027)."
  • The Louisville Metro Housing Authority (LMHA) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund, and it is not taking new applications. Its Section 8 page says "Please be advised that we are not accepting Housing Choice Voucher applications at this time" (checked November 4, 2026). See our LMHA guide. If you already have LMHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call (502) 569-3400 right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • A local voucher-discrimination rule. Since March 6, 2024, Kentucky law (2024 Ky. Acts ch. 3, House Bill 18) says no city or consolidated local government "shall adopt or enforce" a rule stopping a landlord from refusing a tenant whose rent "includes funding from a federal housing assistance program." So a Louisville landlord can legally refuse a voucher. Refusals based on race, disability, family status or another federally protected trait are still illegal; report them to HUD.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight, call 502-637-2337, and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Louisville right now?

Yes, but it is small and by appointment. Louisville Metro's Office of Social Services runs the Housing Stabilization Program, which Stop My Eviction says can pay up to $800 toward rent for working heads of household with 250 hours of documented work in the past 6 months, once every 12 months, and the HOPE Assistance Program for seniors and people with disabilities on fixed incomes. Book by calling (502) 308-3344 (option 1 or 2) or online. The Salvation Army's rent help is on hold until January 2027. Dial 2-1-1 for other options (checked November 4, 2026).

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Louisville?

Call the Legal Aid Society at (502) 584-1254 or (800) 292-1862 and leave a voicemail if no one answers; its intake line is open Monday through Thursday, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., and you can apply online any time at yourlegalaid.org. Louisville Metro's Right to Counsel law covers eviction cases in Jefferson District Court's Eviction Court for tenants whose annual gross income is not more than 125% of the federal poverty guidelines. Call the day you get court papers.

How much notice does a Louisville landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

Seven days. Under Kentucky's URLTA, which Louisville Metro has adopted, if rent is unpaid the landlord must give written notice that the lease will end if you do not pay within 7 days (KRS 383.660). For other lease violations, the notice must give at least 14 days, and fixing the problem in time keeps the lease. A month-to-month tenancy takes 30 days' written notice to end.

How long do I have after an eviction judgment in Kentucky?

Seven days. Either side can appeal within 7 days after the judgment, but to do it you must deposit the rent owed since the case began with the circuit court clerk and keep paying rent into court each month (KRS 383.255). If no appeal is filed by the seventh day, the court issues a warrant of restitution on the landlord's request, and the sheriff can then put you out (KRS 383.245). Talk to Legal Aid before those 7 days run.

Can a Louisville landlord lock me out or shut off my utilities?

No. Only the court process can remove you. If a landlord unlawfully removes or excludes you, or willfully cuts off heat, water, hot water, electric, gas or another essential service, Kentucky law lets you recover possession or end the lease, plus up to three months' rent and attorney's fees (KRS 383.655). Call the Legal Aid Society at (502) 584-1254.

Is the Louisville Section 8 waiting list open?

No. The Louisville Metro Housing Authority's Section 8 page says "Please be advised that we are not accepting Housing Choice Voucher applications at this time" (checked November 4, 2026), and on October 15, 2026 its applicant portal said public housing applications were closed too. LMHA is not an emergency rent fund. Also know that since March 6, 2024, Kentucky law bars local rules that stop landlords from refusing vouchers, so a Louisville landlord can legally say no to Section 8.

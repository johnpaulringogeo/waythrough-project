Emergency Rent Help in Albuquerque: Who Still Pays, Free Eviction Lawyers, and the 3-Day Notice

Matt · Updated October 2026

If you rent in Albuquerque and you are behind, the public door is the City's Emergency Rental & Utility Assistance Program, run out of four Health & Social Service Centers. It is open, but small: the City says "Funding is limited, and all services provided are subject to the availability of funds." The statewide program many people remember is gone. The City's own rental assistance page says: "The New Mexico Emergency Rental Assistance Program has officially closed as of June 30, 2023." Catholic Charities of Central New Mexico also lists rent help as "Available Now," mostly for people already working with a caseworker. For eviction help, New Mexico Legal Aid takes calls at 1-833-545-4357. Everything here was checked on October 16, 2026.

Here's the short version: Call the Health & Social Service Center closest to you (numbers below) on a weekday and ask for emergency rental assistance. Fill out Catholic Charities' assistance form too. Dial 2-1-1 for other rent help. Then call New Mexico Legal Aid at 1-833-LGL-HELP. And if you get court papers, go to court. A New Mexico eviction trial is set 7 to 10 days after you are served.

New Mexico moves fast: the 3-day notice and the 7-to-10-day trial

Under NMSA 1978, section 47-8-33(D), if you do not pay "within three days after written notice from the owner of nonpayment and his intention to terminate the rental agreement," the landlord can end your lease. But the same law protects you if you act in time: "tender of the full amount due, in the manner stated in the notice, prior to the expiration of the three-day notice shall bar any action for nonpayment of rent." Once the landlord files, the trial is set "not less than seven or more than ten days after the service of summons" (section 47-8-43(A)(1)). That is why you should apply for rent help the day you get a 3-day notice.

Rental assistance in Albuquerque in 2026: the doors that pay

Apply to more than one. Each runs on its own money, and none of them promises help.

City of Albuquerque: Emergency Rental & Utility Assistance

The City's Division of Health and Social Services runs this program for renters "at risk of eviction or utility shutoff." It helps with unpaid rent and utilities. The City says "Funding is limited, and all services provided are subject to the availability of funds" (checked October 16, 2026). There is no online application: you contact the Health & Social Service Center closest to where you live.

  • John Marshall, 1500 Walter SE: 505-848-1345
  • Alamosa, 6900 Gonzales SW: 505-836-8800
  • Dede Feldman Los Griegos, 1231 Candelaria NW: 505-761-4050
  • Ruth M. Adams (East Central), 7525 Zuni SE: 505-767-5700

The John Marshall center lists hours of Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., and says rent and utility help is given "Based upon available funds." The City does not post income limits or a document list, so ask when you call. These centers serve Albuquerque residents only. If you live in unincorporated Bernalillo County, the City points you to Bernalillo County's pages; the county rent page it links to showed "Page not found" when we checked, and we found no open county rent program. Not sure which center is yours? Call 311 or 505-768-2000.

Catholic Charities of Central New Mexico

Catholic Charities' homepage reads "Rental & Utility Support Available Now," for help with rent arrears, move-in costs, and utilities. Its housing page adds two limits. For its One-Time Assistance Program and other eviction-prevention help, "Applicants must be actively working with a social service agency," and "the referring agency must provide case management and/or support services throughout the assistance period." It also says "Assistance is not guaranteed and depends on eligibility, documentation, and available funding."

  • How to apply: use the Apply for Assistance form, or call 505-724-4670.
  • Tip: if you already have a caseworker anywhere (a clinic, a school, a shelter, a veterans program), ask them to refer you. That is the path Catholic Charities describes.

211: the switchboard

The City sends people to 2-1-1 for other help. In the Albuquerque area, 211 is answered by United Way of North Central New Mexico, which lists "Resources for food, clothing, shelter and rent assistance." Dial 2-1-1, or call it direct at (505) 245-1735, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.

Hotel and motel vouchers if you are homeless

The City of Albuquerque does pay for hotel and motel vouchers, but there is no public application for them. Its Housing Voucher Data Dashboard says "Funding is allotted to organizations supporting individuals and families," and lists, for example, 42 hotel/motel vouchers used in June 2026. In practice you reach a voucher through a shelter or a caseworker. If you need somewhere to sleep tonight, the City's shelter list starts with these:

  • Gateway West (individuals and couples, up to 2 pets per person), 7440 Jim McDowell NW: 505-839-9193.
  • Family Housing Navigation Center (families; "referrals required"): 505-554-0901.
  • Barrett Foundation (single women and families), 10300 Constitution NE: 505-246-9244.
  • Call 311 for help connecting to shelter, and see I need housing tonight.

Eviction help in Albuquerque: free lawyers and the court's help desk

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can catch mistakes in the landlord's papers, raise defenses, and work out a payment plan.

  • New Mexico Legal Aid (NMLA): call 1-833-LGL-HELP (1-833-545-4357). NMLA says "Intakes by phone are now being accepted during limited hours, Monday-Thursday," and you can also apply online. It handles residential evictions, with "priority given to cases involving subsidized housing," and Section 8 terminations.
  • If you are 60 or older: the Senior Citizens' Law Office, 505-265-2300. If you are 55 or older: Legal Resources for the Elderly Program, 1-800-876-6657.
  • If your income is too high for Legal Aid: the Modest Means Helpline serves New Mexicans at or below 500% of the federal poverty guidelines, 505-797-6013 or 888-857-9935, weekdays 8 a.m. to 4 p.m.
  • Metropolitan Court Self-Help Center: 401 Lomas NW, Room 210, 505-841-9817, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Staff are not lawyers and "CANNOT provide you with legal advice," but they explain the process and help with forms, including the Answer to Petition for Restitution.
  • Mediation: New Mexico courts can refer an eviction case to a facilitator through the Eviction Prevention and Diversion Program. The City notes this happens "only if both landlord and tenant agree," so ask for it at your first hearing.

New Mexico eviction timeline: what happens and when

New Mexico's rules are in the Uniform Owner-Resident Relations Act, NMSA 1978, sections 47-8-1 to 47-8-52. Albuquerque evictions are heard in Bernalillo County Metropolitan Court. These are the steps for a rent case, in order:

  • 1. The 3-day notice. The landlord gives written notice of nonpayment. If you pay the full amount, in the way the notice says, before the 3 days end, the landlord cannot sue you for that nonpayment (section 47-8-33(D)).
  • 2. The petition. If you do not pay, the landlord files a Petition by Owner for Restitution. You can file an Answer any time before the trial date, and list your defenses there.
  • 3. The trial. It is set 7 to 10 days after you are served (section 47-8-43(A)(1)). For good cause, the court can move it up to 7 days later (section 47-8-43(B)). If you do not show up, the court tries the case without you (section 47-8-44).
  • 4. The writ. If the landlord wins, the writ of restitution tells the sheriff to return the home to the landlord "on a specified date not less than three nor more than seven days after entry of judgment" (section 47-8-46(A)).
  • 5. Appeal. An appeal stops the writ only if, within 5 days of filing the notice of appeal, you pay the rent coming due through the end of that rental period to the landlord or into escrow, and keep paying monthly (section 47-8-47(A)).
  • Lockouts and shutoffs are illegal. Without a court order, a landlord cannot change the locks, block the door, cut utilities, or remove your things. If they do, you can stop paying rent for every day you are kept out, ask the court to put you back in, and claim damages and civil penalties (section 47-8-36).

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The Albuquerque Housing Authority (AHA) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund, but two of its lists are open right now. Its How to Apply page reads "Public Housing Units OPEN October 5, 2026 through October 31, 2026" and "Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) OPEN October 16, 2026 through November 15, 2026," with the Project Based Voucher Program "OPEN Until Further Notice" (checked October 16, 2026). Pre-applications are online only, walk-ins are not accepted, and AHA says it does not charge to apply. Phone: (505) 764-3920. Our AHA page explains the lottery and the points system.
  • The old state programs. The New Mexico Emergency Rental Assistance Program closed June 30, 2023. Don't spend your few days on its old portals.

Frequently asked questions

Is there rental assistance in Albuquerque in 2026?

Yes, but it is limited. The City of Albuquerque's Emergency Rental & Utility Assistance Program helps city residents at risk of eviction or a utility shutoff, through four Health & Social Service Centers; call the one nearest you, such as John Marshall at 505-848-1345. Catholic Charities of Central New Mexico also lists rent help as available now, mainly for people referred by a social service agency. The statewide program closed June 30, 2023.

Does Albuquerque give hotel vouchers to homeless people?

The City pays for hotel and motel vouchers, but it gives the money to organizations that serve people, not to the public directly, so there is no application. Ask a shelter or caseworker. For a bed tonight, call Gateway West at 505-839-9193, or call 311.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Albuquerque?

Call New Mexico Legal Aid at 1-833-545-4357; phone intake runs limited hours Monday through Thursday, and you can apply online. If you are 60 or older, call the Senior Citizens' Law Office at 505-265-2300. The Metropolitan Court Self-Help Center at 505-841-9817 helps with forms but cannot give legal advice.

How fast is an eviction in New Mexico?

Very fast. After a 3-day notice for unpaid rent, the landlord can file, and the trial is set 7 to 10 days after you are served. If the landlord wins, the sheriff can return the home to the landlord 3 to 7 days after the judgment. Paying the full amount before the 3-day notice runs out stops a case for nonpayment.

Can my landlord lock me out in New Mexico?

No. Without a court order, a landlord cannot change the locks, block the door, shut off utilities, or remove your belongings. If that happens, you can withhold all rent for each day you are kept out, go to court to get back in, and claim damages and civil penalties.

Is the Albuquerque Section 8 waitlist open?

Yes, for a short window. The Albuquerque Housing Authority's How to Apply page lists Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher pre-applications as open October 16, 2026 through November 15, 2026, and public housing as open October 5 through October 31, 2026. Both are online only, and walk-ins are not accepted. Call AHA at (505) 764-3920 with questions.

Emergency Rent Help in Bakersfield, CA: City Rental Assistance, Free Legal Aid, and California's Deadlines

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Bakersfield and you are behind, there is a real rent door if you live inside city limits: the City of Bakersfield pays The Open Door Network to run a rental assistance program that covers back rent, short-term rent and deposits, through June 30, 2027. Families on CalWORKs can also get up to two months of back rent from Kern County. Free legal help comes from Greater Bakersfield Legal Assistance (GBLA). And California's clocks are short: 3 days after a notice for unpaid rent (weekends and court holidays do not count), then 10 court days to answer an eviction lawsuit. This guide covers Bakersfield and Kern County. Everything here was checked on November 25, 2026.

First calls: The day you get a 3-day notice, call The Open Door Network Housing Department at 661-716-1020 (or dial 2-1-1) and GBLA at (661) 325-5943. If you get court papers, file a response within 10 court days, or the landlord can win without a hearing.

California's clock in Bakersfield: 3 days, then 10 court days

Under Code of Civil Procedure 1161, a tenant who is behind on rent is in "unlawful detainer" only after "three days' notice, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays, in writing, requiring its payment, stating the amount that is due" and who to pay. If the landlord then sues, Code of Civil Procedure 1167 says "the defendant's response shall be filed within 10 days, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays, after the complaint is served upon the defendant." That 10-day rule took effect January 1, 2025. Use the 3 days: that is when the City's rental assistance and a lawyer can still keep a case from being filed.

The City's rent door: The Open Door Network

The City of Bakersfield's Homeless Hub describes its Rental Assistance Program this way (checked November 25, 2026):

  • What it is, in the City's words: "In early 2025, the City of Bakersfield awarded a two-year agreement to The Open Door Network to provide homelessness prevention and diversion services. These services include short-term rental assistance, rental arrears, rental deposits, move in assistance, housing search, and case management to qualifying households residing within the City of Bakersfield."
  • How long it runs: "Services are currently available through June 30, 2027."
  • How to apply: "call 2-1-1 or The Open Door Network Housing Department at 661-716-1020." Have your lease, the notice, a ledger or letter showing what you owe, ID and proof of income ready when you call.
  • Who it is for: households living inside Bakersfield city limits. If you live in an unincorporated part of Kern County or another city, dial 2-1-1 and ask what covers your address. See City of Bakersfield Homeless Hub: Prevention.

The Open Door Network's other services

The Bakersfield Kern Regional Homeless Collaborative lists The Open Door Network's outreach center at 1921 19th Street, (661) 322-9199, and a 24-hour crisis hotline, 661-327-1091, for survivors of intimate partner violence, sexual violence, child abuse and human trafficking, with emergency shelter and transitional housing.

CalWORKs families: Kern County's housing help

Kern County's Department of Human Services (DHS) runs the state's homeless and at-risk programs through its Housing Support Unit. If you get CalWORKs, or appear eligible, ask about these (checked November 25, 2026):

  • Permanent Homeless Arrearages Payment: for active CalWORKs recipients, "payment of up to two months of back owed rent (arrearages) that does not exceed 80 percent of the assisted unit's Total Monthly Household Income (TMHI) to avoid eviction of a family's current residence."
  • Temporary Homeless Assistance: payment for temporary shelter "for a maximum of 16-days in a 12-month period, while the family is seeking permanent housing," and Permanent Homeless Assistance to help pay the costs of moving into a new home.
  • CalWORKs Housing Support Program: rental assistance, housing navigation, case management, security deposits, utility payments, moving costs and legal services for CalWORKs families who are homeless or about to be.
  • How to apply: DHS says "Eligible families can apply for CalWORKs Homeless Assistance by simply contacting your Human Service Technician or visiting any DHS office." Homeless assistance is generally available once every 12 months. See Kern County DHS: Housing Resources.

Free legal help in Bakersfield

A lawyer cannot pay your rent, but can check the notice and the court papers, raise defenses like a bad notice or repairs that never got done, work out a move-out date or payment plan, and make sure you file your response on time.

  • Greater Bakersfield Legal Assistance (GBLA): main office at 615 California Ave., Bakersfield, CA 93304. Call (661) 325-5943, Monday through Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., closed weekends and court holidays. GBLA says it gives "free civil legal services to eligible Kern County residents," and its housing team "may help tenants before and during the eviction process." The homeless collaborative lists GBLA as accepting walk-ins.
  • Kern County Superior Court, Landlord Tenant Assistance: tenants can call (661) 610-6299 or email LTAC@kern.courts.ca.gov. The court's self-help pages walk through the unlawful detainer forms. kern.courts.ca.gov
  • No right to counsel here. Bakersfield and Kern County do not guarantee a free eviction lawyer, so call GBLA as soon as you get a notice, not the day your response is due.

Help paying rent in Bakersfield: the other doors

2-1-1 Kern County

Dial 2-1-1. CAPK runs it, and it answers 24 hours a day. The homeless collaborative calls 2-1-1 "one of the primary ways of engaging the Coordinated Entry System," which matches people who are homeless or about to be with providers. Ask for rent help for your exact address and write down every name and number. 2-1-1 Kern County

The Housing Authority's homeless housing

The Housing Authority of the County of Kern runs supportive housing sites such as 6th Street Apartments and 889 Oak Street for people who are homeless. It says: "To access these services and housing resources, call 2-1-1."

Utility bills: CAPK's Energy Program

  • Utility Assistance (LIHEAP): the Community Action Partnership of Kern's Energy Program helps pay electric, gas and propane bills. To qualify, CAPK says you must "reside in Kern County, have a total household income at or below 150 percent of the federal poverty income guidelines and have a utility bill with at least 22 days of service." It "can only be paid once in a twelve month period," and is "usually paid within 8 weeks directly to your utility company."
  • What to send: the Energy Intake Form (CSD 43), the consent form (CSD 081) and the client education form, plus proof of income for everyone, all pages of your utility bill and any notices from the past 30 days, your rental agreement, a valid Social Security card and a state ID or driver's license. For propane, add an estimate to fill your tank.
  • How to apply: email everything to heapoffice@capk.org, or mail or drop it off at the CAPK Energy Program office, 300 19th Street, Bakersfield, CA 93301. CAPK says appointments are encouraged but walk-ins are welcome. energy.capk.org

California eviction timeline in Bakersfield: what happens and when

These are the steps for an eviction over unpaid rent in Kern County, in order:

  • 1. The 3-day notice. It must state the amount due and the name, phone number and address of the person to pay. You get three days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays or court holidays (Code of Civil Procedure 1161). Call The Open Door Network or 2-1-1, DHS if you have CalWORKs, and GBLA now. Paying the full amount the notice demands within that time is the cleanest fix; get a receipt.
  • 2. The lawsuit. If you do not pay or move, the landlord can file an unlawful detainer case in Kern County Superior Court. A notice alone does not end your tenancy, and only the sheriff can physically remove you.
  • 3. Answer within 10 court days. You have 10 days, not counting weekends and court holidays, after you are served to file your response (Code of Civil Procedure 1167). The Kern court's tenant page still says "Usually, the defendant has 5 days to file a response"; the law changed on January 1, 2025, but file as early as you can. If you miss the deadline, the landlord can ask for a default judgment. Ask the court for a fee waiver if you cannot pay the filing fee.
  • 4. Trial and judgment. If you file a response, the court sets a trial. A settlement can include a payment plan or a move-out date you can live with. Bring receipts, texts, photos and proof of any rental assistance application.
  • 5. The writ and the sheriff. If the landlord wins, the court issues a writ of possession. It must say that if you do not move "within five days from the date of service of a copy of the writ," the sheriff "will remove the occupants" (Code of Civil Procedure 715.010). Things left behind can be stored, and you have 15 days after the landlord takes possession to pay reasonable storage costs and get them back.
  • Just cause and rent limits. Under the Tenant Protection Act, once you have "continuously and lawfully occupied" the home for 12 months, a covered landlord needs a just cause, stated in the notice, to end your tenancy (Civil Code 1946.2), and yearly rent increases are capped at "5 percent plus the percentage change in the cost of living, or 10 percent, whichever is lower" (Civil Code 1947.12). Some homes are exempt, such as many single-family homes. Not paying rent is a just cause, so these rules matter most when the notice is for something else.
  • Lockouts are illegal. A landlord cannot cut your utilities, change the locks, remove doors or windows, or take your belongings to force you out. A landlord who does can owe your actual damages plus up to $100 for each day, and at least $250 (Civil Code 789.3).

Security deposits: most landlords may not charge more than one month's rent. A landlord who is a person (or an LLC of people) and owns no more than two rental properties with no more than four units in all may charge up to two months, unless you are a service member. The landlord must return the deposit, or send an itemized statement of deductions, no later than 21 days after you move out (Civil Code 1950.5).

Vouchers: California's fair housing law makes it illegal to discriminate based on "source of income," which it defines to include "federal housing assistance vouchers issued under Section 8" and HUD-VASH vouchers (Government Code 12955). A Bakersfield landlord cannot turn you away just because you have a voucher. Report a refusal to the California Civil Rights Department, and see our source-of-income protections guide.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The Housing Authority of the County of Kern runs Section 8, project-based vouchers and public housing, not emergency rent. Its voucher page says "The Section 8 Waiting List is closed and applications are not available for this program," and its public housing list is closed too, but its project-based voucher lists have been open since May 1, 2026 (checked November 25, 2026). That is a long-term door, not this month's rent. See our Housing Authority guide. If you already have a voucher or Housing Authority housing and fall behind, call (661) 631-8500 right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • Catch-all "rent relief" sites. The City's program is run by The Open Door Network and reached through 2-1-1 or 661-716-1020. A site that asks for a fee to "apply" for rent help is not part of it.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1. If you are not safe at home, call The Open Door Network's 24-hour crisis hotline at 661-327-1091.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Bakersfield right now?

Yes, for people who live inside Bakersfield city limits. The City pays The Open Door Network to provide short-term rental assistance, back rent, deposits, move-in help and case management, and says services are available through June 30, 2027. Call The Open Door Network Housing Department at 661-716-1020 or dial 211. CalWORKs families can also ask Kern County Human Services about back rent.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Bakersfield?

Call Greater Bakersfield Legal Assistance at (661) 325-5943, Monday through Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., or go to 615 California Ave. GBLA gives free civil legal help to low-income Kern County residents, and its housing team helps tenants before and during an eviction. The Kern County Superior Court's Landlord Tenant Assistance line is (661) 610-6299.

How much notice does a Bakersfield landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

Three days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays or court holidays. The written notice must state the amount due and the name, phone number and address of the person to pay. If you have lived in the home for 12 months and it is covered by the Tenant Protection Act, the landlord also needs a just cause stated in the notice; unpaid rent counts as one.

How long do I have to answer an eviction lawsuit in Bakersfield?

Ten days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays or court holidays, after you are served, under Code of Civil Procedure 1167 as changed on January 1, 2025. Some Kern court pages still say 5 days; go by the 10-day rule but file as early as you can. If you miss the deadline, the landlord can get a default judgment without a hearing.

Where do I get help with my utility bill in Bakersfield?

The Community Action Partnership of Kern's Energy Program pays electric, gas and propane bills once in a 12-month period for Kern County households at or below 150% of the federal poverty guidelines. Email your application and documents to heapoffice@capk.org, or mail or drop it off at 300 19th Street in Bakersfield. CAPK says help is usually paid within 8 weeks.

Can my Bakersfield landlord lock me out or refuse my voucher?

No to both. California law bars a landlord from changing the locks, removing doors or windows, taking your things or cutting utilities to force you out, with damages of up to $100 a day and at least $250. And refusing a renter because of a Section 8 or HUD-VASH voucher is source-of-income discrimination under Government Code 12955.

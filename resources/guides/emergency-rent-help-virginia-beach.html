Emergency Rent Help in Virginia Beach: The Crisis Hotline, Legal Aid, and Virginia's 14-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Virginia Beach and you are behind, here is the honest picture. The City of Virginia Beach does not run a rent fund. Its housing department says that for landlord-tenant problems "our department cannot intervene as these issues are a civil matter," and its housing resources page lists no eviction prevention program (checked November 5, 2026). Virginia Beach is also not one of the localities on the state's list of Virginia Eviction Reduction Program communities. What you do have: the Regional Housing Crisis Hotline, 211 Virginia, free lawyers at the Legal Aid Society of Eastern Virginia, and two strong state-law tools, a 14-day notice and the right to pay and stay. Everything here was checked November 5, 2026.

Here's the short version: Call the Regional Housing Crisis Hotline at (757) 227-5932, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., and dial 2-1-1. If you get a 14-day notice or court papers, call Legal Aid's intake line at 757-827-5078. If you can raise the full amount, or get a nonprofit or government agency to promise it in writing, you can usually stop the case by paying. And go to court: the first hearing is usually within 21 days of filing.

Virginia gives you 14 days, and a right to pay

If rent is unpaid, the landlord must give written notice, and can end the lease only if "the tenant fails to pay rent within 14 days after written notice is served" (Va. Code 55.1-1245(F)). After the case is filed, Virginia's "right of redemption" lets you pay all rent, late charges, attorney fees and court costs on or before your first court date, or bring a "redemption tender," a written promise from a local government or nonprofit to pay within 10 days (Va. Code 55.1-1250). Even after you lose, you can pay all amounts owed "no less than 48 hours before" the time the sheriff has set for the eviction (55.1-1250).

Free legal help in Virginia Beach

We found no law giving Virginia Beach tenants a right to a free lawyer. These are the places to call:

  • Legal Aid Society of Eastern Virginia (LASEV): gives "free legal services in civil matters to qualifying low-income Virginians" in Hampton Roads. Its intake line, 757-827-5078, "is open Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday from 9:00AM-1:00PM." You can also apply online from laseva.org. Main office: 125 St. Paul's Blvd., Suite 400, Norfolk, (757) 627-5423 (checked November 5, 2026).
  • Eviction Defense Center (Virginia Poverty Law Center): FightMyEviction.org (in Spanish, NoDesalojo.org) has plain-language steps and forms. It says it "provides only general legal information about eviction," so use it to get ready, and call Legal Aid for advice on your case.
  • Virginia Beach General District Court, Civil Division: eviction cases (called "unlawful detainers") are heard here, at 2425 Nimmo Parkway, Building 10, second floor. Phone 757-385-4277 (option 3), Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. Clerks cannot give legal advice, but they can tell you your court date and whether the court offers the Eviction Diversion Program.

Help paying rent in Virginia Beach: the doors that are left

There is no single rent office. Call the hotline and 2-1-1 to find who has money this month, and keep calling. None of these programs promises help.

Regional Housing Crisis Hotline and the Housing Resource Center

The City's Department of Housing & Neighborhood Preservation sends people facing homelessness to the Regional Housing Crisis Hotline at (757) 227-5932 (TTY: 711), "Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5:30 p.m." The City's Housing Resource Center at 104 N. Witchduck Road does triage and assessment for people who are homeless or at risk of losing their home, with walk-ins "Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, or Friday, 8 a.m. to noon" (checked November 5, 2026). Ask about any prevention money, and ask for a written promise (a redemption tender) if they agree to pay.

211 Virginia

Dial 2-1-1, text CONNECT to 247211, or use live chat at 211virginia.org. 211 Virginia, a service of the Virginia Department of Social Services, says its resources are "available 24/7," and live help is Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Ask for rent, utility and eviction help in Virginia Beach.

The City's Optional Relocation help

If Code Enforcement or another City action forces you out of an unsafe home, the City offers "One-time assistance to reduce financial hardship for eligible low- to moderate-income tenants," "Dependent upon the availability of funding." Contact Sharon Shoff at 757-385-5836 or sshoff@vbgov.com. It does not pay back rent.

Utility bills: Virginia Energy Assistance

The Virginia Department of Social Services runs Energy Assistance for households with "a heating or cooling expense" and income at or below 150% of the federal poverty level. Its application windows are set by the calendar:

  • Fuel Assistance: from the second Tuesday in October to the second Friday in November. This year that is October 13 to Friday, November 13, 2026 (checked November 11, 2026). Apply by that day. After November 13, Fuel Assistance is closed until next October, and the next chance is Crisis Assistance.
  • Crisis Assistance: November 1 to March 15 for heating equipment and security deposits, and January 1 to March 15, 2027 for fuel and utility bills, if you have a heating emergency such as no heat or a cut-off notice.
  • Cooling Assistance: June 15 to August 17, for households that include someone 60 or older, someone with a disability, or a child under 6.

Apply online through CommonHelp, by phone at 855-635-4370 (Monday to Friday, 7 a.m. to 6 p.m.), or at the Virginia Beach Department of Social Services.

Virginia eviction timeline: what happens and when

Evictions in Virginia go through the General District Court as "unlawful detainer" cases. These are the steps for unpaid rent:

  • 1. The 14-day notice. The landlord must give written notice of the unpaid rent and that the lease will end if it is not paid within 14 days (Va. Code 55.1-1245(F)). Paying in full within the 14 days ends the problem. Starting July 1, 2027, landlords with more than four rental units must also offer a payment plan in some cases (55.1-1245(G)).
  • 2. The summons. The first hearing must be held "as soon as practicable, but not more than 21 days from the date of filing," and no later than 30 days, and you must be served "at least 10 days before the return day" (Va. Code 8.01-126(B)). The Virginia Beach court tells landlords to set the date 18 to 21 days out.
  • 3. The first court date. Go. If you do not show up, the landlord can win by default. This is your main chance to use the right of redemption: pay everything claimed, or present a redemption tender and the court continues the case for 10 days for the money to arrive (55.1-1250(B)).
  • 4. The Eviction Diversion Program, if the court offers it. State law lets "any general district court" run this program (55.1-1260 to 55.1-1262), so ask the clerk whether Virginia Beach does. If it does and you qualify, you pay at least 10% of what is owed at the first court date, then the rest in three payments of 30% by the fifth day of each of the next three months, while you keep paying current rent. You cannot use it if you were in an eviction diversion program or defaulted on a rent payment plan in the last 12 months.
  • 5. Judgment and appeal. If the landlord wins, you have 10 days to appeal (8.01-129). The sheriff cannot remove you before those 10 days are up and must give at least 72 hours' notice before the eviction (8.01-129). A writ not carried out within 30 days of being issued is canceled (8.01-471).
  • 6. Last chance to pay. You can still pay all amounts owed, including court and sheriff fees, "no less than 48 hours before the date and time scheduled by the officer" to carry out the writ (55.1-1250(D)). Get a written receipt and call the sheriff's office if you pay.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • A City of Virginia Beach rent fund. The City's housing resources page lists no eviction prevention or back-rent program, and says the department "cannot intervene" in landlord-tenant issues (checked November 5, 2026).
  • The Virginia Rent Relief Program. This was the state's pandemic-era rent program. It is closed, and its old pages on the state housing department's site now say "You are not authorized to access this page." Ignore old flyers.
  • The state Eviction Reduction Program in Virginia Beach. The state made VERP permanent in 2026, but Virginia Beach is not on DHCD's list of VERP communities, so there is no local VERP grantee to call.
  • Housing & Neighborhood Preservation's voucher list. The City department runs Section 8 for Virginia Beach; there is no separate housing authority. It says "The Virginia Beach Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) waiting list is closed. No new applications are being accepted." The list last "reopened on Tuesday, Nov. 9, 2021, and closed the same day" (checked November 5, 2026). See our Virginia Beach Housing and Neighborhood Preservation guide. If you already have a voucher and fall behind, call your housing specialist at 757-385-5750 right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and call the crisis hotline.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent help in Virginia Beach right now?

Not from a City fund. The City's housing department lists no back-rent program, and Virginia Beach has no state Eviction Reduction Program grantee. Call the Regional Housing Crisis Hotline at (757) 227-5932, Monday to Friday from 8 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., and dial 2-1-1 to find churches and nonprofits that may have money this month. If one agrees to pay, ask for a written promise you can show the court.

How much notice does a Virginia landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

Fourteen days. The landlord must give written notice of the unpaid rent and can end the lease only if you do not pay within 14 days after the notice is served. After that, the landlord can file an unlawful detainer, and the first hearing is usually within 21 days of filing.

Can I stop an eviction in Virginia by paying?

Usually yes. Under Virginia's right of redemption, you can pay all the rent, late charges, attorney fees and court costs on or before your first court date, or bring a written promise to pay from a local government or nonprofit, and the court gives 10 days for the money to arrive. Even after a judgment, you can pay everything owed, including sheriff fees, at least 48 hours before the scheduled eviction. Landlords with four or fewer units can limit this to once per lease period if they told you in writing.

What is the Eviction Diversion Program, and does Virginia Beach have it?

It is a court payment plan. If you qualify, you pay at least 10% of what is owed at your first court date and the rest in three monthly payments, while paying current rent, and the case is dismissed when you finish. State law lets each general district court choose whether to offer it, and the Virginia Beach court's civil page does not mention it, so call the clerk at 757-385-4277 (option 3) and ask.

How do I get a free lawyer for an eviction in Virginia Beach?

Call the Legal Aid Society of Eastern Virginia's intake line at 757-827-5078. It is open Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday from 9 a.m. to 1 p.m. You can also apply online at laseva.org. FightMyEviction.org, from the Virginia Poverty Law Center, has general information and forms, in Spanish at NoDesalojo.org.

Is the Virginia Beach Section 8 waiting list open?

No. The City's Housing & Neighborhood Preservation department says its Housing Choice Voucher waiting list is closed and no new applications are being accepted. It last opened for one day, November 9, 2021, and there is no date for the next opening. Virginia Beach has no separate housing authority and no public housing. Call 757-385-5750 with questions.

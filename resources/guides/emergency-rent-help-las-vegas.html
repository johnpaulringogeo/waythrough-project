Emergency Rent Help in Las Vegas: What Is Left After CHAP, the Eviction Diversion Program, and Nevada's 7-Day Notice

Matt · Updated October 2026

If you rent in Las Vegas or anywhere in Clark County and you are behind, start with the hard news: Clark County's big rent program, CHAP (later renamed Eviction Prevention and Fixed Income Rent Assistance), is out of money and is not processing new or pending applications. Old links, flyers, and even some agency pages still send people there. What is still open is smaller: Clark County Social Service's regular financial assistance, the court-based Eviction Diversion Program for seniors and people on SSI due to a disability, a few nonprofits that open applications when they have funding, and free eviction help from Legal Aid Center of Southern Nevada. Everything here was checked on October 28, 2026.

Here's the short version: If you got a 7-day notice to pay rent or quit, file a tenant's answer before the notice runs out, even if you are applying for help. Las Vegas tenants can file online at lacsn.org/efile or get help at the Civil Law Self-Help Center, 200 Lewis Ave. If you are 62 or older or get SSI due to a disability, say so in your answer: that is how you get into the Eviction Diversion Program. Then call Clark County Social Service at 702-455-4270 about its financial assistance, call Legal Aid Center at (702) 386-1070, and dial 2-1-1 for whatever else is open this month.

Nevada moves fast: 7 judicial days, then 24 to 36 hours

Under NRS 40.253, a landlord can serve a notice requiring you to pay the rent or leave "before the close of business on the seventh judicial day following the day of service." The Civil Law Self-Help Center explains that only judicial days count, so weekends and legal holidays are skipped. Within that same window you can file a Tenant's Affidavit/Answer with the justice court. Legal Aid Center of Southern Nevada is blunt about what happens if you don't: "If you don't file an answer in response to an eviction notice, you will be evicted without a court hearing." It also warns: "You can now be evicted with a pending rental assistance application." Once a judge signs an eviction order, the sheriff or constable posts it within 24 hours and can remove you "not earlier than 24 hours but not later than 36 hours after the posting of the order" (NRS 40.253).

Help paying rent in Las Vegas: the doors that are still open

None of these programs promises help, and several open and close with their funding. Apply to more than one, and keep answering court papers while you wait.

The Eviction Diversion Program (seniors and people on SSI)

This is the strongest door left, but it is narrow. Clark County, Legal Aid Center of Southern Nevada, and the Las Vegas, Henderson, and North Las Vegas Justice Courts run it together. In the County's words: "The EDP covers seniors age 62 years or older and those receiving SSI benefits due to a disability. To qualify applicants must be facing an eviction for non-payment of rent and must file a tenant answer within the notice period" (checked October 28, 2026). The 2025 Nevada Legislature funded its expansion to Henderson and North Las Vegas starting February 2, 2026.

  • How you get in: you do not sign up separately. Legal Aid Center says "Tenants who meet the above criteria are automatically diverted into EDP and will be contacted for an appointment with Clark County Social Services to determine if rental assistance is available." Make sure your answer shows your age or SSI status.
  • What happens next: check your email every day after you file; Legal Aid Center says the scheduling notice is "typically scheduled within ten days." Bring physical copies of the documents the notice asks for. If you cannot make the appointment, contact Clark County Social Service before it, or your rental assistance application will be delayed.
  • What you get: a meeting where Clark County Social Service may have funds for your back rent, and "Free legal representation provided by Legal Aid Center of Southern Nevada." The program only covers nonpayment-of-rent notices.
  • Questions: call 702-671-3322 or email LVJCEDP@clarkcountynv.gov, the contacts Clark County gives for EDP participants.

Clark County Social Service: regular financial assistance

Separate from CHAP, Clark County Social Service (CCSS) still describes a Financial Assistance program for rent and utilities. In its words: "Households of one may be eligible for a maximum of $400.00 a month in assistance. An additional $135.00 a month is available for each additional household member. A household is only eligible for assistance once every twelve months," unless CCSS finds a barrier to employment or you have a disability as defined by the Social Security Administration (checked October 28, 2026). Checks go to you or straight to the landlord.

  • Who it is for: CCSS says it "assists area residents facing financial hardship and who lack sufficient income or resources to pay their rental housing costs, utilities, and other related expenses." Its application asks about every program you receive or have applied for, so expect to show you have tried other help first.
  • How to apply: "Applications may be submitted in-person at one of our 4 locations, by fax at 702-455-6260 or mailed to Clark County Social Services 1600 Pinto Lane, Las Vegas, NV 89106." The offices are 1600 Pinto Lane (Las Vegas), 2432 N. Martin Luther King Blvd., Suite D (North Las Vegas), 3885 S. Maryland Pkwy. (Cambridge Annex), and 1291 W. Galleria Dr., Suite 170 (Henderson), all open Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Phone: 702-455-4270.
  • How it works: the application says it "will be assigned to a Social Service Worker who will contact you to schedule an interview," and that your eligibility is decided at the interview, where you must bring all the documents and verifications requested.

Get the English or Spanish application from the County's Rental/Utility Assistance page.

Nonprofits that open applications when they have money

  • HopeLink of Southern Nevada (Henderson): HopeLink's Housing Stabilization page says it "is now accepting a limited number of applications for rent, utility and deposit assistance each month," by phone at 702-566-0576, Monday through Thursday, 8 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. But its online application page said "We have reached the maximum number of entries for this application cycle" when we checked on October 28, 2026. HopeLink says "Applications are only available when funding is available," that in most cases households must earn at or below 60% of Area Median Income, and that your rent generally has to be below HUD's Fair Market Rent. Payments go directly to the landlord. Office: 178 Westminster Way, Henderson.
  • Economic Opportunity Board of Clark County (EOB): EOB's rental assistance pays one month of rent and/or one month of utilities. Its page says that at the start of each month it reviews "the first 50 applications for eligibility," processes them until the monthly money runs out, and then closes the online portal until the next month. Legal Aid Center reported in February 2026 that EOB was working out new funding agreements, "which will place a temporary hold on funding." Call 702-445-7105 before you count on it.
  • Nevada 211: dial 2-1-1 or 1-866-535-5654, Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 9 p.m., or search the housing expense assistance directory by ZIP code. Legal Aid Center says Nevada 211 "keeps a list of current rental assistance."

Veterans: U.S.VETS Supportive Services for Veteran Families

The VA's list of fiscal year 2027 SSVF grants includes United States Veterans Initiative (U.S.VETS) for Nevada. U.S.VETS Las Vegas says its SSVF program "assists low-income families who are homeless or at risk of becoming homeless in finding safe and affordable transitional or permanent housing," and that it gives more than 400 veteran households rapid re-housing and homeless prevention services each year. Call the SSVF Program Coordinator at 702-947-4471 or the outreach line at 702-947-4446. The office is at 525 East Bonanza Road.

Utility bills

If a power or gas bill is what is pushing your rent out of reach, apply to Nevada's Energy Assistance Program through the Division of Welfare and Supportive Services. You need proof of all household income for at least the last 30 days, ID for the head of household, and your most recent heating or cooling bills; prior-year recipients "may not reapply until approximately 11 months after they received their last benefit." HELP of Southern Nevada also lists Energy SHARE, Southwest Gas utility assistance, among its family services; call (702) 369-4357.

If you are about to lose your home

The City of Las Vegas runs the Courtyard Homeless Resource Center at 314 Foremaster Lane, which the City says "is open seven days a week 24 hours a day." Call 702-229-6117. The City's own report says the Courtyard offers referrals to housing programs, emergency rental assistance, and relocation help. For shelter tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Eviction help in Las Vegas: free lawyers and court help

If you searched for Las Vegas eviction help, these are the answers with names. A lawyer cannot pay your rent, but can check the notice, raise defenses, and negotiate with the landlord.

Legal Aid Center of Southern Nevada

  • Contact: (702) 386-1070, 725 E. Charleston Blvd., Las Vegas. TDD: (702) 386-1059.
  • Who qualifies: households under its income guidelines, which it bases on 65% of the 2025 median family income in Nevada, with less than $10,000 in assets not counting a house or car. Check the table on its Do I Qualify? page.
  • Eviction tools: its Nevada Eviction Prevention Guide and its 2026 Rental Assistance Availability page, which it keeps up to date.

Civil Law Self-Help Center

Legal Aid Center runs the Civil Law Self-Help Center under a contract with Clark County. It is on the first floor of the Regional Justice Center, 200 Lewis Ave., Monday through Thursday, 7 a.m. to 5 p.m., closed on court holidays. It has the free Tenant's Affidavit/Answer forms, the fee waiver form, and step-by-step help on responding to an eviction notice and responding to an eviction order. The Las Vegas Justice Court requires electronic filing, so you will need an email address.

Nevada eviction timeline for unpaid rent

Most Las Vegas landlords use the "summary eviction" process in NRS 40.253. These are the steps, in order:

  • 1. The 7-day notice. The notice gives you until the close of business on the seventh judicial day after service to pay or leave. (If you pay rent weekly and have lived there 45 days or less, the landlord can instead give you until noon of the fourth full day.) The notice must name the court and tell you about your right to file an affidavit.
  • 2. Pay, or file your answer in time. If you pay the rent, the landlord cannot refuse it just because you have not also paid collection fees, attorney's fees, other non-rent costs, a reasonable late fee, or a security deposit (NRS 40.253). If you cannot pay, file a Tenant's Affidavit/Answer with the justice court before the notice runs out. The Self-Help Center stresses that "filing" means submitting the papers to the clerk, not just getting to the courthouse.
  • 3. Diversion, if you qualify. If you are 62 or older or get SSI due to a disability, a timely answer in a nonpayment case puts you in the Eviction Diversion Program before any hearing with a judge.
  • 4. The hearing. Go. If you filed an answer and the landlord files for eviction, the court sets a hearing; the Self-Help Center says it "usually takes place within one week," and that if the tenant fails to appear, "the court will likely grant the eviction." The Las Vegas Justice Court mails the hearing notice, but check your case online too.
  • 5. The order. If the judge grants the eviction, the sheriff or constable posts the order within 24 hours of getting it and can remove you between 24 and 36 hours after posting (NRS 40.253).
  • 6. Asking for more time. Before removal, you can file a Motion to Stay asking for up to 10 more days to move. It costs a $71 filing fee unless you get a fee waiver, most courts allow only one, and the Self-Help Center says judges usually decide it within one or two days.
  • Lockouts and shutoffs are illegal. Under NRS 118A.480 a landlord may not take back the unit, including by cutting off essential services, except through a court case, when you surrender it, or when you have abandoned it. If a landlord locks you out or shuts off service illegally, NRS 118A.390 lets you file a verified complaint for expedited relief within 5 judicial days to get back in, and recover actual damages, up to $2,500 set by the court, or both.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • CHAP / Eviction Prevention and Fixed Income Rent Assistance. Clark County's CHAP site says: "all remaining funds for the Eviction Prevention Program have been fully exhausted. As a result, no new or pending applications can be processed" (checked October 28, 2026). It still has "START HERE" buttons and some nonprofits still point to it; ignore them unless you were referred through the Eviction Diversion Program.
  • The City of Las Vegas RAFT program. The City's pandemic-era Rental Assistance for Tenants program is not a current door: its old web address now shows "Page cannot be found," and we found no current City rent application (checked October 28, 2026). The City's door today is the Courtyard, above.
  • The Nevada Housing Division funds housing development and statewide programs through local partners. We found no rent application for tenants on its site.
  • SNRHA, the Southern Nevada Regional Housing Authority, runs Section 8 and public housing, not an emergency rent fund. Its Housing Choice Voucher page shows "Waitlist is Closed." Its public housing and RAD pages show "Waitlist is Open," and its RentCafe portal says "We Are Currently Accepting Applications For Available Waiting Lists," so check the portal header to see which lists are open (checked October 28, 2026). If you already have SNRHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call (702) 477-3100 right away and get legal help.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight, go to the Courtyard, and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Las Vegas?

Some, but much less than before. Clark County's big rent program (CHAP, later called Eviction Prevention and Fixed Income Rent Assistance) has run out of money. What is left: Clark County Social Service's regular financial assistance (up to $400 a month for one person, plus $135 for each extra household member, generally once every 12 months; call 702-455-4270); the Eviction Diversion Program for tenants 62 and older or on SSI due to a disability who answer a nonpayment eviction notice; and nonprofits such as HopeLink and the Economic Opportunity Board, which take a limited number of applications when they have funding. Dial 2-1-1 for others.

Is CHAP still taking applications in Clark County?

No. Clark County's CHAP site says "all remaining funds for the Eviction Prevention Program have been fully exhausted" and "no new or pending applications can be processed" (checked October 28, 2026). The one exception is tenants referred to the Eviction Diversion Program through the Las Vegas Justice Court; for that program call 702-671-3322 or email LVJCEDP@clarkcountynv.gov.

Who qualifies for the Las Vegas Eviction Diversion Program?

Clark County says the program covers seniors age 62 or older and people receiving SSI benefits due to a disability, who are facing eviction for nonpayment of rent and file a tenant's answer within the notice period. It runs in the Las Vegas, Henderson and North Las Vegas Justice Courts. You do not sign up separately: eligible tenants are diverted automatically and get an appointment with Clark County Social Service, which may be able to pay back rent, and free legal representation from Legal Aid Center of Southern Nevada.

How fast is an eviction for unpaid rent in Nevada?

Very fast. The landlord serves a 7-day notice to pay rent or quit. You have until the close of business on the seventh judicial day after service (weekends and legal holidays do not count) to pay or to file a tenant's affidavit/answer with the justice court. If you file, the court sets a hearing, usually within about a week. If you do not file, you can be evicted without a hearing. Once an eviction order is issued, the sheriff or constable posts it within 24 hours and can remove you between 24 and 36 hours after posting.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Las Vegas?

Call Legal Aid Center of Southern Nevada at (702) 386-1070; its office is at 725 E. Charleston Blvd. It serves households under its income limits, which are based on 65% of Nevada's median family income. For forms and in-person help filing your answer, go to the Civil Law Self-Help Center on the first floor of the Regional Justice Center, 200 Lewis Ave., Monday through Thursday, 7 a.m. to 5 p.m. Las Vegas tenants can file the tenant's answer online at lacsn.org/efile.

Does SNRHA give emergency rent help, and is the Section 8 list open?

No to both, for most people. The Southern Nevada Regional Housing Authority runs Section 8 and public housing, not an emergency rent fund. Its Housing Choice Voucher page shows "Waitlist is Closed" (checked October 28, 2026). Its public housing and RAD pages show "Waitlist is Open," so check the RentCafe applicant portal to see which lists are taking applications. If you already have SNRHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call SNRHA at (702) 477-3100 and get legal help.

Emergency Rent Help in Richmond: Eviction Diversion, Free Lawyers, and Virginia's 14-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in the City of Richmond and you are behind, here is the honest picture. There is no walk-in City rent fund, and Richmond is not one of the communities on the state's Virginia Eviction Reduction Program list (checked November 5, 2026). What you do have: the City's Eviction Diversion Program, free lawyers through Central Virginia Legal Aid Society, which the City funds for a Right to Counsel program, 211 Virginia, energy help, and two strong state-law tools, a 14-day notice and the right to pay and stay. Everything here was checked November 5, 2026.

Here's the short version: If you get a 14-day notice or court papers, call Central Virginia Legal Aid Society at 804-648-1012 and sign up for the City's Eviction Diversion Program workshop the same day. If you can raise the full amount, or get a nonprofit or government agency to promise it in writing, you can usually stop the case by paying. Behind on heat? Virginia's Fuel Assistance window closes Friday, November 13, 2026. And go to court: the first hearing is usually within 21 days of filing.

Virginia gives you 14 days, and a right to pay

If rent is unpaid, the landlord must give written notice, and can end the lease only if "the tenant fails to pay rent within 14 days after written notice is served" (Va. Code 55.1-1245(F)). After the case is filed, Virginia's "right of redemption" lets you pay all rent, late charges, attorney fees and court costs on or before your first court date, or bring a "redemption tender," a written promise from a local government or nonprofit to pay within 10 days (Va. Code 55.1-1250). Even after you lose, you can pay all amounts owed "no less than 48 hours before" the time the sheriff has set for the eviction (55.1-1250).

Free legal help in Richmond

Virginia law does not give tenants a right to a free lawyer. But the City of Richmond funds a Right to Counsel program: on June 4, 2026 the City said "Richmonders who may need legal assistance for a housing matter can apply for assistance from the City-funded" Right to Counsel program, and sent them to Central Virginia Legal Aid Society. These are the places to call:

  • Central Virginia Legal Aid Society (CVLAS): handles "Housing (foreclosure, eviction, lockouts, utilities, repairs, etc.)" for the City of Richmond. Call 804-648-1012 or toll-free 800-868-1012, "M-F 9:00am – 4:30pm except holidays," or apply online from cvlas.org/get-help; "An intake specialist will call you back and confirm eligibility." CVLAS says "Generally, households must have gross income below 125% of the federal poverty guidelines," and it may take some households up to 200%. Office: 115 S. 15th St., Suite 400 (checked November 5, 2026). The City does not publish separate income rules for its Right to Counsel program, so ask CVLAS.
  • Legal Aid Justice Center (LAJC): helps tenants with landlord-tenant problems; it does not advise landlords. Call the Richmond office at 804-643-1086 for phone intake, Monday to Friday, or use its online form at justice4all.org. Office: 626 E. Broad St., Suite 200.
  • Eviction Defense Center (Virginia Poverty Law Center): FightMyEviction.org (in Spanish, NoDesalojo.org) has plain-language steps and forms. It says it "provides only general legal information about eviction," so use it to get ready, and call a legal aid office for advice on your case.
  • Richmond General District Court, Civil: eviction cases (called "unlawful detainers") are heard at the John Marshall Courts Building, 400 N. 9th St., Room 203. Civil phone (804) 646-6461; the clerk's office is open 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. Clerks cannot give legal advice, but they can tell you your court date and how the court's Eviction Diversion Program works.

Help paying rent in Richmond: the doors that are left

There is no single rent office. Start with the City's Eviction Diversion Program and 211, and keep calling. None of these programs promises help.

The City's Eviction Diversion Program (Southside Community Development & Housing Corp.)

The City says "Richmond's Eviction Diversion Program is now administered and run by Southside Community Development & Housing Corp." (SCDHC). The goal is to "bring a tenant's outstanding rent to a zero balance within three months or less." SCDHC lists these rules:

  • You live in the City of Richmond and got a written nonpayment notice or a Summons for Unlawful Detainer.
  • Your household's gross income is no more than 80% of the area median income, and you can keep paying rent, utilities and usual bills on time.
  • Unpaid rent and charges are the only problem with your landlord, and your landlord agrees to take part.
  • You have not received rental assistance from another City-funded source in the last 12 months.

How to apply: "Your first step is to complete the EDP information workshop before we can help." The workshops are live and virtual; register from scdhc.com/eviction-diversion-program, and you get the application at the end. If approved, SCDHC pays your landlord electronically. Neither the City nor SCDHC says whether funds are available right now, and the City's posted rules asked tenants to apply at least two weeks before the court date, so start early. SCDHC's office is at 1624 Hull Street (checked November 5, 2026).

Other places to ask

  • Capital Area Partnership Uplifting People (CAPUP): the state housing department lists its Emergency Financial Assistance Program for Richmond at 804-788-0050. Call to ask whether it has funds this month.
  • 211 Virginia: dial 2-1-1, text CONNECT to 247211, or chat at 211virginia.org. Live help is Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.; the online directory is always on. Ask for rent, utility and eviction help in the City of Richmond.
  • Housing Resource Line: 804-422-5061, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., if you are more than 3 days from losing your home. The City says it "does not, however, provide direct resources and services"; it tells you who does.
  • Richmond Department of Social Services: Emergency Assistance for families with children after a fire, natural disaster or "housing crisis." Dial 3-1-1, or (804) 646-7212 from outside the city.

Utility bills: Energy Assistance, EnergyShare and MetroCare

The Virginia Department of Social Services runs Energy Assistance for households with "a heating or cooling expense" and income at or below 150% of the federal poverty level (for example, $3,331 a month before taxes for a household of three). Its application windows are set by the calendar:

  • Fuel Assistance: from the second Tuesday in October to the second Friday in November. This year that is October 13 to November 13, 2026, so apply now. After November 13, the next chance is Crisis Assistance.
  • Crisis Assistance: November 1, 2026 to March 15, 2027 for heating equipment and security deposits, and from the first workday in January (January 4, 2027) to March 15, 2027 for fuel and utility bills, if you have a heating emergency such as no heat or a cut-off notice. After March 15, 2027, ask about Cooling Assistance or EnergyShare.
  • Cooling Assistance: June 15 to August 15, 2027 (the state's web page says August 17, so apply by August 15 to be safe), for households that include someone 60 or older, someone with a disability, or a child under 6.

Apply online through CommonHelp, by phone at 855-635-4370 (Monday to Friday, 7 a.m. to 6 p.m.), or at Richmond Social Services, 300 E. Franklin St. or the Southside Community Services Center, 4100 Hull Street Road.

  • Dominion Energy EnergyShare: "Help is available year-round, regardless of income," if you have a disconnection notice, need reconnection, or need help keeping a payment plan. The most it pays is $700 in the heating season (October 1 to April 30) and $400 in the cooling season (May 1 to September 30). Local agencies decide; Dominion's list for Richmond names The Salvation Army (804-225-7470) and CAPUP (804-788-0050), or find one through 211.
  • MetroCare (City of Richmond Department of Public Utilities): for gas and water customers with an active account in their name at their home, income at or below 225% of the federal poverty guidelines, and a hardship such as job loss or a family crisis. It pays up to $1,000 each for water and sewer, natural gas, and conservation, up to $3,000 per household per fiscal year. "Funding is limited, and assistance is provided based on eligibility and available funds." Apply in person at the East District Initiative Center, 701 N. 25th St., (804) 646-0600. DPU Customer Care: (804) 646-4646.

Virginia eviction timeline: what happens and when

Evictions in Virginia go through the General District Court as "unlawful detainer" cases. In Richmond, that is the civil division at the John Marshall Courts Building. These are the steps for unpaid rent:

  • 1. The 14-day notice. The landlord must give written notice of the unpaid rent and that the lease will end if it is not paid within 14 days (Va. Code 55.1-1245(F)). Paying in full within the 14 days ends the problem. Starting July 1, 2027, landlords with more than four rental units must also offer a payment plan in some cases (55.1-1245(G)). This is the time to sign up for the City's Eviction Diversion Program workshop.
  • 2. The summons. The first hearing must be held "as soon as practicable, but not more than 21 days from the date of filing," and no later than 30 days, and you must be served "at least 10 days before the return day" (Va. Code 8.01-126(B)).
  • 3. The first court date. Go. If you do not show up, the landlord can win by default. This is your main chance to use the right of redemption: pay everything claimed, or present a redemption tender and the court continues the case for 10 days for the money to arrive (55.1-1250(B)).
  • 4. The court's Eviction Diversion Program. State law lets "any general district court" run this program (55.1-1260 to 55.1-1262), and the Richmond General District Court posts the program's forms. If you qualify, you pay at least 10% of what is owed at the first court date, then the rest in three payments of 30% by the fifth day of each of the next three months, while you keep paying current rent. You cannot use it if you were in an eviction diversion program or defaulted on a rent payment plan in the last 12 months. Ask the clerk at (804) 646-6461.
  • 5. Judgment and appeal. If the landlord wins, you have 10 days to appeal (8.01-129). The sheriff cannot remove you before those 10 days are up and must give at least 72 hours' notice before the eviction (8.01-129). A writ not carried out within 30 days of being issued is canceled (8.01-471).
  • 6. Last chance to pay. You can still pay all amounts owed, including court and sheriff fees, "no less than 48 hours before the date and time scheduled by the officer" to carry out the writ (55.1-1250(D)). Get a written receipt and call the sheriff's office if you pay.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • A walk-in City of Richmond rent fund. The City's eviction prevention page lists the Eviction Diversion Program, the Housing Resource Line and the Homeless Connection Line, and no other rent fund (checked November 5, 2026).
  • The Virginia Rent Relief Program. This was the state's pandemic-era rent program. It is closed. Ignore old flyers.
  • The state Eviction Reduction Program in Richmond. The state made VERP permanent in 2026, but Richmond is not on DHCD's list of VERP communities, so there is no local VERP grantee to call.
  • The old Eviction Legal Helpline. Its old web address now sends you to FightMyEviction.org, which lists no helpline number. Call Central Virginia Legal Aid Society instead.
  • RRHA's voucher list. The Richmond Redevelopment and Housing Authority runs Section 8 for the city. Its voucher waiting list is closed, and on May 20, 2025 it said it "has suspended the issuance of tenant-based vouchers" until "funding is available." We found no notice lifting that pause. See our Richmond Redevelopment and Housing Authority guide. If you already have a voucher and fall behind, call your housing specialist right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home within 3 days, call the Homeless Connection Line at 804-972-0813 and see I need housing tonight.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent help in Richmond right now?

Not from a walk-in City fund. The main door is the City's Eviction Diversion Program, run by Southside Community Development and Housing Corp. It is for City of Richmond renters with a written nonpayment notice or an eviction summons, income at or below 80% of the area median, and a landlord who agrees to take part. Your first step is a free virtual workshop. Also call Capital Area Partnership Uplifting People at 804-788-0050 and dial 2-1-1 to find churches and nonprofits that may have money this month.

How much notice does a Virginia landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

Fourteen days. The landlord must give written notice of the unpaid rent and can end the lease only if you do not pay within 14 days after the notice is served. After that, the landlord can file an unlawful detainer, and the first hearing is usually within 21 days of filing.

Can I stop an eviction in Virginia by paying?

Usually yes. Under Virginia's right of redemption, you can pay all the rent, late charges, attorney fees and court costs on or before your first court date, or bring a written promise to pay from a local government or nonprofit, and the court gives 10 days for the money to arrive. Even after a judgment, you can pay everything owed, including sheriff fees, at least 48 hours before the scheduled eviction. Landlords with four or fewer units can limit this to once per lease period if they told you in writing.

What is the Eviction Diversion Program in Richmond?

There are two. The City's program, run by Southside Community Development and Housing Corp., can help pay what you owe if your landlord agrees; it starts with a virtual workshop. The court program is a payment plan under state law: you pay at least 10% of what is owed at your first court date and the rest in three monthly payments, while paying current rent, and the case is dismissed when you finish. The Richmond General District Court posts the forms for it. Ask the civil clerk at (804) 646-6461 how it works in your case.

How do I get a free lawyer for an eviction in Richmond?

Call Central Virginia Legal Aid Society at 804-648-1012, Monday to Friday from 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., or apply online. The City of Richmond funds a Right to Counsel program, and in June 2026 it sent renters who need legal help with a housing matter to Central Virginia Legal Aid Society. Virginia law does not give you a right to a free lawyer, and CVLAS generally serves households under 125% of the federal poverty guidelines. The Legal Aid Justice Center also takes tenant calls at 804-643-1086.

Is the Richmond Section 8 waiting list open?

No. The Richmond Redevelopment and Housing Authority says its Housing Choice Voucher list is closed, and it has paused issuing new vouchers from the list since May 20, 2025 until funding is available. Its last public housing, senior and project-based opening ran from May 4 to May 8, 2026. Call RRHA at (804) 780-4200 with questions.

Emergency Rent Help in Mesa, Arizona: MesaCAN, Free Eviction Lawyers, Utility Help, and the 5-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Mesa and you are behind, there is one main door for rent money: the Mesa Community Action Network (MesaCAN), run by the nonprofit A New Leaf. It "provides rent and utility assistance to low-income Mesa residents," but rent appointments open only once a month, on the first Thursday, and go fast. Mesa renters cannot use Maricopa County's rent program, which serves the county "but not in the cities of Glendale, Mesa, or Phoenix." For eviction help, any Maricopa County renter facing eviction for unpaid rent can get free legal help from Community Legal Services at 602-385-8880. Everything here was checked on November 5, 2026.

Here's the short version: Mark the first Thursday of the month. Call MesaCAN at (480) 750-9228 between 2 and 3 p.m. and keep trying until you reach a person. The next first Thursdays are December 3, 2026, and January 7, 2027. If you got a 5-day notice or court papers, call Community Legal Services at 602-385-8880 the same day. Call 2-1-1 (9 a.m. to 7 p.m., every day) to ask which charities have money this week. Then go to your court hearing, even if you think you can't win.

A 5-day notice is not an eviction

In Arizona, a landlord who is owed rent must first give you a written notice. Under A.R.S. § 33-1368(B), if you don't pay "within five days after written notice," the landlord may then file a special detainer (eviction) case in court. The law says these are calendar days, so weekends count. You can still save the lease. If you pay "all past due and unpaid periodic rent and a reasonable late fee set forth in a written rental agreement" before the landlord files, the law says the rental agreement "shall be reinstated." After the case is filed, paying still works, but you must also pay the landlord's attorney fees and court costs. The Maricopa County Justice Courts put it plainly: "Paying in full before the judge rules will stop the eviction." After a judgment, letting you stay is up to the landlord (checked November 5, 2026). Keep proof of every payment.

Help paying rent in Mesa: the doors that pay

Call before you count on any of these; all of them run out of money.

MesaCAN rent help: the main door for Mesa residents

MesaCAN is Mesa's Community Action Agency. Arizona's Department of Economic Security (DES) lists it for the City of Mesa at 635 E. Broadway Rd., and the City of Mesa sends rent and utility help there (checked November 5, 2026).

  • Who qualifies: low-income Mesa residents. A New Leaf says "A household must meet established application criteria" and does not publish an income limit.
  • How to get an appointment, in A New Leaf's words: "Please contact MesaCAN on the first Thursday of the month between 2:00-3:00pm or until all appointments are taken." Call (480) 750-9228. "You must reach a live person when you call, who will provide instructions for receiving assistance."
  • The catch: "Only a limited number of appointments can be taken due to limited funding." If you miss out, try again the next first Thursday, and use the other doors below in the meantime.
  • DES Short-Term Crisis Services: DES helps families with "a child under 18" and income at or below 125% of the federal poverty guidelines (150% if someone is 60 or older or has a disability) with rent, utilities and deposits, "limited to once in a 12-month period." DES says "To apply for STCS, contact the local Community Action Agency (CAA) in your area," which for Mesa is MesaCAN.
  • Office: 635 E. Broadway Rd., Mesa, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.

Other doors in Mesa

  • Mesa Hope Line: the City of Mesa's line "for people facing homelessness or housing insecurity." Call 480-644-HOPE (4673) or email hope@mesaaz.gov.
  • St. Vincent de Paul: offers "one-time rent, mortgage and utility assistance" through volunteer groups based in local Catholic churches. "Each church can only serve within its neighborhood," so use the map on St. Vincent de Paul's rent and utility page to find the group that serves your address and call it.
  • City of Mesa Security & Utility Deposit Assistance: this helps you move, not catch up. It pays the refundable part of a security or utility deposit for a home inside Mesa if household income is "at or below 80% of the Area Median Income (AMI)," and you must apply before you sign the lease or move in. The City says "Assistance is based on the availability of funding."

211 Arizona: the switchboard

211 Arizona says "Help is available from 9:00am – 7:00pm, seven days a week: in English and Spanish." Dial 2-1-1, or call 877-211-8661 from any phone. Ask which rent programs in your Mesa ZIP code have money right now.

Utility help in Mesa: LIHEAP, SRP, APS, and City of Mesa utilities

In Mesa, the summer power bill can be as big as the rent. Your electric company depends on your address: SRP, APS, or the City of Mesa itself.

  • MesaCAN utility help: call (480) 833-9200 to schedule an orientation; orientations are "held Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday morning beginning at 8am," and "If you are 60+ you will not need to attend an orientation." A New Leaf says "this is only for City of Mesa residents."
  • LIHEAP: apply on your own through DES's A-to-Z Arizona Portal or with a paper application. DES says the regular benefit is $160 to $640 based on household points, and crisis help (for a shutoff notice) is "up to a maximum of $500." LIHEAP service line: 1-866-494-1981, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. The separate Power AZ program is closed: DES says "Power AZ is Now Exhausted," and it cannot process applications sent after September 21, 2026.
  • SRP: call (602) 236-8888, 24/7. SRP offers payment plans and extensions, and its Income-Qualified Discount gives eligible customers "$10 or $35" a month, "up to $420 a year."
  • APS: the Energy Support Program gives "a monthly discount up to 25% or 60% based on household income," and Crisis Bill Assistance can give "up to $1,000 in bill assistance" through partners such as Wildfire, Chicanos Por La Causa, and Maricopa County Human Services.
  • City of Mesa utilities: if the City of Mesa is your electric company, its Summer Electric Assistance program helps with July, August and September bills, but the 2026 application window ran from April 1 to August 1, 2026, and is closed; check mesaaz.gov in the spring of 2027. Mesa also gives "A 30% discount on the Water Service Rate for seniors with a limited income."

Eviction help in Mesa: free lawyers

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can check the landlord's notice, raise defenses, and work out more time or a payment plan.

  • Maricopa County Legal Assistance for Renters (run by Community Legal Services): the County says this help is "available to all residents of Maricopa County facing eviction due to nonpayment of rent," including advice and representation in court. Mesa is included. Call 602-385-8880.
  • Community Legal Services (CLS): main line (602) 258-3434 or 1-800-852-9075. You can also apply online at apply.azlawhelp.org.
  • Your court: most Mesa eviction cases go to the Maricopa County Justice Courts in Mesa. The East Mesa, Highland, North Mesa and West Mesa precincts share the courthouse at 222 E. Javelina Ave., Mesa. Your summons names your court. The Justice Courts say "All eviction cases can be heard virtually." Office line: (602) 506-8530.
  • Court self-help: the Arizona courts' eviction self-service center (Spanish at azcourts.gov/desalojo) has forms and videos.

Arizona eviction rules: what happens and when

These steps come from the Arizona Revised Statutes on azleg.gov and the Maricopa County Justice Courts:

  • 1. The notice. For unpaid rent, the landlord must give you written notice and wait 5 calendar days (A.R.S. § 33-1368(B)). For other lease problems, the notice is usually 10 days (5 days for health and safety problems).
  • 2. Pay, and the lease comes back. Paying all past-due rent and a reasonable late fee from a written lease before the case is filed reinstates the lease. After filing, you must also pay attorney fees and court costs. The Justice Courts say the amount "will likely include rent, late fees, attorney's fees and court costs."
  • 3. The court case. An eviction for unpaid rent is a "special detainer" case. A.R.S. § 33-1377(A) says the court procedures in Title 12, Chapter 8, Article 4 (A.R.S. § 12-1171 and following) apply, except where § 33-1377 sets its own rules. Under A.R.S. § 33-1377(B), the hearing date on the summons must be "not more than six nor less than three days from the date of the summons." The judge can postpone it for up to 3 days for good cause.
  • 4. The hearing. The judge asks whether you agree you owe the money. Valid defenses include proof that you paid or that the notice was not done right. If you don't show up, you will almost certainly lose by default, so go, or call a lawyer first.
  • 5. After a judgment. Under A.R.S. § 12-1178(C), "No writ of restitution shall issue until the expiration of five calendar days after the rendition of judgment." A constable, not your landlord, carries out the writ.
  • No lockouts. If a landlord locks you out or shuts off your power, gas, or water to force you out, A.R.S. § 33-1367 lets you get back in or end the lease and recover up to two months' rent or twice your damages, whichever is more.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • Maricopa County's rental assistance does not serve Mesa. The County says applicants must live "in Maricopa County, but not in the cities of Glendale, Mesa, or Phoenix." Mesa renters go to MesaCAN.
  • The Arizona Rental Assistance Program (ARAP) has ended. DES says "The Arizona Rental Assistance Program has ended and applications are no longer being accepted"; only applications sent by August 31, 2024 are still being processed.
  • The Mesa Housing Authority runs Section 8 for the City. It is not an emergency rent fund, and it says "The Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher Program Wait List application is currently closed" (checked November 5, 2026). See our Mesa Housing Authority guide. If you already have a voucher and your income dropped, call 480-644-3536 right away.
  • The Housing Authority of Maricopa County (HAMC) is a separate county agency. Its voucher list has been closed since August 25, 2023. HAMC says it is closing all of its open property wait lists, including Clare Feldstadt Apartments in Mesa, "effective 8:00am December 1, 2026 (Arizona Time)"; until then it takes online applications only, and after that the lists stay closed until further notice. HAMC: 602-744-4500.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight, call the Mesa Hope Line, and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Mesa right now?

Yes, but it is limited. MesaCAN, run by A New Leaf, gives rent and utility help to low-income Mesa residents. For rent, call (480) 750-9228 on the first Thursday of the month between 2 and 3 p.m., or until appointments run out. A New Leaf says only a limited number of appointments can be taken because funding is limited. Dial 2-1-1 to ask which charities have money now.

Can I use Maricopa County's rent program if I live in Mesa?

No. Maricopa County's rental assistance is for people who live in the county but not in Glendale, Mesa, or Phoenix. Mesa renters go to MesaCAN instead. Families with a child under 18 can also ask MesaCAN about the state's Short-Term Crisis Services, which can help with rent once in 12 months.

What should I do if I got a 5-day notice or eviction papers in Mesa?

Act the same day. Call Community Legal Services at 602-385-8880; Maricopa County says its legal help for renters is available to all county residents facing eviction for unpaid rent. Try to pay what you owe before the landlord files, if you can. Then go to your hearing, by phone, video or in person, as the summons says.

How fast can an eviction happen in Arizona?

Very fast. After a 5-day notice for unpaid rent, the landlord can file in court. The hearing must be 3 to 6 days after the summons is issued, and the judge can postpone it for only up to 3 days for good cause. If the landlord wins, the court cannot issue a writ of restitution until 5 calendar days after the judgment. From notice to lockout can take only a few weeks.

Can I stop an Arizona eviction by paying what I owe?

Often, yes. Under A.R.S. 33-1368, if you pay all past-due rent and a reasonable late fee from a written lease before the landlord files in court, the lease is reinstated. After the case is filed, you must also pay the landlord's attorney fees and court costs, before the judge rules. After a judgment, letting you stay is up to the landlord. Get a receipt for every payment.

Is the Mesa Section 8 waiting list open?

No. The Mesa Housing Authority says its Section 8 wait list application is currently closed, and it is not an emergency rent fund. The separate Housing Authority of Maricopa County's voucher list has been closed since August 25, 2023, and it says all of its open property lists, including Clare Feldstadt Apartments in Mesa, close at 8:00 a.m. on December 1, 2026, and stay closed after that until further notice.

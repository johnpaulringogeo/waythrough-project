Emergency Rent Help in Atlanta: The ARCHI Application, the Housing Help Center, Free Eviction Lawyers, and Georgia's 7-Day Answer

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Atlanta and you are behind, the main way to ask for rent money is one short online form: ARCHI's shared application for rent, mortgage, and utility help, which sends your request to a group of Atlanta nonprofits. The City of Atlanta's Housing Help Center takes requests too, but it connects you to programs instead of paying rent itself, and ARCHI says its form is "integrated with the City of Atlanta's Housing Help Center." Both Atlanta Legal Aid and the Atlanta Volunteer Lawyers Foundation send people who need rent money to it. Help is limited: ARCHI says the need "exceeds our current available funding." If you already have court papers, you have only 7 days to answer, so get to AVLF's free help desk at the Fulton County Courthouse or call (404) 521-0790. Everything here was checked on November 3, 2026.

What to do first: Fill out ARCHI's application today; it takes about 6 minutes. Then fill out the City's Housing Help Center intake form, or call (404) 492-7640 and leave a message. Text your ZIP code and your need to 898211 to reach United Way 211. If you were served with a dispossessory (eviction) affidavit, count 7 days from the day you were served and get legal help before then.

Georgia moves fast: the 3-day notice and the 7-day answer

Georgia's Safe at Home Act (House Bill 404, signed April 22, 2024) changed O.C.G.A. section 44-7-50. For unpaid rent, the landlord must first give you "a notice to vacate or pay all past due rent, late fees, utilities, and other charges owed to the landlord within three business days." The notice must be "posted in a sealed envelope conspicuously on the door." The Act applies to leases "entered into or renewed on or after July 1, 2024." After that, the landlord can file a dispossessory affidavit in Magistrate Court. Once you are served, the Fulton County Magistrate Court says "the tenant has seven (7) days from the date of service to file an answer," and if you do not, "an eviction may be requested on the eighth (8th) day." A rent application does not stop that clock.

Emergency rental assistance in Atlanta: the doors that pay

Apply to more than one door. Each runs on its own money, and none of them promises help.

ARCHI's shared rent, mortgage, and utility application: the main door

ARCHI (the Atlanta Regional Collaborative for Health Improvement) runs one application that many local groups share. In its words, "18 organizations have come together through ARCHI's pilot initiative to create a single, universal application." It calls it a "6-minute application" that is "accessible online 24/7 and integrated with the City of Atlanta's Housing Help Center" (checked November 3, 2026).

  • Who looks at your request: ARCHI lists these agencies in its group: Atlanta Volunteer Lawyers Foundation, BCM Georgia, Catholic Charities Atlanta, Community Assistance Center, Crossroads Community Ministries, Emmaus House, Midtown Assistance Center, Open Doors, and Star-C. It asks you to "please wait for the matched agency to contact you directly."
  • Documents: none at first. ARCHI says, "We do not need any documents at this stage of the application." If an agency contacts you, it will tell you what it needs. Keep your lease, ID, proof of income, and any late notice or court papers ready.
  • How long it takes: "you should hear back from us or a provider in 2 to 4 weeks." ARCHI also says your "application will expire after two weeks if no agency is able to assist you at this time."
  • Funding: "the volume of need is high and exceeds our current available funding." If you are turned down, ARCHI says it is "due to the high volume of applicants and limited amount of available funding."
  • Scam warning: "ARCHI and our partners will never ask you for money at any point."
  • Questions: ARCHI's office line is (404) 413-0288.

City of Atlanta Housing Help Center

The Housing Help Center is "the City of Atlanta's official housing resource center," and "all of the Housing Help Center navigators and staff are City of Atlanta employees." It does not hand out a rent check itself; it connects you to programs. Its home page says: "The City of Atlanta's Eviction Diversion Program with Star-C has concluded. Individuals seeking general rental assistance can still apply through the Housing Help Center" (checked November 3, 2026).

  • Who gets one-on-one help: navigation "is prioritized for households at or below 60% of area median income, approximately $43,000 for a 1 person household, or $62,000 for a household of 4 residing within Atlanta city limits." Anyone can use its resource lists.
  • How to start: fill out the intake form on housinghelpcenter.com. "A navigator will review your intake form and respond within two business days." If you need help with the form, call (404) 492-7640 and leave a message, or call 311. The City says it typically returns calls "within 7 to 10 business days or sooner."
  • Where and when: 2 Peachtree Street, First Floor, near the Five Points MARTA station. It is "generally open from 9 AM to 5 PM, Monday to Friday," and works by appointment.

AVLF's Atlanta Rising Prevention Pilot: small and by random pick

The Atlanta Volunteer Lawyers Foundation (AVLF) says it is working with Partners for HOME on the Atlanta Rising Prevention Pilot, which gives "flexible financial assistance and supportive services" to households at risk of losing their homes. Because it is part of a research study, "it will begin with a limited number of participants selected at random from among eligible applicants." AVLF's page lists no separate way to apply, so do not wait on it. Use the ARCHI application, and ask AVLF about the pilot at (404) 521-0790.

United Way 211: the switchboard

United Way of Greater Atlanta's 211 connects you to "trained, bilingual 211 Navigators." You can "Dial 211 Monday–Friday from 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m." or "Text your ZIP Code and need to 898211" (checked November 3, 2026). It is not open 24 hours by phone, so if you call at night, text instead or search 211 online. Ask for rent help and eviction prevention, and write down every name and number you get.

Fulton County's prevention doors are for people outside the City

Fulton County's homeless and prevention access points do not serve the City of Atlanta. The County says its Homeless Assessment Centers "service Fulton County citizens who reside in Fulton County outside of the City of Atlanta." If you live in Fulton but outside Atlanta, the North Fulton center is at 404-612-0720 and the South Fulton center is at 404-612-0137, open Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., with walk-ins from 9 a.m. to noon. Inside Atlanta, the County points people to "the United Way at 2-1-1."

Atlanta utility help: LIHEAP and the City's water bill program

  • LIHEAP heating help: Georgia says its heating program "typically begins the first workday of December each year for residents aged 65 and older or medically homebound," and "All other eligible residents may apply for assistance beginning the first workday in January." Your income must be "at or below 60% of the State's Median Income." Money is first come, first served until it runs out. In Atlanta you apply through the Fulton Atlanta Community Action Authority (FACAA), which says LIHEAP "is available to all income eligible households in Fulton County and the City of Atlanta." FACAA's page says the heating start date "is TBD" and to "check back here on December 1, 2026" for the senior start date. Book an appointment at 470-437-4621 or online. FACAA lists its income limit as $34,549 for one person and $66,441 for four.
  • If your power or gas is about to be cut off: FACAA says it can "ONLY service the first 10 walk-ins every Monday - Thursday, starting at 9 a.m." You need a disconnection notice, a shutoff within the next seven days, or service that is already off. Bring printed copies of your documents; FACAA says "Documents cannot be on your phone."
  • Atlanta water bills (Care & Conserve): the City's Department of Watershed Management helps "low to moderate income ratepayers who are having difficulty paying water and sewer bills." You must get a water bill from the City in your name, and the account "must have gone through six (6) billing cycles and have a past due amount of $300 or more." Renters must give the landlord's contact information. Apply online or call (404) 546-3620 for a mailed application.

Paying a utility bill can free up cash for rent. Our utility assistance guide covers other programs.

Eviction help in Atlanta: free lawyers

None of these pays rent. A lawyer can find problems in the landlord's case, get your answer filed on time, and work out a deal.

  • AVLF Housing Court Assistance Center (Fulton County): AVLF gives free help to qualified tenants facing eviction in Fulton County. Walk in, with no appointment, at the Fulton County Courthouse, 185 Central Avenue SW: Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday from 9 a.m. to noon in Room TG-100, and from 1 to 4 p.m. at the Justice Resource Center on the 7th floor; Friday from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. in Room TG-100. AVLF accepts walk-ins "up to 30 minutes before closing." You can also call (404) 521-0790 or fill out the form on its evictions page.
  • Atlanta Legal Aid Society: free civil legal help for people with low incomes. The Fulton County office is at 54 Ellis St. NE, 404-524-5811. Spanish-speaking applicants can call 404-377-5381, and people 60 or older can call Georgia Senior Legal Aid at 404-389-9992. You can also apply online at atlantalegalaid.org/apply. Walk-ins are Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to noon and 1 to 5 p.m., but "Walk-in service is very limited." The offices are closed November 11, November 26 and 27, December 24 and 25, and December 31, 2026, through January 1, 2027. Legal Aid also says "Please do not contact your local Legal Aid office regarding rental assistance," so call it about your court case, not for rent money.
  • Part of Atlanta is in DeKalb County. If your court papers come from DeKalb County, AVLF's courthouse desk is not for your case. Call Atlanta Legal Aid's DeKalb office at 404-377-0701.

Georgia eviction timeline: what happens and when

These steps come from Georgia's Safe at Home Act and the Fulton County Magistrate Court's own pages and tenant pamphlet:

  • 1. The notice. For unpaid rent, a notice to vacate or pay within three business days, posted in a sealed envelope on your door (for leases entered into or renewed on or after July 1, 2024). A notice is not an eviction.
  • 2. The court case. The landlord files a dispossessory affidavit in Magistrate Court, and you are served at home: in person, by leaving it with someone over 18 at your home, or by posting it on your door and mailing you a copy.
  • 3. Your 7 days. File a written answer within 7 days of service. If the last day is a weekend or legal holiday, the court says you can file "by close of the next business day." Fulton requires e-filing, and the court says the Clerk's office in Room TG-100 has public computers where you can e-file for free. A late answer "will not prevent a Landlord" from asking for the writ.
  • Paying can stop it, once a year. The court's pamphlet says: "If this is the first time in 12 months a Tenant has been served, a Tenant may avoid being evicted by paying all monies owed plus the costs of the dispossessory warrant within 7 days of being served." If the landlord refuses your money, file an answer that says so. The court also warns: "Inability to pay rent due to illness, lost job or other financial hardship is not a defense to eviction."
  • 4. The hearing. When you answer, "a court date is given for the following week." The court offers free mediation on the day of the hearing. Bring your lease, notices, photos, and receipts.
  • 5. After a judgment. If you lose, the judge grants the landlord a writ of possession in 7 days: "This gives the Tenant 7 days to vacate the property." You have 7 days from the judgment to appeal, and to stay in the home during an appeal you must pay the judgment and future rent into the court registry.
  • Lockouts are illegal. The court says a landlord may remove you "only under the dispossessory procedure," that self-help eviction "is a tort for which the tenant may recover damages," and that a landlord who cuts off utilities may face misdemeanor prosecution.
  • Two more rules from the Safe at Home Act: every lease now includes a promise that the home "is fit for human habitation" (O.C.G.A. 44-7-13), and a landlord may not charge a security deposit of more than "two months' rent" (O.C.G.A. 44-7-30.1).

If you are not sure where your case stands, call the Fulton County Magistrate Court at 404-613-5360, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m. The court cannot give legal advice. What is not a door for emergency rent

  • Atlanta Housing runs Section 8 and other housing programs. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its FAQ says "the Housing Choice Voucher Program is not accepting applications at this time," and "If you need immediate housing assistance, please call 2-1-1" (checked November 3, 2026). If you already have Atlanta Housing assistance and fall behind, call (404) 892-4700 right away. Our Atlanta Housing page has the waitlist details.
  • The City's Eviction Diversion Program with Star-C has ended. Old flyers and posts still mention it.
  • Atlanta Legal Aid does not handle rent money, in its own words. Call it about your court case.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Atlanta right now?

Some, but it is limited. The main door is ARCHI's free online application for rent, mortgage, and utility help, which sends your request to a group of local nonprofits and is integrated with the City of Atlanta's Housing Help Center. ARCHI says you should hear back in 2 to 4 weeks if an agency can help, and that the need is higher than its funding. Also fill out the Housing Help Center's intake form or call (404) 492-7640, and text your ZIP code and need to 898211 for United Way 211.

How does the ARCHI rent application work?

ARCHI calls it a 6-minute application, open online 24/7. You do not send documents at first; if an agency picks up your request, it tells you what it needs. You should hear from a matched agency in 2 to 4 weeks, and the application expires after two weeks if no agency can help. ARCHI and its partners will never ask you for money.

What should I do if I got eviction papers in Atlanta?

Act within 7 days. In Fulton County you have seven days from the date you were served to file an answer with the Magistrate Court, or the landlord can ask for a writ on the eighth day. Get free help at AVLF's Housing Court Assistance Center in the Fulton County Courthouse, 185 Central Avenue SW, or call AVLF at (404) 521-0790. Atlanta Legal Aid's Fulton County office is 404-524-5811.

How much notice does a Georgia landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

For leases entered into or renewed on or after July 1, 2024, Georgia's Safe at Home Act says the landlord must first give a notice to vacate or pay all past-due rent, late fees, utilities, and other charges within three business days. The notice must be posted in a sealed envelope on your door. Only after that can the landlord file a dispossessory affidavit in court.

Can I stop an eviction in Georgia by paying the rent?

Sometimes. The Fulton County Magistrate Court says that if this is the first time in 12 months you have been served, you may avoid eviction by paying all money owed plus the costs of the dispossessory warrant within 7 days of being served. If the landlord refuses the payment, file an answer that says you offered the money and it was refused. Financial hardship by itself is not a defense.

Does Atlanta Housing give emergency rent help?

No. Atlanta Housing runs Section 8 and other housing programs, and its FAQ says the Housing Choice Voucher Program is not accepting applications at this time. For immediate housing help, Atlanta Housing points people to 2-1-1. If you already have Atlanta Housing assistance and fall behind, call (404) 892-4700 right away.

How to Find Your Section 8 Caseworker (and What to Do When No One Answers)

Matt · Updated November 2026

When something goes wrong with your voucher, you need to reach the person who handles your file. But many people don't know who that is. Caseworkers change, offices get busy, and some housing authorities don't assign one person at all. This guide shows where to find your caseworker's name, how to ask for it, and what to do if nobody answers.

Here's the short version: Look at your letters from the housing authority and its online portal first. If the name isn't there, call the main office and ask, "Who is my caseworker?" If no one is assigned or no one calls back, send your request in writing, ask for a supervisor, and then call HUD's Public and Indian Housing Customer Service Center at 800-955-2232.

Is your voucher about to expire?

Don't wait to find the right person. Send a written request for an extension to the housing authority today, by email, the portal, or a letter you hand in and get stamped with the date. If you need more time because of a disability, say you are asking "as a reasonable accommodation." HUD's rule says the housing authority then must extend the voucher "up to the term reasonably required for that purpose" (24 CFR 982.303(b)(2)).

What a caseworker does

Your caseworker is the person at your public housing agency (PHA) who handles your file. Housing authorities use different job titles, such as housing specialist, case manager, HCV specialist, eligibility specialist, or advisor. They all mean about the same thing.

A caseworker usually handles:

  • Your yearly recertification (when the PHA checks your income and household again).
  • Changes you report, like a new job, lost income, or someone moving in or out.
  • Your share of the rent. It is usually based on about 30% of your adjusted income, which is your income after certain deductions.
  • Voucher extensions, moves, and requests to approve a new unit.
  • Requests for a reasonable accommodation, if you or someone in your home has a disability.

Inspections, landlord payments, and waiting lists are often handled by other teams. Your caseworker can usually tell you who to contact for those.

Where your caseworker's name usually appears

  1. Letters from the PHA. Look at appointment letters, recertification notices, and rent change letters. The caseworker's name, phone number, or email is often at the bottom or near the signature.
  2. Your voucher and briefing packet. When the PHA gave you your voucher, it may have listed the person handling your search.
  3. The PHA's online portal. Many housing authorities now have a participant portal. Some show your caseworker's name and contact details there. You may need your client number or Social Security number to log in.
  4. Your HUD-50058. This is the form the PHA sends HUD with your household and rent information. You can ask for a copy. It shows the agency's name and HUD's code for it, but it usually does not name your caseworker. It does confirm which PHA has your file.

Examples from large housing authorities

Every PHA does this a little differently. Here is what three large ones say on their own websites:

  • Cuyahoga Metropolitan Housing Authority (Cleveland area, Ohio): Its voucher holder FAQ says that to find your caseworker's direct phone number, fax number, and email, you go to its HCVP Participant Portal. It says you need "either your CMHA client number or social security number."
  • Chicago Housing Authority: It says voucher families "are not assigned to a specific caseworker," but to one of three regional offices based on ZIP code or special program. For account help, it tells families to contact its Customer Call Center at 312-935-2600 (TTY 312-461-0079) or hcv@thecha.org.
  • New York City Housing Authority (NYCHA): It says "the best way to contact NYCHA about your Section 8 case" is its Self-Service Portal. Its Customer Contact Center is at 718-707-7771.

If you aren't sure which housing authority has your voucher, our guide on how to find your PHA can help.

How to ask the front desk or call center

Before you call or go in, have these ready:

  • Your full name and address
  • Your client or tenant number, if you have one (it's often on your letters)
  • A pen and paper, or your phone's notes app

Then ask:

"I have a Housing Choice Voucher. Can you tell me the name of my caseworker, their direct phone number or email, and the best way to reach them? If no one is assigned to me, who should I contact about my file?"

Write down the name of the person you spoke with, the date, and what they told you. If you go in person, ask for a business card.

If no one is assigned, or no one answers

Phone calls leave no record. When something is urgent, like an expiring voucher or a rent change you think is wrong, move to writing.

  1. Send a written request. Use email, the portal, or a letter. If you hand-deliver it, bring two copies and ask the front desk to stamp the date on yours. Say what you need and by when. Our guide on how to deal with your housing authority has more tips and sample wording.
  2. Ask for a supervisor. If there's no answer in about a week, write to your caseworker's supervisor or the voucher program manager. Include a copy of your first request.
  3. Use the PHA's customer service or ombudsman office, if it has one. Some large housing authorities have a customer relations office, an ombudsman, or a complaint form on their website.
  4. Ask for the reasonable accommodation or Section 504 coordinator if your request is about a disability. Many PHAs have one person or team for these.
  5. Call HUD. HUD's Public and Indian Housing Customer Service Center at 800-955-2232 answers questions about voucher programs, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Eastern. Explain that your PHA isn't responding and what is at stake.
  6. Contact your local HUD field office. HUD oversees housing authorities. You can find the office for your state in HUD's local office directory.

If the PHA sends you a letter ending your assistance, don't wait for a call back. Ask in writing for an informal hearing right away. HUD's rules require the PHA to offer one before it ends a participant's assistance for something the family did or failed to do (24 CFR 982.555). Free legal aid can help you prepare. Our guide to free legal help for tenants shows how to find it.

Keep good records

Records help you if something goes wrong later, and they make supervisors and HUD take you seriously.

  • Keep a call log. For every call, write the date, time, number you called, who you spoke with, and what they said.
  • Keep copies of every letter, form, and email you send or get. Take a photo of papers before you hand them in.
  • Get proof of delivery. Save sent emails and portal confirmations. Ask for date stamps on papers you hand in.
  • Keep it all in one place, like a folder or envelope, in date order.

Frequently asked questions

What does a Section 8 caseworker do?

A caseworker, often called a housing specialist, handles your file at the housing authority. That usually includes your yearly recertification, changes in income or household, the rent you pay, voucher extensions, moves, and requests for a reasonable accommodation. Inspections are often handled by a different team.

Where can I find my caseworker's name?

Check the letters the housing authority sent you, such as appointment and recertification notices and rent change letters. The name and phone number are often at the bottom. Many housing authorities also list your caseworker in their online portal. If you can't find it, call the main office and ask.

What if my housing authority doesn't assign caseworkers?

Some don't. The Chicago Housing Authority, for example, says voucher families are not assigned a specific caseworker and should contact its Customer Call Center instead. Ask your housing authority how it handles your file, and send important requests in writing so there is a record.

What if my caseworker doesn't call back?

Put your request in writing by email, the online portal, or a letter you hand in and get date-stamped. If there is no answer in about a week, ask for the caseworker's supervisor, and use the housing authority's customer service or ombudsman office if it has one. Keep copies of everything.

Can HUD help if my housing authority won't respond?

HUD doesn't manage your voucher, but it oversees housing authorities. Call HUD's Public and Indian Housing Customer Service Center at 800-955-2232 for help and to find your local HUD field office. HUD's website also lists its local offices by state.

Can I ask for a different caseworker?

You can ask, in writing, to the caseworker's supervisor. There is no general federal right to choose your caseworker, but supervisors can step in when a caseworker isn't responding. If you have a disability and need a different way to communicate, ask for it as a reasonable accommodation.

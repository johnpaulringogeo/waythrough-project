Emergency Rent Help in San Antonio: The City's Monthly Window, Free Eviction Lawyers, and Texas's 2026 Eviction Clock

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in San Antonio and you are behind, the main door is the City of San Antonio's Rental Assistance Program. It can pay up to $3,500 toward rent and $1,500 toward utilities, covering up to three months in a row. But it takes applications for only one hour a month. In November 2026, the portal is open only on Thursday, November 12, from 1 p.m. to 2 p.m.; after 2 p.m. on November 12 that window is closed, so check the City's Rental Assistance page for the next one. You must live inside the city limits, have a Notice to Vacate or an eviction citation, and your landlord must agree to take the money. For eviction help, the City's Right to Counsel program sends tenants to free lawyers at Texas RioGrande Legal Aid (TRLA), 956-996-8752. Texas eviction law also changed on January 1, 2026. Everything here was checked on November 3, 2026.

Here's the short version: Get your papers ready now: ID for everyone, your lease, your Notice to Vacate, proof of income for the last 60 days, and proof of your hardship. Ask your landlord to be ready to sign the City's agreement and send a rent ledger. Apply on the City's page on Thursday, November 12, between 1 and 2 p.m. If you got court papers, call TRLA at 956-996-8752 the same day. If the papers mention "summary disposition," you have only 4 days to respond. And if you get a court date, go.

The City's rent portal opens for one hour a month

The City says: "With the exception of City Holidays & Closures, the City of San Antonio will accept applications on the second Wednesday of each month. The portal will open at 1 p.m. and close at 2 p.m." November's date moved because of Veterans Day: "The November rental portal will be closed on Wednesday, November 11 and open on Thursday, November 12, 2026 from 1 p.m. to 2 p.m." (checked November 3, 2026). After 2 p.m. on November 12, the November window is closed. By the normal rule, the next window would be the second Wednesday of December, December 9, 2026, but holidays can move it, so check the City's page first. "Applications do not carry over to the next month. You must reapply each month."

Rental assistance in San Antonio: the City's Rental Assistance Program

The program is run by the City's Neighborhood & Housing Services Department (NHSD) as part of its Housing Assistance Program (HAP). In the City's words, it "provides financial assistance to renters experiencing financial hardship." Households "can receive up to three consecutive months of rent and utility assistance (past, current and future)," with a "$3,500 maximum for rental assistance" and a "$1,500 maximum for utility assistance." The money goes straight to your landlord and utility company. The City is clear that "Rental assistance is limited and not guaranteed. Not all applications will be processed."

Who qualifies

  • Where you live: you need "A current lease agreement with the applicant's current physical address within the city limits of San Antonio." The City says "Residents who live outside of City Council Districts 1-10 do not qualify for assistance."
  • An eviction notice: "You must upload an active Notice to Vacate and/or active Court Ordered Eviction Citation to demonstrate eviction status when you apply." You must still owe rent while your application is reviewed.
  • A hardship in the last 12 months, such as losing a job, losing work hours, a large medical bill not covered by insurance, or an unexpected car repair (not routine maintenance).
  • Income at or below 80% of the Area Median Income, counted from your household's gross income over the last 60 days. The City's 2026 limits at 80%: 1 person $56,350; 2 people $64,400; 3 people $72,450; 4 people $80,500; 5 people $86,950; 6 people $93,400; 7 people $99,850; 8 people $106,300.
  • Once a year: help "can only be provided once over a 12-month period."
  • If you have a housing voucher, you can still apply, but the program "only covers your portion of the rent." It does not cover RV park fees or single room rentals.

What to have ready

  • ID for everyone in the home: for adults, a driver's license, state ID, passport, or matrícula consular; for children, a birth certificate or another listed document. Everyone with a Social Security number must send a copy of the card.
  • Your lease and your Notice to Vacate or eviction citation.
  • Proof of income from the last 60 days for everyone 18 or older, such as pay stubs, SSI or RSDI letters, unemployment papers, child support, VA, or retirement benefits. If you have no income, bank statements with no deposits for 60 days or a termination letter.
  • Proof of the hardship. Anyone who owns more than $5,000 in assets must send six months of records.
  • An email address and phone number for you and for your landlord.
  • From your landlord: a signed Housing Provider Agreement, a tenant ledger, and vendor registration with the City through PaymentWorks. "Housing Provider participation is required. An application will be denied if the housing provider chooses not to accept funding from the program."

What happens after you apply

The City says "Applicants will receive two phone calls and one email regarding their application," and you have 48 hours to respond after the first contact. If you don't respond, the application is denied and "cannot be reactivated," but you can reapply the next month. You can check your status online, by email, or by calling 210-207-6459. General questions: 3-1-1 or 210-207-6000.

If you live outside the city limits

The City's page for residents outside the city lists two places to call: Bexar County Resources at 210-335-3666, and the Alamo Service Connection, run by the Alamo Area Council of Governments (AACOG), at 210-477-3275. Ask each one what rent help it has this month.

Catholic Charities of the Archdiocese of San Antonio

Catholic Charities says its Guadalupe Community Center and St. Stephen's CARE Center "provide emergency assistance to address food insecurity, reduce poverty, and prevent homelessness," including emergency financial assistance. Its page does not say how much it can pay toward rent, so call the Guadalupe Community Center at (210) 226-6178 and ask what is available.

2-1-1 Texas: the switchboard

Texas Health and Human Services says "Dialing 2-1-1 is a free, easy way to find out about services you can get in your area or through state programs," and "If you can't connect to 2-1-1, call us toll-free at 877-541-7905." Ask for rent help in Bexar County and write down every name and number you get.

Texas Rent Relief and the Texas Eviction Diversion Program are over

The Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs says both programs "launched in February 2021 and closed in the summer of 2023." The City's pandemic-era Emergency Housing Assistance Program (EHAP) is also over. If someone tells you to apply to one of them, that information is out of date.

San Antonio utility help: CPS Energy bills

Paying the CPS Energy bill can free up rent money.

  • City of San Antonio Utility Assistance: the City "offers utility assistance to eligible households needing help with their energy bills," with priority for households with children under 17, people with a disability, and adults 60 or older. Your yearly household income must be at or below 150% of the federal poverty guidelines: 1 person $23,940; 2 people $32,460; 3 people $40,980; 4 people $49,500 (add more for bigger households). You need proof of income for the last 30 days for everyone 18 and older, a copy of your CPS Energy bill and account number, a photo ID for the account holder, and the four-digit PIN on your CPS account. Apply online through the City's page. The City says applications "will take 30 days or more to process." Phone: 210-207-8198, Monday to Friday, 7:45 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., at the Willie Velasquez Learning Center, 1302 N. Zarzamora St.
  • REAP (Residential Energy Assistance Partnership): CPS Energy says REAP "helps families in need pay their utility bills," with "up to $480 in utility bill assistance per year." You must live in San Antonio or Bexar County, have income at or below 150% of the federal poverty guidelines, and meet at least one condition, such as being 60 or older, having a disability, or having children under 17 at home. San Antonio residents apply through the City (210-207-8198). Bexar County residents outside the city call 210-335-3666 or visit the Resource Lab at 233 N. Pecos, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 3 p.m.

Eviction help in San Antonio: free lawyers

A lawyer cannot pay your rent, but can catch mistakes in the landlord's papers, file your response on time, and work out a deal.

The City's Right to Counsel program with Texas RioGrande Legal Aid

  • What it is: the City says it "proudly partners with Texas RioGrande Legal Aid (TRLA) to offer legal aid services to households with low incomes who are experiencing eviction or other housing issues that threaten their housing status." It is free for people who qualify by income.
  • How to get in: "If you have received a notice to appear in Court or have a judgement against you, please ask City of San Antonio Staff for a direct referral to TRLA's Right to Counsel (RTC) program or contact TRLA's intake line." Call TRLA at 956-996-8752 or email righttocounsel@trla.org.
  • Intake hours: TRLA says its intake line runs "Monday through Friday from 9:00 a.m. – 5:00 p.m. central standard time."

At the courthouse

Eviction cases are heard in the Justice of the Peace court; the landlord's petition "must be filed in the precinct where the premises is located" (Rule 510.6(c)). The City says TRLA's Right to Counsel program also shares resources on lockouts, utility shut-offs, evictions, and appeals, so ask about them when you call.

Texas eviction timeline in 2026: what happens and when

Texas rewrote its eviction law with Senate Bill 38, which took effect on January 1, 2026. The rules are in Chapter 24 of the Texas Property Code and in Texas Rule of Civil Procedure 510, which the Supreme Court of Texas says "has been completely rewritten as a standalone rule" for eviction cases. These are the steps for a rent case, in order (checked November 3, 2026):

  • 1. The notice. Your landlord must give you "at least three days' written notice to vacate" before filing, "unless the parties have contracted for a shorter or longer notice period in a written lease or agreement" (Property Code section 24.005(a)). Read your lease. If the case is only about unpaid rent and you were not late before the month of the notice, the notice must be a "notice to pay rent or vacate," which gives you the chance to pay. The notice can come by mail, be left inside your home in a conspicuous place, be handed to anyone in the home 16 or older, or come by email only if you agreed to that in writing (section 24.005(f-3)). This same notice is what the City's Rental Assistance Program asks you to upload.
  • 2. The court case. The landlord files a sworn petition in the Justice of the Peace court for the precinct where your home is. A constable or sheriff must make "a diligent effort" to serve you within 5 business days after filing (section 24.0051(f)).
  • 3. The 4-day answer to a summary disposition motion. This is the new trap. With the petition, a landlord can file a sworn "motion for summary disposition without trial." If you do not file a written response with your supporting facts and documents "not later than the fourth day after the date the tenant is served," the judge can rule for the landlord without a trial (section 24.005106; Rule 510.10(a)). If your papers mention summary disposition, call TRLA the same day.
  • 4. The trial. The court must hold the trial "not earlier than the 10th day or later than the 21st day after the date the petition is filed," and never sooner than the 4th day after you are served. A trial can be postponed for no more than 7 days unless both sides agree in writing (section 24.0051(g)). You do not have to file a written answer to an ordinary eviction suit (Rule 510.11(a)), but you must show up. If you do not, the landlord usually wins by default.
  • 5. The appeal: 5 days. To appeal, file a bond, a cash deposit, or a Statement of Inability to Afford Payment of Court Costs with the justice court "not later than the fifth day after the date the judgment is signed," and affirm that you have a good-faith defense (section 24.005107; Rule 510.19(a)). To stay in your home during the appeal, you must pay one rental period into the court registry within 5 days of filing the appeal and keep paying each period (section 24.0053(a-2)). If you miss a payment, the court can issue the writ of possession without a hearing (section 24.0054(a)).
  • 6. The writ of possession. If you lose and do not appeal, the writ "may not be issued before the sixth day after the date on which the judgment for possession is rendered" (section 24.0061(b)). The officer must post a warning on your front door at least 24 hours before carrying it out (section 24.0061(d)).
  • Lockouts. A landlord "may not intentionally prevent a tenant from entering the leased premises except by judicial process," with narrow exceptions. A landlord who changes the locks on a tenant who is behind must post a notice saying where to get the new key at any hour, and must give you the key "regardless of whether or not the tenant pays any of the delinquent rent." If a landlord breaks these rules, you can get back in or end the lease and recover one month's rent plus $1,000, plus damages and fees (Property Code section 92.0081).

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • Opportunity Home San Antonio (formerly the San Antonio Housing Authority) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. It says "Applications for the Housing Choice Voucher Program are accepted only when the waitlist is open. Openings will be advertised on our home page," and its home page did not announce an opening (checked November 3, 2026). You "can apply any time" for its public housing and other assisted housing, but it lists waits of 2 to 4 years for public housing and says "Wait times can be anywhere between two to seven years." Phone: 210-477-6000, 818 S. Flores St.
  • Old pandemic programs, like Texas Rent Relief and the City's Emergency Housing Assistance Program, are closed. Old flyers and posts still mention them.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in San Antonio right now?

Yes, but it opens for only one hour a month. The City of San Antonio's Rental Assistance Program pays up to $3,500 for rent and $1,500 for utilities, for up to three months in a row. In November 2026 it accepts applications only on Thursday, November 12, from 1 p.m. to 2 p.m.; after that hour the November window is closed, so check the City's Rental Assistance page for the next one. Call 3-1-1 or 210-207-6000 with questions.

When can I apply for San Antonio rental assistance?

The City takes applications on the second Wednesday of each month, from 1 p.m. to 2 p.m., except for City holidays and closures. Because of Veterans Day, the November 2026 window is Thursday, November 12, from 1 p.m. to 2 p.m. After 2 p.m. that day it is closed; by the normal rule the next one would be December 9, 2026, but check the City's page first. Applications do not carry over, so you must reapply each month.

Who qualifies for the City of San Antonio Rental Assistance Program?

You must live inside San Antonio city limits (City Council Districts 1 to 10), have an active Notice to Vacate or court eviction citation, still owe rent, have had a financial hardship in the last 12 months, and have household income at or below 80% of the Area Median Income, which is $56,350 for one person and $80,500 for four. Your landlord must agree to accept the money, and help is given only once in 12 months.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in San Antonio?

The City of San Antonio's Right to Counsel program works with Texas RioGrande Legal Aid. If you got a notice to appear in court or have a judgment against you, ask City staff for a direct referral, or call TRLA's intake line at 956-996-8752, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., or email righttocounsel@trla.org. Call as soon as you get a notice, not the day before court.

How long does an eviction take in Texas in 2026?

It can take less than a month. The landlord must give at least 3 days' written notice to vacate unless the lease says otherwise. After the case is filed, the trial must be held between the 10th and 21st day. If the landlord asks for summary disposition, you must file a written response within 4 days of being served. After a judgment you have 5 days to appeal, and the writ of possession cannot issue before the 6th day.

Can Opportunity Home San Antonio help me pay rent?

Not in an emergency. Opportunity Home runs Section 8 and public housing. It accepts Section 8 applications only when the waitlist is open and announces openings on its home page. You can apply for public housing at any time, but waits run years. If you already have a voucher or live in Opportunity Home housing and fall behind, call 210-477-6000 right away. Voucher holders can also apply to the City's Rental Assistance Program for their share of the rent.

Emergency Rent Help in San Diego: SDHC's Rent Help, Free Eviction Lawyers, and the City's Tenant Protections

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in San Diego and you are behind, there is no big open rent relief fund right now. The doors that can still pay are narrower. Inside the City of San Diego, the San Diego Housing Commission (SDHC) runs the Housing Instability Prevention Program (HIPP), which "helps pay rent and other housing-related expenses" for up to 24 months; you get referred through 2-1-1 or SDHC's hotline at 619-578-7768. Families with children can ask the County about CalWORKs Homeless Assistance, which can pay up to two months of back rent. For eviction help, the City's free Eviction Prevention Program is run by the Legal Aid Society of San Diego at 1-877-534-2524. And the City's Tenant Protection Ordinance means a landlord needs a legal reason to evict you from day one. Everything here was checked on November 3, 2026.

Here's the short version: If you live in the City of San Diego, call SDHC's homelessness hotline at 619-578-7768 or dial 2-1-1 and ask to be referred to HIPP. If you have children and get CalWORKs, or might qualify, call the County at 1-866-262-9881 and ask about Homeless Assistance. If you got a 3-day notice or court papers, call Legal Aid at 1-877-534-2524, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., and file your answer within 10 court days, or the landlord can win without a hearing.

California's two clocks: the 3-day notice and the 10-court-day answer

For unpaid rent, a California landlord must first give a written notice to pay or move out, with "three days' notice, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays" (Code of Civil Procedure 1161). If the landlord then sues, the case is called an unlawful detainer, and your response "shall be filed within 10 days, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays, after the complaint is served" (Code of Civil Procedure 1167, as amended by AB 2347, effective January 1, 2025). If you miss it, the California Courts warn, "the judge can decide without you." In San Diego County, the Superior Court says "All Unlawful Detainer actions must be filed in the Central Division," at the Hall of Justice, 330 W. Broadway.

Emergency rental assistance in San Diego: the doors still open

None of these programs promises help, and none of them is a big open rent fund. Try every one that fits you, and keep your notice, your lease, and proof of your income with you when you call.

SDHC's Housing Instability Prevention Program (HIPP)

In SDHC's words, HIPP "helps pay rent and other housing-related expenses" for families with low income in the City of San Diego who are at risk of homelessness (checked November 3, 2026). SDHC does not say on the page whether it is enrolling new households right now, so ask when you call.

  • What it pays: $250, $500, $750, or $1,000 a month toward rent, depending on the household, for up to 24 months. It can also pay past-due rent and past-due utilities, sent straight to the landlord or utility company. Case managers help you make a plan to stay housed.
  • Who qualifies, per SDHC: you live in the City of San Diego; you spend more than 40% of your gross income on housing; you face a housing crisis, such as an eviction, and are at risk of homelessness; you have no other place to go and no money or support network to fix the crisis; your household income is at or below 80% of the area median; you have less than $2,000 in assets such as bank or retirement accounts; and you do not already get ongoing rental help from another program. SDHC says immigration status does not affect eligibility, and "Additional criteria apply."
  • Who goes first: seniors 55 and older, people with disabilities, families with a child 17 or younger, and young adults ages 18 to 24.
  • How to get referred: call 2-1-1 or SDHC's Homelessness Initiatives hotline at 619-578-7768. Housing specialists then contact referred households to check whether they qualify.
  • Seniors: SDHC's page also describes Seniors Safe at Home, a related program for seniors 55 and older with low income who face a housing crisis, run with Serving Seniors.

CalWORKs Homeless Assistance: back rent for families

If your household has children and gets CalWORKs, or looks eligible for it, the state's CalWORKs Homeless Assistance program can pay "up to two months of rent arrearages" to prevent an eviction, or a security deposit and last month's rent for a new place. It serves "eligible CalWORKs recipients or apparently eligible CalWORKs applicants, who are homeless or at risk of homelessness." You apply through the County with form CW-42, the Statement of Facts for Homeless Assistance. The County of San Diego's CalWORKs line is 1-866-262-9881. Bring your pay-or-quit notice.

2-1-1 San Diego and the County's Office of Homeless Solutions

SDHC's Tenant Protection Guide describes 2-1-1 San Diego as "a free, 24-hour, confidential phone service." Dial 2-1-1 to be referred to HIPP and other local help; SDG&E also sends people to 2-1-1 San Diego for help with LIHEAP utility payments. If you live in an unincorporated part of the county, the County's Office of Homeless Solutions "helps people who are homeless or at-risk of becoming homeless," including help applying for CalWORKs, CalFresh, and General Relief. The County lists it at (619) 980-4168.

Eviction help in San Diego: free lawyers and the courthouse clinic

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can find mistakes in a notice or lawsuit, help you file your answer on time, and work out a payment plan or a move-out date you can live with.

The City of San Diego Eviction Prevention Program (Legal Aid Society of San Diego)

SDHC says the Eviction Prevention Program (EPP) "helps renters with low income in the City of San Diego who are facing eviction for not paying their rent." It is operated by the Legal Aid Society of San Diego under a contract with SDHC (checked November 3, 2026).

  • Who qualifies: you live at a City of San Diego address, you have to pay rent, and your household income is at or below 80% of the area median. SDHC's page lists that limit as $121,250 a year for a family of four; Legal Aid's own EPP page lists $130,800. If you are near the line, call anyway.
  • What you can get: "Full legal representation for Eligible Tenants throughout the pre-eviction and eviction process," including settlement talks and trial, or limited help, such as responding to an eviction notice or an unlawful detainer, or asking for a reasonable accommodation.
  • How to get help: call 1-877-534-2524 (1-877-LEGAL-AID), TTY 1-800-735-2929, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., or email Info@lassd.org. Legal Aid's Midtown office is at 1764 San Diego Avenue, Suite 100.
  • Courthouse walk-in clinics: the Unlawful Detainer Clinic at the Hall of Justice, 330 W. Broadway, is open Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m., except court holidays. The Landlord Tenant Dispute Clinic in the same building is open Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to noon.

Outside the City of San Diego

EPP is only for City of San Diego addresses. Legal Aid Society of San Diego serves lower-income residents across the county at the same number, 1-877-534-2524. The Superior Court says Legal Aid runs a clinic for people without a lawyer at the Central Division, the San Diego Volunteer Lawyer Program runs one at the North County Division, and low-income tenants "may be eligible for a free attorney" through the court's Sargent Shriver program.

San Diego's Tenant Protection Ordinance: just cause from day one

The City of San Diego's Residential Tenant Protections Ordinance took effect June 24, 2023. The City Council's page says "Protections apply on Day 1 of a lease, with a narrow exemption for short term leases." Here is what that means, from SDHC's Tenant Protection Guide:

  • A landlord needs a "just cause" to evict. Not paying rent is an at-fault just cause, so the ordinance does not stop a nonpayment case. It matters most when a notice gives another reason, or no reason.
  • No-fault evictions pay relocation money. For a no-fault eviction, such as an owner move-in, the landlord must pay "two months of actual rent," or three months for tenants who are seniors (62 or older) or disabled.
  • Some homes are not covered, including housing that got its certificate of occupancy in the previous 15 years, a single-family home the landlord lives in, and some deed-restricted affordable housing. Section 8 voucher units are covered.
  • It only applies inside City limits. In Chula Vista, El Cajon, Escondido, and the rest of the county, California's Tenant Protection Act applies instead: just cause after a tenant "has continuously and lawfully occupied" the home for 12 months (Civil Code 1946.2).
  • Rent increases: for most homes at least 15 years old, California caps yearly increases at "5 percent plus the percentage change in the cost of living, or 10 percent, whichever is lower" (Civil Code 1947.12).

California eviction timeline in San Diego: what happens and when

  • 1. The 3-day notice. It must state the amount due and who to pay, and you get three days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or court holidays (Code of Civil Procedure 1161). In the City of San Diego, the notice must state the just cause. This is the moment to call 2-1-1, SDHC's hotline, and Legal Aid.
  • 2. The lawsuit. If you do not pay or move, the landlord can file an unlawful detainer at the Hall of Justice. A notice alone does not end your tenancy.
  • 3. Answer within 10 court days. You have 10 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or court holidays, after you are served to file your response (Code of Civil Procedure 1167). The California Courts say "You must file your response papers within 10 days." If you miss it, the landlord can get a default judgment. The Legal Aid clinic at the Hall of Justice can help you with the forms.
  • 4. Trial or settlement. If you answered, the court sets a trial. A settlement can include a payment plan or a move-out date.
  • Lockouts are illegal. A landlord cannot change the locks, remove doors or windows, or shut off your utilities to force you out (Civil Code 789.3). Only the sheriff can remove you, after the court case.

The California Courts' eviction help for tenants explains each step and the forms. Our step-by-step guide to avoiding eviction covers the whole process, and understanding landlord notices explains the paper you got.

Help with utility bills in San Diego

  • SDG&E CARE and FERA: CARE gives "30% or more off your energy bill," and FERA gives "an 18% monthly discount on electric use" for households with slightly higher incomes. Apply online with no extra documents, or call SDG&E's automated line at 1-877-646-5525. One application checks you for both.
  • LIHEAP: SDG&E says "This federal assistance program offers help with utility bill payments," and sends people to 2-1-1 San Diego to apply. The state warns that "Federal funding for LIHEAP is limited," so apply early.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • SDHC's Section 8 list is closed. SDHC says it "closed its Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher and Public Housing waiting lists February 1, 2026, at 11:59 p.m.," and "We do not expect to be able to pull families from the HCV waiting list for several more years" (checked November 3, 2026). The project-based voucher list closed too. Questions: SDHC's Wait List Call Center, 619-578-7640. Our San Diego Housing Commission (SDHC) page covers what to do if you are already on the list.
  • The County's Section 8 list is closed too. The County says "the Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher waitlist closed on February 20, 2026, at 11:59 p.m." (checked November 3, 2026).
  • Legal Aid's Emergency Flex Funds are on hold. Legal Aid says "our Emergency Flex Funds Program is suspended due to lack of funding," and the program was only for current Legal Aid housing clients.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rental assistance in San Diego right now?

Not a big open fund, but there are narrower doors. Inside the City of San Diego, SDHC's Housing Instability Prevention Program (HIPP) can pay $250 to $1,000 a month toward rent for up to 24 months, plus past-due rent and utilities, for households at or below 80% of the area median who face a housing crisis; get referred through 2-1-1 or SDHC's hotline at 619-578-7768. Families on CalWORKs, or who appear eligible, can ask the County at 1-866-262-9881 about CalWORKs Homeless Assistance, which can pay up to two months of back rent.

What is SDHC's Housing Instability Prevention Program (HIPP)?

It is a San Diego Housing Commission program that helps pay rent and other housing costs for low-income City of San Diego households at risk of homelessness. It pays $250, $500, $750, or $1,000 a month for up to 24 months, with case management. To qualify you must spend more than 40% of your income on housing, face a crisis such as an eviction, have income at or below 80% of the area median, and have less than $2,000 in assets. Immigration status does not affect eligibility. Call 2-1-1 or 619-578-7768 to be referred.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in San Diego?

Call the Legal Aid Society of San Diego at 1-877-534-2524, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. If you live in the City of San Diego and your household income is at or below 80% of the area median, you may qualify for the City's Eviction Prevention Program, which can give you full legal representation. Legal Aid also runs a walk-in Unlawful Detainer Clinic at the Hall of Justice, 330 W. Broadway, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m.

Does San Diego's Tenant Protection Ordinance stop evictions?

It limits them. Since June 24, 2023, a landlord in the City of San Diego needs a just cause to evict, starting on day one of a lease, with a narrow exemption for short leases. Not paying rent counts as a just cause, so it does not stop a nonpayment case. For a no-fault eviction, such as an owner move-in, the landlord must pay two months of rent in relocation help, or three months for tenants who are 62 or older or disabled.

How long do I have to respond to an eviction lawsuit in California?

10 court days. Since January 1, 2025, California law gives a tenant 10 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, and court holidays, to file a response after the summons and complaint are served. If you do not file on time, the landlord can ask for a default judgment and the judge can decide without you. In San Diego County, eviction cases are filed at the Hall of Justice.

Is the San Diego Section 8 waiting list open?

No. The San Diego Housing Commission closed its Section 8, public housing, and project-based voucher waiting lists on February 1, 2026, and says it does not expect to pull families from the Section 8 list for several more years. The County of San Diego's Section 8 waitlist, which covers areas outside the City, closed on February 20, 2026. Neither is a source of emergency rent help.

Emergency Rent Help in San Francisco: SF ERAP, Free Eviction Lawyers, and California's Deadlines

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in San Francisco and you are behind, here is the honest picture. The City still runs a rent fund, the San Francisco Emergency Rental Assistance Program (SF ERAP), but the City says its funding is limited and it cannot help everyone who qualifies. What San Francisco has that most cities do not is a Tenant Right to Counsel: since voters passed Proposition F in 2018, tenants facing eviction can get a free lawyer through the Eviction Defense Collaborative. San Francisco is one city and one county, so these doors cover the whole county. Everything here was checked on November 5, 2026.

Here's the short version: Apply to SF ERAP online and call its helpline at (415) 653-5744 if you need help. If you got a notice ending your tenancy or court papers, call the Eviction Defense Collaborative at (415) 659-9184 the same day. Call the Rent Board's counselors at (415) 252-4600 with questions about a notice or a rent increase. Dial 2-1-1 for anything else. And if you are sued, file your answer within 10 court days, or the landlord can win without a hearing.

California's two clocks

For unpaid rent, a California landlord must first give you a written notice to pay or move out, with "three days' notice, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays" (Code of Civil Procedure 1161; the weekend and holiday rule came from AB 2343 in 2018). If the landlord then sues, your response "shall be filed within 10 days, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays, after the complaint is served" (Code of Civil Procedure 1167, as amended by AB 2347, effective January 1, 2025). In San Francisco, the landlord must also attach the Rent Board's Notice to Tenant to every notice ending a tenancy. That notice tells you how to get a free lawyer.

Help paying rent in San Francisco

None of these is a sure thing. Apply to every one that fits you, and keep your lease, your notice, and proof of your income ready.

SF ERAP: the City's rent fund

The City says SF ERAP is "jointly administered by the Mayor's Office of Housing and Community Development and the Department of Homelessness and Supportive Housing." It can help with back rent and, sometimes, a little future rent or move-in costs. In the City's words, "Funding is limited and SF ERAP will not be able to serve every household who meets minimum eligibility criteria" (checked November 5, 2026). The City's pages do not say the program is closed.

  • Who qualifies, per the City: you must "Be a current resident of San Francisco," "Be 18 years of age or older," "Have a household income at or below 50% of the Area Median Income," be able to show "a financial hardship that occurred within the past 12 months" if you want back rent, and "Not have received SF ERAP assistance in the last 12 months."
  • What it can pay: the City's FAQ says back rent must be tied to your hardship and fall within 12 months of your application, and "Limited future rent assistance may be provided depending on the situation."
  • How to apply: apply online through the SF ERAP portal linked from the City's SF ERAP page. If you need help with the form, the City lists community partners who can help. Helpline: (415) 653-5744 or help@sferap.org.
  • Be ready to wait: the City says "it may take six weeks or longer to hear back about your application." Keep talking to your landlord and get legal help while you wait.
  • Season of Sharing: the City says that "Starting in August 2025, SOS rental assistance applications are processed through the SF ERAP online portal," so one application checks you for both. Season of Sharing help is limited to once every 5 years.

Rent help through the Eviction Defense Collaborative

The Eviction Defense Collaborative (EDC) is also the front door for free eviction lawyers (below). Its rental assistance program, RADCo, "helps tenants who are behind on rent." Email edcradco@evictiondefense.org, or ask about it when you call for legal help.

CalWORKs families: Homeless Assistance for back rent

If you have children and get CalWORKs, or may qualify, ask the San Francisco Human Services Agency about CalWORKs Homeless Assistance. The state says it can cover "up to two months of rent arrearages" for "CalWORKs recipients or CalWORKs eligible applicants, who are experiencing homelessness or at risk of homelessness." You apply with form CW-42 at your county welfare office.

211 Bay Area

211 Bay Area is "a program of United Way Bay Area." Dial 2-1-1 or text your ZIP code to 898-211. It is "Free, confidential, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week," and "Available in multiple languages." Ask about rent help and write down every name and number you get.

Free eviction lawyers: San Francisco's Tenant Right to Counsel

San Francisco voters passed the No Eviction Without Representation Act, Proposition F, on June 5, 2018. The Mayor's Office of Housing and Community Development (MOHCD) says Prop F "established a policy that all residential tenants facing eviction have the right to full-scope legal defense," and that the law "went into effect on July 11, 2019." The program is called Tenant Right to Counsel, and the Eviction Defense Collaborative is its lead partner.

  • What the Rent Board tells every tenant: the Notice to Tenant that landlords must attach to every notice ending a tenancy says, "You are eligible for free assistance through the Tenant Right to Counsel Program. For intake, please contact the Eviction Defense Collaborative by phone at (415) 659-9184, by email at legal@evictiondefense.org, or in person at 976 Mission Street" (Rent Board form dated February 9, 2026, checked November 5, 2026).
  • Walk-in hours: EDC's site says it welcomes San Francisco tenants at 976 Mission Street on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday, 10 to 11:30 a.m. and 1 to 2:30 p.m. Outside those hours, call (415) 659-9184 or email legal@evictiondefense.org.
  • Go early: call as soon as you get a notice, not the week of court. If you get court papers, the answer clock is only 10 court days.
  • Bay Area Legal Aid: its Legal Advice Line, 1-800-551-5554, is "Free to qualified low-income residents living in the Bay Area." It is open Monday and Thursday from 9:30 a.m. to 3 p.m., and Tuesday and Wednesday from 9:30 a.m. to 1 p.m., "or until capacity is reached."

The Rent Board: free counseling on notices and rent increases

  • Phone counseling: (415) 252-4600, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to noon and 1 to 4 p.m. The office is at 25 Van Ness Avenue, Suite 700, open 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. on weekdays. Rent Board counselors give general information, not legal advice, so call EDC too.
  • Rent increases: for units covered by the Rent Ordinance, the Rent Board says "the annual allowable increase amount effective March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027 is 1.6%." A new amount takes effect March 1, 2027; check the Rent Board's site after it is announced.
  • Eviction notices: the Rent Board's Notice to Tenant must be attached to every notice ending a tenancy. The Rent Ordinance has its own just cause rules for the units it covers. Ask a counselor whether yours is covered.

Help with utility bills in San Francisco

  • LIHEAP: CleanPowerSF, a program of the SF Public Utilities Commission, says "Enrollment and credits are run in San Francisco by Peninsula Energy Services (PES)." Call PES at (415) 416-6660. The state says "Federal funding for LIHEAP is limited," so some eligible households may not get a benefit.
  • PG&E CARE and FERA: CARE "provides a monthly discount of 20% or more on gas and 35% or more on electricity." Apply online at PG&E; "If you do not qualify for CARE, we will check to see if you qualify for FERA." The discount also applies to the CleanPowerSF part of your bill.
  • SFPUC water and sewer discount: the Customer Assistance Program gives 25% or 40% off, but only if "Your water and sewer bill is in your name" and "You have a residential single-family account which is individually metered." Most apartment renters do not qualify, because the landlord pays the water bill. Call (415) 551-3000, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.

California eviction timeline in San Francisco: what happens and when

  • 1. The 3-day notice. The notice must state the amount due and who to pay, and you get three days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or court holidays (Code of Civil Procedure 1161). In San Francisco, the Rent Board's Notice to Tenant must be attached. Now is the time to apply to SF ERAP and call EDC.
  • 2. The lawsuit. If you do not pay or move, the landlord can file an unlawful detainer case. A notice alone does not end your tenancy.
  • 3. Answer within 10 court days. You have 10 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or court holidays, after you are served (Code of Civil Procedure 1167). The City's eviction page says a tenant has "10 business days to respond or they risk a default judgment." Do not wait: call EDC the day you are served.
  • 4. Trial or settlement. If you answered, the court sets the case for settlement talks and trial. Your lawyer can work out a payment plan, more time, or a move-out date.
  • 5. Just cause. San Francisco's Rent Ordinance has its own just cause rules for the units it covers. Where it does not apply, California's Tenant Protection Act protects a tenant who "has continuously and lawfully occupied" a home for 12 months (or 24 months in some cases when a new adult joins the lease), and the landlord must state the just cause in the notice (Civil Code 1946.2). Unpaid rent is a just cause under both laws. The state law says it will "remain in effect only until January 1, 2030" unless lawmakers extend it.
  • Lockouts are illegal. A landlord cannot change the locks, remove doors or windows, or shut off your utilities to force you out (Civil Code 789.3). Only the sheriff can remove you, after the court case.

The California Courts' eviction help for tenants explains each step and the forms. Our step-by-step guide to avoiding eviction covers the whole process, and understanding landlord notices explains the paper you got.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The San Francisco Housing Authority (SFHA) runs Section 8, not an emergency rent fund. Its Waitlist page lists every list, Housing Choice Voucher (HCV)/Section 8, Project Based Voucher, RAD, and Public Housing, as "CLOSED" (checked November 5, 2026), with no reopening date. If you already applied, SFHA's Customer Care Center at (415) 715-5200 "can confirm if applicants are active on the waitlist." See our SFHA guide. If you already have an SFHA voucher and your income dropped, report it to SFHA right away and ask about a rent change; your share of rent is generally based on 30% of your adjusted income.
  • DAHLIA lotteries for below-market-rate apartments are a path to a cheaper home, not help with rent you already owe.
  • Old pandemic rent relief. Ignore flyers and old pages about the 2021 rent relief rounds. Use the current SF ERAP portal and helpline.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in San Francisco right now?

Yes, but money is limited. The City's SF ERAP helps San Francisco renters with household income at or below 50% of the Area Median Income who had a financial hardship in the past 12 months. The City says funding is limited and it cannot serve every household that qualifies, and an answer can take six weeks or longer. Apply online through the City's SF ERAP page, and call the helpline at (415) 653-5744 if you need help. If you have a notice or court papers, also call the Eviction Defense Collaborative at (415) 659-9184.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in San Francisco?

Contact the Eviction Defense Collaborative, the intake point for the City's Tenant Right to Counsel program, at (415) 659-9184, legal@evictiondefense.org, or in person at 976 Mission Street. Walk-in hours are Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday, 10 to 11:30 a.m. and 1 to 2:30 p.m. Call as soon as you get a notice ending your tenancy, not just when you get court papers.

Do I have to be low-income to get a Right to Counsel lawyer in San Francisco?

We found no income limit on the City's pages. The City's housing office says Proposition F made it City policy that all residential tenants facing eviction have the right to full-scope legal defense. The Rent Board's notice tells tenants who get a notice ending their tenancy that they are eligible for free help through the Tenant Right to Counsel Program. EDC handles intake and assigns the case.

How long do I have to respond to an eviction lawsuit in California?

10 court days. Since January 1, 2025, California law gives a tenant 10 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, and court holidays, to file a response after the summons and complaint are served. If you do not file on time, the landlord can ask for a default judgment and the judge can decide without you.

How much can my San Francisco landlord raise the rent?

If your unit is covered by the Rent Ordinance, the Rent Board says the allowable annual increase from March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027 is 1.6%. A new amount starts March 1, 2027. Call the Rent Board's counselors at (415) 252-4600 to check whether your unit is covered and whether an increase is legal.

Is the San Francisco Section 8 waiting list open?

No. On November 5, 2026, the San Francisco Housing Authority's Waitlist page listed the Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8), Project Based Voucher, RAD, and Public Housing lists as closed, with no reopening date. If you already applied, SFHA's Customer Care Center at (415) 715-5200 can confirm whether you are still active on the list.

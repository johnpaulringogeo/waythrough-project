Emergency Rent Help in Tulsa: Restore Hope, the Iron Gate Eviction Hub, Free Lawyers, and Oklahoma's 5-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Tulsa and you are behind, the main door for rent money is Restore Hope Ministries. It helps people who live in Tulsa County, and it says the City of Tulsa and other partners are helping fund rent "through April 2027." Its phone line is open only a few hours a week and closes during busy weeks, so plan ahead. If you already have an eviction case, the Social Services Hub at Iron Gate is the in-person door. For eviction help, Legal Aid Services of Oklahoma gives free lawyers with no income limit in five Tulsa ZIP codes, and takes applications from everyone else. We found no rent program that the City of Tulsa or Tulsa County runs directly. Everything here was checked on November 5, 2026.

Here's the short version: Call Restore Hope's rent line at 918-922-8282, 9 a.m. to noon, Monday through Thursday. It closes again from November 23 to December 6, so call before then. If you have court papers, go to the Hub at Iron Gate, 501 W. Archer St., on a Tuesday or Thursday from 1 to 4 p.m. If you live in ZIP code 74105, 74133, 74135, 74136, or 74145, call Legal Aid's Right to Counsel line at (918) 984-4636. Everyone else: apply to Legal Aid at 1-888-534-5243. Dial 2-1-1 for other help. And if you get a court date, go. Oklahoma eviction hearings can come in as little as 5 days.

Oklahoma moves fast: the 5-day notice and the 5-to-10-day hearing

Under 41 O.S. section 131(B), a landlord can end your lease if you do not pay "within five (5) days after written notice of landlord's demand for payment." Once a case is filed, the summons sets your trial "not less than five (5) days nor more than ten (10) days" after it is issued (12 O.S. section 1148.4), and it has to reach you at least 3 days before the hearing (12 O.S. sections 1148.5 and 1148.16). That is why you should call for rent help and a lawyer the day you get a 5-day notice, not the day you get a court date.

Help paying rent in Tulsa: the doors that pay

Apply to more than one. Each runs on its own money, and none of them promises help.

Restore Hope Ministries: Tulsa County's rent program

Restore Hope says that "To qualify you need to live in Tulsa County," along with other rules about past help and income. It says the City of Tulsa, the Tulsa Authority for Economic Opportunity, and the Affordable Housing Trust Fund "are aiding with a portion of funding through April 2027" (checked November 5, 2026).

  • If you have a late notice or a 5-day notice: call the rent intake line at 918-922-8282 for a screening call. Restore Hope says the line "is open 9 am to noon Monday through Thursday except holidays," and that it also closes "during high-volume processing times."
  • The closure dates it lists: closed October 23 to November 4 (reopening November 5); closed November 23 to December 6, reopening December 7; and closed December 21 to January 3, reopening January 4 (checked November 5, 2026). If the line is closed when you call, write down the reopening date, call that morning, and go to the Hub if you have court papers.
  • If you already have an eviction case: Restore Hope recommends going to the Social Services Hub to meet its Emergency Rental Assistance team in person (see below).
  • Main office: 2960 Charles Page Blvd., Tulsa, OK 74127, 918-582-5766. See restorehope.org/get-help.

The Social Services Hub at Iron Gate: for renters being evicted

Housing Solutions Tulsa runs the Hub. Restore Hope says "This Social Services Hub at Iron Gate operates on Tuesdays and Thursdays between 1 and 4pm (501 W. Archer St.)," and that it can connect you with free legal aid and mediation. Housing Solutions' Landlord Tenant Resource Center offers "up-to-date eviction information" and access to "pro bono legal resources." Call or text (918) 218-4138 to make sure the Hub is open that day.

Other rent help Restore Hope points to

  • Broken Arrow and Coweta: Restore Hope's housing page lists limited past-due rent and utility help at 918-251-7781.
  • Catholic Charities of Eastern Oklahoma: application help and referrals at 918-508-7160 (English) or 918-508-7164 (Spanish). Its main line is 918-949-4673.

211 Eastern Oklahoma: the switchboard

Tulsa Area United Way runs 211 for "37 counties in Eastern Oklahoma." Dial 2-1-1, "Available 24/7," or call (877) 836-2111. You can also "Text your zip code to (877)836-2111, M-F, 8AM to 8PM," or use its live chat at 211eok.org. Ask for rent and utility help in Tulsa County and write down every name and number you get. If you do not know your court date, Restore Hope says 211 can help you find it.

Eviction help in Tulsa: free lawyers

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can catch mistakes in the landlord's papers, ask for more time, and work out a payment plan.

Legal Aid Services of Oklahoma (LASO)

  • Right to Counsel in five Tulsa ZIP codes: LASO's Tulsa program covers ZIP codes "74105, 74136, 74135, 74145, and 74133." Call (918) 984-4636, which LASO spells "(918) 984-INFO." It says "there is not an income requirement for this program," and that "this is not a financial assistance program." Don't wait for your hearing: LASO says to call "as soon as you have problems with your landlord."
  • Everywhere else: apply through LASO's general intake at 1-888-534-5243 or 918-428-4357. LASO says "Calls answered Mon – Thurs, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m." Or apply online, then check your email and call the priority number it gives you to finish.
  • Tulsa office: 907 South Detroit Avenue, Suite 725, Tulsa, OK 74120, 918-584-3338 or 800-299-3338. It serves Tulsa and nine nearby counties. LASO offices are open Monday through Thursday, 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., and the Tulsa office closes from noon to 1 p.m.

Lawyer referral (not free)

The Tulsa County Bar Association's "Lawyer Referral & Legal Navigator" line is 918-587-6014. It matches you with a private lawyer, who may charge a fee. The Bar's landlord-tenant page also links an eviction process guide in English and Spanish.

Utility help: LIHEAP through Oklahoma Human Services

In Oklahoma, LIHEAP is run by Oklahoma Human Services (OKDHS). Its page says winter heating open enrollment is anticipated for "Tuesday, December 15, 2026," and that "Several factors could result in delayed open enrollment." The Energy Crisis Assistance Program is expected to open on April 13, 2027. Apply online at OKDHSLive.org during open enrollment, or call (405) 522-5050 and choose the energy assistance options; use the same number to report a life-threatening crisis. OKDHS says each program stays open until the money runs out, so apply early. Before December 15, ask 211 about other utility help.

Oklahoma eviction timeline: what happens and when

Oklahoma's rules are in the Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (Title 41) and the forcible entry and detainer law (Title 12). These are the steps for a rent case, in order:

  • 1. The 5-day notice. The landlord gives a written demand for the rent. If you do not pay within 5 days, the landlord can end the lease (41 O.S. section 131(B)). That demand also counts as a demand to move out, so no other notice is needed.
  • 2. The court case. The landlord files an eviction case in Tulsa County District Court. Restore Hope says "At this time, all eviction hearings are held at the Family Center for Juvenile Justice." Check your summons for the exact place and time, or look up your case on the Oklahoma State Courts Network.
  • 3. The summons and hearing. The hearing is set 5 to 10 days after the summons is issued, and you must get the summons at least 3 days before it (12 O.S. sections 1148.4, 1148.5, and 1148.16).
  • 4. Go. If you do not show up, the landlord usually wins by default. Restore Hope says you can ask for legal help or mediation at your hearing. At court you can point out mistakes in what you owe, ask for more time, or work out a payment plan.
  • 5. After a judgment. A motion for a new trial must be filed within 3 days and does not stop the eviction (12 O.S. section 1148.10). The landlord or officer must give notice that they will return in 48 hours to put the landlord back in possession (12 O.S. section 1148.10A).
  • Lockouts are illegal. If a landlord wrongfully removes or locks you out, you can go to court to get back in or end the lease, and recover up to twice the average monthly rent or twice your actual damages, whichever is more (41 O.S. section 123).

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The Tulsa Housing Authority (THA) runs Section 8 and other housing programs. It is not an emergency rent fund, and its own site does not say whether any waiting list is open. The status system THA tells applicants to use read "There are no open waiting lists at this time" (checked November 5, 2026), and THA says "the estimated wait time for the Voucher program is 12 to 36 months from the time the application was received." A THA page announcing closures "on September 18" prints no year; it is from 2024. See our THA guide. If you already have THA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call THA at (918) 582-0021 right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • CAP Tulsa (Community Action Project of Tulsa County) is an early childhood education and Head Start agency. Its website does not offer rent or utility help, so apply for LIHEAP through OKDHS instead.
  • Mental Health Association Oklahoma is not a rent fund. Its Shelterwell eviction-prevention program offers tenant education and mediation, and its court mediators work in Oklahoma County. Its own housing page lists Restore Hope for rent help.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Yes, through Restore Hope Ministries, for people who live in Tulsa County. Its rent intake line, 918-922-8282, is open 9 a.m. to noon, Monday through Thursday, except holidays and busy processing times. What is the Social Services Hub at Iron Gate?

It is an in-person help desk for Tulsa County renters who are being evicted. Housing Solutions runs it with Restore Hope at Iron Gate, 501 W. Archer St., on Tuesdays and Thursdays from 1 to 4 p.m. Restore Hope says people at the eviction stage should go there to meet its Emergency Rental Assistance team, and the Hub can connect you with free legal aid and mediation. Call or text 918-218-4138 to make sure it is open that day.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Tulsa?

If you live in ZIP code 74105, 74133, 74135, 74136, or 74145, call Legal Aid Services of Oklahoma's Right to Counsel line at (918) 984-4636; there is no income limit. Everyone else can apply to Legal Aid at 1-888-534-5243 or 918-428-4357, Monday through Thursday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., or online. Legal Aid's Tulsa office is at 907 South Detroit Avenue, Suite 725, 918-584-3338. Call as soon as you have a problem with your landlord.

How fast is an eviction in Oklahoma?

Very fast. The landlord can end your lease if you do not pay within 5 days of a written demand. After the case is filed, the hearing is set 5 to 10 days after the summons is issued, and you must get the summons at least 3 days before. After a judgment, the landlord must give 48 hours' notice before you are removed.

Can my landlord lock me out in Oklahoma?

No. Only a court can order you out. If a landlord wrongfully removes you or locks you out, Oklahoma law lets you go to court to get back in, and you can recover up to twice the average monthly rent or twice your actual damages, whichever is more.

Is the Tulsa Section 8 waiting list open, and does THA give emergency rent help?

The status system THA tells applicants to use, waitlistcheck.com, read "There are no open waiting lists at this time" (checked November 5, 2026). THA says the estimated wait for a voucher is 12 to 36 months from the time an application is received. A THA page about closing its lists "on September 18" is from 2024. If you already have THA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call THA at (918) 582-0021 right away.

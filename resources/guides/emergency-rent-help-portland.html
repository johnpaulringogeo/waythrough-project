Emergency Rent Help in Portland: 211info, Multnomah County Rent Assistance, Free Eviction Lawyers, and Oregon's Notice Rules

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Portland and you got an eviction notice, the door that actually pays is Multnomah County's emergency rent assistance, and the way in is 211info: dial 2-1-1. The money comes from the City of Portland, the County, and the State of Oregon for the fiscal year that runs from July 2026 to June 2027, and the County warns that "demand for these resources will be much higher than the funds available." If your landlord has already filed in court, you can also apply to the state's Eviction Prevention Rapid Response (EPRR) program, which takes a limited number of applications each month. For a free lawyer, call the Oregon Law Center's Eviction Defense Project at 888-585-9638 before your first court date. Everything here was checked on November 3, 2026.

Here's the short version: Call 211info at 2-1-1 (or 866-698-6155), Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m., and ask for eviction prevention rent assistance. If you are told referrals are closed, call again the next week. If you have an eviction court case, apply to EPRR starting at 9 a.m. on the first business day of the month, call the Eviction Defense Project at 888-585-9638, and go to court.

In Oregon, rent help that covers what you owe can end the case

ORS 90.395 says a court "shall enter a judgment dismissing a complaint for possession" based on a nonpayment notice if "The tenant has tendered or caused to be tendered rental assistance or any other payment covering the nonpayment amount owed under the termination notice." The court must also dismiss if the landlord did not give you the state's notice about rental assistance and support services, or if the landlord "caused the tenant to not tender rent, including as a result of the landlord's failure to reasonably participate with a rental assistance program" (checked November 3, 2026). Keep every email and letter from a rent help program, and bring proof of payment to court.

Emergency rental assistance in Portland: the doors that pay

The County's rent assistance is the big one, and 211info is the front door to it. Funding is limited all year, so call before you count on any of these.

Multnomah County emergency rent assistance (through 211info): the main door

Multnomah County says: "Emergency rent assistance is available beginning in July 2026 to households facing eviction thanks to funding that the City of Portland, Multnomah County and the State of Oregon have dedicated for the Fiscal Year, which runs from July 2026 to June 2027." It adds, "We anticipate that demand for these resources will be much higher than the funds available, and funding will be distributed throughout the year" (checked November 3, 2026). After June 2027, check the County's page for the next year's funding.

  • Who qualifies, in the County's words: you must "Be a resident of Multnomah County," "Have a household income at or below 65% of the Area Median Income (AMI)," and "Have an eviction notice." The County says rent assistance "is available regardless of citizenship status."
  • How to get in: "If you are facing eviction, contact 211info to be connected with support for emergency rent assistance or legal service providers." 211info has a call-back feature, so you keep your place in line.
  • Weekly limits: the County warns: "Weekly referral capacity is limited and when it is reached, referrals are paused until the following business week. If you are told referrals are closed, please try contacting 211info again the next week."
  • Who helps you: this year's providers are Bienestar de la Familia, El Programa Hispano, IRCO (Immigrant and Refugee Community Organization), Latino Network, Native American Youth and Family Center, and SEI (Self Enhancement Inc.). The County says they "can be accessed most easily through the referral process at 211info."
  • Also through 211info: the County's eviction prevention page says that if you "have received an eviction notice, a written notice to vacate, or are at high risk of losing your housing within the next 21 days, immediate help is available," with crisis support, landlord negotiation, and step-by-step help applying.

Short-Term Rent Assistance (STRA)

The County calls STRA "a unified countywide program that provides limited housing assistance (up to 24 months) to households in Multnomah County that are experiencing homelessness or at risk of homelessness." Home Forward runs it for Multnomah County, the City of Portland, and the City of Gresham, and the money can pay rent or emergency hotel vouchers. You do not apply to Home Forward directly; for rent help to prevent an eviction, the County says to contact 211info.

EPRR: the state's rent help for eviction court cases

Public Partnerships (PPL) runs the Oregon Eviction Prevention Rapid Response program (OR-EPRR) with Oregon Housing and Community Services. PPL says it "provides critical financial intervention to prevent evictions" (checked November 3, 2026).

  • Who can apply: "you must have an active eviction court case number and you have not received assistance in the OHCS current biennium (July 2025 – June 2027)."
  • When: "The EPRR rental assistance program accepts applications at the beginning of each month." PPL says "Applications will be accepted again at 9am on the first business day of each month," and "The application process will close each month once the allowed number of applications has been received." If the online form will not let you submit, that month's limit has been reached; try again on the next first business day.
  • What it can pay: past-due rent; utilities; up to one month of future rent, only together with past-due rent; security deposits; rental fees; and repairs and damage up to $2,500.
  • How: apply online through the PPL EPRR page, or leave a message on the intake line at 833-746-8167 with your full name, phone number, complete address, date of birth, and eviction court case number. PPL warns: "If you do not leave all the required information you will not get a call back." Once approved, it is "about 10 days for the landlord to receive the payment."

211info: the switchboard

211info's core hours are Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Dial 2-1-1 or 866-698-6155 (TTY: dial 711 and call 866-698-6155). You can also text your ZIP code to 898211, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., or email help@211info.org; 211info answers email "within 72 hours, during core operating hours." Interpreters are available.

Utility help in Portland

  • Multnomah County Energy Assistance: the County's program offers "eligible low-income households financial relief for their heating and electric bills," through six local nonprofits, on "a first-come, first-served basis." To apply, contact 211info: dial 2-1-1, text your ZIP code to 898211, or email help@211info.org.
  • PGE Income-Qualified Bill Discount: Portland General Electric offers "a monthly discount between 15% and 80% off your energy use," based on household size and income. Apply online on PGE's bill discount page or call 503-228-6322, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m.

Paying a utility bill can free up cash for rent. Our utility assistance guide covers other programs.

Eviction help in Portland: free lawyers and hotlines

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can check the landlord's notice, raise defenses such as ORS 90.395 (above), and work out more time.

  • Eviction Defense Project (Oregon Law Center): it "provides free legal assistance to low-income tenants across the state facing eviction court cases," and "tenants in Multnomah County may be able to get help even before the court process, if they have received a termination notice from their landlord to move out." Call 888-585-9638 or use the web form, and give your name, case number, and court summons date. "You must contact us before your first appearance date in order for us to help you." Its services are free for eligible low-income Oregonians "no matter your immigration status." It does not help with rent assistance; for that, it says to contact 211.
  • Legal Aid Services of Oregon, Portland Regional Office: offers help with "Housing Law for Tenants" in Clackamas, Hood River, Multnomah, Sherman, and Wasco counties. "Intake is currently only available by phone and we are unable to take walk-ins." Call 503-224-4086 or toll-free 1-800-228-6958, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to noon and 1 to 5 p.m. It has Spanish-speaking staff and free interpretation.
  • Community Alliance of Tenants (CAT) Renters' Rights Hotline: 503-288-0130. CAT explains tenant rights; it is not a law office. It says that, "Due to severe budget cuts to Eviction Protection programs from the Oregon Legislature," the hotline "is only active with extremely limited hours": Monday, Wednesday, Friday, and Saturday, 1 to 5 p.m., and Tuesday, 5 to 8 p.m.

Who pays for eviction defense in Oregon

Much of this help runs on state money. Oregon Housing and Community Services' eviction prevention program (ORE-DAP) "utilizes state General Fund money" for rental assistance, mediation, and "coordination with partners who are providing legal services." Its October 2025 guidance tells local grantees to "work closely with the Oregon Eviction Defense program, a statewide program of the Oregon Law Center." When state budgets shrink, these services shrink too, as CAT's shorter hotline hours show. Call early.

Oregon eviction rules: what happens and when

These steps come from the Oregon Revised Statutes and the Oregon Law Center:

  • 1. The nonpayment notice (ORS 90.394). For a month-to-month or other non-weekly rental, the landlord must give either "At least 10 days' written notice of nonpayment," given "no sooner than on the eighth day of the rental period," or "At least 13 days' written notice," given "no sooner than on the fifth day of the rental period." (The day rent is due counts as day one.) A week-to-week tenant gets at least 72 hours' notice, given no sooner than the fifth day. The notice "must also specify the amount of rent that must be paid and the date and time by which the tenant must pay the rent to cure the nonpayment of rent."
  • 2. The rental assistance notice (ORS 90.395). With any nonpayment termination notice, and with any summons in a nonpayment case, the landlord must give you a copy of the notice the Oregon Judicial Department posts on its website about rental assistance and support services. If the landlord did not, the court must dismiss the case.
  • 3. Pay, and it stops. Paying the amount on the notice by the date and time it gives cures the nonpayment. If you mail your payment within the notice period, ORS 90.394 counts it as on time in most cases, unless the notice was served in person or by certain mail methods and your lease and the notice both say to pay at a set place you can reach. Keep proof of every payment.
  • 4. The court case. If the notice runs out, the landlord can file an eviction case. The Oregon Law Center says your case number usually starts with "26LT," but not always. Call the Eviction Defense Project before your first appearance date.
  • 5. Go to court. The Oregon Law Center warns: "If you don't show up, you may lose automatically, even if you applied for rent assistance." Some courts let tenants call in; follow the instructions on your eviction papers. It also says to call it "before signing any settlement agreements with your landlord."

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • Home Forward's waitlists. Home Forward is Portland's housing authority, not an emergency rent fund for the public. It says "The Housing Choice Voucher waitlist is closed," that it "was last opened in April 2025," and that it "is not issuing new vouchers from our HCV waitlist due to funding and budget constraints in 2026 and 2027." Its "2026 Waitlist Application is closed," and it "expects to notify applicants in late 2026." Two lists are always open: for "anyone who has been diagnosed with a terminal illness and life expectancy of less than 12 months," and for "people who require ADA features" (checked November 3, 2026). Main line: (503) 802-8300; Rent Assistance: (503) 802-8333.
  • Old pandemic-era programs. Old flyers and posts may still mention pandemic rent programs. For what is funded now, start with 211info.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Portland right now?

Yes, for households facing eviction. Multnomah County says emergency rent assistance is available for the fiscal year that runs from July 2026 to June 2027, paid for by the City of Portland, the County, and the State of Oregon. You must live in Multnomah County, have a household income at or below 65% of the area median income, and have an eviction notice. You get in by calling 211info at 2-1-1. The County warns that demand is much higher than the money, and that referrals pause when each week's limit is reached.

What if my landlord already filed an eviction case?

Apply to the state's Eviction Prevention Rapid Response program (EPRR), run by Public Partnerships (PPL) for Oregon Housing and Community Services. You need an active eviction court case number, and you cannot have received this help in the current state budget period, July 2025 to June 2027. It takes a limited number of applications each month, starting at 9 a.m. on the first business day, and closes once that month's limit is reached. Also call the Eviction Defense Project at 888-585-9638 before your first court appearance, and go to court even if you applied for help.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Portland?

Call the Oregon Law Center's Eviction Defense Project at 888-585-9638, or use its web form, and give your name, case number, and court date. It says you must contact it before your first appearance date. In Multnomah County, it may be able to help even before a court case if you got a termination notice. You can also call Legal Aid Services of Oregon's Portland office at 503-224-4086 (toll-free 1-800-228-6958); intake is by phone only, and it has Spanish-speaking staff.

How much notice does an Oregon landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

For a month-to-month or other non-weekly rental, ORS 90.394 allows two choices: at least 10 days' written notice, given no sooner than the eighth day of the rental period, or at least 13 days' notice, given no sooner than the fifth day. Week-to-week tenants get at least 72 hours, given no sooner than the fifth day. The notice must say how much rent to pay and the date and time to pay it by. The landlord must also give you the state court's notice about rental assistance and support services.

Can I be evicted if rental assistance pays what I owe?

Usually not. Under ORS 90.395, a court shall dismiss a nonpayment eviction if you have tendered, or caused to be tendered, rental assistance or any other payment covering the amount owed under the notice. The court must also dismiss the case if the landlord did not give you the required notice about rental assistance, or caused you not to pay, for example by not reasonably working with a rental assistance program. Bring proof of the payment to court.

Is the Home Forward waitlist open?

No. Home Forward says its Housing Choice Voucher waitlist is closed; it was last opened in April 2025, and Home Forward says it is not issuing new vouchers from that list because of funding and budget limits in 2026 and 2027. Its 2026 waitlist application for apartment communities is also closed, and it expects to notify applicants in late 2026. Two lists are always open: one for people with a terminal illness and a life expectancy of less than 12 months, and one for people who need ADA-accessible features. Home Forward's Rent Assistance line is 503-802-8333.

Emergency Rent Help in Salt Lake City: Utah Community Action, Free Legal Help, and Utah's 3-Day Clock

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Salt Lake City and you are behind, here is the honest picture. No City, County or state rent fund is open right now. Salt Lake County says it "does not provide direct affordable housing services," and its 2024 eviction-prevention program ended on December 31, 2024. What you do have: Utah Community Action, which can still help with rent or a deposit, a free mediation line for 3-day notices, 211 Utah, free legal help from Utah Legal Services, and the HEAT program for utility bills. Utah's eviction clock is one of the fastest in the country, so act the same day. Everything here was checked November 5, 2026.

Here's the short version: Call Utah Community Action at (801) 359-2444 for rent help, and its mediation line at (801) 214-3109 if you got a 3-day notice. Call Utah Legal Services at 801-328-8891 (weekdays, 9 a.m. to 2 p.m.). If you are served court papers, you have only 3 business days to answer. Do not let that deadline pass.

Utah gives you 3 business days, twice

For unpaid rent, a tenant is in unlawful detainer only if a written notice to pay or leave "has remained uncomplied with for a period of three business days after service" (Utah Code 78B-6-802(1)(c)). If the landlord then sues, the summons must give you "three business days from the date of service" to appear and defend (Utah Code 78B-6-807(3)(a)). The Utah courts put it plainly: "You only have 3 business days to respond after you receive these court papers." Losing is costly: the court must enter judgment "for the rent and for three times the amount of the damages assessed" (78B-6-811(3)).

Free legal help and mediation in Salt Lake City

Utah has no right to a free lawyer in eviction cases, and we found no City-funded tenant lawyer program in Salt Lake City. These are the places to call:

  • Utah Legal Services: free civil legal help, including evictions. It says "In most cases, income must be at or below 200% of the current federal poverty level." Call 801-328-8891 "between the hours of 9:00 am and 2:00 pm, Monday through Friday," or apply online from utahlegalservices.org. Salt Lake office: 960 S. Main St.
  • People's Legal Aid: a nonprofit that defends Utahns facing eviction and debt collection lawsuits. Utah Community Action lists its phone number as (801) 477-6975. Its website currently shows unrelated content, so call instead.
  • Utah Community Action Landlord-Tenant Mediation: if you got a 3-day pay-or-vacate notice, you can ask for free mediation with your landlord. Call (801) 214-3109 or email mediation@utahca.org. UCA does not give legal representation.
  • Utah Dispute Resolution: community mediation for "security deposits, rent, repairs, damages, and evictions." It says "Mediation fees for disputes range from $20 to $80 per hour per party." Call 801-532-4841. utahdisputeresolution.org
  • Utah Courts Self-Help Center: free help with court forms by phone at 888-683-0009 or text at 801-742-1898, Monday to Friday, 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. It "cannot give you legal advice or represent you in court," but it can help you file your answer on time. The courts' eviction page for tenants has the forms.

Help paying rent in Salt Lake City: the doors that are left

There is no single rent office. Start with Utah Community Action and 211, and keep calling. None of these programs promises help.

Utah Community Action: Case Management & Housing

Utah Community Action (UCA) helps with rent or a deposit when a household has a short-term crisis "beyond their control." UCA says "Clients must have a current lease in place or be able to sign a lease." It pays the landlord directly and does not cover mortgages. Apply online in English or Spanish from utahca.org, or call (801) 359-2444, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. UCA says it processes applications within 3 business days and that appointments are about two weeks out and cannot be sped up. UCA does not post income limits or whether funds are running low, so ask when you call (checked November 5, 2026).

211 Utah

Dial 2-1-1 (or 888-826-9790 from a cell phone), Monday to Sunday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:45 p.m. You can also text your ZIP code to 801-845-2211, or chat on weekdays from 8:30 a.m. to 4:45 p.m. at 211utah.org. 211 Utah is run by the United Ways of Utah. Ask for rent, deposit and eviction help in Salt Lake County.

Salt Lake City Tenant Resource Center

The City runs a Tenant Resource Center with the Community Development Corporation of Utah at 501 East 1700 South, 801-893-3779, Monday to Thursday 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. and Friday 9 a.m. to noon. Its Relocation Assistance for Tenants helps renters forced to move by repairs, demolition or changes to income limits, and can cover a deposit, first and last month's rent and application fees. It is not a back-rent fund. slc.gov/can/renters

Utility bills: HEAT, Rocky Mountain Power and Enbridge Gas

Utah's HEAT program, run by the Department of Workforce Services, helps households whose "total household income is at or below 150% of the Federal Poverty Level." The 2026–27 windows are set by the calendar:

  • Households with someone 60 or older, someone with a disability, or a child under 6: applications accepted October 1, 2026 to September 30, 2027, "or when federal LIHEAP funds are exhausted."
  • Everyone else: applications processed November 1, 2026 to September 30, 2027, or until funds run out. Apply early; after the money is gone, the next chance is the 2027–28 season starting October 1, 2027.
  • Crisis help: if you have a shut-off notice, ask your HEAT office for crisis assistance. Utah Community Action says crisis help needs a 48-hour shut-off notice and is available year-round.

In Salt Lake County, apply through Utah Community Action: call (801) 359-2444 for an appointment or apply online through myCase. UCA says "The HEAT utility assistance program is still accepting applications for seasonal and crisis assistance!" and that processing takes 6 to 8 weeks (checked November 5, 2026).

  • Rocky Mountain Power HELP: "provides income qualified Rocky Mountain Power customers a monthly bill credit." If you qualify for HEAT, "The local HEAT agency can help you enroll."
  • Rocky Mountain Power Lend A Hand: one-time help "administered by The Salvation Army." Call 801-969-0526 or 1-855-969-0526. Seniors and people with disabilities get priority.
  • Enbridge Gas (the gas company formerly called Dominion Energy Utah): once HEAT is applied to your account, you get "a one-time bill credit of $86 from Enbridge Gas," with "No extra forms or steps required."
  • Salt Lake City water bills: Project Water ASSIST, run with The Salvation Army, helps City water customers at or below 150% of the poverty level who have a disconnection notice.

Utah eviction timeline: what happens and when

Evictions in Utah are "unlawful detainer" cases in district court. In Salt Lake County, that is the Third District Court; the Matheson Courthouse is at 450 South State St., (801) 238-7300. These are the steps for unpaid rent:

  • 1. The 3-day notice. The landlord serves a written notice to pay the rent and other amounts due or leave. You have three business days after service (Utah Code 78B-6-802(1)(c)). Paying everything due in that time ends the problem. This is the time to call UCA's mediation line.
  • 2. The summons and complaint. You have "three business days from the date of service" to answer (78B-6-807(3)(a)). The court can give more time only if you object and the facts call for it. File your answer, even if you owe the rent.
  • 3. The occupancy hearing. If either side asks, the court must hold a hearing "within 10 business days after the day on which the defendant files an answer," and decide who stays in the home while the case goes on (78B-6-810(2)). The trial must start within 60 days of service unless both sides agree otherwise (78B-6-810(1)(b)).
  • 4. If you do not show up. When a tenant who got notice fails to appear, "the court shall issue an order of restitution and enter a judgment of default" unless it finds a reason not to (78B-6-810(4)(a)).
  • 5. Judgment. If you lose, the judgment covers the rent plus three times the damages (78B-6-811(3)), and the court "shall award costs and reasonable attorney fees to the prevailing party" (78B-6-811(5)(a)). That is why it is usually cheaper to settle or move before judgment.
  • 6. The order of restitution. It must tell you that you have "three calendar days after service of the order to vacate," unless you and the landlord agree otherwise (78B-6-812(2)(b)). After a sheriff or constable removes you, you must get reasonable access within five business days to collect clothing, ID, financial and immigration papers, and medicine (78B-6-812(4)(c)).

A court change is coming. The Utah courts say a 2026 law (SB 270) lets them set up a statewide district court division for "debt collection and housing actions," and "The legislation takes effect Jan. 1, 2027." The courts have not yet said where cases will be filed or heard. If you are served on or after January 1, 2027, follow the court address on your summons. What is not a door for emergency rent

  • A Salt Lake City or Salt Lake County rent fund. The City's Housing Stability Division lists no rent fund for tenants, and the County's 2024 eviction-prevention program ran from January 1 to December 31, 2024 and has ended (checked November 5, 2026).
  • Utah Rent Relief. The state's pandemic rent program closed on February 5, 2023. Ignore old flyers and links.
  • The Section 8 waiting list. The Housing Authority of Salt Lake City and Housing Connect share one tenant-based list, and HASLC says "All waiting lists managed on AffordableHousing.com are currently closed to new applicants." Housing Connect says its lists "are currently closed and are not accepting applications." See our Housing Authority of Salt Lake City guide. If you already have a voucher and fall behind, call your caseworker right away and get legal help, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • The old haslc.com website. It now sends you to a housing authority in Missouri. The Salt Lake City authority is at haslcutah.org.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, call the Homeless Hotline at 801-990-9999 and see I need housing tonight.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent help in Salt Lake City right now?

Not from a City, County or state fund. None is open, and Utah Rent Relief closed on February 5, 2023. The main door left is Utah Community Action, which can help with rent or a deposit after a short-term crisis if you have a lease or can sign one. Call (801) 359-2444, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., or apply online. Appointments are about two weeks out, so apply the day you fall behind, and dial 211 to find churches and nonprofits that may have money this month.

How much notice does a Utah landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

Three business days. The landlord must give you a written notice to pay the rent and other amounts due or leave. You are in unlawful detainer only if you have not done either within three business days after the notice was served. Weekends and holidays do not count. If you pay everything due within that time, the landlord cannot go to court on that notice.

How long do I have to answer an eviction lawsuit in Utah?

Three business days from the day you are served. Utah law says the summons must give you three business days to appear and defend, and the courts warn that this is all the time you have. If you answer, either side can ask for a hearing, and the court must hold it within 10 business days. If you do not answer or do not show up, the court will usually order you out by default. Call the Utah Courts Self-Help Center at 888-683-0009 for help with the forms.

Can I get a free lawyer for an eviction in Salt Lake City?

There is no right to a free lawyer in Utah evictions, and we found no Salt Lake City program that provides one. Utah Legal Services helps low-income tenants, usually with income at or below 200% of the federal poverty level. Call 801-328-8891 between 9 a.m. and 2 p.m. on weekdays, or apply online. People's Legal Aid also defends tenants; Utah Community Action lists its number as (801) 477-6975. For a 3-day notice, Utah Community Action's free mediation line is (801) 214-3109.

How do I get help with my heating or power bill in Salt Lake City?

Apply for HEAT, Utah's utility help for households at or below 150% of the federal poverty level. In Salt Lake County, Utah Community Action takes applications at (801) 359-2444 or online through myCase. The 2026 to 2027 season runs to September 30, 2027 or until the money runs out. If HEAT approves you, Rocky Mountain Power's HELP program can give you a monthly bill credit, and Enbridge Gas adds a one-time $86 credit. If you have a shut-off notice, ask for HEAT crisis help right away.

Is the Salt Lake City Section 8 waiting list open?

No. The Housing Authority of Salt Lake City and Housing Connect share one Section 8 list for the whole county, and it is closed to new applicants. Housing Connect says all of its own lists are closed too. The Housing Authority of Salt Lake City says the list only opens every several years because of funding limits. Its website is now haslcutah.org, and its phone number is 801-487-2161.

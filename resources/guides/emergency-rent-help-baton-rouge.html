Emergency Rent Help in Baton Rouge: City-Parish Rent Aid, Free Eviction Help, and Louisiana's 5-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Baton Rouge and you are behind, here is the honest picture. The pandemic rent money is gone. What is left is one public door, the City-Parish Office of Social Services, which can make a one-time rent or mortgage payment for people facing eviction after a crisis, while its money lasts. Free legal help comes from Southeast Louisiana Legal Services (SLLS), which runs an eviction desk right at Baton Rouge City Court. Everything else starts with 211. And Louisiana evictions are fast, so the days matter more here than almost anywhere. This guide covers East Baton Rouge Parish. Everything here was checked on November 12, 2026.

Short version: If you got a notice to vacate or court papers, call SLLS at (225) 448-0080 the same day, or find its desk at City Court. Call the Office of Social Services at 225-358-4561 about rent help. Dial 2-1-1 for everything else. And go to your hearing: in Louisiana it can come three days after you are served, and you can be ordered out 24 hours after a judgment.

Louisiana's clock: 5 days, maybe none, then court within days

Under Code of Civil Procedure art. 4701, the landlord's written notice to vacate must allow "not less than five days from the date of its delivery." But "A lessee may waive the notice requirements of this Article by written waiver contained in the lease," and many Baton Rouge leases do, so check yours tonight. After the landlord files, the court sets the hearing "not earlier than the third day after service" (art. 4732). If you lose and do not leave "within twenty-four hours after its rendition," the court issues a warrant to the sheriff, constable or marshal (art. 4733). Under art. 5059, legal holidays are not counted in a period shorter than seven days, so ask a lawyer to count your days with you.

The City-Parish rent door: Office of Social Services

The City of Baton Rouge and Parish of East Baton Rouge run their emergency help through the Office of Social Services, 4523 Plank Road, Baton Rouge, LA 70805, 225-358-4561, Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Two programs can touch rent (checked November 12, 2026):

  • FEMA Emergency Rent/Mortgage Assistance: in the City-Parish's words, it "Assists individuals and families facing eviction or foreclosure due to an unforeseen crisis. Assistance limited to a one-time payment in a 12-month period contingent upon the availability of funds." You need proof of income, picture identification, Social Security cards for everyone in the household, and proof of residency.
  • Community Services Block Grant (CSBG) emergency assistance: "CSBG may assist with utilities, rent/mortgage, and medication payments." It uses an income limit of 125% of the federal poverty guidelines; the City-Parish posts the table on its CSBG page. Same papers as above, and "Assistance is contingent upon the availability of funds."

How to apply

The office's intake rules, revised March 1, 2025, cover crisis, non-crisis and rent/mortgage help:

  • Pick up, drop off or mail an application at 4523 Plank Road. Applications are also at City-Parish community centers, and you can return them to the nearest community center's drop box.
  • Walk in or drop off Monday through Thursday, 8:30 a.m. to 3:00 p.m. The office says "Monday's Seniors Only."
  • Homebound or need help filling it out? Call 225-358-4561.
  • What to expect: "All applications are reviewed and processed on a first-come first-served basis. All communications will be sent via email." Use an email address you check every day, and make every scanned page clear and readable, all four corners showing.

This is not a big program and it does not promise anything. Apply anyway, apply early in the month, and keep calling the other doors below while you wait. See the City-Parish's Office of Social Services page.

Free legal help in Baton Rouge

A lawyer cannot pay your rent, but can check whether the notice was right, whether your lease really waived it, raise defenses like repairs the landlord never made, and make sure you get every day the law allows. In Louisiana, that last part can be the difference between a week and a day.

  • Southeast Louisiana Legal Services (SLLS), Baton Rouge office: 715 St. Ferdinand Street, Baton Rouge, LA 70802. Local (225) 448-0080; intake line (225) 448-0331; toll free (855) 512-3980; statewide 1-844-244-7871. SLLS says it helps "tenants facing housing-related legal issues like evictions, voucher terminations, tenant rights, and security deposit recovery." When you call, "you won't reach an attorney immediately"; leave your name, phone number, parish and a short description, and "Someone will get back to you within two business days." With a hearing coming, say so. slls.org
  • SLLS Eviction Desk at Baton Rouge City Court: SLLS's renters page lists an "East Baton Rouge City Court Eviction Desk" on "Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday starting at 9:00am." Its flyer puts the desk outside Courtroom 309 at Baton Rouge City Court, 233 St. Louis St., from 9:00 to 11:30 a.m. If your hearing is on one of those days, go early and stop at the desk first. SLLS: Renters
  • No Right to Counsel here. New Orleans has a city law giving tenants a free lawyer in eviction cases. Baton Rouge does not, so the free help depends on SLLS's capacity. Call the first day, not the day before court.

Help paying rent in Baton Rouge: the other doors

Capital Area United Way 211

Dial 2-1-1. Capital Area United Way says 211 is "a free confidential service" available "24 hours a day, seven days a week." You can also text your ZIP code to 898-211 (answered Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.). Ask for rent and utility help in your ZIP code, and write down every name and number. cauw.org/211

St. Vincent de Paul of Baton Rouge

St. Vincent de Paul's "Request Other Assistance" page sends you to a parish resource map: search your home address and it shows the phone number for the group that serves your area. Its Bishop Ott Shelter Program, 1623 Convention St., (225) 383-7343, Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., is the place to call if you could lose your home tonight. svdpbr.net

Utility bills: LIHEAP through the Office of Social Services

  • Who runs it: the Louisiana Housing Corporation lists the East Baton Rouge Office of Social Services as the parish's LIHEAP agency, with the main office at 4523 Plank Road, 225-358-4561, and other offices at Caneyville (225-658-9790), Charles Kelly (225-389-5464), the Dr. Leo Butler Community Center (225-389-7804) and Jewel Newman (225-775-3935).
  • Regular help: apply online through the City-Parish's LIHEAP intake site, or on paper at Plank Road or a community center. The City-Parish lists a heating season of November 15 to March 15. The Louisiana Housing Corporation's statewide page lists December 15 to March 31, and says you can get only one LIHEAP benefit each season. Call 225-358-4561 to ask which dates the office is using this year.
  • Crisis help (disconnect notice or service off): available once a year if your service is threatened or already cut. The office says to visit 4523 Plank Road or call 225-358-4561 "to provide name, contact number and account number." The state lists the crisis season as October 1 to September 30, so it runs all year while funds last.
  • Income limit: the Louisiana Housing Corporation's 2026 limit is $30,618 a year for one person and $58,882 for four. Bring utility bills no older than 30 days, all pay stubs from the last 30 days, proof of other income, proof of address, photo ID and Social Security cards for the household.

Louisiana eviction timeline: what happens and when

Inside Baton Rouge city limits, eviction cases are heard at Baton Rouge City Court, 233 St. Louis St., which hears "landlord-tenant cases up to $35,000." Outside the city, a different court may hear yours; its name is on your papers. These are the steps under the Code of Civil Procedure:

  • 1. The notice to vacate. At least 5 days to move, unless your lease waives the notice (art. 4701). For a lease with a set end date, the notice can come up to 30 days before it ends. Call SLLS now.
  • 2. The rule to show cause. If you do not leave, the landlord files a "rule to show cause" in court, which "shall state the grounds upon which eviction is sought" (art. 4731). You are served with the hearing date.
  • 3. The hearing. Go. The court sets it "not earlier than the third day after service" and hears "any defense which is made." If you do not answer or appear, the court rules for the landlord, and the judgment lasts at least 90 days (art. 4732). Bring your lease, receipts, texts, photos of repair problems, and proof of any rent the landlord refused.
  • 4. 24 hours. If you do not leave within 24 hours of the judgment, the court issues a warrant to the sheriff, constable or marshal (art. 4733), who clears the home in front of two witnesses (art. 4734).
  • 5. Appeal. An appeal stops the eviction only if you answered under oath with a defense that would let you stay, and you apply for the appeal and file the bond "within twenty-four hours after the rendition of the judgment of eviction" (art. 4735). This takes a lawyer.
  • Only the court can put you out. Apart from a home the landlord reasonably believes was abandoned after notice (art. 4731(B)), possession is delivered by an officer under a court warrant. If a landlord locks you out or shuts off your utilities, call SLLS and the police.
  • After a hurricane or flood. In a parish under a federal disaster declaration, leaving your home is not treated as abandonment for 30 days after the declaration. A landlord who takes the home anyway can owe you $500 or twice the monthly rent, whichever is greater, and you can ask a court to stop it (art. 4731(C)).

Security deposits: the landlord must return your deposit within one month after the lease ends. Anything kept needs an itemized statement, sent within one month or within 15 days after that, to the forwarding address you give (La. R.S. 9:3251). Our guide to getting your deposit back walks through it.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The pandemic rent programs. The emergency rental assistance that ran through the City-Parish and the state from 2021 on has ended. Ignore old flyers and websites that still advertise it.
  • The East Baton Rouge Parish Housing Authority (EBRPHA) runs Section 8 and public housing, not emergency rent, and its lists are closed. Its homepage says "Our Affordable Housing and Housing Choice Voucher waitlists are currently closed" (checked November 12, 2026). See our EBRPHA guide. If you already have EBRPHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call (225) 923-8100 right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • A voucher-refusal complaint. Louisiana has no statewide law against refusing a Housing Choice Voucher, and we did not find a Baton Rouge ordinance that adds one. Refusals based on race, disability, family status or another federally protected trait are still illegal; file with HUD.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight, call the Bishop Ott Shelter Program at (225) 383-7343, and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Baton Rouge right now?

Some, while funds last. The City-Parish Office of Social Services has a FEMA Emergency Rent/Mortgage Assistance Program for people facing eviction after an unforeseen crisis, limited to one payment in 12 months, and CSBG emergency help that may cover rent or utilities for households at or below 125% of the poverty guidelines. Call 225-358-4561 or go to 4523 Plank Road. Dial 211 for church and nonprofit help.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Baton Rouge?

Call Southeast Louisiana Legal Services at (225) 448-0080, or its statewide line at 1-844-244-7871, and leave a message with your name, phone number, parish and the problem; SLLS says someone will call back within two business days. SLLS also runs an Eviction Desk at Baton Rouge City Court, 233 St. Louis St., on Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday starting at 9:00 am. Baton Rouge has no Right to Counsel law, so call early.

How much notice does a landlord have to give in Louisiana?

The written notice to vacate must give you at least five days. But Louisiana law lets your lease waive that notice, and many leases do, so the landlord may be able to file in court right away. For a lease with a set end date, the notice can come up to 30 days before the lease ends. Read your lease tonight.

How fast can I be evicted in Baton Rouge?

Very fast. After the landlord files, the hearing can be set as soon as the third day after you are served. If the judge rules against you and you do not leave within 24 hours, the court issues a warrant to the sheriff, constable or marshal. Go to the hearing, and call Southeast Louisiana Legal Services the day you get any paper.

Can I appeal a Louisiana eviction?

Only on a short clock. An appeal stops the eviction only if you answered the landlord's rule under oath with a defense that would let you stay, and you apply for the appeal and file the bond within 24 hours after the judgment. That is hard to do alone, so get a lawyer before the hearing, not after.

Where do I get help with my utility bill in Baton Rouge?

The City-Parish Office of Social Services is East Baton Rouge Parish's LIHEAP agency. Apply for regular help online or on paper, and for crisis help, if you have a disconnect notice or your service is off, visit 4523 Plank Road or call 225-358-4561 with your name, phone number and account number. Crisis help is once a year while funds last.

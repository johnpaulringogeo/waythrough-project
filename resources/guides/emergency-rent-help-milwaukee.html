Emergency Rent Help in Milwaukee, Wisconsin: Free Eviction Lawyers, 2-1-1, and Wisconsin's 5-Day Clock

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Milwaukee and you are behind, here is the honest picture. The main door for rent money in the city, Community Advocates' direct rental assistance, has been paused for new applications since January 7, 2026, except for a few emergencies. We found no other open City of Milwaukee or Milwaukee County rent fund. What is still open: free eviction lawyers through Eviction Free MKE, the Rental Housing Resource Center walk-in clinic, Wisconsin Emergency Assistance for families with children, energy help through Keep Warm Milwaukee, and Impact 2-1-1. Everything here was checked November 5, 2026.

Here's the short version: Dial 2-1-1 (from a cell phone, 414-773-0211). Walk in to the Rental Housing Resource Center at 728 N. James Lovell Street, or call (414) 895-RENT. If you get an eviction summons, contact the Legal Aid Society of Milwaukee the same day at (414) 727-5300 for an Eviction Free MKE lawyer. If you have a child under 18, apply for Wisconsin Emergency Assistance right away and tell the court you applied. And go to court: in Wisconsin your court date can be as soon as 5 days after the summons is issued.

Wisconsin moves fast: a 5-day notice

For a month-to-month tenant or a lease of one year or less, a Wisconsin landlord can end your tenancy with a notice to "pay rent or vacate on or before a date at least 5 days after the giving of the notice" (Wis. Stat. 704.17). The court date on an eviction summons must be "not less than 5 days nor more than 25 days from the issue date" (Wis. Stat. 799.05(3)(b)). Two things can buy time. If you apply for Emergency Assistance, the court "shall stay the proceedings" until your eligibility is decided, for up to 10 working days, as long as no writ has been issued yet (Wis. Stat. 799.40(4)(a)). And a judge can delay the writ up to 30 days for hardship if you pay what is owed (Wis. Stat. 799.44(3)).

Free eviction lawyers in Milwaukee

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can catch mistakes in your notice or the landlord's papers, raise defenses like bad repairs, and help you settle. Wisconsin has no state law that gives tenants a free lawyer, so these programs depend on funding and space.

Eviction Free MKE (Legal Aid Society of Milwaukee)

  • What it is, in Legal Aid's words: "EvictionFree MKE is a right to counsel program of the Legal Aid Society that provides eviction defense to Milwaukee County tenants served with an eviction summons and complaint." It started in 2021. Legal Aid also helps with termination notices, habitable conditions, lock-outs and security deposit recovery (checked November 5, 2026).
  • Who qualifies: Milwaukee County tenants who have been served with an eviction summons and complaint. Legal Aid says it serves "qualifying Milwaukee County residents" and does not post its income limit, so fill out the intake form to find out.
  • How to apply: complete the intake form at legalaidmke.com/intake or call (414) 727-5300. The downtown office is at 728 N. James Lovell Street, Third Floor North Suite, open Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m. Community Advocates' January 2026 resource sheet also lists (414) 892-RENT (7368) for Eviction Free MKE. The program's old evictionfreemke.org website no longer works, so use Legal Aid's site.
  • Eviction sealing clinics: Legal Aid holds free monthly clinics with the Rental Housing Resource Center for Milwaukee County residents who have a dismissed eviction on their record and have finished all payment plans. The next dates it lists are November 11 and December 9, 2026; appointments are preferred. After those dates, check legalaidmke.com for the next one.

Legal Action of Wisconsin

Legal Action of Wisconsin gives free civil legal help to people with low incomes, including in evictions. Call its intake line at (855) 947-2529. Community Advocates' resource sheet lists Legal Action's Eviction Defense Project for "Limited scope in-court representation and guidance" and says to call that number "for eligibility and hours."

Help paying rent in Milwaukee: the doors that are left

Money is tight this year. Use 2-1-1 and the resource center to find who has funds this month, and keep calling while you wait for a lawyer. None of these programs promises help.

Rental Housing Resource Center (Rent Help MKE)

The Milwaukee Rental Housing Resource Center calls itself "a collaborative network of local housing and related assistance programs," with partners including Community Advocates, the Legal Aid Society, Legal Action of Wisconsin and Mediate Wisconsin. Community Advocates says it "will continue to offer assessment and eviction prevention resources, including referrals for mediation, conditions issues and case management." The walk-in clinic is at 728 N. James Lovell Street, Monday to Friday. Its posted hours differ (8 a.m. to 4 p.m. on Community Advocates' January notice; 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. on its rent page), so call (414) 895-RENT (7368) or email info@renthelpmke.org first (checked November 5, 2026).

Community Advocates direct rental assistance: paused

Community Advocates' notice, still posted on its 2026 rent assistance page, says: "Effective January 7, 2026, Community Advocates will pause accepting new applications for direct rental assistance due to limited funding available" (checked November 5, 2026). It lists exceptions. Tenants can still apply if they are facing:

  • a placard (a city order that the unit is unsafe to live in),
  • a fire or domestic violence situation, or
  • residency in a Zilber Family Foundation Initiative neighborhood: Lindsay Heights, Clarke Square or Layton Boulevard West.

If one of these fits you, go to the resource center or call (414) 895-RENT (7368). Community Advocates also lists a small Rent Bridge Program for tenants who have lived in the unit at least one year, owe $400 or less, and have some income; call its Housing Department at (414) 449-4777 to ask whether it is taking cases.

Wisconsin Emergency Assistance (families with children)

The Wisconsin Department of Children and Families runs Emergency Assistance (EA) for families facing "homelessness, impending homelessness, domestic violence, natural disaster, fire, or an energy crisis."

  • Who qualifies: "you must be a parent or a relative caring for a child younger than 18," and "Your income must be at or below 115% of the Federal Poverty Level," with limited assets.
  • How much: up to $1,200 for a group of 2 to 5 people, $220 per person for 6 or more, or $750 for an energy crisis. You can get EA once every 12 months.
  • How to apply: online at access.wisconsin.gov, or at your local W-2 agency. W-2 customer service: 855-757-4539. Community Advocates' January 2026 sheet lists the Milwaukee W-2 agencies: Maximus 414-203-8500, UMOS 414-389-6600, Ross Innovative Employment Solutions 414-760-6060 and America Works 414-386-5765.
  • Why it matters in court: if you have an eviction case, tell the judge you applied. Wisconsin law says the court "shall stay the proceedings" while your EA eligibility is decided, for up to 10 working days, unless a writ has already been issued (Wis. Stat. 799.40(4)(a)).

Impact 2-1-1

Impact 2-1-1 covers Milwaukee and eight nearby counties. The City of Milwaukee says: "On a land line, dial 211. On a cell phone, dial 414-773-0211. On a pay phone, dial 1-866-211-3380." You can also text your ZIP code to 898-211 or chat online. Impact also runs Coordinated Entry, the front door to shelter and housing programs for people who are homeless or about to be.

Mediation

Mediate Wisconsin offers free landlord-tenant mediation at (414) 939-8800. A written deal can stop a case, but read it closely and ask a lawyer before you sign.

Utility bills: Keep Warm Milwaukee and WHEAP

The Wisconsin Home Energy Assistance Program (WHEAP) "assists eligible households with their heating and electric bills." In Milwaukee County you apply through Keep Warm Milwaukee: call (414) 270-4-MKE, walk in at the Lovell location Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., or apply online at energybenefit.wi.gov. The 2026–2027 program year runs October 1, 2026 to September 30, 2027. The income limit is 60% of Wisconsin's median income, for example $3,322 a month for one person or $6,388.42 a month for four. Crisis help is for an "actual or imminent loss of essential home energy," and you must have paid at least $25 on your account in the past three months. The state warns that when funds run out, no benefits are issued, so apply early. State line: 1-866-HEATWIS (432-8947).

Wisconsin eviction timeline: what happens and when

Evictions in Wisconsin go through small claims court under Wis. Stat. chapter 799. These are the steps for unpaid rent:

  • 1. The notice. Month-to-month tenants can get a 5-day notice to pay or move, or a 14-day notice to move with no chance to pay (Wis. Stat. 704.17(1p)(a)). Leases of one year or less get a 5-day notice to pay or move; if you get a second late notice within a year, the landlord can give a 14-day notice to move (704.17(2)(a)). Leases longer than one year get a 30-day notice to pay (704.17(3)(a)). Late fees that are past due count as "rent" (704.17(1g)).
  • 2. The summons. The court date must be 5 to 25 days after the summons is issued, and you must be served at least 5 days before it (799.05(3)(b)). Contact Legal Aid that day.
  • 3. Court. Go. If you do not show up, you can lose by default. Bring your lease, notice, receipts, texts and photos of repair problems. Paying late does not end the case on its own: the law says the case "may not be dismissed because the landlord accepts past due rent" (799.40(1m)). Get any deal in writing.
  • 4. Emergency Assistance stay. If you applied for EA, the court must pause the case while eligibility is decided, for up to 10 working days (799.40(4)(a)).
  • 5. Judgment and writ. If the landlord wins, the court orders judgment and a writ of restitution right away (799.44(1)–(2)). If moving now would be a hardship, ask the judge to delay the writ up to 30 days; you must pay the rent due and keep paying for your time there (799.44(3)). An appeal must be started within 15 days (799.445).
  • 6. The sheriff. Only the sheriff carries out the writ, and must do so within 10 days of getting it (799.45(5)(a)). A writ received more than 30 days after it was issued cannot be carried out (799.44(2)). If your things are removed, the sheriff must tell you within 3 days where they are stored (799.45(3)(b)).
  • Lockouts. Your landlord cannot change the locks, shut off utilities or put your things out without a court order and the sheriff. If it happens, call Legal Aid; Eviction Free MKE handles lock-outs.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • A new City or County rent fund. We found no open City of Milwaukee or Milwaukee County rent program for new applicants on official sites on November 5, 2026. Ignore old flyers about pandemic-era rent relief.
  • The Housing Authority of the City of Milwaukee (HACM) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its Section 8 list "remains CLOSED"; a lottery on June 17, 2026 put 5,000 earlier applicants on the list, and HACM says "At this time, HACM is not issuing vouchers." Its public housing and Project-Based Voucher lists are open online at hacm.org/subsidized (checked November 5, 2026). See our HACM guide. If you already have HACM housing or a voucher and fall behind, call HACM right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • Community Advocates' Waukesha County program. It says it "stopped accepting new applications for direct rental assistance in Waukesha County as funding has concluded" on September 26, 2024.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1. If you are not safe at home, Sojourner Family Peace Center's 24-hour hotline is 414-933-2722.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Milwaukee right now?

Very little. Community Advocates paused new applications for direct rental assistance on January 7, 2026 because of limited funding. It still takes applications from tenants facing a placard, a fire or domestic violence, or who live in Lindsay Heights, Clarke Square or Layton Boulevard West. Families with a child under 18 can apply for Wisconsin Emergency Assistance through ACCESS. Start at the Rental Housing Resource Center, 728 N. James Lovell Street, or call (414) 895-RENT (7368), and dial 2-1-1.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Milwaukee?

If you have been served with an eviction summons and complaint and live in Milwaukee County, contact the Legal Aid Society of Milwaukee for its Eviction Free MKE program. Fill out the intake form at legalaidmke.com/intake or call (414) 727-5300. Legal Action of Wisconsin also helps; call (855) 947-2529. Wisconsin has no law that guarantees a free lawyer, so apply the day you are served.

How long is an eviction notice in Wisconsin?

For unpaid rent, often just 5 days. Month-to-month tenants and tenants with a lease of one year or less can get a notice to pay or move within 5 days. A month-to-month tenant can instead get a 14-day notice to move with no chance to pay, and so can a lease tenant who was late again within a year. A lease longer than one year gets 30 days to pay. The court date then comes 5 to 25 days after the summons is issued.

Can I stop a Wisconsin eviction by paying or applying for Emergency Assistance?

Paying within the notice period ends the problem for that notice. After the case is filed, paying late does not force the court to dismiss it, so get any deal with your landlord in writing. If you apply for Emergency Assistance, the court must pause the case while your eligibility is decided, for up to 10 working days, unless a writ has already been issued. After a judgment, a judge can delay the writ up to 30 days for hardship if you pay what is owed.

Where can I get help with my heating or electric bill in Milwaukee?

Apply for WHEAP energy assistance through Keep Warm Milwaukee. Call (414) 270-4-MKE, walk in at the Lovell location Monday to Friday from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., or apply online at energybenefit.wi.gov. The income limit is 60% of the state median income. If you have a shutoff or no heat, ask for crisis help. Wisconsin Emergency Assistance can also pay up to $750 for an energy crisis for families with children.

Is the Milwaukee Section 8 waiting list open?

No. HACM says its Section 8 waiting list "remains CLOSED." A lottery on June 17, 2026 placed 5,000 earlier applicants on the list, and HACM says it is not issuing vouchers right now. HACM's public housing and Project-Based Voucher lists are open, and you apply online at hacm.org/subsidized. HACM's Housing Choice Voucher line is (414) 286-5650.

Emergency Rent Help in San José: The Homelessness Prevention System, the City's Eviction Help, and California's Deadlines

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in San José and you are behind, here is the honest picture. The City of San José does not run its own rent fund. When a tenant cannot pay, the City says: "If you are unable to pay rent, contact the Homelessness Prevention System at preventhomelessness.org or 408-516-5100." That system is Santa Clara County's main door for rent help, run by the County, Destination: Home and Sacred Heart Community Service with a network of nonprofits. The City's Eviction Prevention Program explains your rights, and San José has its own rent and eviction rules. We found no right-to-counsel program on the City's eviction pages, so free legal help comes from legal aid groups. Everything here was checked on November 5, 2026.

Here's the short version: Call the Homelessness Prevention System at (408) 516-5100 to set up a pre-screening. If you live in a ZIP code Sacred Heart serves (list below), also call Sacred Heart at (408) 709-2364. If you got a notice or court papers, call the City's Eviction Prevention Program at 408-975-4444 and the Law Foundation of Silicon Valley at (408) 280-2424. Do not move out before you learn your rights. If you are sued, file your answer within 10 court days.

California's two clocks

For unpaid rent, a California landlord must first give you a written notice to pay or move out, with "three days' notice, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays" (Code of Civil Procedure 1161; the weekend and holiday rule came from AB 2343 in 2018). If the landlord then sues, your response "shall be filed within 10 days, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays, after the complaint is served" (Code of Civil Procedure 1167, as amended by AB 2347, effective January 1, 2025). The City of San José puts it this way: "Tenants must file responses in court within ten business days. If a response to the lawsuit is not filed within ten business days, a tenant may be evicted."

Help paying rent in San José

None of these programs promises help. Apply to every one that fits you, and keep your lease, your notice, and proof of your income ready.

The Homelessness Prevention System (Santa Clara County)

The Homelessness Prevention System (HPS) offers "assistance to low-income families or individuals who are at risk of losing their housing": temporary financial assistance, legal support, and case management. It says it "is administered by a network of 19 non-profit organizations" and is "co-led by The County of Santa Clara Office of Supportive Housing, Destination: Home and Sacred Heart Community Service." HPS also says it "is no longer involved with deploying federal rent relief" (checked November 5, 2026).

  • Who qualifies, per HPS: you must be a "Resident of Santa Clara County," have low income based on federal guidelines, and be "Currently housed but at risk of losing housing within approximately 14 days." A short questionnaire checks how high your risk of homelessness is.
  • How to apply: call (408) 516-5100 or email info@preventhomelessness.org to schedule a "pre-screening" appointment, or contact one of its partner agencies listed at preventhomelessness.org.

Sacred Heart Community Service: rent help for some ZIP codes

Sacred Heart says that "In addition to the Homelessness Prevention System," it has "other sources of funding for emergency rental assistance for past due rent for those who are low-income and live in Santa Clara County."

  • Who qualifies: a Santa Clara County resident with low income (for example, one person "should be making less than $82,450 or less a year"), past-due rent owed to a landlord, and a lease or the ability to get one. You must live in ZIP code 95008, 95110, 95111, 95112 (south of Santa Clara Street), 95113, 95116, 95117, 95118, 95120, 95123, 95124, 95125, 95126, 95128 or 95136.
  • How to apply: call (408) 709-2364 or email housinginfo@sacredheartcs.org to book an appointment. Its page does not say whether funds are available right now, so call first.
  • Other ZIP codes: Sacred Heart says "Zip Codes 95050, 95051, and 95054 are now served by Salvation Army in San Jose, 408-282-1165."

CalWORKs families: Homeless Assistance for back rent

If you have children and get CalWORKs, or may qualify, ask the Santa Clara County Social Services Agency about CalWORKs Homeless Assistance. The state says it can cover "up to two months of rent arrearages" for CalWORKs recipients or eligible applicants who are homeless or at risk of homelessness. You apply with form CW-42.

211 Bay Area

211 Bay Area, "a program of United Way Bay Area," covers Santa Clara County. Dial 2-1-1 or text your ZIP code to 898-211. It is "Free, confidential, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week," with help in multiple languages.

Eviction help in San José: the City's program and free legal aid

  • City of San José Eviction Prevention Program: staff are at San José City Hall, 200 E. Santa Clara Street, 12th floor, Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. The City says: "Do not move out of your apartment without learning your rights, protections and options." Call 408-975-4444 or email evictionhelp@sanjoseca.gov.
  • Law Foundation of Silicon Valley: its Housing Program "provides free legal advice and representation on housing-related matters to low-income families and individuals in Santa Clara County," including "Defending eviction lawsuit." Call (408) 280-2424.
  • Bay Area Legal Aid: Legal Advice Line 1-800-551-5554, Monday and Thursday 9:30 a.m. to 3 p.m., Tuesday and Wednesday 9:30 a.m. to 1 p.m., "or until capacity is reached."
  • Help filing your court response: the Santa Clara County Superior Court's Self Help Center, 201 N. First St., San José, is open Monday through Thursday 8:30 a.m. to 1:30 p.m. and Friday 8:30 a.m. to noon. Remote help is by phone at (408) 882-2926 on Monday, Tuesday and Thursday, 8:30 a.m. to 1:30 p.m.
  • Santa Clara County Bar Association Lawyer Referral Service: (669) 302-7803. This is a referral to a private lawyer, which may cost money.

San José's renter rules and its Rent Stabilization Program

  • Ask the City: the Rent Stabilization Program helps with "evictions, rent increases, tenant/landlord disputes." Call 408-975-4480.
  • Just cause: the City says its Tenant Protection Ordinance "covers most apartment buildings in San José," and "landlords may only terminate tenancies for 13 just causes, and must follow specific procedures."
  • Rent increases: in apartments covered by the Apartment Rent Ordinance (three or more units, "built and occupied prior to September 7, 1979"), "the maximum allowable rent increase is one 5% increase in a 12-month period." Call 408-975-4470 to check whether your building is covered.
  • Free mediation: the City's Housing Mediation Program is "voluntary and there is no cost to participate," and covers disputes including "non-payment of rent." It can help you and your landlord agree on a payment plan.
  • Vouchers: the City's Housing Payment Equality Ordinance "prohibits discrimination against housing voucher holders who apply for rental housing in San José."

Help with utility bills in San José

  • LIHEAP through Sacred Heart: Sacred Heart's HEAP is "a non-emergency federally-funded program that assists low-income households with paying home energy bills once a year." It says "a Priority Plan is in effect" and "We encourage you to apply but cannot guarantee assistance." Apply online through the state's LIHEAP application, or call 1-877-278-6455 or email heap@sacredheartcs.org. Expect a letter "within 8 weeks."
  • Water bill help: with Valley Water, Sacred Heart's WRAP program says "qualifying households can receive up to $1,000 in assistance once per program year" if you are "responsible for paying a residential water bill." Call (408) 916-5014 or email waterassistance@sacredheartcs.org.
  • San Jose Water discount: San Jose Water's Customer Assistance Program "provides a 15% discount on the total water bill," and it "automatically qualifies customers enrolled in PG&E's rate assistance CARE program." The water bill must be in your name. Call (408) 279-7900.
  • PG&E CARE and FERA, and San José Clean Energy: CARE "provides a monthly discount of 20% or more on gas and 35% or more on electricity"; "If you do not qualify for CARE, we will check to see if you qualify for FERA." For past-due electric bills, the City points to San José Clean Energy's discount programs at (833) 432-2454.

California eviction timeline in San José: what happens and when

  • 1. The 3-day notice. The notice must state the amount due and who to pay, and you get three days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or court holidays (Code of Civil Procedure 1161). In most San José apartment buildings, the Tenant Protection Ordinance also sets rules for notices. Call HPS and the Eviction Prevention Program now.
  • 2. The lawsuit. If you do not pay or move, the landlord can file an unlawful detainer case in Santa Clara County Superior Court. A notice alone does not end your tenancy.
  • 3. Answer within 10 court days. You have 10 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or court holidays, after you are served (Code of Civil Procedure 1167). The Self Help Center can help you file. If you miss the deadline, the landlord can get a default judgment.
  • 4. Trial or settlement. If you answered, the court sets a trial. A settlement can include a payment plan or a move-out date.
  • 5. Just cause. San José's Tenant Protection Ordinance covers most apartment buildings. Where it does not apply, California's Tenant Protection Act protects a tenant who "has continuously and lawfully occupied" a home for 12 months (or 24 months in some cases when a new adult joins the lease), and the notice must state the just cause (Civil Code 1946.2). The state law says it will "remain in effect only until January 1, 2030" unless lawmakers extend it.
  • Lockouts are illegal. A landlord cannot change the locks, remove doors or windows, or shut off your utilities to force you out (Civil Code 789.3). Only the sheriff can remove you, after the court case.

The California Courts' eviction help for tenants explains each step and the forms. Our step-by-step guide to avoiding eviction covers the whole process, and understanding landlord notices explains the paper you got.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The Santa Clara County Housing Authority (SCCHA) runs Section 8 for San José, not an emergency rent fund. It says "our new online interest lists are open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week," but "If rental assistance is available, applicants will be chosen randomly and added to the waiting list" (checked November 5, 2026). Signing up is worth it, but it will not pay this month's rent. See our SCCHA guide. If you already have an SCCHA voucher and your income dropped, report it to SCCHA right away and ask about a rent change; your share of rent is generally based on 30% of your adjusted income.
  • Pandemic-era rent relief. The Homelessness Prevention System says it "is no longer involved with deploying federal rent relief." Ignore old flyers about state or federal rent relief portals.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in San José right now?

Yes, but it is limited and goes to people at high risk. The City of San José sends tenants who cannot pay rent to Santa Clara County's Homelessness Prevention System at (408) 516-5100. It helps low-income county residents who could lose their housing within about 14 days. Sacred Heart Community Service also has rent help for past-due rent in some San José ZIP codes; call (408) 709-2364.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in San José?

Call the Law Foundation of Silicon Valley at (408) 280-2424; it gives free legal advice and representation on housing to low-income people in Santa Clara County, including eviction lawsuits. You can also call Bay Area Legal Aid at 1-800-551-5554. The City's Eviction Prevention Program at 408-975-4444 can explain your rights, and the court's Self Help Center can help you file a response.

How long do I have to respond to an eviction lawsuit in California?

10 court days. Since January 1, 2025, California law gives a tenant 10 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, and court holidays, to file a response after the summons and complaint are served. If you do not file on time, the landlord can ask for a default judgment and the judge can decide without you.

Can a San José landlord evict me without a reason?

Not in most apartment buildings. The City says its Tenant Protection Ordinance covers most apartment buildings in San José and that landlords may only end a tenancy for one of 13 just causes, following set procedures. If you get a notice, contact the Eviction Prevention Program at 408-975-4444 or evictionhelp@sanjoseca.gov before you move out.

How much can my San José landlord raise the rent?

If your apartment is covered by the Apartment Rent Ordinance, meaning a building of three or more units built and occupied before September 7, 1979, the limit is one 5% increase in a 12-month period. Call 408-975-4470 to check whether your building is covered, or the Rent Stabilization Program at 408-975-4480.

Is the San José Section 8 waiting list open?

You can sign up any time, but it is a random draw, not a line. The Santa Clara County Housing Authority says its online interest lists are open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, and that when rental assistance is available, applicants are chosen randomly and added to the waiting list. Call SCCHA at (408) 275-8770.

Emergency Rent Help in Cincinnati: Access to Counsel, Pay to Stay, and Ohio's 3-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Cincinnati and you are behind, start with the bad news: we found no open Hamilton County rent fund you can apply to on your own. What the City does have is Access to Counsel, a City-funded program that can pair rent help with a lawyer for eligible renters facing eviction, and a Pay to Stay law: in a nonpayment case, paying everything you owe by the hearing is a defense. The way in is Legal Aid and United Way 211. This guide covers the City of Cincinnati and Hamilton County. Everything here was checked on November 5, 2026.

What to do now: If you get a 3-day notice or court papers, call Legal Aid at 513-241-9400 and ask for housing intake the same day. Dial 2-1-1 (or 513-721-7900) and ask for rent help. And go to court: in Ohio the hearing can come about a week after you are served, and in Cincinnati, paying all the rent, late fees and costs by the hearing can win a nonpayment case.

Ohio moves fast: a 3-day notice, then court about a week later

Before filing an eviction, an Ohio landlord must give you notice to leave "three or more days before beginning the action," and the notice must say: "You are being asked to leave the premises. If you do not leave, an eviction action may be initiated against you" (Ohio Revised Code 1923.04). The summons must be served "at least seven days before the day set for trial" (ORC 1923.06). A 3-day notice does not mean you must be out in three days; it means the landlord may file. Use those days to call Legal Aid and apply for rent help, and do not skip the hearing.

Access to Counsel: rent help and a lawyer from the City

United Way of Greater Cincinnati describes the City's Access to Counsel program this way: "This eviction-prevention program provides rent assistance and legal counsel to families facing the loss of their homes. The program's partners are United Way; the city of Cincinnati, which funds the program; and Legal Aid Society of Greater Cincinnati." The rules are in Cincinnati City Code Chapter 868, passed in December 2023 (checked November 5, 2026).

  • Who it covers, in the City Code's words: a person who rents a home "in the city of Cincinnati," "including any tenant in a building owned, operated, or managed by the Cincinnati Metropolitan Housing Authority," whose "annual gross income is not in excess of sixty percent of the area median income," who "Is presently subject to imminent threat of eviction," and who "Has not been awarded rental assistance funds from the city's Access to Counsel Pilot Program within twelve months."
  • What counts as a threat of eviction: an eviction case that has been filed, or "receipt of a notice to leave premises, as defined by R.C. 1923.04, within sixty days prior to application." So you can ask for help as soon as you get a 3-day notice.
  • What you may get: the code says covered renters "shall receive legal services, rental assistance funds, or both" from the City's partners.
  • The limits: help is "contingent upon" the City setting aside money, and "If the projected or actual need ... exceeds the available funds," help "may be prioritized." The code also says it does not "create a private right of action." If the money runs out, being eligible does not get you paid.
  • How to ask: neither the City nor United Way posts a separate sign-up page. Call Legal Aid at 513-241-9400 and ask for housing intake, and dial 211 to ask about Access to Counsel.

Free legal help in Cincinnati

  • Legal Aid Society of Southwest Ohio (Legal Aid Society of Greater Cincinnati), 215 E 9th Street, Suite 200: "Call us at 513.241.9400 or 1.800.582-2682." Intake hours are "Monday – Friday 8:45 AM to 4:30 PM." Legal Aid serves households with income under 200% of the federal poverty guidelines. Hamilton County's relief site says to call and ask for "housing intake," and "Tenants who have court papers should call our intake line as soon as they are served." See lascinti.org.
  • Court Help Center, Hamilton County Courthouse, 1000 Main Street, Room 113, 513-946-5650. Hours: Monday to Thursday 8:00 am to 3:30 pm and Friday 8:00 am to 3:00 pm, closed daily 12:45 to 1:45 pm. Its eviction guide offers "a free 40 minute appointment with a lawyer. They cannot represent you in court." See cincyhelpcenter.org.
  • Northern Kentucky renters: Kentucky law and courts are different. United Way says to "contact Legal Aid of the Bluegrass at 859-431-8200."

Help paying rent in Cincinnati: the other doors

United Way 211

United Way's 211 is "available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year." Dial 2-1-1, 513-721-7900 or 1-800-233-4357. It covers Hamilton, Butler, Clermont, Warren, Clinton, Brown and Adams counties in Ohio and Boone, Campbell, Kenton and Grant counties in Kentucky. Ask for rent and utility help in your ZIP code and write down every name and number you get. Its online directory is at uwgc211.org.

Hamilton County Job & Family Services: PRC

The Prevention, Retention and Contingency program "helps families residing in Hamilton County overcome immediate employment-related barriers." You must be 18 or older with at least one child under 18 in the home (or be at least 6 months pregnant), meet income rules, and be working or have a job offer. The County says "The PRC program is not a cash payment and not ongoing support," and its current page does not list rent among the things it pays for, so ask before you count on it. "All PRC applications are now completed on line." Questions: 513-946-1000, 1701 Patricia McCollum Way.

Central Access Point: if you could lose your home

Call 513-381-SAFE (7233) for shelter, Monday to Friday 9:00 am to 5:00 pm and Saturday and Sunday 9:00 am to 1:00 pm. Strategies to End Homelessness, which runs it, notes: "we do not provide rental assistance or services beyond shelter placement."

Utility bills: Winter Crisis, HEAP, PIPP Plus and Duke Energy

  • Who runs it now: Ohio's energy programs say "Effective April 6, 2026, this program will be administered by the Ohio Department of Job and Family Services."
  • HEAP Winter Crisis Program: "The program runs from Nov. 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027," for households with "income at or below 175 percent of the federal poverty guidelines" who have a disconnect notice, have been shut off, or are low on bulk fuel. In Hamilton County, the Community Action Agency books appointments at (513) 685-4478: "Option #1" for an appointment, "Option #2" for same day. CAA says "Our phone lines are open Monday thru Thursday 9am –11am." After March 31, 2027, Winter Crisis closes; ask 211 about the summer program and your utility about payment plans.
  • Regular HEAP: a once-a-year credit for income at or below 175% of the federal poverty guidelines. Apply online through the Energy Assistance Portal on your OHID account. The state warns: "There is a 12-week processing time."
  • PIPP Plus: you pay a share of your income instead of the full bill: "5% of your gross household income for your natural gas bill, and 5% ... for your electric bill," or 10% if you heat with electric, with a "minimum monthly payment of $10.00." The state says "If you make 24 on-time and in-full payments, your outstanding balance ... will be eliminated."
  • Duke Energy Share the Light Fund: "Share the Light Fund is administered by The Salvation Army" and gives "one-time assistance to eligible customers to help avoid disconnection or maintain service." You must have a past-due balance, unpaid deposit or reconnection charge, and you "Cannot be on the Percentage of Income Payment Plan (PIPP) Plus program." Apply online or call 513-762-5636; Duke says to expect a "callback within 72 hours."

Ohio eviction timeline: what happens and when

Ohio evictions are "forcible entry and detainer" cases under Ohio Revised Code chapter 1923. In Cincinnati and the rest of Hamilton County they are heard at Hamilton County Municipal Court. These are the steps:

  • 1. The notice. The landlord must deliver a notice to leave at least three days before filing, by certified mail, in person, or by leaving it at your home (ORC 1923.04). Ending a month-to-month lease for other reasons takes notice at least 30 days before the rent due date (ORC 5321.17). A 3-day notice already counts as a "threat of eviction" for Access to Counsel, so call Legal Aid now.
  • 2. The summons. The clerk mails the summons and complaint, and it must be served "at least seven days before the day set for trial" (ORC 1923.06). The complaint often includes a second claim for back rent or damages; you have 28 days from service to answer that money claim (ORC 1923.06). The summons tells you, "You have the right to seek legal assistance."
  • 3. Court. Go. The Court Help Center says to "Show up to court at least 30 minutes early." Bring your lease, receipts, texts, photos of repair problems, and proof of any rent you offered. The law limits a continuance to eight days unless the landlord agrees or you post a bond to cover the rent (ORC 1923.08). If the case is about unpaid rent, you can raise counterclaims, and the court may order you to pay rent into court while it is pending (ORC 1923.061).
  • 4. Paying in Cincinnati: Pay to Stay. Inside the City of Cincinnati, City Code 871-9(c) says that in a nonpayment case, "the tenant's tender of all past due rent; reasonable late fees ...; court costs; and reasonable attorney fees, not to exceed $125.00, whether tender is made before or after the filing of the eviction action, so long as tender is made no later than at the municipal court eviction hearing, shall constitute an affirmative defense." A "voucher from an accredited social service, nonprofit, governmental, or quasi-governmental agency that guarantees the payment" counts. Late fees are capped at "fifty dollars ($50.00) or five percent (5%) of the monthly contract rent, whichever amount is greater" (City Code 871-9(a)(10)). It does not stop an eviction for reasons other than unpaid rent, and Ohio law gives no statewide right to pay and stay.
  • 5. After the judgment. The Court Help Center says that if you lose, "the landlord gets a court order called a "writ" to put you out after 7 or less days," and "At the set-out, the bailiff comes and your possessions are removed from the home." The officer must carry out the order "within ten days after receiving a writ of execution" (ORC 1923.14). Move what matters before that day.
  • Lockouts are illegal. No landlord may shut off utilities, lock you out, or threaten to, to get you out "other than as provided in Chapters 1923., 5303., and 5321." of the Revised Code, and a landlord who does is liable for your damages and attorney's fees (ORC 5321.15). In Cincinnati you can also recover "an amount not less than $1,000" (City Code 871-10). Call Legal Aid if it happens.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • A Hamilton County rent fund you can apply to on your own. We found none open on November 5, 2026. The pandemic-era emergency rental assistance has ended, and some City pages still show COVID-era eviction notices; ignore old flyers and websites.
  • The Cincinnati Metropolitan Housing Authority (CMHA) runs Section 8 and public housing, not emergency rent. Its page says "The waiting list for CMHA's Housing Choice Voucher Program is currently closed," while public housing takes preliminary applications at any time (checked November 5, 2026). See our CMHA guide. If you already have CMHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call CMHA at (513) 721-4580 right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • A rule against refusing vouchers. Ohio has no statewide source-of-income protection, and the protected classes in Cincinnati's anti-discrimination code (Chapter 914) do not include source of income. Refusals based on race, disability, family status or another protected trait are still illegal; report them to HUD.
  • The Central Access Point places people in shelter; it does "not provide rental assistance." If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and call 513-381-7233.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Cincinnati right now?

Not from an open county fund you can apply to on your own. The main door is the City's Access to Counsel program, which can give rent help, a lawyer or both to Cincinnati renters at or below 60% of the area median income who have an eviction case or got a 3-day notice in the last 60 days. Help depends on City funding. Call Legal Aid at 513-241-9400 and ask for housing intake, and dial 211 or 513-721-7900 for other rent help.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Cincinnati?

Call the Legal Aid Society of Southwest Ohio at 513-241-9400 or 1-800-582-2682, Monday to Friday, 8:45 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., and ask for housing intake as soon as you are served. Legal Aid serves households under 200% of the federal poverty guidelines, and it is a partner in the City's Access to Counsel program. The Court Help Center at 513-946-5650 also offers a free 40-minute meeting with a lawyer who cannot represent you in court.

What is Cincinnati's Pay to Stay law?

In a nonpayment eviction inside the City of Cincinnati, City Code 871-9(c) says that paying all past-due rent, reasonable late fees, court costs and attorney fees of no more than $125, before or after the case is filed but no later than the eviction hearing, is a defense. A voucher from a social service or government agency that guarantees the payment also counts. Bring proof to court.

How fast is an eviction in Ohio?

Fast. The landlord must give a 3-day notice to leave before filing, and the summons must be served at least seven days before the hearing. If the landlord wins, the Court Help Center says the order to put you out comes after 7 or fewer days, and the officer must carry it out within ten days of getting it. Call Legal Aid the day you get a notice.

Can my landlord shut off my utilities or change the locks?

No. Ohio law says a landlord may not shut off utilities, lock you out, or threaten to, to make you leave, except through the court process, and a landlord who does is liable for your damages and attorney's fees. In Cincinnati, City Code 871-10 lets you recover at least $1,000. Call Legal Aid at 513-241-9400 if it happens.

Is the Cincinnati Section 8 waiting list open?

No. The Cincinnati Metropolitan Housing Authority says the waiting list for its Housing Choice Voucher Program is currently closed, and people picked in its 2026 lottery wait behind the current list. CMHA public housing is different: CMHA says you can submit a preliminary application at any time through its portal. Call CMHA at (513) 721-4580 with questions.

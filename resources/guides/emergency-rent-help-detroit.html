Emergency Rent Help in Detroit: Right to Counsel, State Emergency Relief, Local Rent Help, and the 7-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Detroit and you are behind, the strongest help is a free lawyer. Detroit's Right to Counsel program, run by the United Community Housing Coalition (UCHC), "guarantees free legal representation for low-income Detroit renters facing eviction" in the 36th District Court. Call UCHC's Eviction Defense Hotline at (313) 725-4646 as soon as you get a notice or court papers. For money, the state's State Emergency Relief (SER) can help with back rent once a court summons or judgment would leave you homeless, and the Society of St. Vincent de Paul Detroit takes rent-help calls at 313-393-2930. Michigan law also gives you a last chance: if you pay what the judgment says, plus court costs, within the time allowed, the court cannot issue the order to put you out. Everything here was checked on November 3, 2026.

Here's the short version: Call UCHC at (313) 725-4646 the day you get a notice. Apply for State Emergency Relief on MI Bridges if you have court papers. Call St. Vincent de Paul at 313-393-2930 for rent help, and dial 2-1-1 for other local help. If you have a court date, go to it: Right to Counsel staff are at the 36th District Court, near the landlord-tenant division.

A 7-day notice is not an eviction, and a judgment is not the end

Under Michigan law (MCL 554.134(2)), "If a tenant neglects or refuses to pay rent," the landlord can end the tenancy "by giving the tenant a written 7-day notice to quit." The notice does not mean you have to leave. The landlord then has to file a case and win it. Even after a judgment, MCL 600.5744(5) says the order to remove you (a writ of restitution) "must not be issued until the expiration of 10 days after the entry of the judgment for possession." And under MCL 600.5744(7), in a case for unpaid rent, "the writ of restitution must not be issued if, within the time provided, the amount stated in the judgment, with the taxed costs, is paid to the plaintiff" (checked November 3, 2026). So the judgment amount is a target: if you can pay it in time, with help from SER, St. Vincent de Paul, or family, you can stay. The 36th District Court also says that "Only a court officer has the authority to execute the Order," and "A landlord cannot carry out an Order and evict you."

Emergency rental assistance in Detroit: the doors that pay

Detroit has no large rent fund that we could confirm open today. The doors below are real, but each one is narrow. Call before you count on any of them.

State Emergency Relief (SER): help with back rent when you have court papers

SER is run by the Michigan Department of Health and Human Services (MDHHS). Its relocation help covers "rent, rent arrearage, security deposits or moving expenses" if one of a short list of situations applies to your household (checked November 3, 2026). For a renter facing eviction, the key one is: "A court summons or judgment was issued which will result in the family group becoming homeless."

  • Other situations that count: you are homeless, living in a shelter, a car, or on the street; you are staying with others after a fire or natural disaster in the last 60 days; you are staying with others to escape domestic violence; your family needs housing to avoid a foster care placement or to bring a child home; or you got a final written notice to vacate condemned housing.
  • Rules to know: MDHHS says the housing "must be affordable (total housing costs cannot exceed 75 percent of the group's net income)," and "Applicant(s) must have good cause for non-payment of their shelter obligation during the last six months."
  • Amounts are small. MDHHS's "Relocation Services Maximum" table lists $410 for one person, $520 for two, $620 for three, $740 for four, $870 for five, and $1,040 for six or more.
  • How to apply: online through MI Bridges, or through your local MDHHS office. The 36th District Court's housing resources page lists SER for "rental assistance, burial services, first months rent/security deposit, etc."

Society of St. Vincent de Paul Detroit

St. Vincent de Paul Detroit says it gives "$900K in rent assistance annually," along with more than $3.6 million in utility help. In its words: "If you need rent assistance and live in the Metro-Detroit area, please call 313-393-2930." For energy bills, call 313-393-2855 (checked November 3, 2026). Its main line is 313-393-2930, toll-free 1-877-788-4623.

UCHC housing stability help

Along with its lawyers, UCHC has counselors who "work alongside attorneys to help families remain housed, access utility and financial assistance, and connect to other resources for long-term stability." It does not run a public rent fund, but if you are a Right to Counsel client, its case managers help you find money for rent and utilities. Call the Eviction Defense Hotline at (313) 725-4646.

Wayne Metro Community Action Agency

Wayne Metro is Wayne County's community action agency. Its housing programs page lists homeless services, housing help for survivors of domestic violence, and Supportive Services for Veteran Families, but no general rent-help program for tenants who are still housed (checked November 3, 2026). It is still the place for utility and water help (see below). Its Connect Center is (313) 388-9799, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m., and Saturdays, 9 a.m. to noon. It has recorded messages in English, Spanish, and Arabic, and staff who speak several languages.

United Way for Southeastern Michigan 2-1-1

Dial 2-1-1. United Way says its "Community Care Advocates are available 24 hours a day, seven days a week, 365 days a year to connect you to the help you need."

Utility help in Detroit: SER, MEAP, and water

  • SER for heat and electric: MDHHS says SER "may be available for eligible low-income families and individuals when heating or electric service has been or will be shut off," and can pay enough "to restore or continue the service for at least 30 days." You pay a share first: for heat, $40 for one person, $53 for two, $64 for three, and $78 for four. MDHHS says it "will only issue one payment for heat and one payment for non-heat electricity between October 1 and September 30 each year." Apply on MI Bridges.
  • MEAP through Wayne Metro: Wayne Metro says "All clients must complete a pre-screening before accessing an application or scheduling a navigation appointment." You may qualify for pre-screening if your utilities are shut off, you have a shutoff notice, or your home has an adult 60 or older, a person with a disability, or a child under 5. Call (313) 388-9799 (Option 3). Its online application pauses each week when it is full and "will automatically reopen each Monday at 8:00AM."
  • Water: Wayne Metro takes applications for the Detroit Water and Sewerage Department's Lifeline Plan, which gives "an affordable fixed bill based on household income and size," and for water bill help for residents with a past-due balance or a shutoff. Wayne Metro says water help "applications open and close based on funding availability."
  • St. Vincent de Paul energy help: 313-393-2855.

Paying the utility bill can free up cash for rent. Our utility assistance guide covers other programs.

Eviction help in Detroit: Right to Counsel and free lawyers

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can check the landlord's notice and case, raise problems such as repairs, and work out a payment plan or more time.

  • Right to Counsel (UCHC): UCHC says that, "As Detroit's Right to Counsel program administrator, we ensure eligible low-income renters receive free legal representation in eviction cases." To qualify, you must be facing eviction in the 36th District Court in Detroit, meet low-income guidelines, and have a landlord-tenant or other housing problem. UCHC's eviction defense program serves Detroit residents with income "at or below 200% of the federal poverty level." Right to Counsel staff are at the court: "Look for our Right to Counsel representatives near the landlord-tenant division of the court." You can also call before your court date. UCHC works with Michigan Legal Services, Lakeshore Legal Aid, and the Legal Aid and Defender Association.
  • UCHC phones: Eviction Defense Hotline (313) 725-4646; main office (313) 963-3310, weekdays from 8:30 a.m. UCHC's office at 300 River Place Dr., Suite 1200, is closed to the public without an appointment, so call first.
  • Funding is tight, so call early. A 2026 funding request filed with the Michigan Senate says an extra $1.5 million in state money "will allow Right to Counsel to continue post ARPA funding in July 2026," with full representation for "approximately 1500 tenants."
  • Legal Aid and Defender Association (LAD): call 313-967-5800 for legal assistance.
  • Lakeshore Legal Aid: free civil legal help for people with low incomes, older adults, and survivors of violence. Apply by phone at (888) 783-8190 or online. Its site has a Spanish page.
  • What to bring: UCHC asks you to bring the complaint, summons, notice to quit, or demand for possession; your lease; rent receipts such as money orders; repair receipts; any City of Detroit (BSEED) correction orders; photos of health or safety problems; and utility bills.

Michigan eviction timeline in Detroit: what happens and when

These steps come from Michigan law and the 36th District Court's Landlord-Tenant Division page:

  • 1. The notice. For unpaid rent, a written 7-day notice to quit or demand for possession (MCL 554.134(2) and MCL 600.5714(1)(a)). The court says you then have the time on the form "to correct the situation, pay the rent owed, move or seek legal advice."
  • 2. The court case. After the notice period ends, the landlord can file a complaint and summons with the Landlord-Tenant Division, and a hearing date is set. The summons lists the case number and the date and time of your hearing.
  • 3. Go to court. The court says "both the landlord and the tenant have the right to have an attorney present during this court hearing and to request a jury trial." Bring your evidence. The judge may help both sides reach a Consent Judgment. "If a tenant does not show up for the court date a default judgment is issued by the court in favor of the landlord."
  • 4. After the judgment. The writ cannot issue for 10 days after a judgment for possession (MCL 600.5744(5)). In a rent case, paying the judgment amount plus taxed costs in that time stops the writ (MCL 600.5744(7)).
  • 5. The Order of Eviction. If the judgment's terms are not met, the landlord can ask for an Order of Eviction. The court sends you a notice. To ask for a delay, you "must request a Stay (delay) of the Writ at the Civil counter on the 2nd floor," and the court says "Any request for a stay usually must be accompanied by the deposit of one month's rent."
  • Lockouts are illegal. Under MCL 600.2918(2), a tenant whose possession is unlawfully interfered with by the owner can recover "actual damages or $200.00, whichever is greater, for each occurrence." That includes changing the locks, removing your things, or cutting off heat, water, or electric service. If you are locked out, call UCHC.
  • Repairs: the court lists the City of Detroit Rental Inspector at (313) 224-2733 for repair complaints, and says it is illegal for a landlord to retaliate against you for complaining.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The Detroit Housing Commission (DHC) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its site says: "The HCV Waiting list is currently closed, we are only accepting applications for Project Based Properties only" (checked November 3, 2026). Main line: 313-877-8000, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Its Customer Service Center is at 2211 Orleans. Our DHC guide has the status of each list, the state MSHDA program and how to apply.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, call CAM, Detroit's shelter entry line: "If you are experiencing homelessness and need to get help, the process begins with contacting CAM (313) 305-0311." See also I need housing tonight.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Detroit?

Some, but it is narrow. State Emergency Relief can help with back rent once a court summons or judgment would leave you homeless; apply on MI Bridges. St. Vincent de Paul Detroit takes rent-help calls at 313-393-2930. Dial 2-1-1 for other local help. The strongest protection is a free lawyer through Detroit's Right to Counsel program at (313) 725-4646.

What is Detroit's Right to Counsel, and who qualifies?

It gives free lawyers to low-income Detroit renters facing eviction in the 36th District Court. The United Community Housing Coalition runs it with partner legal aid groups. UCHC's eviction defense serves Detroit residents at or below 200% of the federal poverty level. Call (313) 725-4646, or look for Right to Counsel staff near the court's landlord-tenant division on your court day.

Can State Emergency Relief pay my back rent in Detroit?

It can, in some cases. MDHHS says SER relocation help covers rent, back rent, security deposits, or moving costs when, for example, a court summons or judgment would leave your family homeless. Your housing costs must be no more than 75% of your net income, and you need good cause for not paying rent in the last six months. Apply online on MI Bridges.

How much notice does a Michigan landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

A written 7-day notice to quit under MCL 554.134(2). The notice does not mean you have to leave. The landlord must file a case in the 36th District Court and win, and only a court officer can carry out an eviction order. A landlord who locks you out or shuts off your utilities can owe you your actual damages or $200, whichever is greater.

Can I still stop an eviction after the judge rules?

Often, yes. In Michigan the order to remove you cannot issue until 10 days after the judgment. In a case for unpaid rent, if you pay the amount in the judgment plus the court costs within the time allowed, the court must not issue it. You can also ask for a stay at the court's Civil counter, which usually requires a deposit of one month's rent. Call UCHC first.

Does the Detroit Housing Commission give emergency rent help?

No. DHC runs Section 8 and public housing. Its Housing Choice Voucher waiting list is closed, and it is only taking applications for Project Based properties. If you already have DHC housing or a voucher and fall behind, call DHC right away at 313-877-8000.

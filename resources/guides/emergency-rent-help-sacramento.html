Emergency Rent Help in Sacramento: What's Still Open, Free Eviction Help, and the 10-Court-Day Deadline

Matt · Updated October 2026

If you rent in Sacramento or anywhere in Sacramento County and you are behind, start with the hard news: the big pandemic-era program, the Sacramento Emergency Rental Assistance (SERA) program, is closed. The Sacramento Housing and Redevelopment Agency (SHRA) says "As of December 11th, 2024, all rental assistance funds have been disbursed and the SERA program is closed." We found no City, County, or SHRA rent fund that replaced it. What is left are narrower doors: 2-1-1 Sacramento and the Coordinated Access System for people who could lose their home, CalWORKs Homeless Assistance for families, SSVF for veterans, and free legal help to slow down or settle an eviction. Everything here was checked on October 28, 2026.

Here's the short version: Dial 2-1-1 (or 916-498-1000) and say you are about to lose your housing; it is the way into Sacramento's Coordinated Access System. If you have children and get CalWORKs, or might qualify, ask the County about CalWORKs Homeless Assistance, which can pay up to two months of back rent. Veterans should call Volunteers of America at (916) 228-3153. If you got court papers, call Legal Services of Northern California at (916) 551-2150 at 8:30 a.m. and file a response within 10 court days, or the landlord can win without a hearing.

California's two clocks: the 3-day notice and the 10-court-day answer

For unpaid rent, a California landlord must first give a written notice to pay the rent or move out. The law gives you "three days' notice, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays" (Code of Civil Procedure 1161). If the landlord then sues, the case is called an unlawful detainer, and your response "shall be filed within 10 days, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays, after the complaint is served" (Code of Civil Procedure 1167, as amended by AB 2347, effective January 1, 2025). Legal Services of Northern California warns that if you have not filed by the end of the 10 days, "the landlord can ask the court for a 'default judgment,'" which means you never get to tell your side.

Help paying rent in Sacramento: the doors still open

None of these programs promises help, and none of them is a big open rent fund. Try every one that fits you, and keep your notice and lease with you when you call.

2-1-1 Sacramento and the Coordinated Access System

2-1-1 Sacramento, a program of Community Link Capital Region, answers "24 hours a day, seven days a week," with help in other languages. Dial 2-1-1, 916-498-1000, or toll-free 1-844-546-1464; if you are hearing impaired, call 7-1-1 and ask for 2-1-1. Sacramento County's Department of Homeless Services and Housing sends people there for shelter and services.

2-1-1 is also the front door to the Coordinated Access System (CAS), which Sacramento Steps Forward manages with the City and County. In its words, "A person experiencing or at risk of homelessness will call 2-1-1, work with a street outreach team, or visit a CAS-enrolled provider to start the process." A staff member asks about your situation and explores "immediate options," and CAS includes homelessness diversion and prevention help. Be ready for the limits it states plainly: "Because resources are limited, not everyone is referred right away," and those with the highest risk go first.

CalWORKs Homeless Assistance: back rent for families

If your household has children and gets CalWORKs, or looks eligible for it, the state's CalWORKs Homeless Assistance program can pay "up to two months of rent arrearages" to prevent an eviction, or a security deposit and last month's rent for a new place. It serves "eligible CalWORKs recipients or apparently eligible CalWORKs applicants, who are homeless or at risk of homelessness." You apply through the county welfare office with form CW-42, the Statement of Facts for Homeless Assistance. In Sacramento County, that is the Department of Human Assistance (DHA), which the County lists at 1-800-560-0976. Bring your pay-or-quit notice. Families who need shelter now are told to call 2-1-1.

Veterans: Volunteers of America's SSVF program

Volunteers of America Northern California & Northern Nevada runs Supportive Services for Veteran Families (SSVF), funded by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, for "very low-income Veteran families living in or transitioning to permanent housing." Its services include case management, benefits claims, child care, transportation, and rental assistance. Call (916) 228-3153 or go to 10636 Schirra Ave, Mather. If you are a veteran who already has a HUD-VASH voucher or SHRA housing, call SHRA too.

If you already have SHRA housing or a voucher

SHRA runs Section 8 and public housing for the City and County. It is not an emergency rent fund, and its waiting list site says "SHRA does not provide immediate emergency housing or hotel vouchers." But if you already have a voucher or live in SHRA public housing and your income dropped, call SHRA at (916) 440-1390 right away and ask about a rent change; SHRA's office at 630 I Street is open by appointment only. An eviction can cost you your assistance, so get legal help too. Our SHRA guide lists its open waiting lists, phone numbers, and how to apply.

Eviction help in Sacramento: free lawyers, court clinic, and mediation

None of these pays rent, but they can catch mistakes in a notice or lawsuit, help you file your answer on time, and work out a payment plan or a move-out date you can live with.

Legal Services of Northern California (LSNC)

  • What it does: free civil legal help for low-income people. Its Sacramento office serves Sacramento County, and its cases include housing.
  • How to apply: call (916) 551-2150. General intake is Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday, 8:30 a.m. to noon and 1 p.m. to 5 p.m., "or until full." LSNC says "Our intake list can fill quickly due to high demand. It is best to call when our office opens at 8:30 a.m." If you cannot call during the day because of work, school, or a disability, its evening line is (866) 815-5990; leave a message and someone will call back within one to two business days.
  • The office: 515 12th Street, Sacramento. Bring any papers that relate to your problem.
  • Free self-help: LSNC's eviction page has an "Answer to Eviction" packet, a fee waiver packet, and a flow chart of the California eviction timeline.

Sacramento Superior Court Advisory Clinic (free, walk-in)

The court's Advisory Clinic is "a free public service" staffed by attorneys, open to landlords and tenants who do not have a lawyer. It answers questions like "I got a 3-day notice. What should I do?" and "How do I respond to an eviction case?" and reviews forms.

  • Walk in: Carol Miller Justice Center, 301 Bicentennial Circle, Room 300, Sacramento 95826. "No appointment necessary or possible." Monday through Thursday, 7 a.m. to noon and 1 p.m. to 5 p.m., except court holidays.
  • Phone: 916-875-7846. Leave a message and an attorney will call back within two business days. Email: scudadvisors@saccourt.ca.gov.
  • Free online workshop: the second Wednesday of each month at 5 p.m. on Zoom, open to everyone.

Court mediation, the Renters Helpline, and the City's Tenant Protection Program

  • Court mediation: the court's Small Claims and Unlawful Detainer Mediation Program offers mediation before trial (call 916-875-7843 to schedule) and on the day of trial at the Carol Miller Justice Center. Both sides have to agree. A settlement can include a payment plan.
  • Sacramento Renters Helpline: (916) 389-7877, Monday through Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m. It is free, it has contracts with Sacramento County and the cities of Sacramento (through SHRA), Elk Grove, Rancho Cordova, Folsom, and Citrus Heights, and it offers tenant counseling and "tenant-landlord dispute resolution."
  • City of Sacramento Tenant Protection Program: for tenants inside city limits. Call 916-808-8121 or email tpp@cityofsacramento.org with questions about a rent increase or eviction notice. See the rules below.

California eviction timeline: what happens and when

These are the steps for an eviction over unpaid rent in Sacramento County, in order:

  • 1. The 3-day notice. The landlord gives you a written notice to pay or move. It must state the amount due and who to pay, and you get three days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or court holidays (Code of Civil Procedure 1161). This is the moment to call 2-1-1 and LSNC. Paying the full amount the notice demands within that time is the cleanest fix; get a receipt.
  • 2. The lawsuit. If you do not pay or move, the landlord can file an unlawful detainer. In Sacramento County, the court's Unlawful Detainer Division at the Carol Miller Justice Center handles eviction cases asking for up to $35,000. A notice alone does not end your tenancy; the court says "The sheriff schedules and conducts the eviction, not the court," and only after a judgment.
  • 3. Answer within 10 court days. You have 10 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or court holidays, after you are served to file your response (Code of Civil Procedure 1167). LSNC says to "start counting with the day after you received the papers." If you miss it, the landlord can get a default judgment. If you cannot afford the filing fee, ask for a fee waiver.
  • 4. Mediation and trial. On the trial date, a court mediator can meet with both sides outside the courtroom. If you settle, "the mediator writes the settlement agreement, the court accepts it, and there is no trial." If not, a judicial officer hears the case.
  • 5. Just cause and rent limits. Under California's Tenant Protection Act, once you have "continuously and lawfully occupied" the home for 12 months, a covered landlord needs a just cause, stated in the notice, to end your tenancy (Civil Code 1946.2), and yearly rent increases are capped at "5 percent plus the percentage change in the cost of living, or 10 percent, whichever is lower" (Civil Code 1947.12). Not paying rent is a just cause, so these rules matter most when the notice is for something else. Inside the City of Sacramento, the Tenant Protection Program caps increases at 8.6% from July 1, 2026, and tenants who have lived in the unit more than 12 months can file a Petition for Pre-Eviction Hearing.
  • Lockouts are illegal. A landlord cannot change the locks, remove doors or windows, or shut off your utilities to force you out (Civil Code 789.3). Only the sheriff can remove you, after the court case.

The California Courts' eviction help for tenants explains each step and the forms. Our step-by-step guide to avoiding eviction covers the whole process, and understanding landlord notices explains the paper you got.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • SERA is closed. SHRA's page says "As of December 11th, 2024, all rental assistance funds have been disbursed and the SERA program is closed," and its waiting list closed December 15, 2023 (checked October 28, 2026). Old links, flyers, and news stories about SERA, SERA2, or "ERA" money in Sacramento are out of date.
  • The SHRA Section 8 list is not a quick fix. Its waiting list portal says "Tenant-Based Housing Choice Voucher Program waiting list (formerly known as the Section 8 program) is currently closed." When we checked, project-based voucher lists for the City and County, Phoenix Park, and Mirasol Village, and public housing lists for City Housing, County Housing, and Walnut Grove, were open, but only for some unit sizes, mostly 3 to 5 bedrooms. SHRA says its waiting lists are "ONLY posted on Sacwaitlist.com," and it warns that flyers pointing to "www.govassistance.org" are "NOT LEGITIMATE." Applying never costs a fee.
  • Sacramento Self-Help Housing: its old website address, sacselfhelp.org, now leads to an unrelated site, so we could not confirm any current program. For tenant counseling, call the Renters Helpline at (916) 389-7877.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Sacramento right now?

Not a general fund you can apply to on your own. The Sacramento Emergency Rental Assistance (SERA) program closed when its money ran out in December 2024. What is left: dial 2-1-1 (or 916-498-1000) to be screened through Sacramento's Coordinated Access System, which includes homelessness diversion and prevention help; families on CalWORKs, or who appear eligible, can ask the County for CalWORKs Homeless Assistance, which can pay up to two months of back rent; and veterans can call Volunteers of America's SSVF program at (916) 228-3153.

Is the Sacramento Emergency Rental Assistance (SERA) program still open?

No. SHRA says: "As of December 11th, 2024, all rental assistance funds have been disbursed and the SERA program is closed." Its waiting list closed on December 15, 2023. Ignore old flyers, links, and news stories that send you to SERA.

How do I get free help with an eviction in Sacramento?

Call Legal Services of Northern California's Sacramento office at (916) 551-2150. General intake runs Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday, 8:30 a.m. to noon and 1 to 5 p.m., or until the list fills, so call at 8:30. The Superior Court's free Advisory Clinic at the Carol Miller Justice Center, 301 Bicentennial Circle, Room 300, gives walk-in help Monday through Thursday, or call 916-875-7846 and an attorney will call back within two business days.

How long do I have to respond to an eviction lawsuit in California?

10 court days. Since January 1, 2025, California law gives a tenant 10 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, and court holidays, to file a response after the eviction papers (the summons and complaint) are served. Start counting the day after you get them. If you do not file on time, the landlord can ask for a default judgment and win without a hearing.

Is the SHRA Section 8 waiting list open?

No. SHRA's waiting list site says the "Tenant-Based Housing Choice Voucher Program waiting list (formerly known as the Section 8 program) is currently closed." When we checked, some project-based voucher and public housing lists were open for larger units, mostly 3 to 5 bedrooms. SHRA lists its waiting lists only at sacwaitlist.com, and it says it does not provide immediate emergency housing or hotel vouchers.

Does the City of Sacramento protect tenants from rent increases and evictions?

Yes, for most multifamily rentals inside city limits. The City's Tenant Protection Program limits yearly rent increases to 5 percent plus inflation; the maximum is 8.6% from July 1, 2026. After a tenant has lived in the unit more than 12 months, the landlord needs a just cause to evict, and the tenant can petition for a pre-eviction hearing. Call the program at 916-808-8121 or email tpp@cityofsacramento.org.

Emergency Rent Help in Fort Worth: What Is Still Open, Free Legal Aid, and Texas's 2026 Eviction Clock

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Fort Worth and you are behind, start with the hard news: the City's rent program is out of money. The City of Fort Worth's Community Action Partners (CAP) says "all funding for Emergency Rental Assistance has been exhausted." Tarrant County's own prevention program does not serve Fort Worth renters. What is left is smaller: CAP's utility help and its rent help for veterans, 2-1-1, and local charities. For eviction help, Legal Aid of NorthWest Texas takes landlord-tenant cases at 855-548-8457. Texas eviction law also changed on January 1, 2026, and the deadlines are short. Everything here was checked on November 5, 2026.

Here's the short version: Dial 2-1-1 and ask who has rent money in Tarrant County this month. Call CAP at 817-392-2276 for help with electric, gas or propane bills, which can free up rent money; if you are a veteran, ask about rent too. Call Legal Aid's landlord-tenant line the day you get a notice. If your court papers mention "summary disposition," you have only 4 days to respond. And if you get a court date, go.

Texas moves fast, and the rules changed on January 1, 2026

A Texas landlord needs to give only "at least three days' written notice to vacate" before filing, unless your lease says otherwise (Property Code section 24.005). The trial must be held between the 10th and the 21st day after the case is filed (section 24.0051(g)). Under SB 38, if the landlord files a motion for summary disposition, you have until the 4th day after you are served to file a written response, or the judge can rule without a trial (section 24.005106). After a judgment you have 5 days to appeal (section 24.005107). That is why you should call for help the day you get a notice, not the day you get a court date.

Help paying rent in Fort Worth: what is open and what is not

Apply to more than one. Each runs on its own money, and none of them promises help.

City of Fort Worth Community Action Partners (CAP): no rent money right now

CAP is the City's anti-poverty program. Its page says: "At this time, all funding for Emergency Rental Assistance has been exhausted," and it does not know "when or if additional funds will become available" (checked November 5, 2026). Here is what CAP still lists:

  • For Tarrant County residents: case management, utility assistance for electric, gas and propane bills, and HVAC service and repair. CAP says water bill help is not available.
  • For Tarrant County veterans: utility assistance, veterans assistance for rent, mortgage and utilities, and HVAC repairs. If you served, ask about rent help when you call.
  • How to apply: CAP takes online applications in short weekly windows and says applications close "once capacity is reached." Its page still showed "Online Applications Open: Fridays in October 2026 at 9 a.m." and had not posted November dates when we checked on November 5, 2026, so call 817-392-2276 or check CAP's page before Friday morning. CAP warns that call volume may be high. Office hours are 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., and appointments are required.
  • Where: CAP lists centers at the Como, Northside, Martin Luther King Jr. and Worth Heights community centers, the Hazel Harvey Peace Center for Neighborhoods, and an Arlington CAP Center.

Tarrant County: prevention help only outside Fort Worth

Tarrant County's Emergency Solutions Grant program gives "short-term homeless prevention assistance to income-qualified persons," but only "within Tarrant County, outside of Arlington, Fort Worth and Grand Prairie." You must have low income and "a notice to vacate or eviction letter." If you live in another Tarrant County city, apply with the County's Prevention Screening Form on its Emergency Solutions Grant page, or call the program manager at 817-850-7952. The page does not say whether funds are available this month.

2-1-1 Texas: the switchboard

United Way of Tarrant County runs 2-1-1 locally. It says the hotline is "free, anonymous and available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year." Dial 2-1-1. Texas Health and Human Services says "If you can't connect to 2-1-1, call us toll-free at 877-541-7905." Ask for rent help in Tarrant County, ask about churches and charities such as Catholic Charities Fort Worth, St. Vincent de Paul and The Salvation Army, and write down every name and number you get.

Texas Rent Relief and the Texas Eviction Diversion Program are over

The Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs says both programs "launched in February 2021 and closed in the summer of 2023." A Tarrant County justice court page still describes the Texas Eviction Diversion Program and its rent payments. That program is closed, so do not count on it.

Eviction help in Fort Worth: free lawyers

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can catch mistakes in the landlord's notice, file your response on time, and work out a deal. We found no right-to-counsel program for Fort Worth or Tarrant County on the City's or the County's websites (checked November 5, 2026), so there is no guaranteed lawyer. Call early.

At the courthouse

Tarrant County eviction cases are heard in its Justice of the Peace courts. Justice Court 1 says "Evictions MUST be filed in the proper Precinct (where the premises is located) or the case will be dismissed," and that "Eviction cases are governed by Rule 510 of Part V of the Rules of Civil Procedure." It posts a free "Self-Help Legal Information Packet: When an Eviction Case Has Been Filed Against You" on its eviction cases page, along with a defendant's form to respond to a summary disposition motion. Check your citation for your precinct, courtroom and time.

Texas eviction timeline in 2026: what happens and when

Texas rewrote its eviction law with Senate Bill 38, which took effect on January 1, 2026. The rules are in Chapter 24 of the Texas Property Code and in Texas Rule of Civil Procedure 510, which the Supreme Court of Texas rewrote as a stand-alone eviction rule for cases filed on or after that date. These are the steps for a rent case, in order:

  • 1. The notice. Your landlord must give you "at least three days' written notice to vacate" before filing, "unless the parties have contracted for a shorter or longer notice period in a written lease" (Property Code section 24.005(a)). Read your lease. If the case is only about unpaid rent and you were not late before the month of the notice, the notice must be a "notice to pay rent or vacate," which gives you the chance to pay. The notice can come by mail, be left inside your home in a conspicuous place, be handed to anyone in the home 16 or older, or come by email only if you agreed to that in writing (section 24.005(f-3)).
  • 2. The court case. The landlord files a sworn petition in the Justice of the Peace court (section 24.00505) for the precinct where your home is. The officer must make a diligent effort to serve you within 5 business days after filing (section 24.0051(f)).
  • 3. The 4-day answer to a summary disposition motion. This is the new trap. With the petition, a landlord can file a sworn "motion for summary disposition without trial." If you do not file a written response with your supporting facts and documents "not later than the fourth day after the date the tenant is served," the judge can rule for the landlord without a trial (section 24.005106; Rule 510.10(a)). If your papers mention summary disposition, get legal help the same day.
  • 4. The trial. The court must hold the trial "not earlier than the 10th day or later than the 21st day after the date the petition is filed," and never sooner than the 4th day after you are served. A trial can be postponed for no more than 7 days unless both sides agree in writing (section 24.0051(g)). You do not have to file a written answer to an ordinary eviction suit (Rule 510.11(a)), but you must show up. If you do not, the landlord usually wins by default.
  • 5. The appeal: 5 days. To appeal, file a bond, a cash deposit, or a Statement of Inability to Afford Payment of Court Costs with the justice court "not later than the fifth day after the date the judgment is signed," and affirm that you have a good-faith defense (section 24.005107; Rule 510.19(a)). To stay in your home during the appeal, you must pay one rental period into the court registry within 5 days of filing the appeal and keep paying each period (section 24.0053(a-2)). If you miss a payment, the court can issue the writ of possession without a hearing (section 24.0054(a)).
  • 6. The writ of possession. If you lose and do not appeal, the writ cannot issue "before the sixth day after the date on which the judgment for possession is rendered" (section 24.0061(b)). The officer must post a warning on your front door at least 24 hours before carrying it out (section 24.0061(d)).
  • Lockouts. A landlord "may not intentionally prevent a tenant from entering the leased premises except by judicial process," with narrow exceptions. A landlord who changes the locks on a tenant who is behind must post a notice saying where to get the new key at any hour, and must give you the key "regardless of whether or not the tenant pays any of the delinquent rent." If a landlord breaks these rules, you can get back in or end the lease and recover one month's rent plus $1,000, plus damages and fees (Property Code section 92.0081).

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • Fort Worth Housing Solutions (FWHS), the city's housing authority, runs Section 8 and affordable communities. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its voucher page says "FWHS is not currently accepting Wait List applications" (checked November 5, 2026). FWHS posted one short Project-Based Voucher window for Hughes House III, Sedona Village Senior Living and Cowan Place: it "will open Sunday, November 1, 2026, at 8:00 AM, and will close on Thursday, November 5, 2026, at 5:00 PM," online through FWHS's RentCafe portal. After 5:00 PM on November 5, 2026, that window is closed; check fwhs.org for the next one. Our FWHS guide has the details. If you already have FWHS housing or a voucher and fall behind, call FWHS at 817-333-3400 (Monday to Thursday) right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • The Tarrant County Housing Assistance Office runs a separate Section 8 list. It says its wait list is "CLOSED" and expects it to stay closed for years.
  • Vouchers and landlords. Texas has no source-of-income protection and bars cities from adopting one, so a Fort Worth landlord may legally refuse a voucher. Refusals based on race, disability, family status or another protected trait are still illegal.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Fort Worth right now?

Not from the City. The City of Fort Worth's Community Action Partners (CAP) says "all funding for Emergency Rental Assistance has been exhausted" and it does not know when or if more money will come (checked November 5, 2026). CAP still helps Tarrant County residents with electric, gas and propane bills, and helps Tarrant County veterans with rent. Tarrant County's prevention program does not serve renters in Fort Worth. Dial 2-1-1 for churches and charities that have money this month.

Does Tarrant County help Fort Worth renters with rent?

Not through its main prevention program. Tarrant County's Emergency Solutions Grant prevention help is only for renters "outside of Arlington, Fort Worth and Grand Prairie" who have low income and a notice to vacate or eviction letter. If you live in another Tarrant County city, apply with the County's Prevention Screening Form or call 817-850-7952. Fort Worth renters should call CAP at 817-392-2276 and dial 211.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Fort Worth?

Call Legal Aid of NorthWest Texas. Its landlord-tenant line is 855-548-8457, its Legal Aid Line is 1-888-529-5277, and you can also apply online. Its Fort Worth office is at 600 East Weatherford St., 817-336-3943, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. We found no right-to-counsel program for Fort Worth or Tarrant County, so call as soon as you get a notice, not the day before court.

How long does an eviction take in Texas in 2026?

It can take less than a month. The landlord must give at least 3 days' written notice to vacate unless the lease says otherwise. After the case is filed, the trial must be held between the 10th and 21st day. If the landlord asks for summary disposition, you must file a written response within 4 days of being served. After a judgment you have 5 days to appeal, and the writ of possession cannot issue before the 6th day.

Is the Fort Worth Housing Solutions waiting list open?

The Section 8 list is not: FWHS says "FWHS is not currently accepting Wait List applications" (checked November 5, 2026). FWHS posted one short window for Project-Based Vouchers at Hughes House III, Sedona Village Senior Living and Cowan Place, from 8:00 AM Sunday, November 1 to 5:00 PM Thursday, November 5, 2026, online through its RentCafe portal. After 5:00 PM on November 5, 2026 that window is closed; check fwhs.org or call 817-333-3400 for the next opening. A voucher would not come in time to stop an eviction.

Is Texas Rent Relief or the Texas Eviction Diversion Program still open?

No. The Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs says both programs "launched in February 2021 and closed in the summer of 2023." Some Tarrant County justice court pages still describe the Texas Eviction Diversion Program, but it is not taking applications.

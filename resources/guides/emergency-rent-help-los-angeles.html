Emergency Rent Help in Los Angeles: What's Open, Free Eviction Lawyers, and the 10-Court-Day Deadline

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Los Angeles and you are behind, know that the big LA County Emergency Rent Relief Program is not taking applications. Its site says "Application Period Closed." But some money is still moving. The LA County Affordable Housing Solutions Agency (LACAHSA) says "Emergency rental assistance funds are now available" through local nonprofits for renters who had a crisis in the last 12 months. 2-1-1 screens you for LAHSA's homelessness prevention programs, and families on CalWORKs can ask the County's DPSS for help with back rent. If you got court papers, Stay Housed LA at 1-888-694-0040 is the way to a free lawyer, and City of LA tenants may have a right to one. Everything here was checked on November 3, 2026.

What to do first: Email the LACAHSA prevention provider for your area (the list is below) and say you are behind on rent. Call or text 2-1-1 (or 800-339-6993) the same day. If you have children and get CalWORKs, call DPSS at 1-866-613-3777. If you got a 3-day notice or court papers, call Stay Housed LA at 1-888-694-0040 right away and file your answer within 10 court days, or the landlord can win without a hearing.

California's two clocks, plus one LA rule

For unpaid rent, a California landlord must first give you a written notice to pay or move out, with "three days' notice, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays" (Code of Civil Procedure 1161). If the landlord then sues, your response "shall be filed within 10 days, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays, after the complaint is served" (Code of Civil Procedure 1167, as amended by AB 2347, effective January 1, 2025). Inside the City of Los Angeles there is one more rule: the LA Housing Department (LAHD) says landlords "may not evict a tenant who falls behind in rent unless the tenant owes an amount higher than the Fair Market Rent," which depends on your unit size. You still owe the rent, but a small debt cannot be the reason for an eviction in the City.

Emergency rental assistance in Los Angeles: the doors still open

None of these is a big, open-to-all rent fund, and none promises help. Try every one that fits you, and have your lease, your notice, and proof of your income ready when you call or write.

LACAHSA Renter Protection and Homelessness Prevention (RPHP)

LACAHSA is a new countywide housing agency. It does not take applications itself. It gives money to nonprofits, and it says "LACAHSA awarded $11.4M in Renter Protection and Homelessness Prevention funds" and "Emergency rental assistance funds are now available" (checked November 3, 2026). In its words, the help is "temporary and short-term, designed for individuals and families who are currently housed," and it does not pay long-term rent subsidies. LACAHSA lists the program's run as February 1, 2026, to June 30, 2027; after June 30, 2027, this round ends, and money can run out sooner, so ask now.

  • Who qualifies, per the provider flyer LACAHSA posts: you "Must live in Los Angeles County and be in a rental situation," you "Must have experienced an emergency, crisis, or loss of income within the last 12 months that puts the household at risk of losing housing," and your income must be under these limits: 1 person $84,850; 2 people $96,950; 3 people $109,050; 4 people $121,150. "There will be a prioritization for people who are low-income."
  • What it can offer: temporary rental assistance, case management, other resources, and help relocating if you have to move.
  • Who to contact, by area (from the same flyer):
    • Metro LA (Downtown, Boyle Heights, Echo Park, Hollywood, Silver Lake, Westlake, and others) and the Antelope Valley: The People Concern, Tpcprevention@thepeopleconcern.org
    • West LA (Brentwood, Westwood, Venice, Culver City, Santa Monica, and others): St. Joseph Center, Sjcprevention@stjosephctr.org
    • South LA (Crenshaw, Watts, Hyde Park, Compton, South Gate, and others): HOPICS, Epp@hopics.org
    • San Fernando Valley (Van Nuys, Canoga Park, Sherman Oaks, Woodland Hills, and others): LA Family Housing, Referrals@lafh.org
    • East LA and the South Bay (including San Pedro and Harbor City): PATH, Pathprevention@epath.org
    • San Gabriel Valley: Union Station Homeless Services, Ushsprevention@ushs.org
  • Other funded groups: LACAHSA also funds the City of Los Angeles Community Investment for Families Department, CHIRLA, Better Angels, Interval House, Little Tokyo Service Center, SALVA, the Watts Labor Community Action Committee, and the County's Department of Consumer and Business Affairs. Their flyers are on LACAHSA's Get Help page.

2-1-1 and LAHSA's Homeless Prevention programs

LA County says: "If you are facing eviction today or have nowhere to sleep tonight, call or text 2-1-1." 211 LA says to dial 2-1-1 or 800-339-6993, "available 24/7," with "support in multiple languages." 2-1-1 is how you reach the Los Angeles Homeless Services Authority's (LAHSA) two prevention programs, which the County describes like this (checked November 3, 2026):

  • Homeless Prevention for Families "Provides short-term assistance for low-income families who are at-risk of homelessness." It is for families with children under 18 who are at risk of homelessness or fleeing domestic violence, with income at or below 50% of the area median (up to 80% in some subsidized housing).
  • Homeless Prevention for Adults and Youth "Provides short-term assistance for low-income participants who are imminently at risk of homelessness," with the same income limits.

LAHSA's own information line, for questions about services, is (213) 233-4798, Monday to Friday, 7:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.

DPSS help for CalWORKs families and General Relief

The County's Department of Public Social Services (DPSS) runs several programs that can pay back rent. All of them use one phone number: 1-866-613-3777.

  • Permanent Homeless Assistance Arrearages "Can pay up to two months of rental arrearages" for CalWORKs families facing eviction whose rent is no more than 80% of the family's income. The state says this help is for "eligible CalWORKs recipients or apparently eligible CalWORKs applicants, who are homeless or at risk of homelessness," and you apply with form CW-42.
  • Emergency Assistance to Prevent Eviction "Provides up to $5,000 to pay for late rent and/or utilities" for CalWORKs families who have used up, or don't qualify for, the arrearage payment, are in GAIN Welfare-to-Work or Post-Time Limit services, and can show financial hardship.
  • CalWORKs Diversion is a one-time payment for a specific need, "such as an eviction notice," for eligible applicants.
  • General Relief Housing Subsidy: for adults on General Relief who are homeless or at risk, and who are disabled and applying for SSI or employable and in the START program. The County lists $575 a month for one person or $1,150 for a couple, paid to the landlord.

Other doors for specific groups

  • People who get County mental health services: the Department of Mental Health's Housing Assistance Program offers deposits and "ongoing rental assistance" for people at risk of eviction who receive DMH services. Call 1-800-854-7771.
  • If you already have HACLA housing or a voucher and your income dropped, call HACLA at (833) 422-5248, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., and ask about a rent change. An eviction can cost you your assistance, so get legal help too.

Eviction help in Los Angeles: free lawyers and Right to Counsel

None of these pays rent. A lawyer can find mistakes in a notice or lawsuit, file your answer on time, and get you a payment plan or more time.

  • Stay Housed LA: a partnership of LA County, the City of Los Angeles, and legal aid groups that LAHD says "provides free legal assistance to tenants facing wrongful eviction." Apply online at stayhousedla.org/get-legal-help or call 1-888-694-0040. A County flyer says that since January 1, 2025, tenants who get an eviction lawsuit "may be eligible for free legal services through the County's Tenant Right to Counsel Program in partnership with Stay Housed LA."
  • City of LA Right to Counsel: LAHD says City tenants served with an eviction lawsuit (a Summons and Complaint for Unlawful Detainer) may qualify if they live in the City, have household income at or below 80% of the area median, do not live with the landlord, and "apply to SHLA within 30 days of receiving the UD (and not in default with the court)." It also says: "Regardless of household income, apply for legal help immediately after receiving a UD." City landlords must attach a Notice of Right to Counsel to any eviction notice.
  • Legal Aid Foundation of Los Angeles (LAFLA): call 800-399-4529, weekdays 9 a.m. to noon and 1 p.m. to 5 p.m., or apply online in English, Spanish, Korean, Chinese, or Vietnamese. Its eviction defense covers "Wrongful evictions, subsidized housing issues, slum conditions, rent control." LAFLA says it generally helps people with income below 125% of the federal poverty guidelines.
  • Neighborhood Legal Services of LA County (NLSLA): general legal help at 800-433-6251. NLSLA runs Self-Help Legal Access Centers in courthouses that help people "halting an eviction."
  • LAHD hotline (City of LA): 1-866-557-7368. Ask whether your notice was filed with LAHD and whether your unit is under the Rent Stabilization Ordinance or the Just Cause Ordinance.
  • Unincorporated LA County: the County's Department of Consumer and Business Affairs runs a tenant hotline, rent stabilization, and free landlord-tenant mediation at 1-800-593-8222, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.

California eviction timeline in Los Angeles: what happens and when

  • 1. The 3-day notice. The notice must state the amount due and who to pay, and you get three days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or court holidays (Code of Civil Procedure 1161). In the City of LA, an at-fault notice "must be filed with the Los Angeles Housing Department (LAHD) within three (3) business days of service," and a Notice of Right to Counsel must be attached. Now is the time to email your LACAHSA provider, call 2-1-1, and call Stay Housed LA.
  • 2. The lawsuit. If you do not pay or move, the landlord can file an unlawful detainer case. A notice alone does not end your tenancy.
  • 3. Answer within 10 court days. You have 10 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or court holidays, after you are served (Code of Civil Procedure 1167). The California Courts say "You must file your response papers within 10 days," and "If you didn't file any type of response, the judge can decide without you." LAHD gives the same warning for Right to Counsel: file an answer within 10 days to avoid a default.
  • 4. Trial and judgment. If you answered, the court sets a trial. A settlement can include a payment plan or a move-out date.
  • 5. Just cause. In the City of LA, most rentals need a legal reason to evict: RSO units, and, under the Just Cause Ordinance, most other units "at the end of their first lease, or 6 months after a new lease, whichever comes first." Elsewhere, California's Tenant Protection Act applies after a tenant "has continuously and lawfully occupied" a home for 12 months (Civil Code 1946.2). Unpaid rent is an at-fault just cause under both, so these rules matter most when a notice is for something else.
  • Lockouts are illegal. A landlord cannot change the locks, remove doors or windows, or shut off your utilities to force you out (Civil Code 789.3). Only the sheriff can remove you, after the court case.

The California Courts' eviction help for tenants explains each step and the forms. Our step-by-step guide to avoiding eviction covers the whole process, and understanding landlord notices explains the paper you got.

Help with utility bills in Los Angeles

  • LIHEAP through LADWP: LADWP says "LIHEAP can offer a benefit of up to $1,500" for heating or cooling bills and emergencies such as a "utility disconnection," once each program year. Anyone 18 or older in the household can apply. Apply through the local provider LADWP lists for your area: Maravilla (San Fernando Valley, Northeast LA, East LA) at 323-721-4162 or 800-906-4651; PACE (West, Central, and South LA) at 213-989-3177; or Long Beach Community Action Partnership (Harbor area) at 888-351-4061. The state warns that "Federal funding for LIHEAP is limited," so apply early.
  • LADWP EZ-SAVE: a discount for income-qualified customers. See LADWP's financial assistance programs, which also include Level Pay for past-due amounts.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The LA County Emergency Rent Relief Program is closed. Its 2026 round paid up to six months of past-due rent, up to $15,000 per unit, and its site now says "Application Period Closed" and "Submitted applications are in review" (checked November 3, 2026). If you applied, check your status in its portal or call (877) 849-0770, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. The pandemic-era City and County ERAP programs are also over.
  • HACLA Section 8 is not a quick fix. HACLA's page says "The Section 8 Waiting List is currently closed." HACLA's public housing list is open, but "HACLA is not accepting 1-bedroom applications for Public Housing at this time" (checked November 3, 2026). Our HACLA guide has the details.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Los Angeles right now?

Yes, but not one big open fund. The LA County Affordable Housing Solutions Agency (LACAHSA) says emergency rental assistance funds are now available through local nonprofits for LA County renters who had an emergency, crisis, or loss of income in the last 12 months; email the provider for your area. Call or text 2-1-1 (or 800-339-6993) to be screened for LAHSA's homelessness prevention programs. CalWORKs families can ask DPSS at 1-866-613-3777 about up to two months of back rent.

Is the LA County Emergency Rent Relief Program open?

No. Its website says "Application Period Closed," and applications already submitted are in review. The 2026 round paid up to six months of past-due rent, up to $15,000 per unit. If you applied, check your status in its portal or call (877) 849-0770, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Los Angeles?

Call Stay Housed LA at 1-888-694-0040 or apply on its website as soon as you get court papers. City of LA tenants with household income at or below 80% of the area median who do not live with their landlord may qualify for the City's Right to Counsel program if they apply within 30 days of being served and are not in default. You can also call the Legal Aid Foundation of Los Angeles at 800-399-4529 or Neighborhood Legal Services at 800-433-6251.

How long do I have to respond to an eviction lawsuit in California?

10 court days. Since January 1, 2025, California law gives a tenant 10 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, and court holidays, to file a response after the summons and complaint are served. If you do not file on time, the landlord can ask for a default judgment and the judge can decide without you.

Can I be evicted in the City of Los Angeles for owing a small amount of rent?

No. The LA Housing Department says a landlord may not evict a tenant who falls behind unless the tenant owes more than HUD's Fair Market Rent for the unit size. You still owe the rent. Call the LAHD hotline at 1-866-557-7368 to check a notice, and Stay Housed LA at 1-888-694-0040 for free legal help.

Does HACLA give emergency rent help?

No. HACLA runs Section 8 and public housing, not an emergency rent fund. Its Section 8 waiting list is closed, and its public housing list is open except for 1-bedroom applications. If you already have HACLA housing or a voucher and your income dropped, call HACLA at (833) 422-5248 right away and ask about a rent change.

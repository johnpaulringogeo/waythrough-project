Emergency Rent Help in Cleveland: Free Eviction Lawyers, PRC, 211, and Ohio's 3-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Cleveland or anywhere in Cuyahoga County and you are behind, the picture is thin. There is no open City of Cleveland or Cuyahoga County rent fund for everyone right now: the pandemic-era rent programs are over, and we found no newer City or County round on November 4, 2026. What is left is narrower. Families with children can apply to the County's Prevention, Retention and Contingency (PRC) program, and CHN Housing Partners' family program reopens in December. What Cleveland does have is strong legal help: a Right to Counsel for low-income families in Cleveland Housing Court, and a Pay to Stay law that lets City tenants use the rent they offered as a defense. Everything here was checked on November 4, 2026.

What to do first: If you have court papers or a 3-day notice, call Free Eviction Help at 216-861-5835 today. If you have a child at home, apply to Cuyahoga County PRC (information line (216) 987-7392). Dial 2-1-1 for whatever rent help has money this month. If your gas or electric is off or about to be, call Step Forward at (216) 480-HEAP (4327) for a Winter Crisis appointment. And if you get a summons, go to court: in Ohio the hearing can come about a week after you are served.

Ohio moves fast: a 3-day notice, then court about a week later

Before filing an eviction, an Ohio landlord must give you notice to leave "three or more days before beginning the action," and the notice must say: "You are being asked to leave the premises. If you do not leave, an eviction action may be initiated against you" (Ohio Revised Code 1923.04). The summons must be served "at least seven days before the day set for trial" (ORC 1923.06). Legal Aid's Free Eviction Help project explains that "The 3-day notice does NOT mean the tenant must move out of the property within 3 days." It means the landlord may be getting ready to file. Use those days to call for a lawyer and for rent help.

Free eviction lawyers in Cleveland

None of these pays rent. What a lawyer can do is catch mistakes in the landlord's papers, raise defenses like bad repairs or a refused payment, negotiate time or a payment plan, and connect you to rent money.

Free Eviction Help and Right to Counsel - Cleveland (RTC-C)

Free Eviction Help is the front door. In its words: "In Cuyahoga County, United Way and Legal Aid are working together to provide legal help to tenants facing eviction so those tenants may stay in their homes or locate better housing" (checked November 4, 2026).

  • How to ask: "If you think you qualify for help, contact The Legal Aid Society of Cleveland (Legal Aid) immediately at 216-861-5835." You can also apply online at freeevictionhelp.org or through United Way 2-1-1.
  • Who has a right to a lawyer: Right to Counsel - Cleveland "gives eligible low-income Cleveland tenants free legal representation in eviction hearings in Cleveland Housing Court. To qualify, a tenant's income must be at or below the federal poverty guideline, and the tenant must have at least one child in their household." The City Council ordinance behind it passed in October 2019, and the right began in July 2020.
  • If your court date is close: Free Eviction Help says you "still may be able to get legal help on the day of your eviction hearing. For Cleveland Housing Court, ask the Court at your hearing you would like a lawyer assigned to you." The court may postpone the hearing so you can meet your lawyer.
  • Rent money through RTC-C: the project says RTC-C connects families to rent help, including CHN Housing Partners' Family Stability Initiative and EDEN, "which supports RTC-C by providing rental assistance." EDEN's number on the Free Eviction Help page is 216-961-9690.

Legal Aid Society of Cleveland: everyone else who qualifies

  • What it does: Legal Aid serves low-income people in Cuyahoga, Ashtabula, Geauga, Lake and Lorain counties, and its services "are free for clients." Some tenants who do not qualify for RTC-C, including tenants without children and tenants in suburban courts, may still get a Legal Aid lawyer.
  • Apply by phone: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., and Thursday, 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. English 216-687-1900 or toll-free 888-817-3777; Spanish 216-586-3190. Other languages have their own lines on Legal Aid's Apply for Free Legal Aid page.
  • Apply online: the form "takes about 10 minutes to complete" and "is available in English and Spanish," but you must book a callback with an intake specialist to finish it. If no callback times are open, try again later or call.
  • Walk in: the Cleveland office at 1223 West Sixth Street takes applicants weekdays from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.
  • Quick questions: Legal Aid's Tenant Information Line is (216) 861-5955, according to Cleveland City Council.

Cleveland Housing Court

Cleveland evictions are heard by the Cleveland Municipal Court's Housing Division (Cleveland Housing Court), 1200 Ontario Street, Cleveland. The court says it is "one of two specialized housing courts in the state of Ohio," that "Housing Court Specialists are also available to provide help and information," and that first cause (eviction) hearings are returning to in-person under a new administrative order, so read your summons for where to go. Its Neighborhood Housing Court Kiosks at Cleveland Public Library branches, including Hough, Mt. Pleasant and South, let Clevelanders appear without going downtown, walk-in from 10 a.m. If you rent in a suburb, your case is in that city's municipal court instead.

Help paying rent in Cleveland: the doors that are left

There is no single rent office. Most of what is left is for families with children, and every program depends on limited money. Apply to more than one, and keep calling while you wait for a lawyer.

Cuyahoga County Prevention, Retention and Contingency (PRC)

  • What it is: the County says "If you have an emergency that is a one-time, short-term crisis, the Prevention, Retention, and Contingency (PRC) program may be able to help," including rental assistance, security deposits and utility assistance. "All payments are made directly to the vendor," meaning your landlord or utility.
  • Who qualifies: "You must live with at least one minor child, be pregnant, or be the non-custodial parent of a child," your family's income must be "at or below 200% of the Federal Poverty Level" ($5,500 a month for a family of four, per the County's table dated January 16, 2026), you must have limited liquid assets, and you must be a U.S. citizen or qualified alien.
  • How much: "There is a maximum issuance of $1,500 per year."
  • How to apply: fill out the PRC application from the County's PRC page and send it with proof of income, children and residency by email to Cuy-PRC-Application@jfs.ohio.gov, by fax to (216) 987-8655, or in a Neighborhood Family Service Center drop box. To get a paper application mailed, or to check your status, call (216) 987-7392 and leave a message; the County says to "allow 48 hours for a response." A decision comes "no later than 10 calendar days" after all your documents are in.

CHN Housing Partners: Family Stability Initiative (closed until December 7)

  • Who it helps: CHN says it can help "If you are a renter in Cuyahoga County" who has "suffered a hardship (e.g., loss of regular income or unexpected major expense)," is struggling with rent, utilities or a security deposit, and has a child under 18 or in grades K-12. Housing counseling is required, you must show you can keep paying rent afterward, and help is "available once every 24 months per household."
  • Status, in CHN's words: "This program is currently closed and will reopen on Monday, December 7 at 8 a.m." CHN adds that it "accepts a limited number of new applications" each month (checked November 4, 2026).
  • How to apply: online only, through the CHN Portal on CHN's Renter Supports page; "This program does not accept paper applications." Have photo IDs, Social Security cards, your children's birth certificates and school enrollment, 30 days of income and bank statements, last year's tax return and W-2, proof of the hardship, and your utility bills ready before 8 a.m. on December 7. CHN's main number is (216) 574-7100.

United Way 211

United Way of Greater Cleveland's 211 is "a free, confidential service that is the first point of contact for people in crisis" in seven Ohio counties, including Cuyahoga. It says "Every hour of every day, trained 211 Navigators connect individuals" to local services, including rental assistance. Dial 2-1-1 and ask for rent help, eviction prevention and utility help in your city, and write down every name and number you get.

Gas and electric: Step Forward and the Winter Crisis Program

Keeping the heat on frees money for rent. Since October 16, 2025, Step Forward has been the only provider of Ohio's energy assistance programs in Cuyahoga County, including HEAP, PIPP and the Winter and Summer Crisis programs; the Ohio Department of Development said CHN's energy clients moved to Step Forward that day. The state's HEAP Winter Crisis Program, run by the Ohio Department of Job and Family Services since April 6, 2026, "runs from Nov. 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027." It helps households at or below 175 percent of the federal poverty guidelines that are "facing disconnection, have been disconnected, need to establish new service, need to pay to transfer service, or have less than a 25 percent supply of bulk fuel." Step Forward says "All crisis assistance is by appointment only. Walk-in, in-person crisis assistance is not available." Call its 24/7 hotline at (216) 480-HEAP (4327) or book on its Utility Assistance page, and bring photo ID, proof of income, your utility bill or account number and Social Security numbers.

Ohio eviction timeline: what happens and when

Ohio evictions are "forcible entry and detainer" cases under Ohio Revised Code chapter 1923. In the City of Cleveland they go to Cleveland Housing Court; in the suburbs, to that city's municipal court. The steps:

  • 1. The notice. The landlord must deliver a notice to leave at least three days before filing, by certified mail, in person, or by leaving it at your home (ORC 1923.04). Ending a month-to-month lease for other reasons takes notice at least 30 days before the rent due date (ORC 5321.17).
  • 2. The summons. The clerk mails the summons and complaint, and it must be served "at least seven days before the day set for trial" (ORC 1923.06). The complaint often includes a second claim for back rent or damages; you have 28 days from service to answer that money claim (ORC 1923.06). The summons tells you, "You have the right to seek legal assistance."
  • 3. Court. Go. Bring your lease, receipts, texts, photos of repair problems, and proof of any rent you offered that the landlord refused. If you want more time, ask, but the law limits a continuance to eight days unless the landlord agrees or you post a bond to cover the rent (ORC 1923.08). If the case is about unpaid rent, you can raise counterclaims, and the court may order you to pay rent into court while the case is pending (ORC 1923.061).
  • 4. Paying in Cleveland: Pay to Stay. Inside the City of Cleveland, the Pay to Stay ordinance (Ord. 484-2022) lets you use the rent, late fees and court costs you tendered as a defense in a nonpayment case. The City says "if a tenant is summoned to court for an eviction, they can stop the proceeding if they have the full amount of rent owed, plus any late fees," and that a rental assistance voucher from an accredited agency that guarantees the past-due rent, reasonable late fees and court costs counts as payment. Legal Aid's Free Eviction Help FAQ says the ordinance caps late fees at "$25 or 5% of the monthly rent, whichever is larger." It does not apply to evictions for other reasons, and Ohio law gives no statewide right to pay and stay.
  • 5. After the judgment. If the landlord wins, the officer who gets the court's order to restore the landlord must carry it out "within ten days" (ORC 1923.14). Free Eviction Help says move-out time "generally ranges from 7-14 days," that Cleveland requires the landlord to hire licensed, bonded movers, and that most courts, including Cleveland, have the movers put your belongings on the tree lawn. Move what matters before that day.
  • Lockouts are illegal. No landlord may shut off utilities, lock you out, or threaten to, to get you out "other than as provided in Chapters 1923., 5303., and 5321." of the Revised Code, and a landlord who does is liable for your damages and attorney's fees (ORC 5321.15). Call Legal Aid if it happens.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The pandemic-era rent programs run through CHN and Cuyahoga County are over. The County's assistance page still describes its Rent Relief Fund as launched "to combat housing instability due to the pandemic," and the City of Cleveland's Rental Assistance Program page now shows "You are not authorized to access this page" (checked November 4, 2026). Ignore old flyers that send you there.
  • The Cuyahoga Metropolitan Housing Authority (CMHA) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its Housing Choice Voucher list is open all year ("Preliminary applications are accepted at any time!"), but picks are by lottery and "your name may come up within a few days, or it may take months" (checked November 4, 2026). See our CMHA Cleveland page. If you already have CMHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call CMHA at (216) 348-5000 right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Cleveland right now?

Only in small pieces. On November 4, 2026 we found no open City of Cleveland or Cuyahoga County rent fund for everyone. Families with a minor child, a pregnancy, or a non-custodial parent can apply to Cuyahoga County's Prevention, Retention and Contingency (PRC) program, which pays up to $1,500 a year to the landlord or utility. CHN Housing Partners' Family Stability Initiative helps renters with a child, but it says it is closed and will reopen on Monday, December 7 at 8 a.m. Everyone else should dial 2-1-1, and anyone with court papers should call Free Eviction Help at 216-861-5835 the same day.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Cleveland?

Call Free Eviction Help, run by the Legal Aid Society of Cleveland with United Way, at 216-861-5835, or apply online at freeevictionhelp.org. If you live in the City of Cleveland, have at least one child in your home, and your income is at or below the federal poverty guideline, you have a right to a free lawyer in Cleveland Housing Court through Right to Counsel - Cleveland (RTC-C). If you did not hear about it before your court date, you can ask the court at your hearing for a lawyer to be assigned to you. Other low-income tenants can still apply to Legal Aid.

Does Cleveland's Right to Counsel cover me if I have no children or live in a suburb?

Not as a right. Right to Counsel - Cleveland covers eviction cases in Cleveland Housing Court for tenants whose income is at or below the federal poverty guideline and who have at least one child in the household. Tenants without children, and tenants in Lakewood, Parma, Euclid or other suburbs with their own municipal courts, can still apply to the Legal Aid Society of Cleveland, which says some low-income tenants who do not qualify for RTC-C may qualify for a Legal Aid lawyer.

How fast is an eviction in Ohio?

Fast. The landlord must give a written notice to leave at least three days before filing (Ohio Revised Code 1923.04). The 3-day notice does not mean you must be out in 3 days; it means the landlord may file. The summons must be served at least seven days before the hearing (ORC 1923.06), so the first hearing can come about a week after you are served. A continuance longer than eight days needs the landlord's consent or a bond from you (ORC 1923.08). If the landlord wins, the court's bailiff schedules a supervised move-out.

Can I stop a Cleveland eviction by paying the rent?

Often, yes, if you rent inside the City of Cleveland and the case is only about unpaid rent. Cleveland's Pay to Stay ordinance (Ord. 484-2022) lets a tenant who tenders, or offers to pay, the rent owed plus late fees and court costs raise that as a defense. The City says a rental assistance voucher from an accredited agency that guarantees the past-due rent, reasonable late fees and court costs counts as payment. Keep proof of every payment you offered and every refusal. Pay to Stay is not a defense to evictions for other reasons, and Ohio state law has no general right to pay and stay.

Is the Cleveland Section 8 waiting list open?

Yes. The Cuyahoga Metropolitan Housing Authority (CMHA) says "Preliminary applications are accepted at any time!" and that its Housing Choice Voucher waiting list "will have no closing date." Selection is by lottery, so it can take days or months, and an application stays active for 18 months. It is not an emergency rent fund: a voucher will not arrive in time to stop an eviction.

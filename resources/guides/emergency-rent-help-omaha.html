Emergency Rent Help in Omaha: Who Still Pays, What Happened to MACCH, and the 7-Day Clock

Matt · Updated October 2026

If you rent in Omaha and you are behind, there is no single rent fund to apply to, and there is no MACCH rent application, online or anywhere else. MACCH, the Metro Area Continuum of Care for the Homeless, now calls itself Threshold Continuum of Care, and it tells renters who are still housed to call 211. The government door is Douglas County General Assistance, a "program of last resort" that pays landlords directly but only for very low incomes. The nonprofit doors are Together's walk-in Housing Stability Clinic, Heartland Family Service's Homeless Prevention program, and the St. Vincent de Paul helpline. ENCAP, which older lists still name, says it no longer gives emergency financial assistance. For eviction court, volunteer lawyers from the Tenant Assistance Project help tenants who show up for their hearing. Everything here was checked on October 16, 2026.

Here's the short version: Dial 2-1-1 (or 866-813-1731) and ask for rent help in Douglas County. If your household's monthly net income is $850 or less, call Douglas County General Assistance at (402) 444-6215. If you have some income, walk in to Together's Housing Stability Clinic at 2419 Leavenworth Street on a Monday, Tuesday, or Thursday between 10 a.m. and 2 p.m. If you could lose your home within 14 days, fill out Heartland Family Service's prevention form. Call Legal Aid of Nebraska's AccessLine at 1-877-250-2016. And if you get a court summons, go to your hearing: that is where the free lawyers are.

Nebraska's clock: 7 days to pay, then a trial 10 to 14 days after the summons

Under Neb. Rev. Stat. section 76-1431(2), if rent is unpaid the landlord can end your lease if you fail to pay "within seven calendar days after written notice by the landlord of nonpayment and his or her intention to terminate the rental agreement if the rent is not paid within that period of time." Paying the full rent inside those 7 days stops that notice. Once a case is filed, the trial is held "not less than ten nor more than fourteen days after the issuance of the summons" (section 76-1446). That is why every rent door below asks for your notice: by the time you have a court date, you may have only days left.

Help paying rent in Omaha: the doors that pay

Apply to more than one. Each runs on its own money, and none of them promises help.

Douglas County General Assistance: the County's last-resort fund

Nebraska law requires counties to help people who have nowhere else to turn, and Douglas County does it through its Department of General Assistance (DCGA). It calls its programs "programs of last resort." Its shelter assistance pays "rent, mortgage, utilities, and/or other fees required within the rental agreements," and "All General Assistance payments are made directly to the landlord and/or utility company" (checked October 16, 2026).

  • Who qualifies, in its words: "Assistance is limited to those persons in Douglas County between the ages of 19 and 64-67 (dependent upon when an individual is eligible for Social Security benefits) who are not eligible for other public assistance and/or do not have sufficient income or resources to meet all or part of their shelter needs." The income line is low: "Households with monthly net income that exceeds $850 are not eligible for assistance." Up to $50,000 of equity in a home and $12,000 in one vehicle do not count.
  • How long: if you can work, DCGA says you are "eligible for up to six (6) months of assistance annually," for "crisis stabilization due to an unforeseen event," and you must "show a change in their income, resources, and/or expenses and demonstrate an ability to sustain housing." If a verified disability keeps you from working for at least three months, help can continue while your Social Security application is pending.
  • What it will not pay: "Assistance for first month's rent, security deposits and/or application fees are not provided." You need current housing costs and "a copy of their current lease."
  • How to apply: call the appointment and screening line, (402) 444-6215. The County's page lists its hours as Tuesdays, Thursdays and Fridays in one place and Tuesdays, Wednesdays and Fridays in another, 8:30 a.m. to noon and 1 to 2:30 p.m. both times, so call on a Tuesday or Friday if you can. The front desk is (402) 444-7249. The office is at 1111 S. 41st Street, Suite 220, open weekdays 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., closed noon to 1 p.m. Start at the General Assistance website.

Together: the walk-in Housing Stability Clinic

Together (once called Together Omaha) runs a walk-in clinic for anyone in a housing crisis, from an eviction notice to a night outside. Its page says the clinic may offer "Rental or utility assistance when funding and eligibility allow," along with problem-solving, prevention, and Coordinated Entry connections. It is also honest about limits: "Visiting the Housing Stability Clinic does not guarantee financial assistance or placement into a housing program" (checked October 16, 2026).

  • Where and when: 2419 Leavenworth Street. "Walk-In Days: Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday. Walk-In Entry: 10:00 a.m.–2:00 p.m." Arrive inside that window. Threshold CoC's list of access points shows different days for the same address, so call Together at (402) 345-8047 before you go.
  • Who it helps with money: "Financial assistance is a case by case basis. Some funding sources may be based on income, and many other cases there must be at least some income in the household to qualify." On how long help lasts, it says: "Generally it is a one time assistance." You must show you can keep up after the help.
  • What to bring: photo ID, Social Security cards or other ID, your current lease, the eviction notice or court papers, any utility shutoff notice, proof of household income such as recent pay stubs or benefit letters.

Heartland Family Service: Homeless Prevention

Heartland Family Service's Homeless Prevention program is for people who are about to lose their home. Its rule is strict: you "Must be at imminent risk of homelessness (losing primary residence within the next 14 days)." It is mostly short-term case management, "for up to three months," and "Financial assistance to address barriers to housing stability is also available on a limited basis" (checked October 16, 2026). It serves Douglas and Sarpy counties and Pottawattamie County, Iowa, and there is no fee.

To see if you qualify, fill out the online eligibility form on its Homeless Prevention page or email HomelessPrevention@HeartlandFamilyService.org. The office is at the Benson Campus, 3300 N. 60th Street, Building C, (531) 200-3500, Monday through Thursday 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m. and Friday 8:30 a.m. to 2:30 p.m.

St. Vincent de Paul helpline

The Society of St. Vincent de Paul Omaha lists rent and utilities among the money it can help with. You apply through its online Helpline form, linked from its Get Help page. It says help "may vary, depending on available resources and geographic locations" within the metro, and to "allow 1-3 business days for a response."

211: the switchboard

Nebraska 211 is run by United Way of the Midlands. It is "Available 24/7": dial 2-1-1 or 866-813-1731, text your ZIP code to 898211, or search its directory online. It says callers who do not speak English are helped by bilingual staff or an interpreting service with "access to 240 languages." Ask for rent help in Douglas County and write down every name and number you get.

What happened to MACCH? (Threshold CoC and Coordinated Entry)

If you searched for "MACCH rental assistance," you will not find an application, because MACCH does not hand out rent. It coordinates the homeless-services network for Douglas, Sarpy, and Pottawattamie counties and now goes by Threshold Continuum of Care. Its resources page says: "If you or someone you know is currently housed and looking for supportive resources, please contact 211 to find a list of prevention resources available" (checked October 16, 2026).

If you have already lost your home, Threshold's "Coordinated Entry Access Point is the starting place to resolving a housing crisis." That is the door to rapid re-housing and other homeless housing programs. Omaha access points on its list include:

  • Siena Francis House, 1117 North 17th Street, open 24 hours, 7 days a week: (402) 341-1821
  • Stephen Center, 2723 Q Street, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. weekdays: (402) 731-0238
  • Together, 2419 Leavenworth Street (see the hours above): (402) 345-8047
  • Youth Emergency Services, for ages 10 to 25, 2602 Harney Street, 10 a.m. to 3 p.m. weekdays: (402) 502-1636
  • Nebraska/Western Iowa VA Community Resource and Referral Center, for veterans, 825 Dorcas Street, 8 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. weekdays: (402) 977-4444

Eviction help in Omaha: free lawyers

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can catch mistakes in the landlord's papers, ask for more time, and work out a deal to stay or move on your terms.

The Tenant Assistance Project (TAP), at the courthouse

The Nebraska State Bar Association's Volunteer Lawyers Project runs TAP in Omaha. In its words, TAP "mobilizes pro bono attorneys and law students to provide free legal services to unrepresented low-income tenants who appear for their eviction hearings at the Douglas and Lancaster County Courthouses" (checked October 16, 2026). There is no sign-up for tenants: the help is at your hearing, so go to court on the date on your summons and arrive early. The Volunteer Lawyers Project says it does not take requests for help by phone, email, or mail; its line, (402) 986-6501, press 3, plays a recorded message about services.

Legal Aid of Nebraska

  • How to apply: "No walk-ins. Please call our AccessLine or apply online." The statewide AccessLine is 1-877-250-2016, answered Monday and Wednesday 8:30 to 11:30 a.m. and Tuesday and Thursday 1 to 3 p.m. Central. If you are 60 or older, use the ElderAccessLine, 1-800-527-7249. You can also apply online through its LawHelpNE portal, linked from the Call for Help page. Interpreters are free.
  • What it does: Legal Aid's Housing Justice Project serves "low-income Nebraskans statewide," and it says "Providing legal assistance to prevent families from being evicted has long been a focus of Legal Aid." It also makes rental assistance referrals.
  • Do not walk in to the Omaha office. 209 S. 19th Street is Legal Aid's administrative office, and (402) 348-1069 is its administrative number, not the intake line.

Nebraska eviction timeline: what happens and when

Most Omaha rentals are covered by the Nebraska Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA), Neb. Rev. Stat. sections 76-1401 and following. Its own court process is in sections 76-1440 to 76-1447. These are the steps for a rent case, in order:

  • 1. The 7-day notice. The landlord gives written notice that rent is unpaid and that the lease will end if you do not pay within 7 calendar days. If you do not pay in that time, the landlord can end the lease (section 76-1431(2)).
  • 2. The court case. The landlord files a "complaint for restitution" with the county or district court clerk. It has to say which law it relies on and show that the landlord followed the URLTA notice rules (section 76-1441). In Omaha, eviction cases are civil cases in Douglas County Court, whose Civil and Small Claims Division is in the Douglas County Civic Center, 1819 Farnam Street, open 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. weekdays, (402) 444-5424.
  • 3. The summons. It must be served within 3 days of being issued, not counting days the court is closed, and it states the time and place of the trial (section 76-1442). If the landlord cannot serve you in person after real effort, it can post the summons on your front door and mail you a copy (section 76-1442.01), but then the court cannot give the landlord a money judgment in that case (section 76-1446).
  • 4. The trial, 10 to 14 days after the summons. A judge decides the case, with no jury (section 76-1446). You can answer and raise "any legal or equitable defense, setoff, or counterclaim" (section 76-1445). If you do not show up, the court tries the case as though you were there (section 76-1444), and you lose your chance to meet a TAP lawyer. The court can grant one continuance for good cause; if a later delay runs into the next rent period, it may make you pay that rent to the court clerk (section 76-1443).
  • 5. After a judgment. If you lose, the court can issue a writ of restitution telling the sheriff or constable to give the landlord possession "on a specified date not more than ten days after issuance of the writ" (section 76-1446). An appeal stops the writ only if you deposit the judgment and costs or post a bond, and then pay the monthly rent into court (section 76-1447).
  • Lockouts are illegal. If a landlord unlawfully removes or locks you out, or shuts off electric, gas, water, or other essential service, you can get back in or end the lease, and recover "an amount equal to three months' periodic rent" plus a reasonable attorney's fee (section 76-1430).
  • Not covered by URLTA? The older forcible entry and detainer law, sections 25-21,219 and following, says it does not apply to homes covered by URLTA. For the places it does cover, the landlord must give a 3-day notice to leave before filing (section 25-21,221), and the trial is also 10 to 14 days after the summons (section 25-21,223).

You can check Douglas County Court's daily schedule on the Nebraska court calendar. Our step-by-step guide to avoiding eviction covers the whole process, and understanding landlord notices explains the paper you got.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • ENCAP (Eastern Nebraska Community Action Partnership). Many older lists still send Omaha renters to ENCAP. Its own page now says: "We no longer provide emergency financial assistance" (checked October 16, 2026). It points people to Together, St. Vincent de Paul, and Douglas County General Assistance instead.
  • The City of Omaha. The City's only rent program for tenants is HOME Tenant-Based Rental Assistance, which the Omaha Housing Authority runs with City HOME funds and which "can provide up to 24 months of assistance." You cannot apply to it yourself: "All applicants for this program are to be referred to OHA by an existing transitional housing or support service program." If you are in such a program, ask your case manager about it.
  • The Omaha Housing Authority (OHA) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its homepage says "The Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) wait list is currently closed," and its public housing list is open only for one-bedroom senior tower apartments for people 62 and older (checked October 16, 2026). Main line: (402) 444-6900. Our OHA page has the details.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Omaha?

Yes, but it is limited and there is no single application. Dial 2-1-1 for the current list. If your household's monthly net income is $850 or less, Douglas County General Assistance can pay rent directly to your landlord; call (402) 444-6215. Together's walk-in Housing Stability Clinic at 2419 Leavenworth Street helps with rent when funding allows, and Heartland Family Service helps people who could lose their home within 14 days.

Can I apply for MACCH rental assistance online?

No. MACCH, the Metro Area Continuum of Care for the Homeless, is now called Threshold Continuum of Care, and it does not take rent applications. Its resources page tells renters who are still housed to contact 211 for a list of prevention resources. If you have already lost your home, go to one of its Coordinated Entry access points, such as Siena Francis House, open 24 hours.

Who qualifies for Douglas County General Assistance rent help?

Douglas County residents ages 19 to 64-67 who are not eligible for other public assistance and whose household monthly net income is not over $850. It pays rent, mortgage, or utilities straight to the landlord or utility, but not a first month's rent, deposits, or application fees. People who can work can get up to six months of help a year. Call the appointment line at (402) 444-6215.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Omaha?

Go to your eviction hearing. The Tenant Assistance Project sends volunteer lawyers and law students to help unrepresented low-income tenants who appear for their hearings at the Douglas County Courthouse. Before that, call Legal Aid of Nebraska's AccessLine at 1-877-250-2016, Monday and Wednesday 8:30 to 11:30 a.m. or Tuesday and Thursday 1 to 3 p.m., or apply online. Legal Aid does not take walk-ins.

How fast is an eviction in Nebraska?

Fast. Your landlord can end your lease if you do not pay within 7 days of a written notice. After the case is filed, the summons must be served within 3 days, and the trial is held 10 to 14 days after the summons is issued. If you lose, the sheriff can put the landlord back in possession within 10 days after the writ is issued.

Does the Omaha Housing Authority give emergency rent help?

No. OHA runs Section 8 and public housing, and its Section 8 waiting list is closed. Its public housing list is open only for one-bedroom senior tower apartments for people 62 and older. The City-funded rent program that OHA runs takes referrals only from transitional housing and support programs. If you already have OHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call OHA at (402) 444-6900 right away.

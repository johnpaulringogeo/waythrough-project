Emergency Rent Help in Wichita, KS: Center of Hope, Free Legal Aid, and Kansas's 3-Day Rule

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Wichita and you are behind, the pandemic rent money is gone and the City has no rent fund you can apply to directly. The main door for back rent is the Center of Hope, a nonprofit that pays emergency rent and utility bills after a phone interview on weekday mornings. Free legal help comes from Kansas Legal Services, and the Sedgwick County District Court runs an Eviction Resolution Program with mediation. Kansas LIEAP utility help opens November 30, 2026. And Kansas moves fast: 3 days after a written notice for unpaid rent, a court date as soon as 3 days after the summons, and 7 days to appeal a judgment. This guide covers Wichita and Sedgwick County. Everything here was checked on November 25, 2026.

First calls: The day you get a 3-day notice, call the Center of Hope at 316-267-0222 (9 a.m. to noon, weekdays) and Kansas Legal Services at 316-267-3975. Dial 2-1-1 for everything else. And go to court: if you miss the first court date, the landlord wins by default.

Kansas's clock in Wichita: 3 days, then court within 2 weeks

Under the Kansas Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, "The landlord may terminate the rental agreement if rent is unpaid when due and the tenant fails to pay rent within three days, after written notice by the landlord of nonpayment and such landlord's intention to terminate the rental agreement if the rent is not paid within such three-day period" (K.S.A. 58-2564(b)). The three days are "three consecutive 24-hour periods," with two more days if the notice was mailed. Once the landlord files, the summons sets your court date "not less than three nor more than 14 days after the date the summons is issued" (K.S.A. 61-3805). Use the 3 days: that is when the Center of Hope and a lawyer can still keep a case from being filed.

The main rent door: Center of Hope

The Center of Hope, 1100 E. 1st Street, Wichita, is a homeless prevention nonprofit. Its call-for-assistance line is 316-267-0222 (checked November 25, 2026).

  • What it is, in its own words: "The Center of Hope provides emergency rent and utility assistance to those in danger of being evicted from their homes or having their utilities shut off." It says assistance "is available to those residing in the Wichita area and all counties immediately surrounding Wichita," and that it helps about 4,000 households a year.
  • How to reach it: "We take calls from 9am to 12 noon Monday through Friday. Please note that at times the call volume is high but please keep calling as we would like to try to help you." It has English- and Spanish-speaking staff.
  • Have these ready for the phone interview: photo ID; Social Security cards for everyone in the household; your eviction notice and lease; all utility bills; rent receipts; proof of income for everyone in the household for the past 30 days; and your DCF Vision (EBT) card if you get cash assistance or food stamps.
  • What happens next: a volunteer fills out an intake sheet with you, then a case manager calls back to review it. The Center says "Assistance is typically provided immediately unless additional information is needed." See centerofhopeinc.org: Emergency Assistance.

Sedgwick County's Eviction Resolution Program

The 18th Judicial District Court runs an Eviction Resolution Program that "can assist landlords and tenants in Sedgwick County by providing information about the eviction court process and connecting individuals to services in the community that may be able to help," including mediation, "a chance for a tenant and landlord to work with a neutral party to reach an agreement." Fill out the tenant intake form on the court's Eviction Resolution Program page, or call 316-660-5659 if you cannot use the online form. The court also posts an eviction process flowchart in English and Spanish there.

Free legal help in Wichita

A lawyer cannot pay your rent, but can check whether the notice was done right, raise defenses such as rent the landlord refused or repairs that never got done, work out a deal, and make sure you get every day the law allows.

  • Kansas Legal Services (KLS), Wichita office: call 316-267-3975 or 1-800-723-6953 "to see what services you qualify for," or apply online at kansaslegalservices.org. You may be asked about your income.
  • Free advice at the courthouse: the court says KLS attorneys are at the Sedgwick County Courthouse, 525 N. Main, Wichita, KS 67203, in courtroom 5-5 on the 5th floor, on "Wednesdays: 8:30 a.m. - 4:00 p.m. (same day appointments only, made on a first come first served basis)."
  • Kansas Free Legal Answers: an online clinic where volunteer attorneys answer civil legal questions in writing, with an income screen. kansas.freelegalanswers.org
  • No right to counsel here. Kansas City, Missouri gives tenants a free eviction lawyer by city law. Wichita does not, so call KLS early, not the morning of court.

Help paying rent in Wichita: the other doors

United Way of the Plains 211

Dial 2-1-1 (Monday through Friday, 7 a.m. to 7 p.m., with translation), text your ZIP code to 898-211, or search 211Kansas.org. Rental assistance was one of 211's five most requested needs in 2025. The Kansas Housing Resources Corporation's county directory also sends Sedgwick County renters looking for homelessness prevention help to 211. Ask for rent and utility help in Sedgwick County and write down every name and number you get. United Way of the Plains: 211

The Salvation Army, Wichita Citadel Corps

The Salvation Army's Wichita Citadel Corps Community Center, 1739 S. Elpyco, lists "Utility, Mortgage, and Rent Assistance." Call 316-685-8699 and ask what is open; it posts no fixed hours online.

If you are already homeless

The City of Wichita's Rapid Re-Housing program pays rent and utilities so people who are homeless can move into permanent housing, by referral from agencies such as the Wichita Police Homeless Outreach Team, United Methodist Open Door, The Salvation Army, Catholic Charities and HumanKind Ministries. Catholic Charities says its Rapid Rehousing "does NOT pay past due utility or rental bills" and takes referrals only from homeless service providers; call 211 for one.

Utility bills: LIEAP, Evergy and the Cold Weather Rule

  • Kansas LIEAP, November 30, 2026 to March 31, 2027: the Kansas Department for Children and Families says "The 2027 LIEAP application period begins November 30, 2026, at 8 a.m. and ends March 31, 2027, at 5 p.m." Apply through the DCF Self-Service Portal, or ask for a paper application at a DCF Service Center or at 1-888-369-4777. An adult at the address must pay the heating costs, and gross household income must be at or below 150% of the poverty level, for example $1,995 a month for one person or $4,125 for four. It is a one-time benefit per year.
  • In-person LIEAP help in Wichita: DCF lists application events at the Wichita DCF Office, 2601 S. Oliver St., on November 30, 2026 (8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.), and at the Evergreen Branch Library, 2601 N. Arkansas Ave., on December 17, 2026 (9 a.m. to 1 p.m.), with more Wichita dates in January and February. Bring proof of income for everyone, all fuel bills and your lease.
  • Project DESERVE (Evergy): the Center of Hope runs this electric bill help for "Evergy customers with active service established in their name." It takes applications daily and pays at the end of each week. centerofhopeinc.org: Project DESERVE
  • The Cold Weather Rule, November 1 to March 31: the Kansas Corporation Commission says it "helps ensure you will have electric, gas, and water service for your home during the winter," but "You must make pay arrangements with your utility." You can pay 1/12 of the past-due amount plus 1/12 of the current bill and fees, then spread the rest over 11 months. A regulated utility cannot disconnect you when the temperature is forecast to drop below 35 degrees within the next 48 hours. Questions: KCC at 1-800-662-0027.

Kansas eviction timeline in Wichita: what happens and when

In Wichita, an eviction is filed in the Sedgwick County District Court (18th Judicial District), at the Sedgwick County Courthouse, 525 N. Main. These are the steps for unpaid rent:

  • 1. The notice. The landlord gives written notice that the lease will end if you do not pay within three days, counted as three full 24-hour periods from delivery or posting, or five days if mailed (K.S.A. 58-2564(b)). For other lease violations, the notice gives you 14 days to fix the problem and ends the lease no sooner than 30 days after you get it; a second similar violation does not get a second chance (K.S.A. 58-2564(a)). A month-to-month tenancy takes 30 days' written notice (K.S.A. 58-2570). Call the Center of Hope and KLS now. Kansas Legal Services says rent counts as paid when you mail it, stamped and addressed to the landlord, so keep proof of the date.
  • 2. The summons and petition. They may come by mail, from the sheriff, or taped to your door. The court date on the summons will be 3 to 14 days after it was issued (K.S.A. 61-3805). You can appear in person or file a written answer on or before that date (K.S.A. 61-3806).
  • 3. Court. Go. "IF you do not go to court on this first date, the landlord automatically wins (called default)," Kansas Legal Services says. In cases under the landlord-tenant act, the court "shall consider mediation," and if a trial is needed, it must be held within 14 days of the first court date (K.S.A. 61-3806, 61-3807). Bring your lease, receipts, texts, photos of repair problems, and anything showing the landlord took or refused your rent. If the Center of Hope approved help, bring that proof.
  • 4. The 7 days after judgment. To appeal the part of the judgment that gives the landlord the home, "notice of appeal shall be filed within seven days after entry of judgment" (K.S.A. 61-3902). An appeal does not stop the eviction unless you file a supersedeas bond (K.S.A. 61-3904). KLS warns that after a judgment you may get "possibly as little as 24 hours" to move out.
  • 5. The writ and the sheriff. At the landlord's request, the court issues a writ of restitution, and the officer named in it must carry it out "within 14 days after the person named in the writ receives it" (K.S.A. 61-3808). Move your things before then if you can. Belongings left behind can be stored, sold or thrown away after 30 days, with notice published in the newspaper.
  • Lockouts and shutoffs are illegal. If a landlord "unlawfully removes or excludes the tenant from the premises or willfully diminishes services to the tenant by interrupting or causing the interruption of electric, gas, water or other essential service," you can get back in or end the lease and recover up to one and a half months' rent or your damages, whichever is more (K.S.A. 58-2563). The City of Wichita says a tenant can also call the police and file a report. Call KLS.
  • Retaliation. A landlord may not raise rent or cut services because you complained to a code agency or the landlord about repairs, or joined a tenant union, though that protection does not apply if you are behind on rent (K.S.A. 58-2572). Wichita's own retaliatory eviction ordinance, passed January 2, 2024, takes signed, notarized complaints at the City Clerk's Office, 455 N. Main, 13th Floor, or RetaliatoryEvictions@wichita.gov.

Security deposits: for an unfurnished home, a landlord may not charge more than one month's rent (more is allowed for furniture or pets). If the landlord keeps part of it, the itemized balance is due "within 14 days after the determination of the amount" of the charges "but in no event to exceed 30 days after termination of the tenancy, delivery of possession and demand by the tenant." A landlord who does not follow the rule owes the amount due plus one and a half times what was wrongly kept (K.S.A. 58-2550).

Clearing an old eviction: under K.S.A. 61-3809 you can ask the court, at no cost, to expunge an eviction case that was dismissed, or a judgment once three years have passed with no new eviction judgment and nothing owed on it. The Kansas courts post the forms at srl.kscourts.gov.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The pandemic rent programs. The Kansas Housing Resources Corporation no longer lists emergency rental assistance among its renter programs, and the City of Wichita's old Housing Stability page now returns "page not found." Some sites still link to them. They are over.
  • The Wichita Housing Authority (WHA) runs Section 8 and public housing, not emergency rent. Its Section 8 list is open: the City says "Preliminary applications are being accepted online," and the portal shows the list open since July 22, 2020 (checked November 25, 2026). That is a long-term door, not this month's rent. See our WHA guide. If you already have WHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call 316-462-3700 right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • A local voucher-discrimination rule. Neither Kansas law nor Wichita's Non-Discrimination Ordinance protects source of income, and Senate Bill 391, passed over the Governor's veto in 2026, bars Kansas cities and counties from requiring landlords to accept vouchers. So a Wichita landlord can legally refuse a voucher. Refusals because of race, disability, family status or another protected trait are still illegal, and Wichita's ordinance adds sexual orientation, gender identity, age and veteran status; complaints go to the City Clerk within 180 days.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1. If you are not safe at home, call the Wichita Family Crisis Center's 24-hour line at 316-267-7233.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Wichita right now?

Yes, through the Center of Hope, which pays emergency rent and utility bills for people in danger of eviction or a shutoff in the Wichita area and the counties around it. Call 316-267-0222 between 9 a.m. and noon, Monday through Friday, and keep calling if the line is busy. The City of Wichita has no rent fund you apply to directly; dial 211 for other agencies.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Wichita?

Call Kansas Legal Services at 316-267-3975 or 1-800-723-6953, or apply online. On Wednesdays from 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m., its attorneys give free advice at the Sedgwick County Courthouse, 525 N. Main, courtroom 5-5, same day and first come, first served. Wichita has no right-to-counsel law, so call as soon as you get a notice.

How much notice does a Wichita landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

Three days. Under Kansas law, the landlord can end the lease if you do not pay within three days after a written notice, counted as three consecutive 24-hour periods, with two extra days if the notice was mailed. For other lease violations you get 14 days to fix the problem, and a month-to-month tenancy takes 30 days' written notice.

How long do I have after an eviction judgment in Wichita?

Seven days to file a notice of appeal on the move-out order, and an appeal does not stop the eviction unless you post a bond. The officer must carry out the writ of restitution within 14 days of receiving it, and Kansas Legal Services warns you may get as little as 24 hours to move. Get help before your court date, not after.

Where do I get help with my utility bill in Wichita?

Kansas LIEAP takes applications from November 30, 2026 at 8 a.m. through March 31, 2027 at 5 p.m., online through the DCF Self-Service Portal, for households at or below 150% of the poverty level. Evergy customers can also apply to the Center of Hope's Project DESERVE. From November 1 to March 31, the Cold Weather Rule lets you spread a past-due gas or electric bill over 12 months.

Can my Wichita landlord lock me out or cut off my utilities?

No. Kansas law lets you get back in, or end the lease, and recover up to one and a half months' rent or your actual damages, whichever is more, if a landlord locks you out or cuts off electric, gas, water or another essential service. The City of Wichita says you can also call the police and file a report. Only the sheriff, after a court judgment, can remove you.

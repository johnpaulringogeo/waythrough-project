Emergency Rent Help in New Orleans: Free Right to Counsel Lawyers, VIA LINK 211, and Louisiana's Fast Eviction Clock

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in New Orleans and you are behind, start with the bad news: the City's rent and utility money is gone. Its Office of Community Development says the funding "has now been depleted, and the program has ended." What New Orleans does have is a Right to Counsel law: since 2022, tenants facing eviction in the city can ask for a free lawyer, and coverage starts as soon as you get a notice. Rent money now comes from scattered nonprofits, and the way to find them is VIA LINK 211. Utility help comes through LIHEAP, Entergy and the Sewerage & Water Board. This guide covers Orleans Parish. Everything here was checked on November 5, 2026.

What to do now: If you got a notice to vacate or court papers, call Southeast Louisiana Legal Services at (504) 529-1000 or the Louisiana Fair Housing Action Center at (504) 596-2100 the same day. Dial 2-1-1 (or 504-269-2673) and ask for rent help. And go to your hearing: in Louisiana it can come three days after you are served, and you can be put out 24 hours after a judgment.

Louisiana moves fast, and your lease may skip the notice

Under Code of Civil Procedure art. 4701, the landlord must deliver a written notice to vacate that allows "not less than five days from the date of its delivery." But "A lessee may waive the notice requirements of this Article by written waiver contained in the lease," and many leases do. After that, the court sets the hearing "not earlier than the third day after service" (art. 4732). If you lose and do not leave "within twenty-four hours after its rendition," the court issues a warrant to the constable or marshal (art. 4733). Under art. 5059, legal holidays are not counted in a period shorter than seven days, so ask a lawyer to count your days. Call for a lawyer the day you get any notice.

Free eviction lawyers: Right to Counsel in New Orleans

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can catch mistakes in the notice or the court papers, raise defenses like bad repairs or a waiver that does not apply, negotiate time to move or a payment plan, and protect your Section 8 or public housing.

What the law says

The City Code's Tenant Eviction Assistance Program and Right to Counsel (Chapter 50, Article V, adopted by Ord. No. 29019 M.C.S. on May 5, 2022) creates a program "to provide legal counsel as a matter of right to residential tenants facing eviction proceedings within the City of New Orleans." A City planning report on nola.gov says the Council "unanimously passed a law guaranteeing the right to counsel to unrepresented persons facing eviction" (checked November 5, 2026).

  • What it covers: "Any legal or administrative proceeding to evict or otherwise terminate the tenancy or housing subsidy of a residential tenant living in New Orleans," plus court cases to stop an illegal eviction and appeals "where there exists good grounds for an appeal."
  • When it starts: "an eviction proceeding shall be deemed to commence when a tenant receives notice of eviction, termination of tenancy, or lease non-renewal." You do not have to wait for court papers.
  • Income: the Code text sets no income limit, but it says "Participation may be subject to the availability of funding."

Who to call

  • Southeast Louisiana Legal Services (SLLS): the City's community development page says "If you are facing eviction and need legal representation, please contact Southeast Louisiana Legal Services at (504) 529-1000." The New Orleans office is at 1340 Poydras, Suite 600; you can also call 1-844-244-7871. SLLS says "The best way to get help is to apply online, but don't do this if it's an emergency," and "If your legal issue is an emergency, the best way to get help is to call us at the office closest to you." If you leave a message, "Someone will get back to you within two business days." SLLS also lists an eviction desk at First City Court "Any day there is an eviction docket." slls.org
  • Louisiana Fair Housing Action Center (LaFHAC): "LaFHAC offers free legal representation to people facing eviction in Orleans Parish." Call (504) 596-2100 or (877) 445-2100, "Monday - Friday 9 am - 5 pm." lafairhousing.org
  • The Civil District Court's website lists both: "Contact SLLS at (504)529-1000 ext. 223 or LaFHAC at (504) 596-2100 or (877) 445-2100."

Help paying rent in New Orleans: the doors that are left

There is no City rent office to walk into right now. Use 211 to find who has money this month, and keep calling while you talk to a lawyer. None of these programs promises help.

VIA LINK 211

VIA LINK runs 211 for Orleans and nine nearby parishes. "Call 2-1-1, 504-269-2673 or 800-749-2673," or "text your zip code to 898211." It says "We are here 24/7," "in over 150 languages." Ask for rent and utility help in Orleans Parish and write down every name and number. You can also search its directory at resources.vialink.org.

Homeless prevention and rapid rehousing

The City's Health Department says "Rapid Rehousing provides short term rental assistance," but "There are limited resources for housing subsidies through homeless programs." If you are about to lose your home, ask 211 for a homeless prevention referral, or call UNITY of Greater New Orleans at 504-570-9812. NOLA 311 is (504) 539-3266.

Utility bills: LIHEAP, Entergy and the Sewerage & Water Board

  • LIHEAP (Total Community Action): in Orleans Parish, LIHEAP goes through Total Community Action. Its page says to pick up an application packet from the box outside 1410 S. Norman C. Francis Parkway and return it inside, and that TCA "is not accepting any applications via e-mail or over the phone." Office hours are "8:00 am – 4:30 pm on Monday – Thursday. 8:00 am – 4:00 pm on Friday." Questions: 504-875-2660. The Louisiana Housing Corporation lists the seasons as "Heating: December 15 – March 31" and "Crisis: October 1 - September 30," with income limits of 60% of state median income ($58,882 a year for a family of four in 2026). So the heating season opens December 15, 2026 and ends March 31, 2027; after that, ask TCA what help is open. Crisis help is available all year while funds last.
  • Entergy New Orleans: a deferred payment arrangement for "Qualifying customers who are experiencing financial hardship," with "up to four months to pay their current bill and/or unpaid balances," and payment extensions: pay by the new due date and "you will be protected from disconnection of service." Ask through myEntergy or the Entergy app, or call 1-800-368-3749. Entergy New Orleans help paying
  • The Power to Care: Entergy's fund for "customers who are 60 years of age or older or have disabilities," handled in New Orleans by the New Orleans Council on Aging: "please call (504) 821-4121." NOCOA says "The number of individuals who can be assisted is limited by the funding received."
  • Sewerage & Water Board (SWBNO): interest-free payment plans for a "past-due balance of $50 or more," and a Water Help Program that "contributes up to $200 annually" for customers who are at least 62 or have a disability, with applications in person at Total Community Action, (504) 324-8609. SWBNO's page was last updated in 2024, so call 504-529-2837 to confirm.

Louisiana eviction timeline: what happens and when

Evictions on the east bank of New Orleans go to First City Court, 421 Loyola Avenue, Room 201 (504-407-0400, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m.). The court says "Evictions in Algiers, zip codes 70114 and 70131, are processed at Second City Court," 225 Morgan Street, Room 206, (504) 407-0435. These are the steps:

  • 1. The notice to vacate. At least 5 days from delivery, for nonpayment or any other reason, unless your lease waives it (art. 4701). For a lease with a set end date, the notice can come up to 30 days before it ends. Ask for a Right to Counsel lawyer now.
  • 2. The rule to show cause. If you do not leave, the landlord files a "rule to show cause" in court, which "shall state the grounds upon which eviction is sought" (art. 4731). You are served with the hearing date.
  • 3. The hearing. Go. The court sets it "not earlier than the third day after service" and hears "any defense which is made." If you do not answer or appear, the court rules for the landlord (art. 4732). Bring your lease, receipts, texts, photos of repair problems, and any proof the landlord took rent after the notice. If you have a defense, answer under oath; you need that to appeal.
  • 4. Judgment and 24 hours. If you do not leave "within twenty-four hours" of the judgment, the court issues a warrant to the constable or marshal (art. 4733), who clears the home in front of two witnesses (art. 4734).
  • 5. Appeal. An appeal stops the eviction only if you answered under oath with a defense that would let you stay, and you apply for the appeal and file the bond "within twenty-four hours after the rendition of the judgment of eviction" (art. 4735). This takes a lawyer.
  • Only the court can put you out. Apart from a home the landlord reasonably believes was abandoned after notice (art. 4731(B)), possession is delivered by the constable or marshal under a court warrant. If a landlord locks you out or shuts off your utilities, call SLLS or LaFHAC; Right to Counsel covers court cases to stop an illegal eviction.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The City of New Orleans Emergency Rental Assistance Program. The City's page says the federal money "has been depleted," "both the Pandemic and program have ended," and "we are unable to provide direct assistance to residents." Some court and older pages still describe it; they are out of date.
  • Louisiana's statewide rent program. The state says "The application period for the program closed on December 21, 2022." The Louisiana Housing Corporation's rental page lists no emergency rent program now.
  • The Housing Authority of New Orleans (HANO) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund, its voucher list is closed with "no future reopen date," and its portal says "There are currently no waiting lists open" (checked November 5, 2026). See our HANO guide. If you already have HANO housing or a voucher and fall behind, call (504) 670-3300 right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • A voucher-discrimination complaint. Neither Louisiana law nor the City's fair housing code protects source of income, so a landlord can legally turn down a voucher. Refusals based on race, disability, family status or another protected trait are still illegal.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in New Orleans right now?

Not from the City. The Office of Community Development says its rent and utility funding "has now been depleted, and the program has ended," and Louisiana's pandemic rent program closed to applications on December 21, 2022. Dial VIA LINK 211, or 504-269-2673, and ask who has rent money this month. If you have a notice to vacate or court papers, ask for a free Right to Counsel lawyer the same day. Checked November 5, 2026.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in New Orleans?

Call Southeast Louisiana Legal Services at (504) 529-1000, or apply online from slls.org if it is not an emergency, or call the Louisiana Fair Housing Action Center at (504) 596-2100 or (877) 445-2100, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. The Civil District Court's website names these two groups for free help under the City's Right to Counsel law. SLLS also staffs an eviction desk at First City Court on eviction docket days.

Who is covered by the New Orleans Right to Counsel law?

The City Code covers residential tenants facing eviction in New Orleans, including cases that would end a housing subsidy such as Section 8, appeals with good grounds, and court cases to stop an illegal eviction. Coverage starts when you receive a notice of eviction, termination of tenancy, or lease non-renewal, so you do not have to wait for court papers. The Code sets no income limit, but it says participation may depend on available funding.

How fast is an eviction in Louisiana?

Very fast. The landlord must give a written notice to vacate of at least 5 days, unless your lease waives it. Then the court can hold the hearing as early as the third day after you are served. If the landlord wins and you have not left within 24 hours, the court issues a warrant to the constable or marshal. To stop the eviction with an appeal, you must have answered under oath with a defense and file the appeal and bond within 24 hours of the judgment.

Can my lease waive the 5-day notice to vacate?

Yes. Louisiana law says a tenant may waive the notice to vacate in a written lease, and then the landlord can file in court as soon as your right to stay ends. Many New Orleans leases include this waiver, so read yours. Even with a waiver, the landlord still needs a court judgment, and only the constable or marshal can put you out. Under the City's Right to Counsel law, you can ask for a lawyer as soon as you get any notice.

Is the New Orleans Section 8 waiting list open?

No. On November 5, 2026, HANO's voucher page said the list is closed and "no future reopen date has been determined," and its applicant portal said "There are currently no waiting lists open." HANO last took voucher applications in November 2022. Section 8 is not a fast fix for back rent either way. If you already have a HANO voucher or public housing and your income dropped, ask HANO about a rent change right away.

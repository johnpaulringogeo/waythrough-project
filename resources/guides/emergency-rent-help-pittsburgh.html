Emergency Rent Help in Pittsburgh: Eviction Rent Help, Free Legal Aid, and Pennsylvania's 10-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Pittsburgh and you are behind, here is the honest picture. We found no open City of Pittsburgh or Allegheny County rent fund that you can apply to before a court case. The main rent program, the Allegheny Housing Stabilization Collaborative run by ACTION-Housing, helps renters who already have an eviction case or a signed mediation agreement. Free legal help comes from Neighborhood Legal Services, and the way into everything else is PA 211. Everything here was checked on November 5, 2026.

Here's the short version: Dial 2-1-1 (or text your ZIP code to 898-211) and ask for rent help. If you get a notice to quit, call Neighborhood Legal Services at 1-866-761-6572 the same day. If your landlord files in court, call ACTION-Housing's hotline at 412-248-0021 for rent help. And go to your hearing: in a case about unpaid rent, paying the rent owed and the costs can stop the eviction up until it is carried out.

Pennsylvania's clock: a 10-day notice, then court within about two weeks

For unpaid rent, Pennsylvania's Landlord and Tenant Act says the notice to quit "shall specify that the tenant shall remove within ten days from the date of the service thereof" (68 P.S. 250.501). But "The notice above provided for may be for a lesser time or may be waived by the tenant if the lease so provides," so read your lease. After the landlord files, court rules set the hearing "not less than seven or more than fifteen days from the date the complaint is filed" (Pa.R.Civ.P.M.D.J. 504). The good news: in a case only about unpaid rent, "At any time before any writ of possession is actually executed," you can "supersede and render the writ of no effect by paying to the writ server, constable or sheriff the rent actually in arrears and the costs" (68 P.S. 250.503(c)).

Rent help once an eviction is filed: ACTION-Housing

The Allegheny Housing Stabilization Collaborative (AHSC) is run by ACTION-Housing with partner groups. It lists three services: "Rental Assistance," "Landlord-Tenant Mediation," and "Legal Support" (checked November 5, 2026).

  • Who qualifies, in ACTION-Housing's words: you may be eligible if you "Have an active eviction case filed in the Magisterial District Courts or Allegheny County Court of Common Pleas" or "Have a completed Mediation Agreement with your landlord through Just Mediation Pittsburgh." You will need proof of income, your eviction docket and your lease.
  • How to apply: "Call the hotline at 412-248-0021" (412-248-0021) or email rentalassistance@actionhousing.org. A partner agency then calls you to review your case. The County's January 2026 guide says to leave a voicemail and that "A staff member will return your call within 10-14 business days," so call the day the case is filed.
  • City or county? ACTION-Housing's page speaks to anyone "facing eviction in Allegheny County," but the County's own resource page lists AHSC for renters "within the City of Pittsburgh" with an eviction filed, and sends others to 211 and Just Mediation. Ask when you call.
  • What to know: "Applying for services does not guarantee approval," and services depend on "available funding." The County adds: "If you have previously received rental assistance you may not be eligible for AHSC funds." ACTION-Housing's walk-in center closed for good on June 30, 2026; the phone and email still work. See actionhousing.org.

Before a case is filed: Just Mediation Pittsburgh

If your landlord has not filed yet, call Just Mediation Pittsburgh at (412) 228-0730 to set up a free mediation. Your landlord has to agree to take part, and the County notes that "Just Mediation Pittsburgh does not provide rental assistance." A finished mediation agreement can make you eligible for AHSC rent help. Without a court filing or an agreement, the County's guide is blunt: "You are not eligible for specific funds to assist with your rent as this time, so you should contact PA Southwest 2-1-1."

Free legal help in Pittsburgh

A lawyer cannot pay your rent, but can check the landlord's papers, raise defenses like bad repairs, and make sure you get every day the law allows.

  • Neighborhood Legal Services (NLS), 928 Penn Avenue, Pittsburgh, PA 15222, lists "Landlord-Tenant Defense" among its services. Request help at 1-866-761-6572 (toll free) or online at nlsa.us. Allegheny County's resource guide also gives 412-255-6700 for NLS when an eviction has been filed, "if you meet financial eligibility guidelines."
  • Pittsburgh's eviction-prevention money. City Code § 237.06 says that part of the Stop the Violence Fund "may be used for eviction prevention for tenants at or below eighty percent (80%) of the area median income," including mediation, help finding rental assistance, and that "Free legal representation shall be offered to tenants who are facing eviction proceedings in court." The City does not post a separate sign-up line for it. In practice, ask for legal help when you call AHSC or NLS.
  • Housing Court Help Desk (Allegheny County Court of Common Pleas), City-County Building, 414 Grant St., 1st Floor, (412) 350-4462. It handles landlord-tenant appeals. Hours are Monday to Thursday 8:30 am to 4:00 pm and Friday 8:30 am to 3:00 pm, and the court says "You must arrive one hour before closing if you need to fill-out and file paperwork."

Help paying rent in Pittsburgh: the other doors

PA 211 Southwest

Dial 2-1-1 or text your ZIP code to 898-211. It is free and open 24/7. Ask for rent and utility help in your neighborhood and write down every name and number you get. You can also search at pa211.org.

Allegheny Link: if you could lose your home

Allegheny County says "Allegheny Link is the place to call for housing assistance in Allegheny County." Call 866-730-2368, Monday to Friday, 8:00 am to 5:00 pm, or walk in Monday to Friday, 8:00 am to 4:00 pm, at the Human Services Building, One Smithfield Street, First Floor. The Link handles shelter and housing referrals; it is not a rent fund.

Utility bills: LIHEAP, Dollar Energy Fund and your utility

  • LIHEAP (Pennsylvania Department of Human Services): "The season runs November 2, 2026 through April 23, 2027." Cash grants range "from $200 to $1,000" and go straight to your utility or fuel company. Crisis grants for a shutoff notice or running out of fuel are paid "within 10 business days of applying." Apply online on COMPASS or on paper at the Allegheny County Assistance Office. The income limit is $23,940 a year for one person and $49,500 for four. Hotline: 1-866-857-7095. After April 23, 2027, LIHEAP closes until next season; ask 211 and your utility about their own programs instead.
  • Dollar Energy Fund: its site says it "Opens 2026–2027 Hardship Program for Households Facing a Utility Crisis," and help is given on a "first come, first served basis while funding is available." Apply online at myappconnect.com or through a partner agency, or call 1-888-282-6816.
  • Duquesne Light: its Customer Assistance Program (CAP) offers "affordable monthly payments, debt forgiveness and, if needed, a security deposit waiver" for customers with "a household income at or below 150% of the federal income poverty guidelines." It also offers payment arrangements. See duquesnelight.com.
  • Peoples Gas: lists CAP, Dollar Energy Fund grants, LIHEAP and other help. To ask, "call 1-800-400-WARM (9276)."

Pennsylvania eviction timeline: what happens and when

In Allegheny County, evictions start at your neighborhood's Magisterial District Court. Appeals go to the Court of Common Pleas. These are the steps for a case about unpaid rent:

  • 1. The notice to quit. For unpaid rent, the notice gives you 10 days to leave. To end a lease of one year or less (or a month-to-month lease) it is 15 days, and for a lease of more than one year, 30 days. A written lease can shorten or waive the notice (68 P.S. 250.501). The notice can be handed to you or posted on the property. Call NLS now, while you still have time.
  • 2. The complaint and hearing. The landlord files with the Magisterial District Judge. The hearing is set 7 to 15 days after filing (Rule 504). Call ACTION-Housing at 412-248-0021 as soon as you are served.
  • 3. Court. Go. If you do not show up, the judge can rule against you. Bring your lease, receipts, texts, photos of repair problems, and any proof the landlord refused your rent.
  • 4. Appeal: 10 days. You can appeal a judgment for possession "within 10 days after the date of the entry of judgment" (Rule 1002), at the Housing Court Help Desk. To stay in your home during the appeal, you must deposit rent with the court: the lesser of three months' rent or the rent in arrears, and then rent every 30 days (Rule 1008). A tenant with low income can file an affidavit and start with one-third of a month's rent. Miss a payment and the landlord can ask to end the stay.
  • 5. Order for possession. If you do not appeal, the landlord can ask for an order for possession "after the 10th day" following the judgment (Rule 515). It is served on you, and the constable or sheriff can remove you "on or after the 11th day following the service of the order" (Rule 519). Move your belongings before that day if you can.
  • 6. Paying to stop it. In a case only about unpaid rent, you can stop the eviction at any time before it is carried out by paying the officer the rent in arrears and the costs. The officer must give you "a signed receipt for any such payment" (68 P.S. 250.503(c); Rule 518). This is where AHSC money or another agency's check can save the home.
  • Only the court process can put you out. Possession is delivered by a constable or sheriff with a court order (Rule 519). If your landlord changes the locks or shuts off your utilities on their own, call NLS and the police.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • A City of Pittsburgh or Allegheny County rent fund you can apply to on your own. We found none open on November 5, 2026. The pandemic-era emergency rental assistance has ended; ignore old flyers and websites that still advertise it.
  • The Housing Authority of the City of Pittsburgh (HACP) runs Section 8 and public housing, not emergency rent. Its page says "The Housing Authority of the City of Pittsburgh Housing Choice Voucher Program Waiting List is now CLOSED," although some public housing and project-based lists are open (checked November 5, 2026). See our HACP guide. If you already have HACP housing or a voucher and fall behind, call 412-456-5000 right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • The Allegheny County Housing Authority (ACHA) serves the county outside the city. Its page says "THE HCVP WAITING LIST IS NOW CLOSED" and "The ACHA does not have any emergency housing."
  • Just Mediation Pittsburgh helps you reach a deal with your landlord, but "does not provide rental assistance."
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and call the Allegheny Link at 866-730-2368.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Pittsburgh right now?

Mostly for renters with an eviction case. ACTION-Housing's Allegheny Housing Stabilization Collaborative offers rent help, mediation and legal support if you have an active eviction case or a completed mediation agreement through Just Mediation Pittsburgh. Call 412-248-0021 or email rentalassistance@actionhousing.org. Help depends on funding and is not guaranteed. We found no open City or County rent fund you can apply to before a case is filed; dial 211 for other help.

What if my landlord has not filed in court yet?

Call Just Mediation Pittsburgh at (412) 228-0730 to ask for a free mediation with your landlord. It does not pay rent, but a completed mediation agreement can make you eligible for ACTION-Housing's rent help. Also call Neighborhood Legal Services at 1-866-761-6572 and dial 211. Allegheny County's guide says that without a court filing you are not eligible for its specific rent funds at this time.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Pittsburgh?

Call Neighborhood Legal Services at 1-866-761-6572 or request help online at nlsa.us. Pittsburgh's City Code says Stop the Violence Fund money set aside for tenants at or below 80% of the area median income should be used to offer free legal representation in eviction cases, and ACTION-Housing's program lists legal support for tenants with a case filed. Ask for a lawyer as soon as you get a notice or court papers.

How much notice does a landlord have to give in Pennsylvania?

For unpaid rent, the notice to quit must give you 10 days to leave. To end a lease of one year or less, or a month-to-month lease, it is 15 days, and for a lease of more than one year, 30 days. But the law lets a written lease shorten or waive the notice, so read your lease. The landlord then has to file in court and win before you can be removed.

Can I stop a Pennsylvania eviction by paying the rent?

In a case only about unpaid rent, yes, up to the last minute. Pennsylvania law says that at any time before the writ of possession is actually carried out, you can pay the constable or sheriff the rent in arrears and the costs, and the writ has no effect. Get a signed receipt. This does not work if the case is about something other than rent.

How long do I have to appeal a Pittsburgh eviction?

10 days from the date the judgment is entered. File at the Housing Court Help Desk in the City-County Building, 414 Grant St., or call (412) 350-4462. To stay in your home while the appeal is pending, you must pay rent into court: the lesser of three months' rent or what you owe, then rent every 30 days. Low-income tenants can file an affidavit and pay in smaller first steps.

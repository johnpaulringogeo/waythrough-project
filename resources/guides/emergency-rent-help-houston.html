Emergency Rent Help in Houston: What's Open, Free Eviction Lawyers, and Texas's 2026 Eviction Clock

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Houston and you are behind, here is the honest picture: there is no big city or county rent fund taking open applications right now. Catholic Charities' Harris County rent help says "The application is currently closed," and Harris County's own rent program sends people to 2-1-1. The doors still open are smaller neighborhood ministries, like West Houston Assistance Ministries (WHAM) and Memorial Assistance Ministries (MAM). BakerRipley's electric and gas bill help is closed and expected to reopen on Monday, November 9. For eviction help, Lone Star Legal Aid has free lawyers working in all 16 Harris County Justice of the Peace courts: call or text "APPLY" to 361-35EVICT. Texas eviction law also changed on January 1, 2026. Everything here was checked on November 3, 2026.

Here's the short version: Dial 2-1-1 (or 877-541-7905) and ask for rent help in Harris County. If you live in a west Houston ZIP code that WHAM serves, call 713-780-2727, ext. 200, at 10 a.m. on a Monday. If you got court papers, call or text Lone Star Legal Aid at 361-35EVICT the same day. If the papers mention "summary disposition," you have only 4 days to respond. And if you get a court date, go.

Texas moves fast, and the rules changed on January 1, 2026

A Texas landlord needs to give only "at least three days' written notice to vacate" before filing, unless your lease says otherwise (Property Code section 24.005). The trial must be held between the 10th and the 21st day after the case is filed (section 24.0051(g)). Under Senate Bill 38, if the landlord files a motion for summary disposition, you have until the 4th day after you are served to file a written response, or the judge can rule without a trial (section 24.005106). After a judgment you have 5 days to appeal (section 24.005107). That is why every door below asks for your notice: by the time you have a court date, you may have only days left (checked November 3, 2026).

Emergency rental assistance in Houston: what is open and what is not

Apply to more than one. Each runs on its own money, and none of them promises help.

Harris County: the rent program goes through 2-1-1

Harris County Housing and Community Development lists a Community Resilience Rental Assistance Program "designed to assist households at risk of homelessness or eviction." Its stated goal is to prevent eviction and homelessness for people and households with low to moderate incomes. The County's page sends you to 2-1-1 to get in, and it does not say whether the program is taking new households right now (checked November 3, 2026). So call 2-1-1, say you live in Harris County and are behind on rent, and ask about this program by name.

  • If you are about to become homeless: the County's Housing Support Intake Line, 832-531-6041, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 1 p.m., is for people living in a shelter or a place not meant for living, or "within days of falling into homelessness." It is not for people who are couch surfing or unstably housed; the County sends them to 2-1-1. Eligible people are put on a waitlist for housing referrals.
  • The County's main line: 832-927-4955, 1111 Fannin St., 9th Floor.

2-1-1 Texas / United Way Helpline: the switchboard

United Way of Greater Houston says, "If you need help with food, housing, or utility assistance, call 2-1-1." The line is open "24 hours a day, every day of the year." If 2-1-1 does not connect, call 1-877-541-7905. You can also text your ZIP code to 898211. Rent and mortgage help is one of the top reasons people call, so write down every name and number you get.

West Houston Assistance Ministries (WHAM): west Houston ZIP codes

WHAM says it has "limited funds to provide financial aid to help with the cost of rent, utilities, hotels or childcare." Rent help is only for these ZIP codes: 77036, 77042, 77057, 77063, 77072, 77077, 77082, 77083, and 77099. There are no income limits (checked November 3, 2026).

  • Step 1, register: you must be registered in WHAM's system first. New client registration is "Mondays from 8am to 10am in person only," with "Only 15 spots available per registration day," at 10501 Meadowglen Lane, Houston, TX 77042. Bring 2 forms of ID (and two non-photo IDs for children), proof of income, and proof of where you live.
  • Step 2, call for an appointment: financial help appointments are made by phone on Mondays at 10 a.m. "until funds are exhausted." Call 713-780-2727, ext. 200. WHAM warns that "hundreds are calling at the same time," so if you don't get through, try again the next Monday and call 2-1-1 in the meantime.
  • Client services are closed the last Friday of every month.

Memorial Assistance Ministries (MAM): Spring Branch and the Heights

MAM says that to get its help, "Speaking with our team in person is the best way," and asks you to visit its main location at 1625 Blalock Rd., Houston, TX 77080, Monday to Thursday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., or Friday from 8:30 a.m. to early afternoon. Its Heights Campus is at 1015 E. 11th St. It also warns that "Coming in person is not a guarantee of enrollment, financial assistance, or resources." Main office: (713) 468-4516; Heights: (713) 574-7545.

Society of St. Vincent de Paul (Galveston-Houston)

St. Vincent de Paul helps through volunteer groups at local Catholic parishes, which can "provide financial assistance to neighbors in underserved areas during times of crises," often after a home visit. What each group can pay depends on where you live and what it has. Call 713-741-8234 or use the contact form on its site.

Catholic Charities of the Archdiocese of Galveston-Houston: closed right now

Catholic Charities' housing page lists rent help for Harris County, but says "The application is currently closed. Continue to check here to see if it has re-opened" (checked November 3, 2026). Its help for City of Houston residents with an eviction court order or court date is also closed, and so is its utility help. When the Harris County help is open, it is for residents who have a late notice and are behind on this month's rent or mortgage, and "Funds are limited." Main office: 713-526-4611, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.

Texas Rent Relief and the Texas Eviction Diversion Program are over

The Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs says both programs "launched in February 2021 and closed in the summer of 2023." The big Houston-Harris County rent relief program from the pandemic years is also over. If someone tells you to apply to one of them, that information is out of date.

Houston utility help: BakerRipley's electric and gas bill program

Paying an electric bill can free up rent money. BakerRipley's Utility Assistance Program, funded by the Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs, helps people in Harris, Brazoria, and Galveston counties pay electricity, gas, or propane bills. It "does not cover water bill payment."

  • Status: BakerRipley's page says "Applications are currently closed. Applications are expected to reopen on Monday, November 9" (checked November 4, 2026). That is an expected date, not a promise. If November 9 has passed, check BakerRipley's Utility Assistance page to see whether it opened, or closed again.
  • In-person appointments: to apply in person in November, call the BakerRipley Utility Assistance Hotline at 713-590-2327. Call the same line if anyone in your home has no SSN or ITIN.
  • Income limits (yearly, effective January 26, 2026): 1 person $23,940; 2 people $32,460; 3 people $40,980; 4 people $49,500; 5 people $58,020; 6 people $66,540.
  • What to have ready: proof of income from the last 30 days for everyone 18 and older, ID and a Social Security card for every household member, proof of citizenship or legal status, and a copy of your current electric, gas, or propane bill. Files must be PDF or JPEG, up to 5 MB each.
  • Rules: "Only one application per household per calendar year (January–December) is allowed." Incomplete applications are not reviewed. BakerRipley says you will get a written answer "within 6 weeks."

Eviction help in Houston: free lawyers

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can catch mistakes in the landlord's papers, file your response on time, and work out a deal.

Lone Star Legal Aid: Eviction Right to Counsel in all 16 JP courts

  • What it is: Lone Star Legal Aid says its Eviction Right to Counsel Initiative, part of the Harris County Eviction Defense Coalition, "helps people in all 16 Harris County Justice of the Peace courts" and works "like a public defender's office." If you meet its income rules, it will try to represent you fully in eviction court, "depending on our available resources."
  • How to reach it: "If you have a court date, don't wait – call 361-35EVICT (3-8428) or text 'APPLY' for our help as soon as you can." That is 361-353-8428. You can also apply online, or call the main office at 713-652-0077 or toll-free (800) 733-8394.
  • On your court day: its lawyers are regularly at many Harris County JP courts. It says to "come early, at least 30 minutes before your court time," and that "Even if you don't see your court listed, you can still ask for our help."
  • The coalition: Lone Star Legal Aid, Houston Volunteer Lawyers, South Texas College of Law Houston, Thurgood Marshall School of Law's Earl Carl Institute, and the University of Houston Law Center.

Harris County Eviction Defense Program

Harris County's program gives "free legal advice and representation" to renters at risk of eviction. To qualify, you must live in Harris County and "Have a total household income at or below 200% of current Federal Poverty Guideline." It can help before a case is filed "when an imminent threat of eviction exists," in court, on appeal, and in mediation. The County lists Lone Star Legal Aid as the provider and 832-927-4955 as the contact number.

Houston Volunteer Lawyers

Houston Volunteer Lawyers, a member of the Eviction Defense Coalition, takes calls for legal help at 713-228-0735. Its office is at 1000 Louisiana Street, Suite 3600.

At the courthouse

In Harris County, an eviction case "must be filed in the Justice Court in the precinct of the county in which the rental property is located." The courts remind tenants that judges and clerks "are not allowed to give legal advice," so ask a lawyer, not the clerk, what to file. You can look up your case on the Harris County Justice Courts site.

Texas eviction timeline in 2026: what happens and when

Texas rewrote its eviction law with Senate Bill 38, which took effect on January 1, 2026. The rules are in Chapter 24 of the Texas Property Code and in Texas Rule of Civil Procedure 510, which the Supreme Court of Texas says "has been completely rewritten as a standalone rule" for eviction cases. These are the steps for a rent case, in order (checked November 3, 2026):

  • 1. The notice. Your landlord must give you "at least three days' written notice to vacate" before filing, "unless the parties have contracted for a shorter or longer notice period in a written lease or agreement" (Property Code section 24.005(a)). Read your lease. If the case is only about unpaid rent and you were not late before the month of the notice, the notice must be a "notice to pay rent or vacate," which gives you the chance to pay. The notice can come by mail, be left inside your home in a conspicuous place, be handed to anyone in the home 16 or older, or come by email only if you agreed to that in writing (section 24.005(f-3)).
  • 2. The court case. The landlord files a sworn petition in the Justice of the Peace court for the precinct where your home is. A constable or sheriff must make "a diligent effort" to serve you within 5 business days after filing (section 24.0051(f)).
  • 3. The 4-day answer to a summary disposition motion. This is the new trap. With the petition, a landlord can file a sworn "motion for summary disposition without trial." If you do not file a written response with your supporting facts and documents "not later than the fourth day after the date the tenant is served," the judge can rule for the landlord without a trial (section 24.005106; Rule 510.10(a)). If your papers mention summary disposition, call Lone Star Legal Aid the same day.
  • 4. The trial. The court must hold the trial "not earlier than the 10th day or later than the 21st day after the date the petition is filed," and never sooner than the 4th day after you are served. A trial can be postponed for no more than 7 days unless both sides agree in writing (section 24.0051(g)). You do not have to file a written answer to an ordinary eviction suit (Rule 510.11(a)), but you must show up. If you do not, the landlord usually wins by default.
  • 5. The appeal: 5 days. To appeal, file a bond, a cash deposit, or a Statement of Inability to Afford Payment of Court Costs with the justice court "not later than the fifth day after the date the judgment is signed," and affirm that you have a good-faith defense (section 24.005107; Rule 510.19(a)). To stay in your home during the appeal, you must pay one rental period into the court registry within 5 days of filing the appeal and keep paying each period (section 24.0053(a-2)). If you miss a payment, the court can issue the writ of possession without a hearing (section 24.0054(a)).
  • 6. The writ of possession. If you lose and do not appeal, the writ "may not be issued before the sixth day after the date on which the judgment for possession is rendered" (section 24.0061(b)). The officer must post a warning on your front door at least 24 hours before carrying it out (section 24.0061(d)).
  • Lockouts. A landlord "may not intentionally prevent a tenant from entering the leased premises except by judicial process," with narrow exceptions. A landlord who changes the locks on a tenant who is behind must post a notice saying where to get the new key at any hour, and must give you the key "regardless of whether or not the tenant pays any of the delinquent rent." If a landlord breaks these rules, you can get back in or end the lease and recover one month's rent plus $1,000, plus damages and fees (Property Code section 92.0081).

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • Housing Alliance HTX (the Houston Housing Authority) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. In June 2026 it said "The Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) waitlist remains closed, and no new HCV applications will be accepted," and its public housing lottery for eight properties closed on July 7, 2026, after about 56,000 people applied (checked November 3, 2026). Phone: 713-260-0500. Our Housing Alliance HTX page has the details.
  • Old pandemic programs, like Texas Rent Relief and the Houston-Harris County Emergency Rental Assistance Program, are closed. Old flyers and posts still mention them.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Houston right now?

Only from smaller programs. There is no big city or county rent fund taking open applications, and Catholic Charities' Harris County rent help says "The application is currently closed" (checked November 3, 2026). Dial 2-1-1 and ask about Harris County's Community Resilience Rental Assistance Program. West Houston Assistance Ministries helps renters in nine west Houston ZIP codes by phone appointment on Mondays at 10 a.m. at 713-780-2727, ext. 200, and Memorial Assistance Ministries sees people in person at 1625 Blalock Rd.

Does Harris County help with rent?

Harris County lists a Community Resilience Rental Assistance Program for households at risk of eviction or homelessness, but you get to it through 2-1-1, and the County's page does not say whether it is taking new households right now. Dial 2-1-1 (or 877-541-7905) and ask for it by name. If you will be homeless within days, call the County's Housing Support Intake Line at 832-531-6041, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 1 p.m.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Houston?

Call or text "APPLY" to Lone Star Legal Aid's eviction line, 361-35EVICT (361-353-8428). Its Eviction Right to Counsel Initiative helps tenants in all 16 Harris County Justice of the Peace courts. Harris County's Eviction Defense Program, run with Lone Star Legal Aid, is for Harris County residents at or below 200% of the federal poverty guideline. Call as soon as you get a notice, and come to court at least 30 minutes early.

How long does an eviction take in Texas in 2026?

It can take less than a month. The landlord must give at least 3 days' written notice to vacate unless the lease says otherwise. After the case is filed, the trial must be held between the 10th and 21st day. If the landlord asks for summary disposition, you must file a written response within 4 days of being served. After a judgment you have 5 days to appeal, and the writ of possession cannot issue before the 6th day.

Can I get help with my electric bill in Houston?

BakerRipley's Utility Assistance Program helps with electricity, gas, and propane bills, not water, for people in Harris, Brazoria, and Galveston counties. Its page says applications are closed and expected to reopen on Monday, November 9 (checked November 4, 2026); if that date has passed, check BakerRipley's Utility Assistance page. For an in-person appointment, call 713-590-2327. The income limit is $23,940 a year for one person and $49,500 for four.

Can Housing Alliance HTX (the Houston Housing Authority) help me pay rent?

Not in an emergency. Housing Alliance HTX runs Section 8 and public housing. Its Section 8 waiting list is closed and is not taking new applications, and its public housing lottery closed on July 7, 2026. If you already have a voucher or live in its housing and fall behind, call 713-260-0500 right away.

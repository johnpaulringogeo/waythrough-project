Emergency Rent Help in Austin: Who Still Pays, Free Eviction Lawyers, and Texas's 2026 Eviction Clock

Matt · Updated October 2026

If you rent in Austin and you are behind, know this first: the City's I Belong in Austin rent portal is closed. The City says it "will close indefinitely" and is putting the money it has left into legal help and negotiated eviction settlements. The door with the clearest rules now is El Buen Samaritano's Upstream Together Collaborative, which takes calls during the first seven days of each month at (512) 522-1097 for very low-income households at risk of eviction. Austin Public Health still has limited rent help by phone, and Catholic Charities of Central Texas takes rent requests online. For eviction help, Texas RioGrande Legal Aid is the City's legal partner, at (833) 329-8752. Texas eviction law also changed on January 1, 2026. Everything here was checked on October 16, 2026.

Here's the short version: On the 1st of the month, call the Upstream Together Collaborative at (512) 522-1097 (8 a.m. to 8 p.m. on weekdays). Call Austin Public Health's Rent and Utility line at 512-972-5780. Send a request to Catholic Charities of Central Texas online. Dial 2-1-1 for other rent help. Call Texas RioGrande Legal Aid at (833) 329-8752 as soon as you get a notice. If your court papers mention "summary disposition," you have only 4 days to respond. And if you get a court date, go.

Texas moves fast, and the rules changed on January 1, 2026

A Texas landlord needs to give only "at least three days' written notice to vacate" before filing, unless your lease says otherwise (Property Code section 24.005). The trial must be held between the 10th and the 21st day after the case is filed (section 24.0051(g)). And under SB 38, if the landlord files a motion for summary disposition, you have until the 4th day after you are served to file a written response, or the judge can rule without a trial (section 24.005106). After a judgment you have 5 days to appeal (section 24.005107). That is why every rent door below asks for your notice: by the time you have a court date, you may have only days left.

Help paying rent in Austin: what is open and what is not

Apply to more than one. Each runs on its own money, and none of them promises help.

I Belong in Austin: closed

The City of Austin's rental assistance page says "The Austin Housing I Belong in Austin emergency rental assistance application portal will close indefinitely after the final April 1-7, 2026 application period" (checked October 16, 2026). The City's February 27, 2026 announcement gave the reason: the FY2026 budget cut the program from $4 million to $3 million, and the remaining dollars go to negotiated eviction settlements through the City's legal service partners. The City says renters already getting help, or already working with its legal partners, will be contacted directly. For everyone else, its advice is: "If you are currently facing eviction, please visit Texas RioGrande Legal Aid or Volunteer Legal Services." That is the way to reach the settlement money that is left: through a lawyer, not an application.

Upstream Together Collaborative (El Buen Samaritano): the first seven days of each month

El Buen Samaritano's housing stability case management takes new households once a month: "Applications are accepted during the first seven days of each month," and "the application period may close before the 7th once capacity has been reached" (checked October 16, 2026).

  • Who qualifies, in its words: "Household income must be at or below 30% of the Area Median Income (AMI)"; you "Live within Travis County or the City of Austin Full Purpose Jurisdiction"; the "Household must be at risk of eviction or housing loss"; an adult in the home is on the lease; and you have a source of income to keep up the rent after short-term help.
  • What it can pay: case management plus limited help that may include rent, utilities, transportation and application fees. "If rental assistance is determined to be necessary, the landlord must be willing to participate in the program." It says "Enrollment and financial assistance are not guaranteed."
  • How to apply: call (512) 522-1097. "The hotline is staffed: Monday through Friday from 8am to 8pm, or until program capacity has been reached." Services are in English and Spanish.
  • Not this one: El Buen Samaritano's separate One-Time Rental Assistance for renters who owed three months of rent says "The application period for this rental assistance opportunity is now closed."

Austin Public Health: the Rent and Utility line

The City's neighborhood centers page says "Neighborhood Services Unit has limited funds for Rent and Austin Energy assistance," and "We will no longer be accepting walk-in requests for Rent or Austin Energy assistance at neighborhood centers." Call the Rent and Utility line at 512-972-5780. Its rules, in its words: "Must be a resident of Austin/Travis County," "Must be a renter with your name on lease or contract," "Rent/utility bill must be PAST DUE in order to receive help," your household income must be "at or below 200% Federal Poverty Income Guidelines," and you need "proof of recent crisis or financial trouble," such as losing a job or hours, medical bills, or funeral costs. Have 30 days of pay stubs or other proof of income ready. The page does not give amounts or the line's hours.

Catholic Charities of Central Texas

Catholic Charities of Central Texas says "We are currently accepting applications for two types of financial assistance," one of them rent or mortgage help. "Financial assistance requests are accepted online only," through its online request form. It warns that "we are currently experiencing higher-than-normal demand for rent and mortgage assistance," so expect a long wait, and that sending more than one request will not speed it up.

Travis County Health and Human Services: rent help through case management

Travis County no longer runs an open rent program. Its 2025 recovery report says "ERAP and LFRF rental assistance funds have been fully expended and the County is no longer providing direct rental assistance through these sources," and that the County "has returned to providing rental assistance only through its General Fund, which supplies rental and utility assistance programs to those who participate in case management services."

To get into case management, apply for Travis County social work services through its online application portal or at any of its six community centers, including Central Austin at 5325 Airport Boulevard, 512-854-4120. Travis County residents who meet its income rules "may qualify for additional resources such as bus passes, rent and utility assistance referrals, and more." Bring a photo ID, proof that you live in Travis County, your current utility bills, and proof of all income for the last 30 days for everyone 18 and older.

2-1-1 and ConnectATX

Texas Health and Human Services says "Dialing 2-1-1 is a free, easy way to find out about services you can get in your area or through state programs," and "If you can't connect to 2-1-1, call us toll-free at 877-541-7905." The City of Austin also points renters to ConnectATX, United Way's local directory.

Displacement Prevention Navigators

If you live in Dove Springs (78744), Montopolis (78741), Colony Park (78724), or within a mile of the planned Project Connect transit lines, the City's Displacement Prevention Navigators can help you find programs and fill out applications. They do not pay rent themselves; the City says "assistance is not guaranteed." Call El Buen Samaritano at 512-648-3169 or City of Austin Housing at 512-974-3100.

Eviction help in Austin: free lawyers

If you searched for eviction help in Austin or Austin eviction protection, these are the answers with names. None of them pays rent directly, but a lawyer can catch mistakes in the landlord's papers, file your response on time, and negotiate a settlement.

Texas RioGrande Legal Aid (TRLA)

  • The City's partner: the City of Austin says it partnered with TRLA to offer "emergency eviction counseling, representation, and prevention services for low-income renters."
  • How to apply: TRLA says "If you need legal assistance call (956) 996-8752 or (833) 329-8752 toll-free," and "Our phone line remains open on weekdays from 9:00 a.m. until 5:00 p.m. (Central Time)." Call (833) 329-8752.
  • The Austin office is at 4920 North I-35, Austin, TX 78751.

Austin Tenants Council

The Austin Tenants Council is a TRLA project. The City says it offers "counseling to help tenants prepare for and understand the eviction process." Its staff "are not attorneys and cannot offer legal advice," but they can explain your rights and your notice. Call 512-474-1961 for telephone counseling, "Monday through Thursday, 9AM - 12PM and 1PM - 4PM" and "Friday, 9AM - 12PM," or use the intake form on its page.

Volunteer Legal Services of Central Texas

Volunteer Legal Services lists landlord-tenant cases among the civil matters it helps with. Call its main line at (512) 476-5550 or start at its Get Help page. It says income, assets, service area and its own capacity decide who it can take.

Eviction appeals

The Travis County Law Library has an eviction appeal help page and forms. It is in the Civil and Family Courts Facility, 1700 Guadalupe Street, 2nd floor, (512) 854-8677. It warns: "There are court deadlines. If the tenant misses a deadline, the landlord can ask for a Writ of Possession." We could not find a Travis County eviction diversion program that takes applications.

Texas eviction timeline in 2026: what happens and when

Texas rewrote its eviction law with Senate Bill 38, which took effect on January 1, 2026. The rules are in Chapter 24 of the Texas Property Code and in Texas Rule of Civil Procedure 510, which the Supreme Court of Texas rewrote as a stand-alone eviction rule for cases filed on or after that date. These are the steps for a rent case, in order:

  • 1. The notice. Your landlord must give you "at least three days' written notice to vacate" before filing, "unless the parties have contracted for a shorter or longer notice period in a written lease" (Property Code section 24.005(a)). Read your lease. If the case is only about unpaid rent and you were not late before the month of the notice, the notice must be a "notice to pay rent or vacate," which gives you the chance to pay. The notice can come by mail, be left inside your home in a conspicuous place, be handed to anyone in the home 16 or older, or come by email only if you agreed to that in writing (section 24.005(f-3)).
  • 2. The court case. The landlord files a sworn petition in the Justice of the Peace court (section 24.00505). In Travis County that is the Justice of the Peace court for the precinct where your home is. The officer must make a diligent effort to serve you within 5 business days after filing (section 24.0051(f)).
  • 3. The 4-day answer to a summary disposition motion. This is the new trap. With the petition, a landlord can file a sworn "motion for summary disposition without trial." If you do not file a written response with your supporting facts and documents "not later than the fourth day after the date the tenant is served," the judge can rule for the landlord without a trial (section 24.005106; Rule 510.10(a)). If your papers mention summary disposition, get legal help the same day.
  • 4. The trial. The court must hold the trial "not earlier than the 10th day or later than the 21st day after the date the petition is filed," and never sooner than the 4th day after you are served. A trial can be postponed for no more than 7 days unless both sides agree in writing (section 24.0051(g)). You do not have to file a written answer to an ordinary eviction suit (Rule 510.11(a)), but you must show up. If you do not, the landlord usually wins by default.
  • 5. The appeal: 5 days. To appeal, file a bond, a cash deposit, or a Statement of Inability to Afford Payment of Court Costs with the justice court "not later than the fifth day after the date the judgment is signed," and affirm that you have a good-faith defense (section 24.005107; Rule 510.19(a)). To stay in your home during the appeal, you must pay one rental period into the court registry within 5 days of filing the appeal and keep paying each period (section 24.0053(a-2)). If you miss a payment, the court can issue the writ of possession without a hearing (section 24.0054(a)).
  • 6. The writ of possession. If you lose and do not appeal, the writ cannot issue "before the sixth day after the date on which the judgment for possession is rendered" (section 24.0061(b)). The officer must post a warning on your front door at least 24 hours before carrying it out (section 24.0061(d)).
  • Lockouts. A landlord "may not intentionally prevent a tenant from entering the leased premises except by judicial process," with narrow exceptions. A landlord who changes the locks on a tenant who is behind must post a notice saying where to get the new key at any hour, and must give you the key "regardless of whether or not the tenant pays any of the delinquent rent." If a landlord breaks these rules, you can get back in or end the lease and recover one month's rent plus $1,000, plus damages and fees (Property Code section 92.0081).

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The Austin Housing Authority (AHA), legally the Housing Authority of the City of Austin (HACA), runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its Housing Choice Voucher page says "Waitlist Applications Closed" and "If no housing waitlist opening is posted on our website, we are not currently accepting applications" (checked October 16, 2026). Headquarters: (512) 477-4488, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. The Housing Authority of Travis County is a separate agency for areas outside the city. Our Austin page has the details.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Austin right now?

Yes, but not from the City's old portal. The door with the clearest rules is El Buen Samaritano's Upstream Together Collaborative: call (512) 522-1097 during the first seven days of the month, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. It is for Travis County households at or below 30% of Area Median Income who are at risk of eviction, and it closes early once its openings fill. Austin Public Health also has limited rent help through its Rent and Utility line at 512-972-5780, and Catholic Charities of Central Texas takes rent requests online.

Is the I Belong in Austin rent assistance program still open?

No. The City of Austin says the I Belong in Austin emergency rental assistance application portal "will close indefinitely" after its final application period in spring 2026, and that remaining dollars go to legal advocacy and negotiated eviction settlements through its legal partners. If you are facing eviction, the City says to contact Texas RioGrande Legal Aid or Volunteer Legal Services.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Austin?

Call Texas RioGrande Legal Aid, the City of Austin's eviction legal partner, at (833) 329-8752 toll-free or (956) 996-8752, weekdays 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Central. The Austin Tenants Council, a TRLA project, gives free telephone counseling at 512-474-1961, though its staff are not attorneys. Volunteer Legal Services of Central Texas also takes landlord-tenant cases at (512) 476-5550.

How long does an eviction take in Texas in 2026?

It can take less than a month. The landlord must give at least 3 days' written notice to vacate unless the lease says otherwise. After the case is filed, the trial must be held between the 10th and 21st day. If the landlord asks for summary disposition, you must file a written response within 4 days of being served. After a judgment you have 5 days to appeal, and the writ of possession cannot issue before the 6th day.

Does Travis County help with rent?

Only through case management now. Travis County's 2025 recovery report says its pandemic rental assistance funds "have been fully expended" and that the County "has returned to providing rental assistance only through its General Fund, which supplies rental and utility assistance programs to those who participate in case management services." You can apply for Travis County social work and case management online or at any of its six community centers.

Does the Austin Housing Authority give emergency rent help?

No. The Austin Housing Authority, legally the Housing Authority of the City of Austin (HACA), runs Section 8 and public housing. Its Housing Choice Voucher page says "Waitlist Applications Closed." If you already have an AHA voucher or live in AHA housing and fall behind, call AHA at (512) 477-4488, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., right away.

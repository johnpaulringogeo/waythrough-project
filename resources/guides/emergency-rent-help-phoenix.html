Emergency Rent Help in Phoenix: City Crisis Assistance, Maricopa County, Free Eviction Lawyers, and the 5-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in Phoenix and you are behind, the door that actually pays is the City of Phoenix Human Services Department's Crisis Assistance program. It offers eviction prevention and emergency utility help by appointment: call 602-534-AIDE (2433) or apply online. The City is honest that "Funding for rent and utility assistance is limited due to high demand," and if you can't get an appointment, it says to "try again next week." If you live somewhere else in Maricopa County, the County's Community Action Program offices take rent applications, but not from people in Phoenix, Mesa, or Glendale; Mesa renters go to MesaCAN instead. For eviction help, Phoenix residents can get a free lawyer through the City's Eviction Legal Services Program at (602) 262-7210. Everything here was checked on November 3, 2026.

Here's the short version: Gather your ID, your lease, your landlord's notice, and proof of your income now. Call the City's appointment line at 602-534-2433 or apply on the City's online portal. If you got a 5-day notice or court papers, fill out the City's eviction legal help screening form or call (602) 262-7210 the same day. Call 2-1-1 (9 a.m. to 7 p.m., every day) to find out which charities have money this week. Then go to your court hearing, even if you think you can't win.

A 5-day notice is not an eviction

In Arizona, a landlord who is owed rent must first give you a written notice. Under A.R.S. § 33-1368(B), if you don't pay "within five days after written notice," the landlord may then file a special detainer (eviction) case in court. The law says these are calendar days, so weekends count. You can still save the lease. If you pay "all past due and unpaid periodic rent and a reasonable late fee set forth in a written rental agreement" before the landlord files, the law says the rental agreement "shall be reinstated." After the case is filed, paying still works, but you must also pay the landlord's attorney fees and court costs. The Maricopa County Justice Courts put it plainly: "Paying in full before the judge rules will stop the eviction." After a judgment, letting you stay is up to the landlord (checked November 3, 2026). Keep proof of every payment.

Emergency rental assistance in Phoenix: the doors that pay

Which door you use depends on where you live. Call before you count on any of them; all of them run out of money.

City of Phoenix Crisis Assistance: the main door for Phoenix residents

The Human Services Department runs this program through its three Family Services Centers. In the City's words, the help "is dependent on current funding" and includes eviction prevention, move-in assistance, and emergency utility assistance (checked November 3, 2026).

  • Who qualifies: you must be a Phoenix resident (the City says some exemptions may apply) and be "In a crisis situation." The City says eligibility rules "may vary."
  • How to apply: call the Appointment Intake Line at 602-534-AIDE (2433) or toll-free 1-866-882-1778, or apply online at cophsdfsc.communityos.org. If you can't get an appointment, the City says, "please try again next week."
  • Documents: proof of identity, proof that you live in Phoenix, proof of your rent or utility bill, proof of household income, and proof of who lives with you. The City says "Additional documentation may be requested."
  • Family Services Centers (Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., closed on City holidays): John F. Long, 3454 N. 51st Ave.; Sunnyslope, 914 W. Hatcher Rd.; and Travis L. Williams, 4732 S. Central Ave. All three use the same phone line.
  • HOPWA: if you or someone in your home is living with HIV/AIDS, the same line handles short-term help with move-in costs, eviction prevention, and utility shutoffs.

Maricopa County rent help (outside Phoenix, Mesa, and Glendale)

Maricopa County's Human Services Department takes rental assistance applications through its Community Action Program (CAP) offices. The County says you qualify only if you live "in Maricopa County, but not in the cities of Glendale, Mesa, or Phoenix" (checked November 3, 2026).

  • Who qualifies: you must be past due on rent and have had a crisis, such as losing income, a big unexpected expense, or something that puts your health or safety at risk. You need papers that show the crisis. The County says "Funding may require verification of U.S. Citizenship or Qualified Noncitizen Status."
  • How to apply: start online on the County's Rental Assistance page (through its Client Portal), or contact your local CAP office. Appointments are "first-come, first-served," and you "must reside in the CAP office's service area in order to apply." Questions: 602-506-0589.
  • Documents: a photo ID, proof of all household income from the 30 days before you apply, and proof of the crisis.
  • The 2026 Eviction Prevention Pilot is closed. The County's pilot for four ZIP codes (85008, 85040, 85041, and 85042) paid up to $3,200 in rent, but the County says "Applications for the Eviction Prevention Pilot Program closed Friday, August 21 at 4 p.m."

Mesa and Glendale renters

  • Mesa: the City of Mesa sends rent and utility help to the Mesa Community Action Network (MesaCAN), run by A New Leaf. MesaCAN "provides rent and utility assistance to low-income Mesa residents." For rent, call (480) 750-9228 on the first Thursday of each month between 2 and 3 p.m., or until appointments run out. A New Leaf warns that "Only a limited number of appointments can be taken due to limited funding." For utility help, call (480) 833-9200 to schedule an orientation. See our Mesa housing resources page for more.
  • Glendale: the County's program does not serve Glendale either. Glendale residents should ask the City of Glendale or 2-1-1 which office takes their applications.

Charities, and DES help for families with children

  • St. Vincent de Paul: offers "one-time rent, mortgage and utility assistance" through volunteer groups based in local Catholic churches. Each church "can only serve within its neighborhood," so use the map on St. Vincent de Paul's rent and utility page to find the one that serves your address and call it. Hours vary because volunteers run each group.
  • DES Short-Term Crisis Services: the Arizona Department of Economic Security helps with rent, utilities, and deposits for families that have "a child under 18" in the home and income at or below 125% of the federal poverty guidelines (150% if someone is 60 or older or has a disability). You can get it once in 12 months. To apply, DES says to "contact the local Community Action Agency (CAA) in your area," which in Phoenix means the Family Services Centers above.

211 Arizona: the switchboard

211 Arizona is run by Solari Crisis & Human Services. It "operates 9 a.m. to 7 p.m. daily," with live operators in English and Spanish and interpreters for other languages. Dial 2-1-1, or call 877-211-8661 from any phone. Its Housing Crisis Hotline helps people "who are at risk of becoming homeless." Ask which rent programs in your ZIP code have money right now.

Utility help in Phoenix: LIHEAP, APS, and SRP

In Phoenix, the summer power bill can be as big as the rent. Getting help with it can free up money for rent.

  • LIHEAP (Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program): apply through DES's A-to-Z Arizona Portal, at a Family Services Center, or with a paper application. DES says the regular benefit is up to $640 based on income, energy costs, and who lives with you, and crisis help (for a shutoff notice) is up to $500. It is "first-come, first-served" and limited to once in 12 months. LIHEAP service line: 1-866-494-1981, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. The state's separate Power AZ program is closed: DES says "Power AZ funds have been exhausted," and applications sent after September 21, 2026 cannot be processed.
  • APS Crisis Bill Assistance: APS says customers with a temporary hardship "may qualify for up to $1,000 in bill assistance" a year through partners such as Wildfire, Chicanos Por La Causa, and Maricopa County Human Services. APS also has an Energy Support Program with a monthly discount for lower-income customers, and payment arrangements for past-due bills.
  • SRP: call (602) 236-8888 "right away," SRP says; its customer service team is there 24/7. SRP offers payment plans and extensions, and the SRP Income-Qualified Discount gives eligible customers a $10 or $35 monthly discount. SRP's Customer Resource Counselors can also connect you "with community agencies that may assist with rent."
  • Chicanos Por La Causa (CPLC) utility help: call (602) 805-1666 and choose option 1. For APS help, CPLC takes calls Monday to Wednesday, 9 a.m. to noon and 2 to 4 p.m., first come, first served. When we checked on November 3, 2026, its page said: "SRP funds have been exhausted. Please check back in November."
  • City of Phoenix Targeted One-Time Crisis Assistance: this is only for Phoenix households that lost SNAP or KidsCare benefits. It pays up to $600 toward a utility account plus $100 for transportation, for help given between July 1, 2026 and June 30, 2027, and the City says applications stop once the money is all given out. It does not pay rent.

Eviction help in Phoenix: free lawyers

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can check the landlord's notice, raise defenses, and work out more time or a payment plan.

  • City of Phoenix Eviction Legal Services Program: free legal help, including "full or limited legal representation at court," mediation, and help after an eviction. You qualify if you live in Phoenix or get City of Phoenix water service, are at risk of eviction or were already evicted, and have household income at or below 80% of the Maricopa County area median income (for example, $62,850 for one person or $89,750 for four, using the City's 2025 limits). Fill out the online screening form in English or Spanish, email HSD.EvictionHelp@phoenix.gov, or call (602) 262-7210. The City aims to contact you "within 1 business day" and warns that the eviction process moves fast, so answer your phone. In person: Travis L. Williams Family Services Center, 4732 S. Central Ave.
  • Maricopa County Legal Assistance for Renters (run by Community Legal Services): the County says this help is "available to all residents of Maricopa County facing eviction due to nonpayment of rent," including advice and representation in court. The County says to "Contact CLS directly at 602-385-8880" (602-385-8880).
  • Community Legal Services (CLS): CLS "provides free legal assistance for a variety of civil legal matters," including preventing evictions. Main line (602) 258-3434 or 1-800-852-9075; office at 305 S. 2nd Ave. You can also apply online at apply.azlawhelp.org.
  • Court self-help: the Arizona courts' eviction self-service center (Spanish at azcourts.gov/desalojo) has forms and videos. The Maricopa County Justice Courts' office line is (602) 506-8530, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.

Arizona eviction rules: what happens and when

These steps come from the Arizona Revised Statutes on azleg.gov and the Maricopa County Justice Courts:

  • 1. The notice. For unpaid rent, the landlord must give you written notice and wait 5 calendar days (A.R.S. § 33-1368(B)). The Justice Courts say "the notice must give the tenant five days before the case is filed." For other lease problems, the notice is usually 10 days (5 days for health and safety problems).
  • 2. Pay, and the lease comes back. Paying all past-due rent and a reasonable late fee from a written lease before the case is filed reinstates the lease. After filing, you must also pay attorney fees and court costs.
  • 3. The court case. Phoenix eviction cases are heard in the Maricopa County Justice Courts. Under A.R.S. § 33-1377(B), the hearing date on the summons must be "not more than six nor less than three days from the date of the summons." The judge can postpone it for up to 3 days for good cause. The Justice Courts say hearings can be by phone or video, and the summons explains how to join.
  • 4. The hearing. The judge asks whether you agree you owe the money. The Justice Courts say valid defenses include proof that you paid or that the notice was not done right. If you don't show up, you will almost certainly lose by default, so go, or call a lawyer first.
  • 5. After a judgment. Under A.R.S. § 12-1178(C), "No writ of restitution shall issue until the expiration of five calendar days after the rendition of judgment." A constable, not your landlord, carries out the writ.
  • No lockouts. If a landlord locks you out or shuts off your power, gas, or water to force you out, A.R.S. § 33-1367 lets you get back in or end the lease and recover up to two months' rent or twice your damages, whichever is more.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The City of Phoenix Housing Department runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. It says "The Phoenix Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) program waiting list is currently closed" (checked November 3, 2026). See our Phoenix Housing Department page.
  • The Housing Authority of Maricopa County (HAMC) serves the county outside Phoenix. Its Housing Choice Voucher list has been closed since August 25, 2023. Its open property waiting lists (Casa Bonitas, Clare Feldstadt in Mesa, and Heritage at Surprise and Heritage Senior in Surprise) take online applications only until 8 a.m. on December 1, 2026; after that, HAMC says applications "will no longer be accepted" until further notice, and it does not take paper applications. HAMC: 602-744-4500.
  • The Arizona Rental Assistance Program (ARAP) has ended. DES says "applications are no longer being accepted"; only applications sent by August 31, 2024 are still being processed.
  • Chicanos Por La Causa helps with utility bills, but its page says "We do not have Rental Assistance funds."
  • The Weidner settlement fund on the City's page "has temporarily reached capacity and is no longer accepting applications."
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Phoenix right now?

Yes, but money is limited. The City of Phoenix Human Services Department offers eviction prevention and emergency utility help by appointment through its Family Services Centers. Call 602-534-2433 or 1-866-882-1778, or apply online. The City says funding is limited because of high demand, and if you can't get an appointment, try again the next week. Dial 2-1-1 to ask which charities have money now.

Who helps with rent if I live in Mesa or somewhere else in Maricopa County?

If you live in Maricopa County but not in Phoenix, Mesa, or Glendale, apply for rental assistance through Maricopa County's Community Action Program offices, online or by calling your local office. Mesa residents go to MesaCAN instead: call 480-750-9228 on the first Thursday of the month between 2 and 3 p.m. The County's Eviction Prevention Pilot for four ZIP codes closed on August 21, 2026.

What should I do if I got a 5-day notice or eviction court papers?

Act the same day. If you live in Phoenix or get City of Phoenix water, call the City's Eviction Legal Services Program at (602) 262-7210 for a free lawyer if your income is at or below 80% of the area median. Anyone in Maricopa County facing eviction for unpaid rent can call Community Legal Services at 602-385-8880. Then go to your hearing, by phone, video, or in person, as the summons says.

How fast can an eviction happen in Arizona?

Very fast. After a 5-day notice for unpaid rent, the landlord can file in court. The hearing must be 3 to 6 days after the summons is issued, and the judge can postpone it for only up to 3 days for good cause. If the landlord wins, the court cannot issue a writ of restitution until 5 calendar days after the judgment. From notice to lockout can take only a few weeks.

Can I stop an Arizona eviction by paying what I owe?

Often, yes. Under A.R.S. 33-1368, if you pay all past-due rent and a reasonable late fee from a written lease before the landlord files in court, the lease is reinstated. After the case is filed, you must also pay the landlord's attorney fees and court costs, before the judge rules. After a judgment, letting you stay is up to the landlord. Get a receipt for every payment.

Does the Phoenix Housing Department give emergency rent help?

No. It runs Section 8 and public housing, and it says its Housing Choice Voucher waiting list is currently closed. The Housing Authority of Maricopa County's voucher list has been closed since August 25, 2023, and its property waiting lists take applications only until 8 a.m. on December 1, 2026; after that they are closed until further notice. For rent help, call the City's Crisis Assistance line at 602-534-2433.

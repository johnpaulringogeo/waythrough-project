Emergency Rent Help in Denver: TRUA's Monthly Window, CERA's Random Selection, Free Eviction Lawyers, and the 10-Day Notice

Matt · Updated November 2026

If you rent in the City and County of Denver and you are behind, there are two doors that actually pay rent, and both open only for a short time. The City's Temporary Rental and Utility Assistance (TRUA) program opens for about one day a month, then picks households at random. The state's Colorado Emergency Rental Assistance (CERA) program also picks at random, from a short monthly window. If you already have eviction court papers, the CARE Center at (303) 838-1200 can enter you in CERA's daily selection on any weekday. And the City pays for free eviction lawyers for renters at or below 80% of the area median income. Everything here was checked on November 5, 2026, unless we say otherwise.

Here's the short version: Get your lease, your ID, proof of income, and your Demand for Rent ready now. Apply to TRUA on the City's Rent and Utility Help page when it opens: the November window runs 10 a.m. Tuesday, November 17 to 9:59 a.m. Wednesday, November 18, 2026, and the next listed date is December 15. Apply to CERA in its next monthly window, 10 a.m. Monday, December 7 to 5 p.m. Wednesday, December 9, 2026, or the one after it. If you have a court summons, call the CARE Center. Call a free lawyer the day you get a demand, and go to court if you are sued.

A 10-day demand is not an eviction

To evict you for unpaid rent, a Colorado landlord must first serve a written demand giving you 10 days to pay or move out (C.R.S. § 13-40-104(1)(d)). Colorado Legal Services calls this paper a "Demand for Rent or Possession" and says: "Your landlord cannot file an eviction in court earlier." Paying still works after a case is filed: under C.R.S. § 13-40-115(4), a landlord who gave you a nonpayment notice "shall accept" your full payment of what the notice says you owe, plus any rent that has come due since, "at any time until a judge issues a judgment for possession." In Denver, that same Demand for Rent is the paper that lets you apply to TRUA and to CERA's monthly selection, so keep it safe.

Help paying rent in Denver: the doors that pay

Apply to both TRUA and CERA if you can. Each one picks at random, and neither promises help.

TRUA: the City and County of Denver's rent program

TRUA is run by the City's Department of Housing Stability (HOST). Its page says TRUA is "currently not accepting applications" and that it "anticipates reopening for new applications at 10 a.m. on Tuesday, Nov. 17 and closing at 9:59 a.m. on Wednesday, Nov.18." Its 2026 schedule lists the last opening of the year as "Dec. 15," and it warns that openings are subject to change "depending on availability of funds" (checked November 11, 2026). No 2027 dates are posted yet, so after the December window, check the City's page for them.

  • Who qualifies, in the City's words: household income at or below 80% of the Area Median Income; a financial hardship or other housing crisis; and a primary residence in "both the City AND County of Denver." The City also says: "Households who received funding from TRUA in 2025 will not be eligible to receive TRUA funding in 2026."
  • You need an eviction paper: a current Demand for Rent, a Denver County Court summons, a Denver Sheriff notice, or other proof of an active eviction case. An email from your landlord asking for payment does not count. The demand must show the amount due, the date it was served, the address, the deadline, and the landlord's signature.
  • Have these ready: proof of income for everyone 18 or older, proof you live there (such as a lease or utility bill), proof of the rent you owe, and a government photo ID.
  • What it pays: up to 6 months of rent, one time, which can cover back rent, current rent, and up to one month ahead. It can also pay Denver Water or Xcel Energy bills, but only together with rent help. If the eviction cannot be stopped, it can help with move-in costs instead.
  • How it picks: one application per household per month. After the window closes, applicants are chosen at random, and the ones picked get an email asking for more information. If you are not picked, you can apply again the next month.

CERA (Colorado Emergency Rental Assistance): the state's door

CERA is run by the Colorado Division of Housing. It says "The maximum total assistance a household can receive is $15,000." Your household must be at or below 60% of the area median income for your county, be behind on rent, and not have received CERA or other state rent help in the last 24 months.

  • Monthly Random Selection: the November window closed November 6. The Division of Housing now says "Pre-applications for the Monthly Random Selection are currently closed" and lists the next window as "Opens: Monday, December 7, 2026 at 10:00 a.m." and "Closes: Wednesday, December 9, 2026 at 5:00 p.m." (checked November 11, 2026). After that, check the CERA page for the next monthly window. A demand for rent counts as your eviction paper. Applying early in the window does not improve your chances.
  • Daily Random Selection (court papers): if you have a court summons, an eviction answer, a stipulation, a writ of restitution, or a sheriff's notice, call the CARE Center at (303) 838-1200, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. A demand for rent alone does not qualify for the daily selection.
  • Help applying: you can call or text the CARE Center at the same number, or chat at carecenter.us. The City sends TRUA applicants there for help too.

Denver Human Services and 211

  • Denver Human Services Emergency Assistance: the City's rent help page lists 720-944-4DHS (720-944-4347) for limited emergency funds, including help with Xcel bills. Ask what is available this month.
  • Mile High United Way 211: dial 2-1-1, or text your ZIP code to 898-211. It says "No matter where you live in Colorado, 211 can connect you to support close to home," including rental assistance. Ask for rent help in Denver County and write down every name and number you get.
  • Neighborhood help with applications: the City lists BuCu West Community Connectors in Westwood, (720) 541-9324, and, for ZIP code 80220 only, the East Colfax Community Collective, (720) 295-0152.

Free eviction lawyers in Denver

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can check the landlord's demand, raise defenses, and work out more time or a payment plan.

City-funded eviction lawyers (80% of area median income or less)

The City's Eviction Help page says "HOST provides funding for free legal services for low to moderate income households (at or below 80% of the area median income) facing an eviction" (checked November 5, 2026). It lists these providers:

  • Colorado Poverty Law Project: (303) 532-2641, or its online intake form. It notes that "Income restrictions may apply" and helps people remotely.
  • Colorado Legal Services, Denver office: 303-837-1313, 1560 Broadway, Suite 1100. It serves Denver and seven nearby counties. New applicants: 8:30 a.m. to 3 p.m., Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday, or fill out the online pre-application.
  • Colorado Affordable Legal Services: (303) 996-0010.
  • Community Economic Defense Project (CEDP): the City still lists it, but CEDP's own site says "We are not currently accepting new clients to help with defending against evictions," and sends people facing eviction to the CARE Center at (303) 838-1200.

The free eviction clinic and other help

  • Free Eviction Clinic: the City says to "attend our Free Eviction Clinic" in Room 163 of the Denver City and County Building, 1437 Bannock St., from 8 a.m. to noon, Monday through Friday.
  • Colorado Housing Connects: free landlord-tenant counseling at 1-844-926-6632.
  • Mandatory mediation if you get certain benefits: under HB23-1120, if you receive SSI, Social Security Disability Insurance, or cash assistance through Colorado Works, your landlord generally must go to mediation with you before filing an eviction. For a tenant who gets cash assistance, the law also says officers may not carry out a writ of restitution for "at least 30 days after the entry of judgment," with some exceptions. Small landlords with five or fewer single-family homes are exempt. Tell a lawyer right away if this applies to you.

Utility help: LEAP and Energy Outreach Colorado

  • LEAP (Low-income Energy Assistance Program): the state says "LEAP will start taking applications Nov. 1, 2026," and you can apply "at any time between Nov. 1 through April 30." Apply online through Colorado PEAK or call 1-866-HEAT-HELP (1-866-432-8435). LEAP "usually pays between $200 to $1,000," and it may be able to process an emergency application faster if your heat could be shut off.
  • Energy Outreach Colorado: the City's rent help page points Denver residents to Energy Outreach Colorado for help with Xcel electric or gas bills. See energyoutreach.org.
  • TRUA's utility help: TRUA can pay current and past-due Denver Water and Xcel Energy bills, one time each, but only together with rent help.

Paying the utility bill can free up cash for rent. Our utility assistance guide covers other programs.

Colorado eviction rules: what happens and when

Denver evictions are heard in Denver County Court, which lists "Forced Entry and Detainer (FED) for Evictions" among its civil cases; papers can be filed at the clerk's office in the City and County Building. These steps come from the Colorado statutes, the Colorado Judicial Branch's self-help page on residential evictions, and Colorado Legal Services:

  • 1. The demand. For unpaid rent, the landlord must serve a written demand giving you 10 days to pay or move out (C.R.S. § 13-40-104(1)(d)). A landlord who owns five or fewer single-family rental homes can use a 5-day notice if the lease says the 10-day period does not apply. Colorado Legal Services says landlords that get certain federal benefits or have federally backed mortgages "often have to give you 30 days." The Judicial Branch says "The demand must be in the tenant's primary language."
  • 2. Pay, and it stops. If you pay everything the demand asks for in time, Colorado Legal Services says "your landlord can't file the eviction against you." Keep proof of every payment.
  • 3. The court case. After the notice period ends, the landlord can file an eviction complaint and summons in county court. The court date on the summons must be at least 7 and no more than 14 days after the summons is issued (C.R.S. § 13-40-111), and the Judicial Branch says service must be made "at least 7 days before your hearing date."
  • 4. Your answer. File an answer (form JDF 103) by the date on the summons. The Judicial Branch warns: "If you do not file an answer or do not appear in court, the court may rule against you. This is called a Default Judgment." Go to the free eviction clinic in Room 163 if you need help with it.
  • 5. After a judgment. If the landlord wins, the court can issue a writ of restitution, which "will allow the Sheriff to physically remove a tenant." Only the sheriff, with that court order, can remove you.
  • Late fees have limits. Under C.R.S. § 38-12-105, a landlord cannot charge a late fee until rent is at least 7 calendar days late, cannot charge more than the greater of $50 or 5% of the past-due rent, and cannot start an eviction because you did not pay late fees.
  • Most landlords need a reason to end your tenancy. HB24-1098, in effect since April 19, 2024 (C.R.S. §§ 38-12-1301 to 38-12-1307), says a covered landlord needs cause, such as unpaid rent or a lease violation, or a "no-fault" reason that needs at least 90 days' written notice. It does not cover tenants who have lived there less than 12 months, short-term rentals, and some owner-occupied homes.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The Denver Housing Authority (DHA) runs Section 8 and public housing. It is not an emergency rent fund. Its lottery page says "The lottery closed at 11:59 p.m. on Friday, September 18, 2026," that "DHA did not select names from the Housing Choice Voucher lottery in 2025 or 2026," and that "Based on current funding, DHA may not be able to select names from the 2027 lottery" (checked November 5, 2026). See our DHA guide for the lottery and DHA's site-based waiting lists. If you already have DHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call DHA at (720) 932-3023 right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • The state's general voucher program run by the Colorado Division of Housing reads "Program Status: Pause." It would not arrive in time to stop an eviction anyway.
  • Old pandemic rent programs. The federal Emergency Rental Assistance money of 2021 to 2023 is gone. Ignore old flyers and portals; TRUA and CERA are the current doors.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. They are not a fund for a tenant who is still housed. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in Denver right now?

Yes, but only in short windows. The City's Temporary Rental and Utility Assistance (TRUA) program says it is "currently not accepting applications" and lists its November window as 10 a.m. Tuesday, November 17 to 9:59 a.m. Wednesday, November 18, 2026; the next listed date is December 15 (checked November 11, 2026). The state's CERA program closed its November window on November 6; its next Monthly Random Selection window runs from 10 a.m. Monday, December 7 to 5 p.m. Wednesday, December 9, 2026 (checked November 11, 2026). After that, check the Division of Housing's CERA page for the next window. If you have court papers, call the CARE Center at (303) 838-1200 on a weekday. Dial 211 for other help.

How does Denver's TRUA program work?

TRUA is run by the City's Department of Housing Stability (HOST). It opens for about one day starting on the third Tuesday of each month, as funds allow, and then picks applicants at random. Your household must be at or below 80% of the area median income, live in the City and County of Denver, and have a current Demand for Rent, a Denver County Court summons, a sheriff's notice, or other proof of an active eviction case. Households that got TRUA in 2025 cannot get it in 2026. It can pay up to 6 months of rent, one time.

What if I already have eviction court papers in Denver?

Act the same day. Call the CARE Center at (303) 838-1200, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., to apply for CERA's Daily Random Selection, which is for households with a court summons or later eviction papers. Call a free eviction lawyer from the City's list, such as Colorado Legal Services at 303-837-1313 or the Colorado Poverty Law Project at (303) 532-2641. The City also runs a free eviction clinic in Room 163 of the City and County Building, 1437 Bannock St., weekdays from 8 a.m. to noon. File your answer by the date on the summons, and go to court.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in Denver?

The City says HOST pays for free legal services for households at or below 80% of the area median income that are facing an eviction. It lists the Colorado Poverty Law Project at (303) 532-2641, Colorado Legal Services at 303-837-1313, Colorado Affordable Legal Services at 303-996-0010, and the Community Economic Defense Project, though CEDP says it is not taking new eviction defense clients right now. Call as soon as you get a demand for rent, not the day before court.

How much notice does a Colorado landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

Usually 10 days. Under C.R.S. 13-40-104(1)(d), the landlord must serve a written demand giving you 10 days to pay or move out, and cannot file in court before that. A landlord who owns five or fewer single-family rental homes can use 5 days if the lease says so, and some landlords with federal funding or federally backed mortgages must give 30 days. If you pay the full amount, the landlord has to accept it any time until a judge enters a judgment for possession.

Does the Denver Housing Authority give emergency rent help?

No. DHA runs Section 8 and public housing, not a rent fund. Its 2027 voucher lottery closed on September 18, 2026, and DHA says it did not select names from the lottery in 2025 or 2026 and may not be able to select names from the 2027 lottery (checked November 5, 2026). If you already have DHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call DHA at (720) 932-3023 right away.

Emergency Rent Help in St. Louis: Free Eviction Lawyers, 211, and Missouri's Rent-and-Possession Clock

Matt · Updated October 2026

If you rent in St. Louis and you are behind, here is the honest picture. The City of St. Louis has no open rent fund right now: its last Emergency Rental Assistance round (ERA 3) closed to applications on December 16, 2024. What the City does have is strong: HELP-STL, a Right to Counsel program that gives City renters facing eviction a free lawyer through Legal Services of Eastern Missouri. St. Louis County is a separate government with its own housing authority and its own court, and it has no Right to Counsel program. Rent money in both places now comes from nonprofits with limited funds, and the way in is United Way 2-1-1. Everything here was checked on October 28, 2026.

Here's the short version: Dial 2-1-1 (or 1-800-427-4626) and ask for rent help in your county, and call the St. Louis Housing Help Line at (314) 802-5444. If your landlord has filed in court and you live in the City, call HELP-STL at (314) 657-1671 that day. If you live in the County, apply to Legal Services of Eastern Missouri at (314) 534-4200 or online. And if you get a summons, go to court: in Missouri the court date can come within days, and paying all the rent and court costs at the hearing can end the case.

Missouri moves fast: no notice period for unpaid rent

In Missouri, a landlord can file a rent-and-possession case once rent "has become due and payable, and payment has been demanded" and not made. The statute says the one-month termination notice "is not required prior to filing" (RSMo 535.020). The summons must be served "at least four days before the court date," and the court date can be no more than 21 business days after the summons is issued (RSMo 535.030). The good news: if you pay the landlord, or bring into court, "all the rent then in arrears, and all the costs" on the date of the money judgment, "further proceedings in the action shall cease and be stayed" (RSMo 535.160). That is why you should start calling for rent help and a lawyer the day you fall behind.

Free eviction lawyers in St. Louis

None of these pays rent, but a lawyer can catch mistakes in the landlord's papers, raise defenses like bad repairs, negotiate a payment plan, and make sure you get every day the law allows.

HELP-STL: Right to Counsel for City of St. Louis renters

HELP-STL, the Housing Eviction Law Program, is the City's Right to Counsel program. It started in August 2024 under City Ordinance 71694. The City says it "was created to provide access to legal representation for tenants facing eviction or equivalent proceedings," and that it contracted with Legal Services of Eastern Missouri to provide the free legal services (checked October 28, 2026).

  • Who qualifies, in the City's words: "To be eligible for the program, you need to be a renter living in the City of St. Louis." The City's request page links an address checker so you can confirm you are inside City limits and not in St. Louis County.
  • How to ask for help: "Call the Department of Human Services at (314) 657-1671 or (314) 657-1654." The Department's hours are weekdays, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., at 1520 Market Street, 4th Floor.
  • What to have ready: the City says "If you have received an eviction notice from your landlord, have that eviction notice information with you," and the same for any court date.
  • What it is not: HELP-STL is legal help, not a rent payment. Legal Services of Eastern Missouri decides how much help each case gets.

Legal Services of Eastern Missouri (LSEM): City and County

  • What it does: LSEM gives "free civil legal assistance to low-income individuals and the elderly in 21 counties of eastern Missouri." Its Housing Law Program says "We assist families and individuals facing eviction, loss of housing assistance, poor living conditions," and lists St. Louis City, St. Louis County, Jefferson County and St. Charles County.
  • How to apply: call (314) 534-4200 or 1-800-444-0514. In LSEM's words: "APPLICATIONS FOR HELP BY PHONE ARE AVAILABLE 9:00 AM TO 1:00PM ON MONDAY, WEDNESDAY & THURSDAY ONLY." You can leave a message from 1 to 5 p.m. on those days, or use the "Apply Now" link on its Get Help Now page at any time. Be ready to give your household income and assets, and "If you have received a summons to appear in court, please have this information with you."
  • Interpreters: LSEM says to ask for one "when you make your first call to the intake line."
  • Free self-help: LSEM runs MOTenantHelp.org, which it describes as "free, easy-to-use, and mobile-friendly resources to Missouri tenants facing eviction or other housing needs."

Help paying rent in St. Louis: the doors that are left

There is no single rent office to walk into. Use 211 and the Housing Help Line to find who has money this month, and keep calling while you wait for a lawyer. None of these programs promises help.

United Way 2-1-1 and the St. Louis Housing Help Line

  • United Way 2-1-1: run by United Way of Greater St. Louis, "Serving United Way of Greater St. Louis' service area and the entire state of Missouri." Dial 2-1-1 or 1-800-427-4626 to reach a resource specialist; rent and utility assistance are among the needs it covers. You can also text 1-800-427-4626 or chat online, Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Ask for rent help in your county (City or County) and write down every name and number you get.
  • St. Louis Housing Help Line, (314) 802-5444: the City's Homeless Services Division lists this number as the "St. Louis Housing Help Line," and Gateway 180 lists the same number as its Housing Resource Hotline. Neither page spells out the line's hours or rules, so call it alongside 211, not instead of it.

Nonprofits the City lists for rent help

  • Guardian Angel Settlement Association, (314) 773-9027: listed on the City's Housing Help page. Its Emergency Assistance Program "provides direct assistance to families, seniors, and individuals who are at risk for eviction, foreclosure/or utility shut-off," and people who get housing aid also take part in self-sufficiency counseling or workshops. Call to ask whether money is available and what to bring.
  • Society of St. Vincent de Paul, St. Louis: says it offers "rent and utility help" across 13 counties through local volunteer groups called conferences. To ask for help, use the Conference Finder Map on its Need Help page or dial 2-1-1. Its main number is (314) 881-6000.

St. Louis County: call 211

St. Louis County's Department of Human Services lists "Homeless Prevention Services" and "Rapid Rehousing Services" among what its Office of Community Development provides, working through nonprofit partners. We found no County page where a renter can apply for rent money directly (checked October 28, 2026). County renters should dial 2-1-1 and ask for homelessness prevention or rent help in St. Louis County. The Department's number is (314) 615-4444, Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., at 500 Northwest Plaza Drive, St. Ann.

Veterans: St. Patrick Center's SSVF program

St. Patrick Center runs Supportive Services for Veteran Families (SSVF), which it says is "Funded by the U.S. Veterans Administration" and helps veterans and their families with case management, homelessness prevention, and moving into housing. In its words: "For veterans or households with a veteran who are homeless or at-risk of homelessness, call 314-356-0657 to connect with our SSVF program." Call 314-356-0657.

If the May 2025 tornado or a condemnation forced you out (City only)

The City's Impacted Tenants Fund pays a one-time amount "equal to one month of rent based on current HUD 2025 Fair Market Rate (FMR)" to City renters whose unit was condemned by the City's Building Division or made uninhabitable by the May 16 tornado. Apply online from the City's Impacted Tenants Fund page or in person at Employment Connection, 2838 Market Street, weekdays 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.; questions go to 314-333-5627. The page does not give a deadline, so call before you count on it. It is not a fund for ordinary late rent.

Missouri eviction timeline: what happens and when

Most St. Louis evictions for unpaid rent are "rent and possession" cases under RSMo chapter 535. If the landlord says your lease is over and you stayed, the case is usually "unlawful detainer" under chapter 534. City of St. Louis cases are heard by the 22nd Judicial Circuit at the Civil Courts Building, 10 North Tucker Boulevard; St. Louis County cases go to the County's circuit court. These are the steps for a rent case:

  • 1. The demand. The landlord demands the rent. No waiting period or written notice is required before filing for unpaid rent (RSMo 535.020). Ending a month-to-month tenancy for other reasons takes at least one month's written notice (RSMo 441.060).
  • 2. The summons. The court date must be no more than 21 business days after the summons is issued, unless the landlord agrees in writing to a later date, and you must be served at least 4 days before it (RSMo 535.030). If the summons is only posted on your door and mailed, that has to happen at least 10 days before court, and if you then miss court, the judge cannot give the landlord a money judgment against you, but can still order you out (RSMo 535.030).
  • 3. Court. Go. If you were served in person and do not show up, the judge can rule against you for both the money and possession. Bring your lease, receipts, texts, photos of repair problems, and any letter showing the landlord refused your rent. The 22nd Circuit's landlord and tenant resources page posts documents on rent-and-possession defenses and counterclaims.
  • 4. Paying at the hearing. If the rent due "with all costs" is tendered before the judge at the hearing, the judge does not award possession (RSMo 535.040). If you pay all the rent and costs on the date of the money judgment, the case stops; if you pay the judgment and costs after the trial but before the judgment is final, the eviction is stayed (RSMo 535.160). This is where 211 money or a nonprofit check can save the home. It does not work if the judgment is only for possession.
  • 5. The 10 days after judgment. An order to put you out "shall be issued no sooner than ten days after the judgment" (RSMo 534.350). To stay in the home during a new trial or appeal, you must post a bond covering the rent, damages and costs within ten days after the judgment, and keep paying rent into court as it comes due (RSMo 535.110). The deadline to ask for a new trial can also be 10 days (RSMo 512.190), so call a lawyer right away.
  • 6. The sheriff. The officer must deliver possession to the landlord within five days of receiving the order (RSMo 535.040). In the City, the landlord calls the Sheriff's Office to schedule the eviction, and a deputy posts a Final Notice to Vacate. The Sheriff's policy says "The Sheriff's Deputies will not stand-by while the personal property of the tenants is moved," so move your belongings before that day if you can.
  • Lockouts are illegal. A landlord who removes or shuts you out "without judicial process and court order," removes the doors or locks, or cuts off electric, gas, water or sewer to force you out is "guilty of forcible entry and detainer" (RSMo 441.233). If it happens, call the police and a lawyer.

What is not a door for emergency rent

  • The City's Emergency Rental Assistance program (ERA 3) is closed. The City's page says "Applications for ERA3 are closed as of December 16th, 2024 at 8:00 AM CST," and we found no newer round on October 28, 2026. Ignore old flyers and news stories that send you there.
  • St. Patrick Center (outside its veterans program above) says: "Currently, St. Patrick Center does not have rental assistance available." It still does in-person intake for people experiencing homelessness, weekdays at 8 a.m. and 1 p.m., and its number is (314) 802-0700.
  • The St. Louis Housing Authority (SLHA) runs Section 8 and public housing in the City. It is not an emergency rent fund. It says "The Public Housing and Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) waiting lists are currently closed," while "The SLHA Elderly-Only waiting list, however, remains open throughout the year" (checked October 28, 2026). If you already have SLHA housing or a voucher and fall behind, call (314) 531-4770 right away and get a lawyer, because an eviction can cost you your assistance.
  • The Housing Authority of St. Louis County serves the County. It says "we do not have an exact date when our waitlist will reopen" and that it will post a reopening on its website 30 days in advance (checked October 28, 2026). Phone: (314) 428-3200.
  • Shelters serve people who are already homeless. If you could lose your home tonight, see I need housing tonight, call the Housing Help Line at (314) 802-5444, and dial 2-1-1.

Frequently asked questions

Is there emergency rent assistance in St. Louis right now?

Not from a city-wide fund. The City of St. Louis's last Emergency Rental Assistance round (ERA 3) closed to applications on December 16, 2024, and we found no open City or St. Louis County rent program on October 28, 2026. Rent help now comes from nonprofits with limited money. Start with United Way 2-1-1 (dial 211 or 1-800-427-4626) and the St. Louis Housing Help Line at (314) 802-5444. If you have court papers, call HELP-STL or Legal Services of Eastern Missouri the same day.

How do I get a free eviction lawyer in St. Louis?

If you rent in the City of St. Louis, call HELP-STL, the City's Right to Counsel program, at (314) 657-1671 or (314) 657-1654. The City contracts with Legal Services of Eastern Missouri to provide the lawyers. Have your eviction notice or court papers with you. If you rent in St. Louis County, apply to Legal Services of Eastern Missouri directly at (314) 534-4200 or 1-800-444-0514 (phone applications Monday, Wednesday and Thursday, 9 a.m. to 1 p.m.) or online any time.

Does HELP-STL cover St. Louis County?

No. The City says that to be eligible "you need to be a renter living in the City of St. Louis." St. Louis County is a separate government with no Right to Counsel program. County renters can still apply to Legal Services of Eastern Missouri, which lists St. Louis County in its housing law service area, and can dial 211 for rent help.

How fast is an eviction in Missouri?

Fast. For unpaid rent, the landlord only has to demand the rent; no written notice period is required before filing. The court date in a rent-and-possession case can be no more than 21 business days after the summons is issued, and you must be served at least 4 days before it. If the judge rules for the landlord, the order to put you out cannot issue until 10 days after the judgment. After that, the sheriff schedules the eviction.

Can I stop a Missouri eviction by paying the rent?

In a rent-and-possession case, often yes. Missouri law says that if you pay the landlord, or bring into court, all the rent owed and all court costs on the date of the money judgment, the case stops. If you pay the judgment and costs after the trial but before the judgment is final, the eviction is stayed. Bring a money order, cashier's check, or cash for the full amount and proof you paid. A lawyer can tell you the exact amount.

Is the St. Louis Section 8 waiting list open?

Mostly no. The St. Louis Housing Authority says its Public Housing and Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) waiting lists are currently closed, but its Elderly-Only list "remains open throughout the year." The Housing Authority of St. Louis County says it does not have an exact date when its waitlist will reopen. Neither one is an emergency rent fund.

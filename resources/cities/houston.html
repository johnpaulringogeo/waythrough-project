Section 8 and Low-Income Housing in Houston, TX

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page is about getting into subsidized or income-restricted housing in Houston and Harris County: the Section 8 lists at Housing Alliance HTX (the Houston Housing Authority, HHA) and the Harris County Housing Authority, HHA's public housing and other communities, and tax credit (LIHTC) apartments. HHA's voucher list is closed and its 2026 public housing lottery closed on July 7, so for now the open doors are mostly tax credit buildings and HHA's communities that take applications at the property. Texas eviction law also changed on January 1, 2026 (SB 38): the notice to vacate is 3 days. If you are behind on rent or facing an eviction right now, start with the guide linked just below.

Section 8 in Houston: HHA and HCHA Waitlist Status

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in the Houston area are administered by two separate authorities. Current status (checked November 3, 2026):

  • Housing Alliance HTX (formerly the Houston Housing Authority, HHA) serves the City of Houston. HCV waitlist is closed: in June 2026 HHA said "The Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) waitlist remains closed, and no new HCV applications will be accepted," and no reopening has been announced. HHA periodically re-registers existing waitlist members; if you're already on the list, watch your email and mail. Failure to re-register removes you from the list
  • Harris County Housing Authority (HCHA): serves unincorporated Harris County and contracting municipalities outside Houston city limits. HCHA's waiting-list page announces no open list, and says it has no anticipated date for contacting applicants on its 2016 waiting list. (713) 578-2100 · hchatexas.org
  • Public Housing waitlist: separate from HCV. HHA's public housing lottery for eight properties closed on July 7, 2026, after about 56,000 people applied; applicants are notified through their RentCafe account (see the next section)
  • Specialty referrals still accepted: HUD-VASH (veterans), Family Unification Program (FUP), Mainstream vouchers for non-elderly people with disabilities
  • Other Texas authorities you can apply to simultaneously: Pasadena Housing Authority, Pasadena Second Ward HA, Galveston Housing Authority, the Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs statewide voucher program
  • Contact: Housing Alliance HTX (713) 260-0500 · alliancehtx.org

For the national application process, see our step-by-step Section 8 guide.

HHA Public Housing and Other Communities in Houston

HHA's 2026 public housing lottery, its first since 2023, covered eight properties: Kelly Village, Lincoln Park, Long Drive, Oxford Place, Bellerive, Lyerly, Independence Heights and Irvinton Village. Bellerive and Lyerly are only for seniors 62 and older. Applicants could enter for up to three of them, and HHA picked new applicants by random lottery. Applications ran June 22 to July 6, 2026, and the lottery closed at 12:01 a.m. on July 7 after about 56,000 people applied; HHA said it expected to add about 10,000 of them to the waitlists and to tell applicants by email through RentCafe (from HHA's lottery notice and its closing notice). If you are already on a public housing list, HHA says you do not need to do anything to keep your spot.

HHA also has communities outside public housing. Its public housing page (checked November 18, 2026) lists mixed-income communities, Section 8 New Construction, RAD properties and tax credit properties, and says to "contact the property's management office directly" about any of them, because third-party management companies run the day-to-day operations. In HHA's income-based housing, residents pay no more than 30% of adjusted household income toward rent, and HHA says qualifying incomes "generally" range from 30% to 80% of area median income. Project-based voucher lists are tied to specific developments, so ask each office about openings. Check your status or create an account at HHA's RentCafe page, or call (713) 260-0500. Our HHA guide tracks each list.

LIHTC (Tax Credit) Apartments in Houston

Tax credit apartments are privately owned buildings that keep rents below market for households under an income limit, most often 60% of area median income. There is no central waiting list and you do not need a voucher: you apply at each property's leasing office. With HHA's lists closed, this is usually the fastest door. Search HUD's LIHTC database for Harris County, then call the buildings and ask which have openings and what their income limits are. Our guide on how to find LIHTC housing walks through the search.

Rent Help and Eviction Help in Houston

The short version: dial 211 (or 1-877-541-7905), which routes you to whichever provider has funds this month, including Harris County's rental assistance program. West Houston Assistance Ministries (WHAM), Memorial Assistance Ministries (MAM) and St. Vincent de Paul give limited help in their own areas, and Catholic Charities' Harris County rent application was closed when we checked. Lone Star Legal Aid gives free eviction defense in all 16 Harris County Justice of the Peace courts: call or text "APPLY" to 361-35EVICT (361-353-8428). BakerRipley's utility help is closed until January 2027. The Salvation Army Houston takes requests through the "Need Assistance?" form on its site; ask what is available. Texas Rent Relief closed in the summer of 2023.

Tenant Rights in Texas (and Recent Changes)

Texas has one of the more landlord-friendly legal frameworks in the country. SB 38, which took effect on January 1, 2026, made evictions even faster. Houston tenants are covered by the Texas Property Code Chapters 24, 92, and 301:

  • Texas SB 38 sped up evictions (effective January 1, 2026): if the landlord files a motion for summary disposition, you have until the 4th day after you are served to file a written response, or the judge can rule without a trial (Texas Property Code § 24.005106). Contact Lone Star Legal Aid immediately
  • 3-day notice to vacate for nonpayment (Texas Property Code § 24.005), among the shortest in the country. Once you get one, act immediately. See how to avoid eviction
  • No source-of-income protection, and cities are preempted: Texas SB 267 (2015) prohibits cities from passing local source-of-income ordinances. Houston cannot enact one. A landlord can lawfully decline to accept a voucher
  • Security deposit return: 30 days after move-out with itemized deductions (TX § 92.103). Texas does not cap deposits
  • Warranty of habitability and right to repair: TX Property Code Chapter 92 requires diligent effort to repair conditions materially affecting health or safety after written notice. Don't withhold rent without legal advice
  • Notice to end month-to-month: one full rental period (usually 30 days), from either side
  • Retaliatory eviction is illegal (TX § 92.331): within the prior 6 months of a tenant's good-faith complaint or organizing
  • Self-help eviction is illegal (TX § 92.0081): landlords cannot change locks, shut off utilities, or remove belongings. They must go through Justice of the Peace court
  • Justice of the Peace court venue: Harris County has 16 JP precincts that hear evictions. The trial must be held between the 10th and 21st day after the petition is filed (§ 24.0051(g)). After a loss you have 5 days to appeal, and the writ of possession cannot issue before the 6th day after the judgment (§ 24.0061(b))
  • Fair housing: federal Fair Housing Act and TX Property Code Chapter 301 cover race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, and disability

For free legal help: Lone Star Legal Aid. Call or text "APPLY" to its eviction line, 361-35EVICT (361-353-8428), or call the main office at (713) 652-0077 or (800) 733-8394. For state-level details, see our Texas housing resources.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Houston HHA Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

No. The Housing Alliance HTX (formerly Houston Housing Authority, HHA) Housing Choice Voucher waitlist is closed: in June 2026 HHA said the HCV waitlist remains closed and no new HCV applications will be accepted, and no reopening had been announced as of November 3, 2026. Its public housing lottery for eight properties closed on July 7, 2026. HHA periodically re-registers existing waitlist members, and failure to re-register removes you from the list, so watch your email and mail. The Harris County Housing Authority (HCHA) runs separate lists and announces no open list. Specialty referrals for HUD-VASH (veterans) and FUP continue. Call HHA at (713) 260-0500.

How do I find low-income apartments in Houston?

HHA's voucher list is closed, and its 2026 public housing lottery for eight properties closed on July 7, 2026, so the main open route is tax credit (LIHTC) apartments: privately owned buildings with income limits that you apply to at each leasing office. Search HUD's database at huduser.gov/lihtc for Harris County. HHA also has mixed-income, RAD and tax credit communities run by management companies; HHA says to contact each property's management office directly. Outside the city, the Harris County Housing Authority runs its own lists at (713) 578-2100. Call HHA at (713) 260-0500 with questions. For rent help, dial 211; our Houston emergency rent help guide has the steps.

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Houston?

Star of Hope Mission ((713) 222-2220) is Houston's largest shelter system with men's, women's, and family centers. Salvation Army Family Residence ((713) 650-6530), SEARCH Homeless Services ((713) 739-7752, day shelter), Magnificat Houses ((713) 655-4000), and The Beacon ((713) 236-0643, day services) round out the system. Covenant House Texas ((713) 523-7900) serves youth 18-24. The Houston Area Women's Center ((713) 528-7273) has a 24-hour DV crisis line. Call 211 to route through the Coalition for the Homeless Coordinated Access.

Can a Houston landlord refuse Section 8?

Yes. Texas has no statewide source-of-income protection, and Texas SB 267 (2015) prohibits cities like Houston from passing local ordinances requiring landlords to accept vouchers. Finding a landlord who already participates is part of the work. HHA can provide a list of participating landlords.

What is Texas SB 38 and how does it affect Houston tenants?

Texas SB 38 took effect on January 1, 2026, and sped up the eviction process. The landlord still gives a 3-day written notice to vacate (Texas Property Code § 24.005) unless the lease says otherwise. If the landlord files a motion for summary disposition, you must file a written response by the 4th day after you are served, or the judge can rule without a trial. Harris County's 16 Justice of the Peace courts hear evictions, and the trial must be held between the 10th and 21st day after filing. If you're served with eviction papers in Houston, call or text Lone Star Legal Aid's eviction line at 361-353-8428 immediately. Its lawyers work in all 16 Harris County JP courts.

Affordable Housing in Richmond, VA

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in the City of Richmond, Virginia, not generic Section 8 advice. Three things to know: RRHA's Section 8 voucher list is closed and voucher issuance has been paused since May 2025; the City's Eviction Diversion Program is the main rent door, and it starts with a workshop; and Virginia law gives you a 14-day notice and a right to pay and stay. Richmond is an independent city, so Henrico and Chesterfield counties have their own programs. The named resources below are where to start.

Quick numbers to write down:
  • Homeless Connection Line (shelter, 3 days or less): 804-972-0813 · 211 Virginia: dial 2-1-1
  • Richmond Redevelopment and Housing Authority (RRHA): (804) 780-4200 · 600 E. Broad St. · rrha.com
  • Central Virginia Legal Aid Society (free eviction help): 804-648-1012 · cvlas.org
  • Housing Resource Line (more than 3 days out): 804-422-5061

Emergency Help Tonight in Richmond

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or could lose your home in the next few days, start here:

  • Homeless Connection Line (HCL): 804-972-0813. The Greater Richmond Continuum of Care's front door for people "experiencing homelessness or are within 3 days of losing their housing." The City lists its hours as Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. and Saturday and Sunday 1 p.m. to 9 p.m.; the HCL's own site shows a midday break on weekdays. If no one answers, leave a voicemail. hclrva.org
  • Housing Resource Line: 804-422-5061, if you are more than 3 days from losing your housing. Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. The City notes it "does not, however, provide direct resources and services"; it points you to who does
  • 211 Virginia: dial 2-1-1 or text CONNECT to 247211. Live help is Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. 211virginia.org

Section 8 in Richmond: RRHA Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers and public housing in the City of Richmond are run by the Richmond Redevelopment and Housing Authority (RRHA), HUD code VA007. Its 2027 agency plan counts 4,060 vouchers and 3,245 public housing units. Current status (checked November 5, 2026):

  • The Section 8 voucher list is closed. RRHA's 2027 plan answers "Is this waiting list closed?" with "Yes" for its voucher list of 3,650 families. When RRHA opened other lists in May 2026, it said: "This waitlist opening does NOT include RRHA's voucher waitlist"
  • Voucher issuance is paused. On May 20, 2025 RRHA said it "has suspended the issuance of tenant-based vouchers" from the waiting list, and "Once funding is available, RRHA will once again begin selecting from the waitlist." We found no RRHA notice lifting the pause. VASH vouchers for veterans are not affected
  • Public housing and senior lists: RRHA's last opening for public housing family communities (4-bedroom units), senior communities and some project-based sites ran from Monday, May 4, 2026 at 8:30 a.m. through Friday, May 8, 2026 at 5 p.m. It has announced no opening since. Watch rrha.com for the next one
  • Project-based vouchers: RRHA uses a separate waiting list for each project-based community, such as Creighton Renaissance and Armstrong Renaissance. Its PBV page says "PBV applications are accepted year-round," but its news releases describe set opening windows. Check the Applicant Portal, or call, before you count on a list being open
  • RRHA's offices: main office 600 E. Broad St., (804) 780-4200. Voucher (HCVP) and Tenant Selection office: 918 Chamberlayne Parkway, Monday to Friday 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., with walk-in hours Tuesday 9 a.m. to noon and Thursday 1 to 4 p.m.
  • Already on a list? Log in to RRHA's Applicant Portal to check your status and change your contact information. RRHA removed applicants who did not update during its March 2–31, 2026 waitlist purge

Emergency Rental Assistance in Richmond (Named Programs)

Start here: our guide to emergency rent help in Richmond walks through each door step by step: the City's Eviction Diversion Program, free lawyers, 211, Energy Assistance and EnergyShare, and Virginia's 14-day notice and right to pay and stay.

There is no open, walk-in City rent fund. These are the doors that exist. Funding changes, so always call to confirm:

  • Richmond Eviction Diversion Program (City of Richmond, run by Southside Community Development & Housing Corp.): for City of Richmond renters with a written nonpayment notice or an eviction summons, household income no more than 80% of the area median, and a landlord who agrees to take part. Your first step is a live virtual information workshop; the application comes at the end of it. Neither the City nor SCDHC posts whether money is available right now. scdhc.com/eviction-diversion-program
  • Capital Area Partnership Uplifting People (CAPUP): the state housing department lists its Emergency Financial Assistance Program for Richmond: 804-788-0050. Call to ask whether it has funds this month
  • Richmond Department of Social Services: Emergency Assistance for families with children after a fire, natural disaster or "housing crisis." Dial 3-1-1, or (804) 646-7212 from outside the city
  • City of Richmond Housing and Community Development: 804-646-1766, HCD@rva.gov, Main Street Station, 1500 E. Main St., Suite 300. rva.gov eviction prevention page

The state's rent programs

The pandemic-era Virginia Rent Relief Program is closed. The state's Virginia Eviction Reduction Program (VERP) became permanent in 2026, but Richmond is not one of the communities on the state housing department's VERP list (checked November 5, 2026).

Utility assistance: Energy Assistance, EnergyShare and MetroCare

Virginia's Energy Assistance Program helps households at or below 150% of the federal poverty level. Fuel Assistance applications close Friday, November 13, 2026. After that, Crisis Assistance runs November 1, 2026 to March 15, 2027 (help with fuel and utility bills starts in January). Apply through CommonHelp or at 855-635-4370. Dominion Energy's EnergyShare helps customers with a disconnection notice "year-round, regardless of income," up to $700 in the heating season (October 1 to April 30). The City's gas and water utility runs MetroCare for customers at or below 225% of the federal poverty guidelines, "based on eligibility and available funds."

Tenant Rights in Richmond & Virginia

Virginia's tenant rules come from the Virginia Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (VRLTA, Va. Code 55.1-1200 through 55.1-1262). The key protections for Richmond renters:

  • Statewide source-of-funds protection: the Virginia Fair Housing Law makes "source of funds," including a Housing Choice Voucher, a protected class (Va. Code 36-96.3). There are exceptions: an owner of four or fewer rental units in Virginia, and a case where the voucher agency does not approve the tenancy within 15 days (36-96.2). File complaints with the Virginia Fair Housing Office
  • 14-day notice for nonpayment: the landlord can end the lease only if "the tenant fails to pay rent within 14 days after written notice is served" (Va. Code 55.1-1245(F))
  • Right to pay and stay: you can pay all rent, late charges, attorney fees and court costs on or before the first court date, or bring a written promise to pay from a local government or nonprofit, and you can still pay in full at least 48 hours before a scheduled eviction (Va. Code 55.1-1250)
  • Court timing: the first hearing must be "not more than 21 days from the date of filing" (Va. Code 8.01-126). Richmond's General District Court hears civil cases, including evictions, at the John Marshall Courts Building, 400 N. 9th St.
  • Security deposit cap: 2 months' rent, returned with itemized deductions within 45 days of move-out (Va. Code 55.1-1226)
  • Retaliation and lockouts are illegal: landlords cannot retaliate for complaints (55.1-1258), and only the sheriff can remove you, after the 10-day appeal period and at least 72 hours' notice (Va. Code 8.01-129)

For free legal help: Central Virginia Legal Aid Society (804-648-1012) takes eviction cases for low-income Richmond tenants, and the City funds a Right to Counsel program through it. The Legal Aid Justice Center (804-643-1086) also helps tenants. For state-level details, see our Virginia housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint. For another Virginia city, see our Virginia Beach page (same state law, different local programs).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Richmond Section 8 waitlist open right now?

No. The Richmond Redevelopment and Housing Authority (RRHA) lists its Housing Choice Voucher waiting list as closed, and when it opened other lists in May 2026 it said that opening "does NOT include RRHA's voucher waitlist." RRHA also said on May 20, 2025 that it had suspended issuing tenant-based vouchers from the list until funding is available, and we found no notice lifting that pause. Its last public housing, senior and project-based opening ran from May 4 to May 8, 2026 at 5 p.m. Watch rrha.com for the next window, and call RRHA at (804) 780-4200 with questions. (Checked November 5, 2026.)

Can a Richmond landlord refuse my Section 8 voucher?

Usually not. The Virginia Fair Housing Law makes source of funds, including a Housing Choice Voucher, a protected class (Va. Code 36-96.3). There are two exceptions: an owner who owns four or fewer rental units in Virginia, and a case where the voucher agency does not approve the tenancy within 15 days of your request (Va. Code 36-96.2). If you are refused, contact the Virginia Fair Housing Office or Central Virginia Legal Aid Society at 804-648-1012.

Where can I get emergency rental assistance in Richmond?

There is no walk-in City rent fund. The main door is the City's Eviction Diversion Program, run by Southside Community Development and Housing Corp. It is for City of Richmond renters with a written nonpayment notice or an eviction summons, income at or below 80% of the area median, and a landlord who agrees to take part. Your first step is a virtual information workshop. You can also ask Capital Area Partnership Uplifting People at 804-788-0050 about emergency financial help, and dial 211 for anything else open this month. Richmond is not on the state's Eviction Reduction Program list. (Checked November 5, 2026.)

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Richmond?

Call the Homeless Connection Line at 804-972-0813. It serves people who are homeless or within 3 days of losing their housing, and the City lists its hours as weekdays 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. and weekends 1 p.m. to 9 p.m. If no one answers, leave a voicemail. If you are more than 3 days from losing your home, call the Housing Resource Line at 804-422-5061, weekdays 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.

Does Richmond have free eviction lawyers?

Virginia law gives no right to a free lawyer in an eviction. But the City of Richmond funds a Right to Counsel program, and in June 2026 the City pointed renters who need legal help with a housing matter to Central Virginia Legal Aid Society to apply. Call CVLAS at 804-648-1012 (toll-free 800-868-1012), Monday to Friday 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., or apply online at cvlas.org. CVLAS generally serves households under 125% of the federal poverty guidelines, and up to 200% in some cases. The Legal Aid Justice Center also takes tenant calls at 804-643-1086. (Checked November 5, 2026.)

Affordable Housing in Omaha, NE

Waythrough Project · Updated October 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Omaha and Douglas County — not generic Section 8 advice. Nebraska adopted the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA), which gives Omaha tenants stronger statutory protections than tenants in many neighboring states — including a 7-day notice for nonpayment, a 1-month deposit cap, and a 14-day deposit return rule. The named resources below are where to start.

Quick numbers to write down:
  • 211 Nebraska (United Way Heartland) — dial 211 (free, 24/7) for any housing emergency in Douglas County
  • Omaha Housing Authority (OHA): (402) 444-6900 · ohauthority.org
  • Threshold CoC (formerly MACCH; homelessness coordinated entry): call 211 for prevention help · thresholdcoc.org/resources
  • Women's Center for Advancement (DV 24-hr): (402) 345-7273

Emergency Help Tonight in Omaha

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or are facing an imminent eviction, these are the local resources to contact first:

  • Siena/Francis House — Omaha's largest comprehensive emergency shelter, day services, and supportive housing campus. (402) 341-1821. sienafrancis.org
  • Open Door Mission — large emergency shelter, soup kitchen, and recovery programs for men, women, and families. opendoormission.org
  • Stephen Center — emergency shelter and addiction recovery programs for men, women, and families. Comprehensive HERO program. stephencenter.org
  • Salvation Army Omaha — emergency shelter at the Western Divisional Headquarters and corps locations
  • The Lighthouse / Compass Center — emergency shelter for youth and young adults experiencing homelessness
  • Women's Center for Advancement (WCA) — domestic violence shelter and 24-hour crisis line: (402) 345-7273. Bilingual advocates
  • Heartland Family Service — emergency shelter and DV/family services with Spanish-language staff
  • Threshold Continuum of Care — Coordinated Entry — central intake operated through MACCH (Metro Area Continuum of Care for the Homeless). Call 211
  • 211 Nebraska — free 24/7 information line for shelters, food, financial assistance, and social services

Section 8 in Omaha: OHA Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Omaha are administered by the Omaha Housing Authority (OHA). The separate Douglas County Housing Authority serves smaller communities around the metro. Current status (checked October 15, 2026):

  • The OHA HCV waitlist is closed. OHA’s homepage says: “The Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) wait list is currently closed.” OHA publishes no closing or reopening date; the most recent opening it documents ran August 8–14, 2023, followed by a lottery. OHA estimates the wait at six months to two years once you are on the list, and says openings are announced in the Omaha World-Herald, the Omaha Star and El Perico at least seven business days ahead. Watch ohauthority.org/housing-vouchers/wait-list-information for the next opening
  • OHA notes that Section 8 is not an emergency housing program — for tonight, use the shelter resources above
  • Public Housing through OHA — a separate program. OHA says that since January 1, 2025 the public housing list is “only open for one bedroom apartments in our Senior Towers to applicants ages 62 and older”
  • Douglas County Housing Authority — a separate authority for “the subdivisions and small community jurisdictions in the greater Omaha metropolitan area”; its site said on October 15, 2026 that it is not accepting Section 8 applications. douglascountyhousing.com
  • Project-Based Voucher (PBV) lists at specific properties may be open
  • Other special programs: HUD-VASH for veterans, Mainstream vouchers for non-elderly people with disabilities — separate referral processes
  • Apply to neighboring authorities too: the Municipal Housing Agency of Council Bluffs (across the river in Iowa), the Bellevue Housing Authority (Sarpy County), and the Nebraska Investment Finance Authority (NIFA, statewide) run separate lists
  • Status check: call OHA at (402) 444-6900 if you've already applied and need to verify your position

Our full guide to OHA: Omaha Housing Authority (OHA): waitlist status, contact and how to apply. For the national application process, see our step-by-step Section 8 guide and how to find your PHA.

Emergency Rental Assistance in Omaha (Named Programs)

If you're behind on rent or can't pay this month, these are the local programs currently operating in Omaha. Funding shifts month to month — always call to confirm current availability. Our Omaha emergency rent help guide walks through each door: who qualifies, what it pays, how to apply, and Nebraska's eviction timeline.

  • Threshold CoC (formerly MACCH) — in its own words, it "coordinates the collective response to housing justice for Douglas, Sarpy, and Pottawattamie Counties." Its resources page sends renters who are still housed to 211 ("please contact 211 to find a list of prevention resources available") and people experiencing homelessness to its Access Points for Coordinated Entry (checked October 16, 2026). It has no rent application of its own. thresholdcoc.org/resources
  • Douglas County General Assistance — the County's "program of last resort" pays rent or utilities directly to the landlord or utility for Douglas County residents ages 19 to 64-67 who are not eligible for other public assistance; "Households with monthly net income that exceeds $850 are not eligible for assistance" (checked October 16, 2026). No deposits, first month's rent, or application fees. Appointment line (402) 444-6215. generalassist.douglascounty-ne.gov
  • Heartland Family Service Homeless Prevention — for households that "Must be at imminent risk of homelessness (losing primary residence within the next 14 days)"; case management for up to three months, and financial help "on a limited basis." (531) 200-3500. heartlandfamilyservice.org
  • City of Omaha — the City's renter program, HOME Tenant-Based Rental Assistance, is run by OHA and takes referrals only from transitional housing or support service programs; you cannot apply to it directly (checked October 16, 2026). planninghcd.cityofomaha.org/for-renters
  • ENCAP (Eastern Nebraska Community Action Partnership) no longer pays rent. Its own page says: "We no longer provide emergency financial assistance" (checked October 16, 2026). It refers people to Together, St. Vincent de Paul, and Douglas County General Assistance. encapnebraska.org
  • Legal Aid of Nebraska — free legal help for low-income tenants facing eviction. No walk-ins: call the AccessLine at 1-877-250-2016 (Monday and Wednesday 8:30–11:30 a.m., Tuesday and Thursday 1–3 p.m.) or apply online. legalaidofnebraska.org
  • Tenant Assistance Project — the Nebraska State Bar Association's Volunteer Lawyers Project sends volunteer lawyers and law students to help unrepresented low-income tenants who appear for their eviction hearings at the Douglas County Courthouse. Go to your hearing. nevlp.org
  • Catholic Charities of Omaha — food pantries, domestic violence services, and immigration legal services. Its services list did not include rent help on October 16, 2026
  • St. Vincent de Paul Society of Omaha — rent and utility help through its online Helpline application; help varies with available resources and ZIP code. ssvpomaha.org
  • Salvation Army Western Divisional Headquarters — eviction prevention and utility assistance
  • Heart Ministry Center — emergency food, financial assistance, and case management for North Omaha residents
  • Together — walk-in Housing Stability Clinic at 2419 Leavenworth Street (Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday, entry 10 a.m.–2 p.m.); rental or utility assistance "when funding and eligibility allow." (402) 345-8047. togetheromaha.org
  • Latino Center of the Midlands — bilingual case management and emergency assistance

The Nebraska state ERA has ended

The Nebraska Emergency Rental Assistance (ERA) program has closed — all federal pandemic funds are distributed. The Nebraska Investment Finance Authority (NIFA) administered the program. Current paths are 211, Douglas County General Assistance, Together, Heartland Family Service, and the other named nonprofits above. Don't waste time on closed NIFA ERA portals from 2021–2023.

Utility assistance: LIHEAP

Nebraska's LIHEAP is the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program. Apply by calling (402) 595-1258 (Omaha) or 1-800-383-4278 toll-free. OPPD has utility assistance and arrearage programs.

Tenant Rights in Nebraska

Nebraska adopted the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA), giving Omaha tenants stronger statutory protections than tenants in many neighboring landlord-friendly states:

  • Nebraska URLTA (Neb. Rev. Stat. §§76-1401 to 76-1449): applies to most residential rentals statewide. Establishes clear notice periods, deposit rules, habitability standards, and anti-retaliation protections
  • 7-day notice for nonpayment (Neb. Rev. Stat. §76-1431). Among the shorter notice periods, but URLTA gives the tenant the right to pay the back rent within 7 days to stop the eviction
  • 14-day notice for lease violations with a 14-day cure period
  • 30-day notice to end month-to-month from either party
  • Security deposit cap: Nebraska limits the deposit to 1 month's rent (plus a separate pet deposit allowed up to one-quarter of one month's rent). Must be returned with itemized deductions within 14 days of move-out (Neb. Rev. Stat. §76-1416). Tenant must provide a forwarding address
  • Warranty of habitability: Neb. Rev. Stat. §76-1419 requires landlords to maintain habitable conditions, comply with codes, provide working plumbing and heating, and make necessary repairs after notice
  • Retaliatory eviction is illegal under Neb. Rev. Stat. §76-1439 within 1 year of tenant complaints to officials, joining a tenants' association, or exercising legal rights
  • Self-help eviction is illegal: Neb. Rev. Stat. §76-1430 strictly prohibits lockouts, utility shutoffs, and removal of belongings. Violations carry liability for actual damages plus up to 3 months' rent plus reasonable attorneys' fees
  • No statewide source-of-income protection: Nebraska does not have a statewide law preventing landlords from refusing Section 8 vouchers. Omaha has not (yet) passed a local source-of-income protection ordinance. Finding a landlord who already accepts vouchers is part of the work — OHA says a listing of units available for the program may be obtained from OHA
  • Fair housing: discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability is illegal under federal law and the Nebraska Fair Housing Act. The Nebraska Equal Opportunity Commission and the Omaha Human Rights and Relations Department handle complaints

For free legal help: Legal Aid of Nebraska represents low-income tenants; call its AccessLine at 1-877-250-2016 (no walk-ins). For state-level details, see our Nebraska housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Other Housing Programs in Omaha

  • Public housing: OHA owns and manages public-housing communities across Omaha. Application is separate from Section 8; the list is open only for one-bedroom senior tower apartments, ages 62 and older
  • LIHTC (Tax Credit): Omaha has substantial LIHTC inventory. Search HUD's LIHTC database for properties in Douglas County. See how to find LIHTC housing
  • Omaha Affordable Housing Trust Fund — local funding for affordable housing development; new income-restricted apartments come online each year
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): combines a voucher with VA case management. Omaha-area veterans are referred through the Omaha VA Medical Center. See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Rapid Rehousing & Permanent Supportive Housing — coordinated through MACCH and the Threshold CoC. Access via 211
  • Nebraska Investment Finance Authority (NIFA) programs — first-time homebuyer assistance, NIFA Loans, and statewide affordable housing development. nifa.org
  • Holy Name Housing Corporation — North Omaha affordable housing development and rentals
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator — Family Housing Advisory Services and Omaha 100 cover the area

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Omaha Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

No. As of October 15, 2026, the Omaha Housing Authority (OHA) says: "The Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) wait list is currently closed." OHA publishes no closing or reopening date, and the most recent opening it documents ran August 8 to 14, 2023. OHA estimates the wait at six months to two years once you are on the list. Watch ohauthority.org for the next opening. OHA notes that Section 8 is not an emergency housing program. The Douglas County Housing Authority is a separate agency for smaller communities around the metro, and it was not accepting Section 8 applications either. Call OHA at (402) 444-6900 for waitlist status.

Where can I get emergency rental assistance in Omaha?

Threshold CoC (formerly MACCH) leads Omaha's homeless-services network, and its resources page, thresholdcoc.org/resources, says that if you are still housed you should "contact 211 to find a list of prevention resources available"; if you are experiencing homelessness, contact one of its Access Points for Coordinated Entry (checked October 16, 2026); it has no rent application of its own. Douglas County General Assistance pays rent directly to landlords for residents ages 19 to 64-67 whose household net income is not over $850 a month; call (402) 444-6215. Together's walk-in Housing Stability Clinic at 2419 Leavenworth Street helps with rent when funding allows, and Heartland Family Service, (531) 200-3500, helps people who could lose their home within 14 days. ENCAP says it no longer provides emergency financial assistance. For free legal help, call Legal Aid of Nebraska's AccessLine at 1-877-250-2016.

What is the Nebraska URLTA?

Nebraska adopted the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA, Neb. Rev. Stat. §§76-1401 to 76-1449), which applies to most residential rentals statewide. It gives tenants clear protections — including a 7-day notice for nonpayment, 14-day deposit return, 1-month security deposit cap, anti-retaliation rules, and explicit habitability standards. Self-help eviction violations carry liability for 3 months' rent plus attorneys' fees under §76-1430. This is stronger than the framework in many landlord-friendly states.

What's Nebraska's security deposit law?

Nebraska URLTA caps security deposits at 1 month's rent (plus a separate pet deposit allowed up to one-quarter of one month's rent). The landlord must return the deposit, with any itemized deductions, within 14 days of move-out — but the tenant must provide a forwarding address (Neb. Rev. Stat. §76-1416). This is much shorter than most states' 30-60 day return rules and protects tenants from landlords sitting on deposits.

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Omaha?

Siena/Francis House at (402) 341-1821 is Omaha's largest comprehensive emergency shelter and supportive housing campus. Open Door Mission serves men, women, and families. Stephen Center has shelter plus addiction recovery (HERO program). Salvation Army Omaha and The Lighthouse (Compass Center) also operate emergency shelters. Women's Center for Advancement has a 24-hour DV crisis line at (402) 345-7273. Call 211 to route through MACCH Coordinated Entry.

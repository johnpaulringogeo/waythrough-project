Affordable Housing in Memphis, TN

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Memphis and Shelby County, not generic Section 8 advice. If you need shelter tonight, want to know whether MHA's voucher list is open, or are looking for an organization that can help with this month's rent, the named resources below are where to start.

Quick numbers to write down:
  • LINC 2-1-1: dial 211 (Memphis Public Libraries with United Way of the Mid-South) for housing, utility, and other help in Shelby County
  • Memphis Housing Authority (MHA): (901) 544-1100 main · (901) 544-1347 HCV · memphisha.org
  • Shelby County CEAP (rent help by appointment, new slots the 1st Monday of each month): shelbycountycsa.org
  • MIFA (homelessness prevention): (901) 527-0208 · mifa.org

Emergency Help Tonight in Memphis

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or are facing an imminent eviction, these are the local resources to contact first:

  • Hospitality Hub of Memphis: Memphis's main downtown day shelter and engagement center. Connects people experiencing homelessness with shelter, services, and case management. hospitalityhub.org
  • Memphis Union Mission: emergency shelter for men, with meals and addiction recovery programs
  • Salvation Army Memphis Area Command: emergency shelter for women, children, and families, plus rental assistance and disaster response
  • Dorothy Day House: emergency shelter that keeps families together, including fathers (rare in shelter systems)
  • Family Promise of Memphis: congregational network shelter and rapid rehousing for families with children
  • Family Safety Center: domestic violence intake, advocacy, and shelter referrals. 24-hour line: (901) 222-4400
  • YWCA Greater Memphis: domestic violence shelter and crisis services
  • Community Alliance for the Homeless: Memphis-Shelby CoC lead agency, runs Coordinated Entry. cafth.org
  • LINC 2-1-1: Memphis's 211 information line, run by the Memphis Public Libraries with United Way of the Mid-South, for shelters, food, financial assistance, and social services

Section 8 in Memphis: MHA Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Memphis are administered by the Memphis Housing Authority (MHA). Current status (checked September 9, 2026):

  • The HCV waitlist is closed as of September 9, 2026 with no scheduled reopening. MHA opens the list periodically and advertises publicly; watch memphisha.org for announcements
  • If you're already on the waitlist: MHA notifies you by U.S. Mail to a physical mailing address when you reach the top. You must keep your address current with MHA by submitting an Applicant Status Update form. If mail is returned undeliverable or you miss a scheduled appointment, your family is removed from the list
  • Public Housing through MHA is a separate program with its own waitlist; some site-specific public-housing lists open more often than the general HCV list
  • Project-Based Voucher (PBV) lists at specific Memphis properties may be open even when the general HCV list is closed
  • Other special programs: HUD-VASH for veterans, Mainstream vouchers for non-elderly people with disabilities (separate referral processes)
  • Apply to neighboring authorities too: Millington Housing Authority, Fayette County, Tipton County, and the Tennessee Housing Development Agency (THDA, statewide) run separate lists
  • Status check: call MHA at (901) 544-1100, or the HCV line at (901) 544-1347, if you've already applied and need to verify your position on the list

For the national application process, see our step-by-step Section 8 guide and how to find your PHA.

Emergency Rental Assistance in Memphis (Named Programs)

If you're behind on rent or can't pay this month, these are the local programs currently operating in Memphis. Funding shifts month to month, so always call to confirm current availability:

  • Shelby County Comprehensive Emergency Assistance Program (CEAP): operated by the Shelby County Community Services Agency, 901-222-4200. Rent or mortgage help for Shelby County households at or below 200% of the federal poverty guidelines with a documented loss or cut in wages (seniors 60 and older need proof of income). Important: help requires an appointment booked online, and new appointments are released on the first Monday of each month; a "Bad Request" message means the scheduler is full. The CSA's page does not list a dollar amount. Book at shelbycountycsa.org (checked November 3, 2026)
  • MIFA (Metropolitan Inter-Faith Association): Memphis's largest emergency assistance nonprofit. Combines rental help, eviction prevention, Meals on Wheels, and case management. Apply online at mifa.org/applyonline, Monday through Friday from 7 a.m. to noon only. Help Desk (901) 529-4538; main office (901) 527-0208
  • LINC 2-1-1: referrals to current rental, utility, and shelter programs. Dial 211
  • Memphis Area Legal Services (MALS): free legal help for tenants facing eviction. malsi.org
  • Catholic Charities of West Tennessee: (901) 722-4700. Its Emergency Services list food, clothing, hygiene supplies, and disaster relief; its site does not list rent help, so call to ask
  • Society of St. Vincent de Paul, District Council of Memphis: rent and utility help through local parish conferences; ask at a Catholic parish near you
  • Salvation Army Kroc Center: lists rent and utility assistance among the help local donations pay for; call 901-729-8007
  • Welcome Home Memphis (home901.org): housing search and counseling for voucher holders

For the step-by-step version (how CEAP's first-Monday appointments work, MIFA's weekday application window, MLGW bill help, and free eviction lawyers), see our Memphis emergency rent help guide.

The federal pandemic ERA has ended

The Memphis & Shelby County Emergency Rent and Utility Assistance Program from the pandemic era has closed. All federal funds are spent. Current paths in Memphis are CEAP, MIFA, and the named nonprofits above. Don't waste time on old 2021–2023 application portals.

Utility assistance: LIHEAP

In Tennessee, the Low-Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) is administered by community action agencies; in Shelby County, that is the same Shelby County CSA that runs CEAP. As of November 3, 2026, the CSA's utility page says "Utility Assistance is not offered at this time," so check shelbycountycsa.org for its reopening. Meanwhile, MLGW customers can apply for MLGW's Plus-1 help in MLGW My Account or the MLGW app (once per calendar year; Community Office appointments at 901-544-6549), and Neighborhood Christian Centers runs a City of Memphis program for MLGW bills that opens on the third Wednesday of each month while funds are available.

Tenant Rights in Tennessee

Memphis is in Shelby County, which is covered by Tennessee's Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA), and that's good news, because URLTA gives stronger tenant protections than the older common-law rules in Tennessee's smaller counties:

  • Memphis IS covered by URLTA: URLTA applies to counties with more than 75,000 residents: Shelby (Memphis), Davidson (Nashville), Knox (Knoxville), Hamilton (Chattanooga), and others. The protections below apply to Memphis tenancies
  • No source-of-income protection: Tennessee does not have a statewide law preventing landlords from refusing Section 8 vouchers. Memphis has no local ordinance. Finding a landlord who already accepts vouchers is part of the work. MHA can provide a list of participating landlords
  • 14-day notice for nonpayment (TCA §66-28-505), longer than many states. You have 14 days to pay or the landlord can terminate. Use those days to call CEAP or MIFA. Check your lease, though: if it says you "waive" written notice, the landlord can go straight to court once rent is more than 5 days late
  • 30-day notice for month-to-month termination from either party (TCA §66-28-512)
  • Security deposit return: the landlord must mail (or hand-deliver) an itemized list of deductions within 30 days of move-out (TCA §66-28-301). Tennessee does not cap deposit amounts under URLTA
  • Warranty of habitability: TCA §66-28-304 requires landlords to comply with building and housing codes, keep common areas safe, provide working plumbing, heating, hot water, and structural soundness
  • Retaliatory eviction is illegal under TCA §66-28-514 if the landlord acts in response to good-faith tenant complaints, joining a tenants' association, or exercising legal rights
  • Self-help eviction is illegal: Tennessee prohibits landlord lockouts, utility shutoffs, and removal of belongings. They must go through Shelby County General Sessions Court
  • Fair housing: discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability is illegal under federal law and Tennessee Human Rights Act. The Tennessee Human Rights Commission and HUD handle complaints

For free legal help: Memphis Area Legal Services (MALS) represents low-income tenants facing eviction; West Tennessee Legal Services also serves the broader region. For state-level details, see our Tennessee housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Other Housing Programs in Memphis

  • Public housing: MHA owns public-housing communities across Memphis. Application is separate from Section 8. Ask when you call (901) 544-1100
  • LIHTC (Tax Credit): privately owned income-restricted apartments. Memphis has substantial LIHTC inventory. Search HUD's LIHTC database for properties in Shelby County. See how to find LIHTC housing
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): combines a voucher with VA case management. Memphis-area veterans are referred through the Memphis VA Medical Center. See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Rapid Rehousing & Permanent Supportive Housing: coordinated through the Community Alliance for the Homeless. Access via 211 or Coordinated Entry intake
  • Tennessee Housing Development Agency (THDA) programs: Great Choice Home Loan, down-payment assistance, Section 8 in non-MHA counties. thda.org
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator. United Housing and RISE Foundation cover Memphis

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Memphis Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

The voucher list is closed, but five senior public housing lists are open, and that is the part most people miss. On the voucher side MHA says: "MHA's Housing Choice Voucher Program (HCVP) waiting list is currently closed and is not accepting applications at this time," and "MHA will publicly advertise when the waiting list re-opens." No reopening date is published. On the public housing side, MHA's own page reads (its grammar): "A selected Public Housing Waitlists are OPEN!" and names five senior properties: Latham Terrace (901-774-0151), Lyons Ridge (901-948-5165), Fairway Manor (901-930-2878), Cleaborn Senior (901-572-2303) and Legends North (901-244-3200). Most are age 62+; Lyons Ridge also takes ages 55-61 with a disability. MHA publishes no opening or closing date for these, so confirm before you rely on it. If you are already on a public housing list, MHA warns: "you must confirm your continued interest every six (6) months through the Applicant Portal... Failure to update your information may result in removal from the waiting list." Call MHA at (901) 544-1100, or HCV customer service at (901) 544-1347. (Checked September 9, 2026.)

Where can I get emergency rental assistance in Memphis?

Two main paths. The Shelby County Comprehensive Emergency Assistance Program (CEAP) helps with rent by appointment only; new appointments are released on the first Monday of each month at shelbycountycsa.org, and the CSA does not list a dollar amount. MIFA (Metropolitan Inter-Faith Association) takes online applications at mifa.org/applyonline Monday through Friday from 7 a.m. to noon; its main office is (901) 527-0208. The pandemic-era federal ERA has ended.

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Memphis?

Hospitality Hub of Memphis is the main downtown day shelter and engagement center. Memphis Union Mission serves men with addiction recovery. Salvation Army Memphis Area Command shelters women, children, and families. Dorothy Day House keeps families together, including fathers. Family Safety Center has a 24-hour DV line at (901) 222-4400. Call 211 to be routed through the Community Alliance for the Homeless Coordinated Entry.

Can a Memphis landlord refuse Section 8?

Yes. Tennessee has no statewide source-of-income protection, and Memphis has no local ordinance requiring landlords to accept vouchers. Finding a landlord who already participates is part of the work. MHA can provide a list of participating landlords.

How long is the eviction notice in Tennessee?

If you live in Memphis, you're covered by Tennessee's Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA, applies to counties over 75,000 residents). URLTA gives a 14-day notice for nonpayment under TCA §66-28-505, longer than many states' 3-day notices. Use those days to contact CEAP, MIFA, or Memphis Area Legal Services. Self-help eviction (lockouts, utility shutoffs) is illegal. The landlord must go through Shelby County General Sessions Court.

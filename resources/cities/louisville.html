Affordable Housing in Louisville, KY

Waythrough Project · Updated October 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Louisville and Jefferson County, not generic Section 8 advice. Louisville's biggest local advantage: in April 2021, Louisville Metro passed a Right to Counsel law (Metro Code section 151.60) for tenants in Eviction Court whose income is not more than 125% of the federal poverty guidelines, with the free lawyers coming from the Legal Aid Society. Louisville Metro also still runs two small rent programs, Housing Stabilization and HOPE, through Neighborhood Place. Louisville is also one of the Kentucky cities covered by the Kentucky URLTA (stronger tenant protections than the rest of the state). The named resources below are where to start.

Quick numbers to write down:
  • 211 Kentucky / Metro United Way 211: dial 211 (free, 24/7) for any housing emergency in Jefferson County
  • Louisville Metro Housing Authority (LMHA): (502) 569-3400 · lmha1.org · waitlist status line (502) 584-1704 · Section 8 (502) 569-6060
  • Stop My Eviction (Louisville Metro's pointer for rental help): stopmyeviction.org
  • Metro rent appointments (Housing Stabilization / HOPE): (502) 308-3344 · Legal Aid Society (Right to Counsel): (502) 584-1254

Emergency Help Tonight in Louisville

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or are facing an imminent eviction, these are the local resources to contact first:

  • Wayside Christian Mission: Louisville's largest emergency shelter system for men, women, and families. Multiple locations downtown and across the metro. waysidechristianmission.org
  • Salvation Army Center of Hope (Family Lodge): emergency shelter for families and the Center of Hope men's shelter
  • St. John Center for Homeless Men: day shelter and case management. stjohncenter.org
  • Volunteers of America Mid-States: emergency and supportive housing programs across Louisville
  • St. Vincent de Paul Louisville: Center for the Homeless (Holcomb House) for men, plus family services and St. Jude Women's Recovery Center
  • The Center for Women and Families: domestic violence shelter and 24-hour crisis line: (502) 581-7222. Bilingual advocates
  • YouthBuild Louisville and The Sunrise Children's Services: youth and family shelter programs
  • Coalition for the Homeless of Louisville and Southern Indiana Coordinated Entry: single intake point. Call 211
  • 211 Metro United Way: free 24/7 information line for shelters, food, financial assistance, and social services

Section 8 in Louisville: LMHA Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Louisville are administered by the Louisville Metro Housing Authority (LMHA). Current status (checked October 15, 2026):

  • LMHA is not taking Section 8 or public housing applications as of October 15, 2026. LMHA's own wording is: "Please be advised that we are not accepting Housing Choice Voucher applications at this time." Its Rental Process page says the application process "has been put on hold" since July 1, 2024, and its applicant portal says: “New applications are NOT being taken at this time for Section 8 or Public Housing. Applications are closed.” It publishes no reopening date
  • Public Housing through LMHA is a separate program. LMHA’s central list is closed, per its portal. Six privately managed communities, including Liberty Green, Sheppard Square and Villages of Park DuValle, keep their own lists at their own offices, and LMHA publishes no status for them. lmha1.org/housing/application_for_public_housing
  • Project-Based Voucher (PBV) and RAD-converted properties keep separate site-based lists, mostly run by the property owners. LMHA’s PBV site list says “Availability is not guaranteed. Please contact the site for unit information.” Watch lmha1.org/section_8 for LMHA announcements
  • Other special programs: HUD-VASH for veterans, Mainstream vouchers for non-elderly people with disabilities (separate referral processes)
  • Apply to neighboring authorities too: the Housing Authority of Shelbyville and the Housing Authority of Bardstown, the New Albany and Jeffersonville housing authorities across the river in Indiana, and the Kentucky Housing Corporation (KHC) statewide voucher program for areas without a local authority
  • Status check: call (502) 584-1704 for your waitlist position, (502) 569-6060 for Section 8 customer service, or (502) 569-3400 for general LMHA inquiries

Emergency Rental Assistance in Louisville (Named Programs)

Start here: our guide to emergency rent help in Louisville walks through each door step by step: Metro's Housing Stabilization and HOPE programs, Right to Counsel through the Legal Aid Society, 211, LIHEAP, and Kentucky's eviction deadlines.

If you're behind on rent or can't pay this month, these are the local programs currently operating in Louisville. Funding shifts month to month, so always call to confirm current availability:

  • Kentucky's pandemic rent program has ended. Team Kentucky's Healthy at Home Eviction Relief Fund is closed: the Governor's office announced that rent and utility applications through its portal "will no longer be reviewed beginning at 5 p.m. on Friday, Jan. 20, 2023." Its successor, the Team Kentucky Eviction Diversion Program, never covered Jefferson County and "stopped accepting applications on Tuesday, April 1, 2025." For rental help now, Louisville Metro's Eviction Prevention page says: "For information or resources about rental assistance, visit Stop My Eviction." (checked September 21, 2026)
  • Louisville Metro Housing Stabilization Program (Office of Social Services, Neighborhood Place): "both long- and short-term rental assistance and case management" for income-eligible households that meet work-history requirements. Stop My Eviction says it can pay "up to $800 towards rent (paid directly to their landlord) or Louisville Water Company bills" for working heads of household with 250 hours of documented work in the past 6 months, once every 12 months. Book at (502) 308-3344, option 1, or online (checked November 4, 2026). louisvilleky.gov Neighborhood Place assistance programs
  • HOPE Assistance Program (Office of Social Services): for "senior and disabled populations living on a fixed-income," to reduce what they owe their leasing agent, LG&E and Louisville Water. Book at (502) 308-3344, option 2
  • Stop My Eviction: the site Louisville Metro points renters to. It lists the current rent help above, Community Ministries, and court information, and says to call the Legal Aid Society at 502-584-1254 for eviction court. stopmyeviction.org
  • Legal Aid Society Louisville: free legal help for low-income tenants facing eviction. The primary provider of Louisville's Right to Counsel for eviction. Call (502) 584-1254 or (800) 292-1862; intake is Monday to Thursday, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., and you can apply online any time. yourlegalaid.org
  • Catholic Charities of Louisville: emergency financial assistance, food, immigration legal services. Spanish-language services available
  • St. Vincent de Paul Louisville: one-time emergency rental and utility help through local parish conferences
  • Salvation Army Louisville Area Command: Stop My Eviction lists its rent help as "(ON HOLD UNTIL JANUARY 2027)" (checked November 4, 2026)
  • Metro United Way Family Stabilization & Solutions Center: wraparound case management for families at risk of homelessness

The federal pandemic ERA has ended

Federal pandemic Emergency Rental Assistance funds distributed through Kentucky have largely been spent, and both state relief programs have stopped taking applications. Don't waste time on closed pandemic-era portals. Start with Stop My Eviction, which Louisville Metro points to for rental help.

Utility assistance: LIHEAP

Kentucky's LIHEAP is administered locally. In Jefferson County, the Louisville Metro Office of Social Services runs it as the local Community Action Agency. The LIHEAP Fall Subsidy is open now; enrollment runs "Nov. 9-Dec. 18, 2026, or until designated funds are depleted, on a first-come, first-serve basis," for households at or below 150% of the federal poverty guidelines. Book an appointment at (502) 991-8391 or online at louisvilleky.gov/LIHEAP (checked November 11, 2026).

Tenant Rights in Louisville & Kentucky

Louisville has the strongest local tenant protections in Kentucky. Two big features set it apart from the rest of the state:

  • Louisville Right to Counsel for Eviction (April 2021): Louisville Metro Ordinance No. 52-2021 (Metro Code section 151.60) covers "Any proceeding in Jefferson County District Court, Eviction Court" for tenants "whose annual gross income is not in excess of 125% of the federal poverty guidelines." Free legal representation is available for income-eligible tenants in Jefferson District Court eviction cases. Apply through the Legal Aid Society of Louisville at (502) 584-1254 or yourlegalaid.org. Contact them the day you receive a court summons
  • Kentucky URLTA applies in Louisville: Louisville is one of the Kentucky cities that adopted the Kentucky Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA, KRS Chapter 383). This gives Louisville tenants stronger statutory protections than tenants in non-URLTA Kentucky counties
  • 7-day notice for nonpayment under URLTA (KRS §383.660). Use those days to seek rent help through Stop My Eviction and contact Legal Aid
  • 14-day notice for lease violations: the notice must give at least 14 days, and if the tenant fixes the breach before the date in the notice, the lease does not end (KRS §383.660(1))
  • 30-day notice to end month-to-month from either party (KRS §383.695)
  • After a judgment, 7 days: a tenant can appeal within 7 days but must deposit the rent owed into court and keep paying rent there during the appeal (KRS §383.255); if no appeal is filed by the seventh day, the court issues a warrant of restitution on request (KRS §383.245)
  • Security deposits (KRS §383.580): the landlord must keep deposits in a separate account and give signed move-in and move-out damage lists; a landlord who skips those cannot keep any of the deposit. If you leave owing no rent, the landlord must send notice of the refund, and may keep it only if you do not respond within 60 days. The statute sets no cap on the deposit amount
  • Warranty of habitability: KRS §383.595 (under URLTA) requires landlords in covered counties to maintain habitable units. The Louisville Codes & Regulations Department handles complaints
  • Retaliatory eviction is illegal under KRS §383.705 after a tenant complains to a code agency or to the landlord about repairs, or joins a tenants' union; a complaint within 1 year before the landlord's action creates a presumption of retaliation. The protection does not apply if the tenant is behind on rent
  • Self-help eviction is illegal: under KRS §383.655, a tenant who is unlawfully locked out, or whose heat, water, electric, gas or other essential service is willfully cut off, can recover possession or end the lease and recover up to 3 months' rent plus attorney's fees. The landlord must use Jefferson District Court
  • Landlords may refuse vouchers: since March 6, 2024, Kentucky law (2024 Ky. Acts ch. 3, House Bill 18) says no city or consolidated local government "shall adopt or enforce" a rule that stops a landlord from refusing a tenant whose rent "includes funding from a federal housing assistance program," so Louisville cannot require landlords to take Section 8. Finding a landlord who already accepts vouchers is part of the work. LMHA can provide a list of participating landlords
  • Fair housing: discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability is illegal under federal law and the Kentucky Civil Rights Act. The Louisville/Jefferson County Human Relations Commission handles local intakes

For free legal help: Legal Aid Society of Louisville at (502) 584-1254 is the primary Right to Counsel provider. Kentucky Equal Justice Center serves the broader region. For state-level details, see our Kentucky housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Other Housing Programs in Louisville

  • Public housing: LMHA owns and manages public-housing communities across Louisville. Application is separate from Section 8; LMHA’s portal says public housing applications are closed, and six privately managed sites take applications at their own offices
  • LIHTC (Tax Credit): Louisville has substantial LIHTC inventory. Search HUD's LIHTC database for properties in Jefferson County. See how to find LIHTC housing
  • Louisville Affordable Housing Trust Fund: Metro-funded affordable housing development. New income-restricted apartments come online each year
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): combines a voucher with VA case management. Louisville-area veterans are referred through the Robley Rex VA Medical Center. See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Rapid Rehousing & Permanent Supportive Housing: coordinated through the Coalition for the Homeless of Louisville and Southern Indiana. Access via 211
  • Kentucky Housing Corporation (KHC) programs: statewide homebuyer assistance, MyHome Loan, and statewide voucher program for non-LMHA areas. kyhousing.org
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator. Center for Accessible Living and Louisville Urban League cover the area

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Louisville Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

No. As of October 15, 2026, the Louisville Metro Housing Authority (LMHA) states: "Please be advised that we are not accepting Housing Choice Voucher applications at this time," and its applicant portal says: "New applications are NOT being taken at this time for Section 8 or Public Housing. Applications are closed." LMHA dates the pause to July 1, 2024 and publishes no reopening date. Six privately managed public housing communities and the project-based voucher sites keep their own lists with no status published, so call the site. Call (502) 584-1704 for waitlist status if you already applied.

What is Louisville's Right to Counsel for eviction?

Louisville Metro passed its Right to Counsel law in April 2021 (Ordinance No. 52-2021, now Metro Code section 151.60). It covers eviction cases in Jefferson County District Court's Eviction Court for tenants whose annual gross income is not more than 125% of the federal poverty guidelines. The Legal Aid Society provides the lawyers: call (502) 584-1254 or (800) 292-1862 and leave a voicemail if no one answers. Intake runs Monday to Thursday, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., and you can apply online any time at yourlegalaid.org. Call the same day you receive court papers.

Where can I get emergency rental assistance in Louisville?

Kentucky's pandemic rent program is over. The Governor's office announced that rent and utility applications through the Healthy at Home Eviction Relief portal "will no longer be reviewed beginning at 5 p.m. on Friday, Jan. 20, 2023," and the state's later Eviction Diversion Program, which never covered Jefferson County, "stopped accepting applications on Tuesday, April 1, 2025." For rental help now, Louisville Metro's Eviction Prevention page says: "For information or resources about rental assistance, visit Stop My Eviction." That is stopmyeviction.org. Louisville Metro's own Housing Stabilization Program, which Stop My Eviction says can pay up to $800 toward rent for working heads of household, and its HOPE Assistance Program for seniors and people with disabilities both book appointments at (502) 308-3344. Stop My Eviction lists the Salvation Army's rent help as on hold until January 2027. (Checked November 4, 2026.)

What is the Kentucky URLTA and does it apply in Louisville?

Yes. Louisville is one of the Kentucky cities that adopted the Kentucky Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA, KRS Chapter 383). This gives Louisville tenants stronger statutory protections than tenants in non-URLTA Kentucky counties, including a 7-day notice for nonpayment (KRS 383.660), at least 14 days' notice for other lease violations with the chance to fix the problem, separate-account and damage-list rules for security deposits (KRS 383.580), and explicit anti-retaliation and habitability rules. Kentucky lets cities and counties adopt URLTA only in full and without amendment (KRS 383.500).

Can a Louisville landlord refuse Section 8?

Yes. Since March 6, 2024, Kentucky law (2024 Ky. Acts ch. 3, House Bill 18) bars any city or consolidated local government from adopting or enforcing a rule that stops a landlord from refusing a tenant whose rent includes funding from a federal housing assistance program. So Louisville cannot require landlords to accept Section 8. Refusals based on race, disability, family status or another federally protected trait are still illegal. Finding a landlord who already accepts vouchers is part of the work, and LMHA can provide a list of participating landlords.

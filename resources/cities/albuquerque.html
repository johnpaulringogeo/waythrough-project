Affordable Housing in Albuquerque, NM

Waythrough Project · Updated October 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Albuquerque and Bernalillo County — not generic Section 8 advice. AHA takes Section 8 pre-applications only from October 16 to November 15, 2026, and public housing pre-applications only from October 5 to October 31, 2026; after each end date, that list is closed (AHA's site said OPEN for both when we checked October 28, 2026). The City of Albuquerque also runs an Emergency Rental & Utility Assistance Program through its Health & Social Service Centers, with limited funding, and its largest shelter is Gateway West, formerly the Westside Emergency Housing Center. New Mexico's tenant law follows the Uniform Owner-Resident Relations Act. The named resources below are where to start.

Quick numbers to write down:

  • City of Albuquerque Rental Assistance: cabq.gov/help/rental-assistance

Emergency Help Tonight in Albuquerque

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or are facing an imminent eviction, these are the local resources to contact first:

  • Gateway West (formerly the Westside Emergency Housing Center) — the City of Albuquerque calls it "the City’s largest shelter" and says it is "open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week." It is at 7440 Jim McDowell NW, welcomes men, women and couples, and is operated for the City by Chicanos Por La Causa (CPLC), with meals, a bed, secure under-bed storage, and a shuttle the City says "is provided daily" from stops such as Indian School Rd and 1st St NW and HopeWorks Day Shelter. Call the Gateway West mainline at 505-839-9193 or case management at 505-767-5891; for other shelters, food, showers and clothing, the City says to call 311 (checked October 21, 2026). cabq.gov: Gateway West
  • Joy Junction — large emergency shelter and family services campus on the south side. joyjunction.org
  • Steelbridge Albuquerque — emergency shelter and recovery programs (formerly Albuquerque Rescue Mission)
  • Heading Home — rapid rehousing and permanent supportive housing for chronically homeless individuals and families. headinghome.org
  • Salvation Army Albuquerque Corps — emergency shelter and case management
  • Saranam — transitional housing and education for families with children experiencing homelessness
  • New Day Youth and Family Services — emergency shelter and services for youth (12–17) and young adults
  • DV Resource Center (DVRC) — domestic violence shelter and 24-hour crisis line: (505) 248-3165. Bilingual advocates
  • Albuquerque Coordinated Entry System (CES) — single intake operated through the New Mexico Coalition to End Homelessness and partner agencies. Call 211
  • 211 New Mexico — free 24/7 information line for shelters, food, financial assistance, and social services

Section 8 in Albuquerque: AHA Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Albuquerque are administered by the Albuquerque Housing Authority (AHA). Current status (checked October 28, 2026):

  • Section 8 pre-applications: only October 16 to November 15, 2026. AHA’s How to Apply page lists “Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) OPEN October 16, 2026 through November 15, 2026,” and it still said OPEN when we checked October 28, 2026. After November 15, 2026, this window is closed. Pre-applications are online only and walk-ins are not accepted. Selection is by lottery, and AHA’s Administrative Plan says the pool is purged every year, so anyone who applied before and was not selected must apply again. For future openings, sign up at abqha.org/wait-list
  • Project-Based Voucher (PBV) waitlists: as of October 15, 2026, AHA’s PBV page listed two properties taking applications, Felician 2 in Rio Rancho (households with a member 55 or older) and Casitas de Camino. abqha.org/pbv
  • Public Housing through AHA — pre-applications accepted only October 5 through October 31, 2026, online only (AHA’s site said OPEN when we checked October 28, 2026). After October 31, 2026, this list is closed. AHA keeps one community-wide list, ordered by preference points and then by the date and time of the complete application
  • Other special programs: HUD-VASH for veterans, Mainstream vouchers for non-elderly people with disabilities — separate referral processes
  • Apply to neighboring authorities too: Bernalillo County Housing Department, Santa Fe Civic Housing Authority, and the New Mexico Mortgage Finance Authority (MFA, statewide) run separate lists
  • Status check: call AHA at (505) 764-3920 if you've already applied and need to verify your position on the list. Sign up for waitlist notifications at abqha.org/wait-list

  • New Mexico Legal Aid (NMLA) — free legal representation for low-income tenants facing eviction. newmexicolegalaid.org
  • Catholic Charities of Central New Mexico — lists "Rental & Utility Support Available Now" for rent arrears, move-in costs, and utilities, mainly for people referred by a social service agency; apply through its online form or call (505) 724-4670. The New Mexico state ERA has ended

The New Mexico Emergency Rental Assistance Program officially closed on June 30, 2023 — all federal pandemic funds are spent. Today the City's Emergency Rental & Utility Assistance Program, run through its Health & Social Service Centers, is the main public rent door, and its funding is limited. Don't waste time on closed state portals from 2021–2023.

Utility assistance: LIHEAP

New Mexico's LIHEAP is administered by the Income Support Division of the New Mexico Human Services Department. Apply through your local ISD office or by calling 211. PNM and other utilities also offer arrearage management programs.

Tenant Rights in New Mexico

New Mexico's Uniform Owner-Resident Relations Act (UORRA, NMSA 47-8) governs landlord-tenant relationships. It's moderate compared to the most landlord-friendly states, with some specific protections:

  • No statewide source-of-income protection: New Mexico does not have a statewide law preventing landlords from refusing Section 8 vouchers. Albuquerque does not currently have a citywide source-of-income protection ordinance. Finding a landlord who already accepts vouchers is part of the work — AHA can provide a list of participating landlords
  • 3-day notice to pay or vacate for nonpayment (NMSA §47-8-33). New Mexico's eviction process moves fairly quickly
  • 7-day notice for repeated lease violations within 6 months (NMSA §47-8-33)
  • Notice to end month-to-month: 30 days from either party for monthly tenancies; 7 days for weekly tenancies (NMSA §47-8-37)
  • Security deposit cap: for leases under 1 year, the deposit is capped at 1 month's rent (NMSA §47-8-18). For leases of 1 year or more, the landlord may require a larger deposit but must pay interest if it exceeds 1 month's rent. Return within 30 days of move-out with itemized deductions
  • Warranty of habitability: NMSA §47-8-20 requires landlords to maintain the dwelling in habitable condition. These obligations cannot be waived by lease provisions
  • Retaliatory eviction is illegal under NMSA §47-8-39 — landlords cannot retaliate against tenants who exercise legal rights, file complaints, or join tenant organizations
  • Self-help eviction is illegal: landlords cannot lock you out, shut off utilities, or remove your belongings. They must use the Metropolitan Court process
  • Fair housing: discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability is illegal under federal law and the New Mexico Human Rights Act. The City of Albuquerque Office of Civil Rights handles local intakes

For free legal help: New Mexico Legal Aid (NMLA) represents low-income tenants in eviction defense. For state-level details, see our New Mexico housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Other Housing Programs in Albuquerque

  • Public housing: AHA owns and manages public-housing communities across Albuquerque. Application is separate from Section 8; AHA accepts pre-applications for the community-wide list only October 5 through October 31, 2026, and after October 31 the list is closed until AHA posts a new window at abqha.org/how-to-apply
  • LIHTC (Tax Credit): Albuquerque has substantial LIHTC inventory, with several senior-restricted properties. Search HUD's LIHTC database for properties in Bernalillo County. See how to find LIHTC housing
  • City of Albuquerque Workforce Housing Trust Fund — local funding for affordable housing development. New income-restricted apartments come online each year
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): combines a voucher with VA case management. Albuquerque-area veterans are referred through the New Mexico VA Health Care System. See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Rapid Rehousing & Permanent Supportive Housing — coordinated through the New Mexico Coalition to End Homelessness. Access via 211 or Coordinated Entry
  • New Mexico Mortgage Finance Authority (MFA) programs — first-time homebuyer assistance, FirstHome and HomeNow programs, and statewide affordable housing development. housingnm.org
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator — United South Broadway Corporation and YWCA Middle Rio Grande cover Albuquerque

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Albuquerque Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

Only from October 16 through November 15, 2026. The Albuquerque Housing Authority (AHA) How to Apply page at abqha.org/how-to-apply lists Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher pre-applications as open from October 16 through November 15, 2026, online only, and it still said OPEN when we checked October 28, 2026. After November 15, 2026, the window is closed. Selection is by lottery, and AHA purges the pool every year, so anyone who applied before and was not selected must apply again. Public housing pre-applications are accepted only October 5 through October 31, 2026, and that list is closed after October 31. As of October 15, 2026, AHA's project-based voucher page listed two properties taking applications. For later openings, sign up for waitlist notifications at abqha.org/wait-list. Call AHA at (505) 764-3920 with questions.

Where can I get emergency rental assistance in Albuquerque?

The City of Albuquerque's Emergency Rental & Utility Assistance Program helps city residents at risk of eviction or a utility shutoff, but its funding is limited. There is no online application: call the Health & Social Service Center nearest you, such as John Marshall at (505) 848-1345 (details at cabq.gov/help/rental-assistance). Catholic Charities of Central New Mexico also lists rent help, mainly for people referred by a social service agency: (505) 724-4670. The New Mexico state Emergency Rental Assistance Program officially closed June 30, 2023 (checked October 16, 2026).

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Albuquerque?

Gateway West (formerly the Westside Emergency Housing Center), at 7440 Jim McDowell NW, is the City of Albuquerque's largest shelter. The City says it is open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week for men, women and couples, with a daily shuttle; call 505-839-9193 (checked October 21, 2026). Joy Junction is the largest emergency shelter and family services campus on the south side. Steelbridge Albuquerque (formerly Albuquerque Rescue Mission) and Salvation Army Albuquerque Corps also offer shelter. New Day serves youth and young adults. The DV Resource Center has a 24-hour crisis line at (505) 248-3165.

Can an Albuquerque landlord refuse Section 8?

Yes. New Mexico has no statewide source-of-income protection, and Albuquerque does not currently have a citywide source-of-income protection ordinance. Finding a landlord who already accepts vouchers is part of the work — AHA can provide a list of participating landlords.

How long is the eviction notice in New Mexico?

New Mexico's Uniform Owner-Resident Relations Act (NMSA §47-8-33) gives a 3-day notice to pay or vacate for nonpayment of rent. For repeated lease violations within 6 months, the landlord can give a 7-day notice to vacate. Once you receive a notice, contact New Mexico Legal Aid immediately. Self-help eviction (lockouts, utility shutoffs) is illegal — the landlord must use the Metropolitan Court process.

Affordable Housing in Buffalo, NY

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Buffalo and Erie County, not generic Section 8 advice. Three things to know: three agencies hand out vouchers in Buffalo, and the BMHA and Belmont voucher lists are closed, while the Buffalo Municipal Housing Authority takes public housing applications by email, mail or drop-off; eligible tenants can get a free eviction lawyer through the WNY Eviction Prevention Project at (844) 230-7376; and in New York an eviction for unpaid rent starts with a 14-day rent demand. Everything below was checked on November 12, 2026.

  • Buffalo Municipal Housing Authority (BMHA): 716-855-6711 · 300 Perry Street · bmhahousing.com

Emergency Help Tonight in Buffalo

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or could lose your home in the next few days, start here. In Erie County, emergency shelter placement goes through the County's Department of Social Services (DSS) during the day and through 211 WNY after hours:

  • Erie County DSS Homeless Team: during business hours, 158 Pearl Street, 1st floor; DSS main line (716) 858-8000. The County says: "After 5:00 pm, you may call 211WNY, at 1-888-696-9211." erie.gov: Homelessness
  • 211 WNY: dial 2-1-1 or 1-888-696-9211, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Its after-hours program "Provides emergency housing placement after hours for the Erie County Department of Social Services (ECDSS)," Monday to Friday 5 pm to 8 am and around the clock on weekends and holidays. You can also text your ZIP code to 898211 (Monday to Friday, 8:30 am to 3:30 pm). 211wny.org
  • Domestic violence: Haven House's 24-hour hotline, (716) 884-6000. The Family Justice Center, 438 Main Street, Suite 201, 716-558-7233, is open Monday to Friday, 8:30 am to 5 pm. fjcsafe.org
  • Crisis Services: 24-hour crisis hotline, 716-834-3131, or call or text 988.
  • 911 if you are in danger right now.

The Buffalo Municipal Housing Authority does not run shelter. Its own FAQ asks "Does the BMHA provide emergency housing?" and answers "No." For a full walkthrough of the first night, see our emergency housing tonight guide.

Section 8 in Buffalo: Who Runs It and Which Lists Are Open

Buffalo is confusing because three different agencies hand out Housing Choice Vouchers here. HUD's PHA contact report lists the Buffalo Municipal Housing Authority (BMHA) (NY002, public housing, plus a voucher program under NY449) and the Rental Assistance Corporation of Buffalo (RACB) (NY409, "Section 8 City of Buffalo"). New York State's voucher program (HCR) is run in Erie County by Belmont Housing Resources for WNY. Each keeps its own list, so get on every one you can. Status checked November 12, 2026:

  • BMHA voucher list: closed. BMHA's page says: "The Waitlist for the Housing Choice Voucher Program is currently closed. We expect to reopen applications sometime in 2026." It adds that "Future wait list openings will be advertised through local media and our social media accounts." BMHA says its vouchers "can be used to lease apartments in the City of Buffalo and throughout Erie County." Voucher office: 476 Louisiana Street, Monday to Friday 8:30 am to 4:30 pm, 716-855-6711 (press 8).
  • RACB (City of Buffalo vouchers): application window November 30 to December 18, 2026. RACB says its list takes online applications from 8:00 am Monday, November 30, 2026 until 4:00 pm Friday, December 18, 2026. Spots are assigned by lottery after it closes, so applying on the first day doesn't help. First preference goes to people already in a HUD Continuum of Care or Emergency Solutions Grants program, then to people who live or work in Erie County. If you need help with the online form, RACB says you can visit or call once the list opens: 470 Franklin Street, (716) 882-0063. If that window has passed, check racbny.org for the next one.
  • Belmont (New York State HCR vouchers): closed. Belmont says: "Currently the waitlist for Erie County Consortium and the Niagara County program are closed and not accepting application." A September 16 to 18, 2026 opening took Family Unification Program referrals only and "is not open to the general public." The one Belmont list taking applications is the City of North Tonawanda program, with a preference for people who live or work in North Tonawanda. Belmont: 2393 Main Street, (716) 884-7791.
  • BMHA public housing: taking applications. BMHA calls itself "the City of Buffalo's largest landlord and only public housing authority." Download the fillable application from bmhahousing.com and email it to 245elmwood@bmhahousing.com, or mail or drop it off at the Housing Assistance Office, 245 Elmwood Avenue, Monday to Friday, 8:30 am to 4:30 pm. Paper applications in English and Spanish are there and at BMHA's Executive Offices, 300 Perry Street. Questions: 716-855-6711, ext. 500 or 501. BMHA does not post an open or closed status for its developments; its FAQ says the wait "can be from 30 days to several months or years." BMHA's 2026 income limits are $56,850 for one person and $81,200 for four.
  • Already applied? BMHA applicants use the "APPLICANT LOGIN" on the BMHA portal (help: portalhelp@bmhahousing.com). Belmont applicants can log in to Assistance Connect or call the Waitlist Application Update Line, 716-884-2358, ext. 307.

Emergency Rental Assistance in Buffalo (Named Programs)

Start here: our guide to emergency rent help in Buffalo walks through each door: Erie County DSS, Gerard Place, the WNY Eviction Prevention Project, 211 WNY, HEAP, and New York's eviction deadlines.

The honest picture: the pandemic rent program is gone, and the doors that still pay back rent are small and have rules. These are the ones we confirmed:

  • Erie County DSS Emergency Services: helps with homelessness, eviction (back rent, which DSS calls shelter arrears), utility arrears and security agreements. Since October 19, 2026 the office is at 460 Main Street, 2nd floor. Call (716) 858-8000, leave a message for Emergency Services at 716-858-6265, or email TAEmergencyServices@erie.gov. The County says "After 5 PM, Call 2-1-1 for assistance." erie.gov: Emergency Services
  • Gerard Place Preventive Services: "may help with back rent payments up to six months" if you "have a 14-day eviction notice from Housing Court. Must live in the City of Buffalo," and have "income below 30% AMI." Help is given "until funds are exhausted," by appointment only. Call (716) 897-9948, ext. 108. gerardplace.org
  • Buffalo Urban League Homelessness Prevention Program: lists "Payment of past-due rent" among its services. 15 Genesee Street, (716) 250-2400, Monday to Friday 8 am to 5 pm. Its page posts no eligibility rules, so ask when you call. bulny.org
  • 211 WNY: dial 2-1-1 and ask for rent help, food and utility help in your ZIP code
  • Utility help: New York's Regular HEAP benefit opened November 2, 2026, and Emergency HEAP is scheduled to open January 4, 2027. Erie County's HEAP phone center is (716) 858-7644. Details are in our Buffalo rent help guide

The pandemic ERAP has ended

New York's Emergency Rental Assistance Program is closed. OTDA says "New applications stopped being accepted on January 20, 2023," and the ERAP portal and call center have shut down. Ignore anyone who says they can get you ERAP money now. Today's doors are DSS, the nonprofits above, and 211.

Tenant Rights in Buffalo & New York

New York's eviction rules are in the Real Property Actions and Proceedings Law (RPAPL) and the Real Property Law (RPL). What matters most in Buffalo:

  • Rent demand: before filing for unpaid rent, the landlord must give a written demand with "at least fourteen days' notice" (RPAPL 711(2))
  • Late rent notice: if rent is not received within five days of the due date, the landlord must send you a written notice by certified mail. If they did not, you can raise that as a defense in a nonpayment case (RPL 235-e(d))
  • Late fees: no late fee until rent is more than five days late, and the fee "shall not exceed fifty dollars or five percent of the monthly rent, whichever is less" (RPL 238-a)
  • Court: Buffalo City Court, 50 Delaware Avenue. Landlord and tenant cases are in Part 17 on the 7th floor, 716-845-2662. The Western New York Law Center says: "If you live in the city of Buffalo, you can ask for help during your first court appearance at Buffalo City Court." Go to every court date
  • Paying to stop it: before the warrant is carried out, "the court shall vacate a warrant upon tender or deposit with the court of the full rent due," unless the landlord shows you held back rent in bad faith (RPAPL 749(3))
  • The 14-day warning: a city marshal must give you "at least fourteen days' notice, in writing" before an eviction, and can only carry it out on a business day between sunrise and sunset (RPAPL 749(2))
  • More time: you can ask the judge to stay the eviction for up to one year if you cannot find similar housing nearby or moving would cause extreme hardship (RPAPL 753)
  • Lockouts are illegal: changing the locks, removing your things or cutting off your heat, water or power to force you out is a crime, with civil penalties of $1,000 to $10,000 per violation (RPAPL 768)
  • Security deposits: no more than one month's rent, returned with an itemized statement within 14 days after you move out (General Obligations Law 7-108)
  • Rent increases and non-renewals: for an increase of 5% or more, or a non-renewal, the landlord must give 30, 60 or 90 days' notice depending on how long you have lived there (RPL 226-c)
  • Buffalo rental inspections: the City says that if a property "fails to meet the standards for a Certificate of Rental Compliance it will not be permitted to operate as an income property and tenants may not be evicted for non-payment of rent." Report conditions by calling 311 (716-851-4890 from outside city limits or on a cell phone)
  • Vouchers: New York's Human Rights Law bars refusing to rent because of "lawful source of income" (Executive Law 296(5)), and the City of Buffalo's fair-housing notice lists lawful source of income too. Report a refusal to HOME (Housing Opportunities Made Equal), 716-854-1400, or the NYS Division of Human Rights
  • Good Cause Eviction: Buffalo is not on New York's list of cities that have opted in (DHCR's list as of May 4, 2026), so those added protections do not apply here yet
  • Free lawyers: Buffalo has no right-to-counsel law, but eligible tenants can get a free lawyer. The court lists Neighborhood Legal Services, the Center for Elder Law & Justice, Legal Aid Bureau, the Volunteer Lawyers Project and the Western New York Law Center, and the WNY Eviction Prevention Project takes calls at (844) 230-7376

For state-level details, see our New York housing resources. If you face discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint. For the other end of the state, see our New York City page (same state law, very different local programs).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Buffalo Section 8 waitlist open right now?

Not at BMHA or Belmont. On November 12, 2026, the Buffalo Municipal Housing Authority said its Housing Choice Voucher waitlist is closed and that it expects to reopen applications sometime in 2026. Belmont Housing Resources said its Erie County Consortium list is closed too. The Rental Assistance Corporation of Buffalo (City of Buffalo vouchers) is the exception: it takes online applications from 8:00 am November 30 to 4:00 pm December 18, 2026, then places everyone by lottery. If that window has passed, check racbny.org or call (716) 882-0063. BMHA public housing is taking applications; see bmhahousing.com or call 716-855-6711.

How do I apply for BMHA public housing?

Download BMHA's fillable housing application from bmhahousing.com and email it to 245elmwood@bmhahousing.com, or mail or drop it off at the BMHA Housing Assistance Office, 245 Elmwood Avenue, Monday to Friday, 8:30 am to 4:30 pm. Paper applications in English and Spanish are there and at 300 Perry Street. For questions call 716-855-6711, ext. 500 or 501.

Is there emergency rent help in Buffalo?

Some, with rules. Erie County DSS Emergency Services helps with back rent, utility arrears and homelessness; call (716) 858-8000. Gerard Place may pay up to six months of back rent for City of Buffalo renters with a 14-day eviction notice and income below 30% of the area median; call (716) 897-9948, ext. 108. Dial 211 for other help. New York's pandemic ERAP is closed.

How much notice does a Buffalo landlord have to give?

For unpaid rent, New York law requires a written rent demand giving at least 14 days' notice before the landlord can file. If you lose in court, a city marshal must give you at least 14 days' written notice before an eviction. To raise the rent by 5% or more or not renew your lease, the landlord must give 30, 60 or 90 days' notice depending on how long you have lived there.

Can I get a free lawyer for an eviction in Buffalo?

If you are financially eligible, yes. Buffalo has no right-to-counsel law, but the WNY Eviction Prevention Project gives free eviction defense in Erie County; call (844) 230-7376. Neighborhood Legal Services takes walk-ins at 47 Court Street, 716-847-0650, and the Western New York Law Center says city tenants can ask for help at their first appearance in Buffalo City Court.

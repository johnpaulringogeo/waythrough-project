Affordable Housing in Long Beach, CA

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Long Beach — not generic Section 8 advice. HACLB's voucher waitlist is closed with no opening scheduled, and the City's Renter Aid Program is closed until it announces a new window, so the realistic short-term paths are free eviction lawyers through the City's Right to Counsel partnership with Stay Housed LA, LA County prevention money through PATH, and the Multi-Service Center at 1301 W. 12th St. Long Beach renters have California's Tenant Protection Act plus the City's own Just Cause ordinance and relocation rules for no-fault evictions.

Section 8 in Long Beach: HACLB Status

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Long Beach are run by the Housing Authority of the City of Long Beach (HACLB) — a separate authority from HACLA and LACDA. Current status (checked November 5, 2026):

  • The waitlist is closed. HACLB says its "waiting list is currently closed, and no openings are scheduled at this time." HACLB publishes no average wait.
  • Contact: (562) 570-6985, by appointment · longbeach.gov/haclb
  • Emergency Housing Vouchers are ending. HACLB says HUD is ending the program and "EHV rental assistance is estimated to end around October 2026." If you hold one, call HACLB now and talk to your landlord.
  • Public housing: HACLB runs no public housing. LA County's Carmelitos public housing in Long Beach took applications from August 17 to September 16, 2026, and is closed again; Long Beach residents count as local applicants there
  • Apply to neighboring authorities too: the Housing Authority of the City of Los Angeles (HACLA), the Los Angeles County Development Authority (LACDA), and other nearby cities run separate waitlists

Tenant Rights in Long Beach (State Law + Local Layer)

Long Beach renters are covered by California's statewide laws plus the City's own rules:

  • Just Cause for Termination of Tenancies Ordinance (Long Beach Municipal Code Chapter 8.99) — the City says it protects "Tenants who have continuously and lawfully lived in the rental unit for 12 months or more." Landlords need a legal reason to end the tenancy. Unpaid rent is still a legal reason
  • Relocation money for no-fault evictions — the City says tenants get "either $4,500 or two months of the tenant's rent, whichever is greater" for a demolition or substantial remodel, and "relocation assistance equal to one month of the tenant's rent" for other no-fault terminations, paid "within 15 calendar days of issuing the written notice of termination." Questions: Housing & Neighborhood Services, 562-570-6949
  • California Tenant Protection Act (AB 1482; Civil Code 1946.2 and 1947.12) — just cause after a tenant has "continuously and lawfully occupied" the home for 12 months, and yearly rent increases capped at "5 percent plus the percentage change in the cost of living, or 10 percent, whichever is lower." Some homes are exempt, including many single-family homes and buildings built in the last 15 years
  • 3-day notice to pay rent (CCP § 1161) — three days, "excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays." Paying the full amount within that time defeats the eviction
  • 10 court days to answer an eviction lawsuit (CCP § 1167, since January 1, 2025) — not counting weekends or court holidays. Long Beach eviction cases are heard at the Governor George Deukmejian Courthouse, 275 Magnolia Ave.
  • Security deposit cap (Cal Civ Code § 1950.5) — one month's rent for most landlords under AB 12 (effective July 1, 2024). Returned within 21 days with an itemized statement
  • Source-of-income protection (Cal Gov Code § 12955) — California prohibits landlords from refusing Section 8 vouchers. Statewide protection applies in Long Beach
  • No retaliation (Civ Code § 1942.5)
  • Self-help eviction is illegal (Civ Code § 789.3) — $100/day plus actual damages
  • Price gouging during emergencies (Penal Code § 396) — limits rent increases to 10% during a declared state of emergency
  • Fair housing: California Fair Employment and Housing Act protects source of income, sexual orientation, gender identity, age, ancestry, and more. Fair Housing Foundation: 562-989-1206

For free legal help: Stay Housed LA at 1-888-694-0040 runs the City's Right to Counsel program for low-income residents facing eviction. The Legal Aid Foundation of Los Angeles (LAFLA) has a walk-in Long Beach office at 601 Pacific Ave. (Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.) and a hotline at 800-399-4529. The City notes that it "does not provide legal assistance and does not mediate in tenant and landlord disputes." For state-level details, see our California housing resources.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Long Beach Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

No. The Housing Authority of the City of Long Beach (HACLB) says its waiting list is currently closed and no openings are scheduled at this time (checked November 5, 2026). HACLB publishes no average wait. HACLB is a separate authority from HACLA and LACDA, so applying to one does not place you on the others' lists. LA County's Carmelitos public housing lists in Long Beach were open from August 17 to September 16, 2026, and are closed again.

What is the Long Beach Renter Aid Program?

It is the City's short-term rent help for low-income renters in a housing crisis, launched in April 2026 as part of Upstream LB. It pays overdue rent, short-term rent, past-due utilities and moving costs, with payments made directly to landlords or utility providers. You must rent in Long Beach, have household income at or below 50% of the Area Median Income, and have had an emergency in the last 12 months. It is closed for now: the City says it has closed its October application period, is not accepting applications, and is not keeping a waiting list. No reopening date is posted (checked November 5, 2026). Check longbeach.gov/renteraid or email lbrenteraid@longbeach.gov.

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Long Beach?

Call the Long Beach Multi-Service Center at (562) 570-4500 or go to 1301 W. 12th St. It is open Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. and Thursday 8 a.m. to 2 p.m., closed daily from noon to 1 p.m. Long Beach Rescue Mission ((562) 591-1292) runs Samaritan House for men and Lydia House for women and children. For domestic violence, call WomenShelter of Long Beach at (562) 437-4663 or Su Casa at (562) 402-4888, both 24 hours. U.S. VETS Long Beach ((562) 200-7300) serves veterans.

What is the Long Beach Just Cause for Termination of Tenancies Ordinance?

It is Long Beach's own just cause law (Municipal Code Chapter 8.99). The City says it covers tenants who have continuously and lawfully lived in the unit for 12 months or more, so a landlord needs a legal reason, such as unpaid rent or a lease violation, to end the tenancy. For no-fault evictions, the City says landlords must pay relocation money: one month's rent, or for a demolition or substantial remodel, $4,500 or two months' rent, whichever is greater, within 15 calendar days of the notice. California's Tenant Protection Act also applies. Questions: Housing & Neighborhood Services, 562-570-6949.

Does California protect Section 8 voucher holders in Long Beach?

Yes. California Government Code § 12955 prohibits landlords statewide from refusing Section 8 vouchers or other lawful sources of income. This applies in Long Beach. A landlord who refuses your voucher is violating state fair housing law. Long Beach's Just Cause ordinance adds protection once you have lived in the unit 12 months. Report violations to the California Civil Rights Department or the Fair Housing Foundation at 562-989-1206, or call the Legal Aid Foundation of Los Angeles at 800-399-4529.

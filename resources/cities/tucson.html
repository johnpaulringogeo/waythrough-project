Affordable Housing in Tucson, AZ

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Tucson and Pima County — not generic Section 8 advice. The City of Tucson Housing and Community Development closed both the Section 8 and Public Housing waitlists on January 1, 2026, so the realistic short-term path is one-time rental assistance through Pima County's Community Action Agency and the nonprofits in the County's Emergency Services Network, such as Interfaith Community Services and Primavera. The named resources below are where to start.

Quick numbers to write down:
  • 211 Arizona — dial 211 (free; 9 a.m. to 7 p.m. daily) for any housing emergency in Pima County
  • City of Tucson HCD: (520) 791-4171 · tucsonaz.gov/Departments/Housing-and-Community-Development
  • Southern Arizona Legal Aid (SALA): (520) 623-9461 or 1-800-248-6789 · sazlegalaid.org
  • Pima County Community Action Agency (rent and utility help): (520) 724-2667
  • Primavera Foundation — emergency services: (520) 623-5111

Emergency Help Tonight in Tucson

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or are facing an imminent eviction, these are the local resources to contact first:

  • Primavera Foundation — Men's Shelter — emergency shelter for adult men. Stays start at seven nights with extension possible up to 90 days. (520) 623-5111 · primavera.org/emergency-services
  • Primavera — Casa Paloma — emergency shelter and transitional housing for unaccompanied women
  • Primavera — Family Pathways — emergency shelter for families of any configuration with minor children
  • Gospel Rescue Mission — two campuses (Center of Opportunity for men, Center of Hope for women and children). (520) 740-4666 · grmtucson.com
  • Salvation Army Hospitality House — emergency overnight shelter for single adults and families. (520) 795-7830
  • Sister Jose Women's Center — day services, overnight shelter, and case management for women experiencing homelessness
  • Old Pueblo Community Services — permanent supportive housing, rapid rehousing, and short-term rental assistance focused on people exiting incarceration or living with mental illness. (520) 881-0450
  • Our Family Services — Reunite Youth Program — shelter for youth ages 12-17
  • Tucson Pima Collaboration to End Homelessness (TPCH) — Coordinated Entry — central intake. Call 211 · tpch.net
  • City of Tucson Housing First — outreach-led permanent supportive housing for residents experiencing chronic homelessness
  • 211 Arizona — free information line for shelters, food, financial assistance, and social services, 9 a.m. to 7 p.m. daily

Section 8 in Tucson: HCD Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Tucson are administered by the City of Tucson Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD), which also acts as the regional voucher administrator for unincorporated Pima County under an intergovernmental agreement. Current status (checked November 3, 2026):

  • The HCV (Section 8) waitlist is closed as of January 1, 2026, with no reopening date announced. HCD announced the closure in late December 2025 because the existing waitlist already contained more than enough applicants to absorb expected voucher turnover for several years
  • The Public Housing waitlist is also closed as of January 1, 2026, with no reopening date
  • Specialty referrals still accepted: HUD-VASH (veterans), Family Unification Program (FUP — families with open child welfare cases and youth aging out of foster care), and project-based vouchers tied to specific permanent supportive housing buildings
  • South Tucson Housing Authority — separate jurisdiction inside the one-square-mile City of South Tucson. Runs its own small public housing portfolio. Call (520) 629-1136 to check current waitlist status
  • Apply to other authorities too: the Arizona Department of Housing runs a separate statewide voucher program
  • Status check: call HCD at (520) 791-4171 if you've already applied and need to verify your position on the list

For the national application process, see our step-by-step Section 8 guide and how to find your PHA.

Emergency Rental Assistance in Tucson (Named Programs)

Because vouchers are not available to new applicants, the realistic short-term path is one-time rental assistance through the agencies Pima County lists in its Emergency Services Network. The County says "Rent, mortgage, and utility assistance is limited and based on grant funding" (checked November 3, 2026), so call more than one:

  • Pima County Community Action Agency (CAA) — rent, mortgage, and utility assistance. Callers are screened, and appointments depend on available funds. Gather the County's required documents before you call. (520) 724-2667 · pima.gov/emergency-services-program
  • Interfaith Community Services — rent, mortgage, and utility help for all Pima County residents, by phone only and only when it has funds. Call (520) 297-6049 starting at 9 a.m., Monday through Thursday
  • Primavera Foundation — Rent & Utility Assistance — call the Resource Center at (520) 623-5111, Monday–Friday, 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m., to ask about current funding; bring your 5-day notice or past-due bill to intake
  • Chicanos Por La Causa (Tucson) — (520) 882-0018; PPEP rent assistance — (520) 741-4397; Salvation Army — (520) 795-9671 (all listed by Pima County for emergency rent or utility help)
  • St. Vincent de Paul of Southern Arizona — one-time rent, mortgage, and utility help through local parish volunteers. Referral line (520) 628-7837
  • United Way of Tucson and Southern Arizona Emergency Relief Fund — focuses on rental and utility assistance, but works through partner agencies rather than taking applications from individuals · unitedwaytucson.org/emergency-relief-fund
  • DES Short-Term Crisis Services — rent, utility, and deposit help for families with a child under 18 and income at or below 125% of the federal poverty guidelines; apply through Pima County CAA

For the step-by-step version — documents, call times, utility help, and free eviction lawyers — see our Tucson emergency rent help guide.

The pandemic ERAP funds and ARAP have ended

The federal Emergency Rental Assistance Program (ERA1/ERA2) money that flowed through the City of Tucson and Pima County during 2021-2023 has been fully spent, and the state's Arizona Rental Assistance Program (ARAP) has ended: DES says applications are no longer being accepted and only those submitted by August 31, 2024 are still being processed. Current paths are Pima County CAA and the nonprofit options above. Don't waste time on closed pandemic-era portals.

Utility assistance: LIHEAP

The Low-Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) in Arizona helps with heating and cooling bills — essential in the desert. Lowering your electric bill frees up cash for rent. Apply through Arizona DES or with help from Pima County CAA, or call 211 to find your local agency. TEP's Lifeline program also gives qualifying low-income customers a $20 monthly discount.

Tenant Rights in Arizona (Applies in Tucson)

Tucson tenants are covered by the Arizona Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (ARLTA), A.R.S. Title 33, Chapter 10. The same statewide rules apply to renters in Mesa, Phoenix, and every other Arizona city. The points that matter most for Tucson renters facing trouble:

  • Five-day nonpayment notice (A.R.S. § 33-1368). Paying the full amount owed plus reasonable late fees within those five days defeats the eviction
  • Ten-day notice for lease violations (A.R.S. § 33-1368). The tenant has a chance to cure the violation
  • Security deposit cap of 1.5 months' rent (A.R.S. § 33-1321(A)). The deposit must be returned within 14 business days after move-out with an itemized statement of any deductions
  • No source-of-income protection: Arizona does not protect Section 8 voucher holders from refusal under state law, and Tucson does not have a local source-of-income ordinance. A landlord can lawfully decline to accept a voucher. Finding a landlord who already participates is part of the work
  • No rent control: A.R.S. § 33-1329 preempts cities from enacting rent control on private residential property
  • Essential services failure (A.R.S. § 33-1364) — if the landlord fails to maintain running water, heat, hot water, electricity, or gas after written notice, the tenant may obtain reasonable substitute services and deduct the cost from rent
  • Self-help eviction is illegal (A.R.S. § 33-1367) — landlords cannot lock you out, shut off utilities, or remove belongings without a court order. Damages can include actual damages plus two months' rent
  • Justice Court venue: eviction cases are heard in the Pima County Justice Courts. The hearing must be 3 to 6 days after the summons is issued (A.R.S. § 33-1377), and a writ of restitution cannot issue until 5 calendar days after a judgment (A.R.S. § 12-1178)
  • Fair housing: discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability is illegal under federal and Arizona law

For free legal help: Pima County Emergency Eviction Legal Services (EELS) at (520) 724-3357 gives free lawyers to tenants at or below 80% of area median income. Southern Arizona Legal Aid (SALA) takes applications at (520) 623-9461 or 1-800-248-6789 (8:15–11:30 a.m. and 1–4 p.m.) and provides translators for all languages. Step Up to Justice offers free landlord-tenant legal help for low-income Pima County residents; apply online. For state-level details, see our Arizona housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Other Housing Programs in Tucson

  • Public housing: HCD owns and manages public-housing communities across Tucson. Waitlist is closed; specialty referrals still accepted
  • LIHTC (Tax Credit): Tucson has substantial LIHTC inventory. Search HUD's LIHTC database for properties in Pima County. See how to find LIHTC housing
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): Tucson veterans are referred through the Southern Arizona VA Health Care System. See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Rapid Rehousing & Permanent Supportive Housing — coordinated through TPCH. Access via 211
  • Arizona Refugee Resettlement Program (RRP) — Catholic Community Services of Southern Arizona is a primary partner
  • Habitat for Humanity Tucson — homeownership and home-repair programs. habitattucson.org
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Tucson Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

No. The City of Tucson Housing and Community Development closed both the Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) and Public Housing waitlists to new general applications on January 1, 2026, and no reopening date has been announced. Specialty referrals for veterans (HUD-VASH) and the Family Unification Program continue when capacity allows. Call HCD at (520) 791-4171 to ask about specialty programs.

What rental assistance is available in Tucson right now?

Pima County's Community Action Agency at (520) 724-2667 screens callers for rent, mortgage, and utility help and sets appointments when funds are available. Interfaith Community Services takes calls at (520) 297-6049 from 9 a.m., Monday through Thursday, when it has funds. The Primavera Foundation at (520) 623-5111 and St. Vincent de Paul at (520) 628-7837 also help. The state's ARAP program has ended. Dial 2-1-1 for other options.

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Tucson?

The Primavera Foundation operates men's shelter, Casa Paloma for women, and Family Pathways for families with children — call (520) 623-5111. Gospel Rescue Mission ((520) 740-4666) has campuses for men and for women with children. Salvation Army Hospitality House ((520) 795-7830), Sister Jose Women's Center, and Old Pueblo Community Services ((520) 881-0450) round out the system. Call 211 for screening and routing through TPCH Coordinated Entry.

Does Tucson protect Section 8 voucher holders from landlord discrimination?

No. Arizona does not include source of income as a protected class under state fair housing law, and Tucson has not enacted a local source-of-income ordinance. A landlord can legally refuse to accept a Housing Choice Voucher in Tucson. HCD can provide a list of landlords who already participate in the voucher program.

How fast can a Tucson eviction move?

Arizona allows a 5-day pay-or-quit notice for nonpayment of rent under A.R.S. 33-1368. If the tenant does not pay within five days, the landlord can file in Pima County Justice Court, and the hearing must be three to six days after the summons is issued. If the landlord wins, a writ of restitution cannot issue until five calendar days after the judgment. Call Pima County EELS at (520) 724-3357 or Southern Arizona Legal Aid at (520) 623-9461 immediately if served.

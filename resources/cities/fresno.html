Affordable Housing in Fresno, CA

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Fresno and Fresno County — not generic Section 8 advice. Fresno Housing's voucher interest list is closed and the City's rent fund is spent, so the realistic short-term paths are the City's free Eviction Protection Program, Fresno County's CalWORKs Homeless Assistance for families, 211, and the shelters and nonprofits below. Fresno renters are also covered by California's Tenant Protection Act (just cause after 12 months) and statewide source-of-income protection.

  • Catholic Charities Diocese of Fresno — emergency assistance and case management. (559) 237-0851 · ccdof.org

  • 211 Fresno and Madera Counties — run by United Way, "available around the clock, 365 days a year." Dial 211 or 1-866-559-4211

Section 8 in Fresno: Fresno Housing Status

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in the Fresno region are run by Fresno Housing, the shared name of the Housing Authority of the City of Fresno and the Housing Authority of the County of Fresno. Current status (checked November 5, 2026):

  • The voucher list is closed. Fresno Housing's application page says of the Housing Choice Voucher: "Interest list is currently closed." It gives no reopening date.
  • Eight properties were accepting applications: Blossom Trail Commons (Sanger), Citrus Commons (Orange Cove), Corazón del Valle Commons (Huron), Cueva de Oso at William Shockley Plaza (Selma), Manzanilla Commons (Fresno), Pacific Gardens (Fresno), Paseo 55 (Reedley) and Promesa Commons (Fresno). Apply through Fresno Housing's portal; you can be on more than one list.
  • How people are picked: Fresno Housing says "Preferences are used as points for eligibility" and "Random lottery draws may be performed throughout the year," so applying first does not by itself put you ahead.
  • Contact: Fresno Housing (559) 443-8400; application help (559) 441-2318 · fresnohousing.org
  • Clovis has no housing authority of its own. The City of Clovis says its voucher program "is administered by the Fresno County Housing Authority ('Fresno Housing')." Neighboring counties have their own housing authorities with separate lists.

Tenant Rights in California (Statewide Protections Apply in Fresno)

Fresno tenants are covered by California's state tenant-protection laws:

  • California Tenant Protection Act (AB 1482; Civil Code 1946.2 and 1947.12) — once a tenant has "continuously and lawfully occupied" the home for 12 months, a covered landlord needs a just cause to end the tenancy, and yearly rent increases are capped at "5 percent plus the percentage change in the cost of living, or 10 percent, whichever is lower." Some homes are exempt, including many single-family homes and buildings built in the last 15 years
  • Fresno has no local rent control law like LA, SF, or Oakland, so state law sets the limits for covered buildings
  • 3-day notice to pay rent (CCP § 1161) — three days, "excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays." Paying the full amount within that time defeats the eviction
  • 10 court days to answer an eviction lawsuit (CCP § 1167, since January 1, 2025) — not counting weekends or court holidays. Miss it and the landlord can win by default
  • Security deposit cap (Cal Civ Code § 1950.5) — one month's rent for most landlords under AB 12 (effective July 1, 2024). Must be returned within 21 days of move-out with an itemized statement
  • Source-of-income protection (Cal Gov Code § 12955) — California prohibits landlords from refusing Section 8 vouchers or other lawful sources of income. Statewide protection applies in Fresno
  • Habitability and right to repair (Civ Code § 1941 et seq.) — landlords must provide habitable premises. Tenants can use the "repair and deduct" remedy for limited fixes (capped at one month's rent)
  • No retaliation (Civ Code § 1942.5)
  • Self-help eviction is illegal (Civ Code § 789.3) — $100/day plus actual damages
  • Price gouging during emergencies (Penal Code § 396) — limits rent increases to 10% during a declared state of emergency
  • Fair housing: California Fair Employment and Housing Act protects source of income, sexual orientation, gender identity, age, ancestry, and more

For free legal help: the City of Fresno Eviction Protection Program at (559) 621-8400 for City of Fresno residents, and Central California Legal Services (CCLS) at (559) 570-1200 or 1-800-675-8001 anywhere in Fresno County. CCLS and the Fresno Superior Court run a free eviction clinic at the court's Self-Help Center, 1130 O Street. For state-level details, see our California housing resources.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Fresno Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

No. Fresno Housing's application page says its Housing Choice Voucher interest list is currently closed, with no reopening date (checked November 5, 2026). Eight of its properties were accepting applications that day, including Manzanilla Commons, Pacific Gardens and Promesa Commons in Fresno. Clovis has no housing authority of its own; Fresno Housing serves it. Call (559) 443-8400 with questions.

What rental assistance is available in Fresno right now?

No general City or County rent fund is open: the City of Fresno says its Emergency Rental Assistance Program has distributed all its funding and is closed. CalWORKs families can ask Fresno County for up to two months of back rent at (559) 600-5315. Catholic Charities Diocese of Fresno, (559) 237-0851, helps in person when funding is available. City of Fresno renters facing an unlawful eviction can get a free lawyer through the Eviction Protection Program at (559) 621-8400. Dial 211 to find who has money this month.

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Fresno?

Poverello House at (559) 498-6988 is Fresno's largest provider — day services, three meals, dental clinic, recovery program, and shelter at 412 F St. Fresno Mission ((559) 268-0839), formerly Fresno Rescue Mission, runs Rescue the Children for moms with kids. The Salvation Army Fresno Citadel Corps is at (559) 233-0139. Marjaree Mason Center ((559) 233-HELP) is the county's domestic violence shelter with bilingual advocates. Call 211 for Fresno-Madera Continuum of Care Coordinated Entry.

Does California protect Section 8 voucher holders in Fresno?

Yes. California Government Code § 12955 prohibits landlords statewide from refusing Section 8 vouchers or other lawful sources of income. This applies in Fresno. A landlord who refuses your voucher is violating state fair housing law. Report violations to the California Civil Rights Department or call Central California Legal Services at (559) 570-1200.

Does Fresno have local rent control?

No. Unlike Los Angeles, San Francisco, or Oakland, Fresno has no local rent control law. California's Tenant Protection Act (AB 1482) sets the rules for covered buildings: yearly rent increases are capped at 5% plus the change in the cost of living, or 10%, whichever is lower, and after a tenant has lived in the home for 12 months, the landlord needs a just cause to end the tenancy. Exempt homes include many single-family homes and buildings built in the last 15 years. During a declared state of emergency, Penal Code 396 limits rent increases to 10%.

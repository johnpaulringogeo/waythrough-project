Section 8 and Low-Income Housing in Oklahoma City, OK

Waythrough Project · Updated September 2026

This page is about getting into subsidized or income-restricted housing in Oklahoma City and Oklahoma County: the Oklahoma City Housing Authority (OCHA) Section 8 list, OCHA's family and senior public housing properties, and tax credit (LIHTC) apartments. OCHA's voucher list is closed (checked November 18, 2026), so the public housing site lists and tax credit buildings are where to put your effort. Section 8 in Oklahoma City: OCHA Waitlist Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Oklahoma City are administered by the Oklahoma City Housing Authority (OCHA). The Oklahoma Housing Finance Agency (OHFA) administers a statewide HCV program for areas not served by local PHAs. Current status (checked September 9, 2026):

  • The OCHA HCV waitlist is closed with no scheduled reopening. OCHA's page still read "HCV waiting list is closed. Not accepting applications" on November 18, 2026. Watch ochanet.org/applicants/housing_voucher
  • Average wait once on the list: approximately 17 months in Oklahoma City. When the list reopens, you apply through Rent Café
  • Eligibility: household income at or below 50% of Area Median Income (AMI) for Oklahoma County. OHFA publishes the limits annually
  • Public housing through OCHA is a separate program with a waiting list for each property (see the next section)
  • OHFA Statewide Housing Choice Voucher: for residents in counties not served by a local PHA, or as an alternative. ohfa.org/housingchoicevoucher
  • Other special programs: HUD-VASH for veterans, Mainstream vouchers for non-elderly people with disabilities, each with a separate referral process
  • Apply to neighboring authorities too: Norman Housing Authority, Edmond, Midwest City Housing Authority, Choctaw, and the Oklahoma Housing Finance Agency (statewide) run separate lists
  • Status check: call OCHA at (405) 239-7551 if you've already applied and need to verify your position

For the national application process, see our step-by-step Section 8 guide and how to find your PHA.

OCHA Public Housing Properties in Oklahoma City

OCHA owns and manages its public housing directly, and each property keeps its own waiting list. OCHA does not post one open-or-closed roster: you see which site lists are open when you sign in to its online Application Portal. How it works, from OCHA's public housing page (checked November 18, 2026):

  • Applications are online only, and "Applications are only accepted for open waiting lists." You need a valid email address to register
  • Eligible applications "will be entered on the waiting list in the order received (date and time)," so apply as soon as a list you want is open
  • Have the full legal name, Social Security number, date of birth and annual income for everyone in the household
  • Help with the application because of a disability: Public Housing at (405) 602-5553

LIHTC (Tax Credit) Apartments in Oklahoma City

Tax credit apartments are privately owned buildings that keep rents below market for households under an income limit, most often 60% of area median income. There is no central waiting list and you do not need a voucher: you apply at each property's leasing office. Search HUD's LIHTC database for Oklahoma County, then call the buildings and ask which have openings and what their income limits are. Our guide on how to find LIHTC housing walks through the search.

Tenant Rights in Oklahoma City & Oklahoma

Oklahoma has a landlord-friendly framework. The Oklahoma Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (ORLTA, 41 O.S. §§101 et seq.) sets the baseline rules:

  • No statewide source-of-income protection: Oklahoma does not have a statewide law preventing landlords from refusing Section 8 vouchers. Oklahoma City has not (yet) passed a local source-of-income protection ordinance. Finding a landlord who already accepts vouchers is part of the work. OCHA can provide a list of participating landlords
  • 5-day notice for nonpayment (41 O.S. §131), among the shorter notice periods in the country. Once you receive a notice, act immediately
  • 10-day notice for lease violations with a cure opportunity
  • 30-day notice to end month-to-month from either party (41 O.S. §111)
  • Security deposit return: within 30 days (or 45 days if the tenant requested return in writing) with itemized deductions (41 O.S. §115). Oklahoma does not cap deposit amounts
  • Warranty of habitability: 41 O.S. §118 requires landlords to comply with applicable codes, maintain working plumbing and heating, and keep common areas safe
  • Retaliatory eviction is illegal under 41 O.S. §138. Landlords cannot retaliate against tenants who exercise legal rights, file complaints, or join tenant organizations
  • Self-help eviction is illegal: Oklahoma prohibits lockouts, utility shutoffs, and removal of belongings. The landlord must use the small claims/forcible entry and detainer process
  • Fair housing: discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability is illegal under federal law and the Oklahoma Discrimination in Housing Act. The Oklahoma Human Rights Commission (Attorney General's Office) handles complaints

For free legal help: Legal Aid Services of Oklahoma (apply at 1-888-534-5243) represents low-income tenants in eviction defense statewide. For state-level details, see our Oklahoma housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Oklahoma City Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

No. The Oklahoma City Housing Authority (OCHA) Housing Choice Voucher waitlist is closed as of September 9, 2026 with no scheduled reopening. Average wait once on the list is about 17 months. When the list reopens, applications are processed through Rent Café. Eligibility is 50% of AMI or below. The Oklahoma Housing Finance Agency (OHFA) runs a statewide HCV for areas not served by local PHAs. Call OCHA at (405) 239-7551 for status.

How do I find low-income apartments in Oklahoma City?

Two main routes while the OCHA voucher list is closed. OCHA public housing: each family and senior property keeps its own list, applications are online only through OCHA's Application Portal, and they are entered in date and time order, so check the portal for open site lists and apply early. The Reding Senior Center and Reding Annex lists closed on November 1, 2026. Tax credit (LIHTC) apartments: privately owned buildings with income limits; search HUD's database at huduser.gov/lihtc for Oklahoma County and apply at each leasing office. Call OCHA at (405) 239-7551 with questions. For rent help, see our Oklahoma City emergency rent help guide.

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Oklahoma City?

City Rescue Mission (405-232-2709) is OKC's largest emergency shelter for men, women, and families. Salvation Army Center of Hope serves adults and families. The Homeless Alliance WestTown Day Shelter provides central daytime services. Sisters of Mercy/Pivot serves youth. YWCA Oklahoma City has a 24-hour DV crisis line at (405) 949-1866. Call 211 to route through the Homeless Alliance Key to Home coordinated entry.

Can an Oklahoma City landlord refuse Section 8?

Yes. Oklahoma has no statewide source-of-income protection, and Oklahoma City has not passed a local source-of-income protection ordinance. Finding a landlord who already accepts vouchers is part of the work. OCHA can provide a list of participating landlords. Oklahoma's framework is generally landlord-friendly.

How long is the eviction notice in Oklahoma?

Oklahoma Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (41 O.S. §131) gives just a 5-day notice for nonpayment of rent, among the shortest notice periods in the country. Lease violations get a 10-day notice with a cure opportunity. Once you receive a 5-day notice, contact Legal Aid Services of Oklahoma at 1-888-534-5243 immediately. Self-help eviction (lockouts, utility shutoffs) is illegal. The landlord must use the forcible entry and detainer process through court.

Affordable Housing in Miami, FL

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Miami and Miami-Dade County — not generic Section 8 advice. Miami-Dade runs the largest PHA in Florida (more than 19,000 voucher households) and operates an Office of Housing Advocacy hotline that didn't exist before. But the legal landscape changed in July 2023 — Florida's HB 1417 preempted Miami-Dade's Tenants' Bill of Rights and other local protections. The named resources below are where to start.

Quick numbers to write down:
  • 211 Miami-Dade — dial 211 (free, 24/7) for any housing emergency
  • Miami-Dade Housing & Community Development (HCD, formerly PHCD): 786-469-4100 · miamidade.gov/housing
  • Office of Housing Advocacy Hotline: 786-469-4545
  • Camillus House (main shelter): 305-374-1065

Emergency Help Tonight in Miami

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or are facing an imminent eviction, these are the local resources to contact first:

  • Camillus House — Center of Hope — 258-bed emergency shelter providing clean beds, three hot daily meals, on-site health care, and biweekly case management. 305-374-1065. camillus.org
  • Chapman Partnership — emergency shelter at the Homeless Assistance Center (HAC) downtown and another in Homestead. Family and individual beds. chapmanpartnership.org
  • Lotus House — shelter, supportive housing, and wraparound services specifically for women, youth, and children
  • Miami Rescue Mission / Caring Place — emergency shelter for men and women, with addiction recovery programs
  • Salvation Army Miami — Center of Hope shelter and rental assistance at multiple locations
  • Citrus Health Network / Lazarus Project — supportive housing for people experiencing chronic homelessness
  • Miami-Dade Homeless Trust — Homeless Helpline — call 1-877-994-4357 to reach outreach and coordinated entry; to ask about homeless prevention, select your language and press 1 (checked November 3, 2026). homelesstrust.org
  • Lodge Miami (Women in Distress of Broward County extends some Miami services, plus local) — domestic violence shelter. Call the Florida DV Hotline at 1-800-500-1119
  • 211 Miami-Dade — free 24/7 information line for shelters, food, financial assistance, and social services

For a full walkthrough of finding shelter the first night, see our emergency housing tonight guide.

Section 8 in Miami: HCD (formerly PHCD) Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Miami are administered by Miami-Dade Housing and Community Development (HCD), which some County pages still call Public Housing and Community Development (PHCD). HCD says it provides rental assistance to more than 19,000 households through the Housing Choice Voucher program and manages approximately 6,500 public housing units. The City of Miami runs a separate, smaller Section 8 program, which is also closed. Current status (checked November 3, 2026):

  • The HCD waiting list is closed. HCD's Housing and Section 8 page says: "The waiting list is closed for registration" (checked November 3, 2026). It uses one online application, open only during a registration period, for both Section 8 vouchers and project-based units. Watch HCD's Housing and Section 8 page. The last registration ran February 5–19, 2024, and a random drawing on March 18, 2024 picked 5,000 applications for the list
  • City of Miami Section 8 — closed. The City runs a separate, smaller program (HUD code FL145). Its Section 8 page says: "The City's Section 8 program is not accepting new applications" (checked November 3, 2026), and its voucher application period closed in October 2014. People already on the City's list can update their contact information at 305-416-1572. miami.gov
  • Public Housing through HCD — not a separate list: the same single HCD application covers public housing, Moderate Rehabilitation and Section 8 New Construction units, and it is closed too
  • County-owned mixed-income properties such as Lakeside Towers take applications at each property's management office; HCD posts no open or closed status for them, so call the property
  • Important federal note: public housing tenants and tenants on subsidies pay only their portion of rent under federal/state/local program rules — not full market rent. The remainder is paid through the program
  • Other special programs: HUD-VASH for veterans, Mainstream vouchers for non-elderly people with disabilities — separate referral processes
  • Neighboring authorities run separate lists: the Hialeah Housing Authority says its Section 8 list is closed, and the Housing Authority of the City of Miami Beach marked every list on its Waitlists page closed (both checked November 3, 2026). Homestead and the Broward County Housing Authority (north) have their own lists. The Florida Housing Finance Corporation is a state finance agency, not a housing authority, and is not on HUD's Florida PHA list
  • Status check: call HCD's Applicant Leasing Center at 786-469-4206 if you've already applied and need to verify your position (HCD's Contact page lists 786-469-4300 for the same center). HCD's main line is 786-469-4100, and its Section 8 office is 786-654-8440

Emergency Rental Assistance in Miami (Named Programs)

If you're behind on rent or can't pay this month, these are the local programs currently operating in Miami. Funding shifts month to month — always call to confirm current availability. Our Miami emergency rent help guide lays out what is open, what is closed or paused, light bill help, free eviction lawyers, and Florida's 3-day notice and 5-day answer.

  • Miami-Dade Office of Housing Advocacy — the County's rental assistance page tells residents in need of housing assistance to call the Housing Advocacy Hotline at 786-469-4545 or email housingadvocacy@miamidade.gov. The office's old web page now forwards to the County's Community Services Department (checked November 3, 2026). miamidade.gov
  • Miami-Dade Homeless Trust — Homeless Prevention — call the Homeless Helpline at 1-877-994-4357, select your language, and press 1 for prevention information
  • Miami-Dade Community Services — rent help for people who are homeless or facing eviction — up to $1,000 for rent or $1,500 for mortgage, one type of assistance per year, but the County says "Assistance has been temporarily put on hold until further funding becomes available" (checked November 3, 2026)
  • Camillus House Homeless Prevention Program — Camillus House says the program "provides rental support for families in danger of being evicted" and helps with basic bills such as utilities, and that it operates the Homeless Prevention Hotline for all of Miami-Dade. camillus.org
  • City of Miami ERA Program — closed — the City says: "As of July 13, 2023 the City's ERA program permanently closed due to the exhaustion of programmatic funds" (checked November 3, 2026). miami.gov
  • City of Miami Beach Rent/Mortgage/Utility Assistance Fund — for Miami Beach residents. apps.miamibeachfl.gov/housing
  • Catholic Charities of Miami — emergency financial assistance, food, immigration legal services, and case management. Spanish, Haitian Creole, and other language services
  • Legal Services of Greater Miami — free legal help for low-income tenants facing eviction. Phone intake (305) 576-0080, Monday–Thursday, 2–4 p.m. legalservicesmiami.org
  • St. Vincent de Paul of Miami — one-time emergency rental and utility help through local parish conferences
  • Salvation Army Miami — eviction prevention and utility assistance
  • Miami Workers Center — tenant organizing and advocacy in Miami-Dade

ERAP and federal pandemic-era programs have ended

Miami-Dade's Emergency Rental Assistance Program (ERAP) and ERAP 2.4 have closed; federal pandemic funds are spent. The City of Miami's ERA program also closed permanently on July 13, 2023, and the County's own rent help for people facing eviction is on hold. The current paths are the Office of Housing Advocacy Hotline, the Homeless Helpline's prevention line, Camillus House Prevention, and the named nonprofits above. Don't waste time on old 2021–2023 application portals.

Utility assistance: LIHEAP

In Florida, LIHEAP is administered locally. In Miami-Dade, you apply through the County's Community Services Department at its Community Resource Centers (786-469-4640). Regular LIHEAP help is one payment a year of up to $1,350; if your power has been disconnected or faces disconnection, you must visit a Community Resource Center. FPL customers in a crisis can also be screened there for FPL Care to Share, up to $750 once in 12 months, based on funding (checked November 3, 2026).

Tenant Rights in Miami & Florida

Florida has a landlord-friendly framework, and a 2023 state law preempted Miami-Dade's local tenant protections. Knowing the current rules is essential:

  • Florida HB 1417 (effective July 1, 2023) preempted local tenant protections: Florida's state legislature preempted local government tenant ordinances. As a result, Miami-Dade's Tenants' Bill of Rights (Ordinance 22-47) is no longer in effect. The state's landlord-tenant statute is now controlling. This was a significant rollback of local protections
  • Source-of-income protection is unsettled: Florida does not have a statewide law preventing landlords from refusing Section 8 vouchers. Miami-Dade's Human Rights Ordinance (Chapter 11A of the County Code) still lists source of income as a protected class in housing, and the County's Commission on Human Rights still says it enforces that ordinance. HB 1417's section 83.425 preempts local rules on landlord-tenant matters such as tenant screening, but it does not mention source of income, and we found no court ruling on whether it overrides the County's rule. If a landlord refuses your voucher, you can file a complaint with the Commission on Human Rights (305-375-2784) or with H.O.P.E., Inc. (305-651-4673), the City of Miami's fair-housing partner. Finding a landlord who already accepts vouchers is still part of the work — HCD can provide a list of participating landlords
  • No rent control allowed: Florida Statute 125.0103 prohibits cities and counties from passing local rent control except in declared housing emergencies (very rare). Miami landlords can raise rent freely at lease end
  • 3-day notice for nonpayment (Florida Statute §83.56) — excludes Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. Among the shortest in the country. Act fast
  • Security deposit return: within 15 days with no deductions, or 30 days with itemized deductions sent by certified mail (FS §83.49). Florida does not cap deposit amounts
  • Notice to end month-to-month: 30 days from either party (FS §83.57, as amended by HB 1417 in 2023; up from 15 days previously). This is one of the few HB 1417 changes that helped tenants
  • Warranty of habitability: Florida Statute §83.51 requires landlords to comply with building, housing, and health codes; provide working plumbing, heat in winter, hot water, and structural soundness
  • Retaliatory eviction is illegal under FS §83.64 if you've complained about code violations or organized tenants within the prior year
  • Self-help eviction is illegal: changing locks, shutting off utilities, or removing belongings violates FS §83.67 — the landlord can be liable for actual and consequential damages or 3 months' rent, whichever is greater, plus court costs and attorney's fees
  • Fair housing: discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability is illegal under federal and Florida law. The Miami-Dade Office of Human Rights and Fair Employment Practices handles local intakes

For free legal help: Legal Services of Greater Miami represents low-income tenants in eviction defense. For state-level details, see our Florida housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint. For cross-county comparison, see our Jacksonville page (different programs, same state law).

Other Housing Programs in Miami

  • Public housing: HCD says it manages approximately 6,500 public housing units across Miami-Dade. It uses the same single HCD application as Section 8, which is closed for registration
  • LIHTC (Tax Credit): Miami-Dade has substantial LIHTC inventory. Search HUD's LIHTC database for properties in Miami-Dade County. See how to find LIHTC housing
  • Miami-Dade Documentary Stamp Surtax — county-funded affordable housing development. New income-restricted apartments come online each year
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): combines a voucher with VA case management. Miami-area veterans are referred through the Miami VA Healthcare System (Bruce W. Carter VAMC). See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Rapid Rehousing & Permanent Supportive Housing — coordinated through the Miami-Dade Homeless Trust. Access via the Homeless Helpline at 1-877-994-4357
  • Florida Housing Finance Corporation programs — SHIP (State Housing Initiatives Partnership) administered by the County for down-payment assistance and home repair. floridahousing.org
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator — Miami Beach CDC and Neighborhood Housing Services of South Florida cover Miami-Dade

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Miami Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

No. Miami-Dade Housing and Community Development (HCD), which some County pages still call Public Housing and Community Development (PHCD), says its waiting list is closed for registration (checked November 3, 2026). HCD says it provides rental assistance to more than 19,000 households through the Housing Choice Voucher program. The City of Miami also runs a separate, smaller Section 8 program, and it is not accepting new applications either. If you already applied, call HCD's Applicant Leasing Center at 786-469-4206 to verify your position.

What happened to Miami-Dade's Tenants' Bill of Rights?

Florida HB 1417 (effective July 1, 2023) preempted local tenant protections statewide. As a result, Miami-Dade's Tenants' Bill of Rights (Ordinance 22-47) is no longer in effect. The state's landlord-tenant statute (Chapter 83) is now the controlling law in Miami. One small upside from HB 1417: notice to end month-to-month tenancy is now 30 days (up from 15).

Where can I get emergency rental assistance in Miami?

Funding is thin. Miami-Dade's Emergency Rental Assistance Program (ERAP) is closed, the City of Miami's ERA program permanently closed on July 13, 2023, and the County's rent help for people facing eviction (up to $1,000 for rent) is on hold until more funding comes in. Start with the Office of Housing Advocacy Hotline at 786-469-4545, call the Homeless Helpline at 1-877-994-4357 and press 1 for prevention, and ask Camillus House about its Homeless Prevention Program. (Checked November 3, 2026.)

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Miami?

Camillus House Center of Hope (305-374-1065) is the 258-bed main downtown shelter. Chapman Partnership operates the Homeless Assistance Center downtown and Homestead. Lotus House serves women and children. Miami Rescue Mission serves men and women. Call the Miami-Dade Homeless Helpline at 1-877-994-4357 to reach outreach and coordinated entry.

Can a Miami landlord refuse Section 8?

It is unsettled. Florida has no statewide source-of-income protection, but Miami-Dade's Human Rights Ordinance (Chapter 11A) still lists source of income as a protected class in housing, and the County's Commission on Human Rights (305-375-2784) takes complaints. Florida's 2023 preemption law (HB 1417, section 83.425) covers landlord-tenant rules such as tenant screening but does not mention source of income, and we found no court ruling on whether it overrides the County's rule. Florida Statute 125.0103 also prohibits local rent control, so landlords can raise rent freely at lease end. Finding landlords who already accept vouchers is part of the work — HCD maintains a list of participating landlords.

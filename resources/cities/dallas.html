Affordable Housing in Dallas, TX

Waythrough Project · Updated October 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Dallas and the surrounding North Texas counties — not generic Section 8 advice. If you need shelter tonight, want to know whether DHA's waitlist is open, or are looking for an organization that can help with this month's rent, the named resources below are where to start.

Quick numbers to write down:
  • 211 Texas — dial 211 (free, 24/7) for any housing emergency in Dallas County
  • Dallas Housing Authority (DHA): 214-951-8300 · dhantx.com/applicants
  • The Bridge Homeless Recovery Center: (214) 670-1100 — 1818 Corsicana St, downtown Dallas

Emergency Help Tonight in Dallas

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or are facing an imminent eviction, these are the local resources to contact first:

  • The Bridge Homeless Recovery Center — shelter and day center in downtown Dallas, "open 24/7, 365 days a year," for people experiencing homelessness who are 18 or older; "No ID is required to enter." 1818 Corsicana St; general information (214) 670-1100, guest intake (214) 670-1507 (checked October 28, 2026). bridgehrc.org
  • Family Gateway — assessment and diversion, emergency shelter, and supportive housing for families with at least one child under 18 in Dallas or Collin County who are homeless or will be within 72 hours. Call 1-888-411-6802 (option 3), 24/7, or use its online assessment; main office 214-823-4500. It says it "does not have shelter or resources available for migrant/refugee/asylee families" (checked October 28, 2026). familygateway.org
  • Austin Street Center — shelter for women 18 and over and men 45 and over. You need "A Valid State or Federal Government I.D."; walk-up intake is Monday to Friday, 11 a.m. to 4 p.m., by lottery (the Welcome Center opens at 8 a.m.). 1717 Jeffries St, (214) 428-4242 (checked October 28, 2026). austinstreet.org
  • Genesis Women's Shelter & Support — emergency shelter and support for women and children experiencing domestic violence. Call or text 214-946-HELP (4357), 24 hours a day, in English or Spanish (checked October 28, 2026)
  • OurCalling — "We meet people in crisis through Street Outreach and our Navigation Center." Navigation Center: 1702 S Cesar Chavez Blvd, 7 a.m. to 3 p.m.; 214-444-8796 (checked October 28, 2026)
  • Housing Forward (formerly Metro Dallas Homeless Alliance) — Coordinated Access System — the front door to homeless services. Its access points are "Emergency Shelters, Street Outreach, and the Homeless Crisis Line": call the Homeless Crisis Helpline at 888-411-6802. For rent or utility help, Housing Forward says to call 2-1-1 (checked October 28, 2026). housingforwardntx.org
  • 211 Texas — "a free, anonymous social service hotline available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year." Dial 211 or (877) 541-7905 (checked October 28, 2026)

Section 8 in Dallas: DHA Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Dallas are administered by the Dallas Housing Authority (DHA Housing Solutions for North Texas). Current status (checked October 15, 2026):

  • DHA accepts a preliminary application at any time. DHA's own how-to-apply page says "You can submit a preliminary application at any time" and "DHA accepts pre-applications on an ongoing basis instead of limiting it to a few days" — so there is no opening window to watch for. Apply at dhantx.com/applicants
  • Your application expires after 18 months. DHA: "Once you apply, your application will stay on the waiting list until you are selected, or for up to 18 months." Its applicants page adds that if you are not selected in a lottery within 18 months the application expires "but you may re-apply" — so diary the date and re-apply rather than assuming you are still on the list
  • Public Housing through DHA — a separate program from Section 8, also open for preliminary applications at any time; you choose the properties. Since July 13, 2026 DHA has run weekly property lotteries on a schedule it publishes on its lottery page
  • Other DHA programs that may have separate eligibility: Project-Based Vouchers (PBV), Mainstream vouchers for non-elderly people with disabilities, and HUD-VASH for veterans
  • Apply to neighboring authorities too: you are not limited to DHA. The housing programs of Mesquite, Grand Prairie, Garland, McKinney and Plano, the Dallas County Housing Assistance Program, and the Texas Department of Housing & Community Affairs (state) operate separate lists with their own openings
  • Status check: DHA says "You can check the status of your application at any time by logging into the RentCafe Applicant Portal," which is linked from its applicants page (checked October 15, 2026). DHA's phone, 214-951-8300, is the number on HUD's PHA contact list and on DHA's own forms

Emergency Rental Assistance in Dallas (Named Programs)

The City of Dallas's own page says "Rental Assistance-Not accepting applications at this time."

If you're behind on rent or can't pay this month, these are the local organizations currently operating in Dallas. Funding shifts month to month — always call to confirm current availability:

  • Home Point — its Emergency Rental and Utility Assistance page says it provides "emergency financial assistance to households facing evictions and/or utility shut-offs," that "Commitments for financial assistance are made on first-come-first-serve basis," and that the amount "depends on a household’s income, available resources, and is subject to some limitations." The page does not say whether money is available right now, so call 214-828-4244 to ask (checked October 21, 2026). Home Point also holds a free Tenant Legal Workshop with Legal Aid of NorthWest Texas and the Dallas Volunteer Attorney Program on "every second and fourth Wednesdays of the month" at 2800 Live Oak, Dallas 75204. It is for "Low-Income households only," "Arrival by 5 PM is strongly encouraged," and the volunteer attorneys "offer advice only." homepoint.org
  • Dallas County Health & Human Services — Welfare Assistance — rent help only for residents with a disability. The County says it provides "temporary financial and supportive services," which may include "Rent or Mortgage Support" and "Utility Bill Assistance," for Dallas County residents 18 or older with limited income who are disabled (with medical documentation) and "Not receiving housing assistance (e.g., Section 8)." Appointments are required: call (214) 819-1800, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. (checked October 21, 2026). dallascounty.org
  • Catholic Charities Dallas — utility bill help, not rent help. Its Resource Support Services page lists one-time utility assistance for City of Dallas residents, Atmos Energy help ("one-time up to $500") for Atmos customers in Dallas and eight nearby counties, and up to "$600 per calendar year" for customers of NRG-affiliated electric companies such as Reliant; apply online. Its housing programs "do not accept outside referrals, calls for residency, or walk-ins." Helpline 866-223-7500 (checked October 28, 2026). ccdallas.org
  • St. Vincent de Paul North Texas — parish-conference volunteers help with emergency needs including "food, rent or utility assistance." Ask through its online Need Assistance form, once; "Conference volunteers usually respond within 48 hours." Its phone, (469) 607-0909, is for general information and inquiries (checked October 28, 2026). svdpdallas.org
  • Salvation Army of North Texas — "Serving North Texans with grocery, rent, and utility assistance." Call Emergency Family Services in Dallas at 214-424-7050 to ask what is available (checked October 28, 2026). salvationarmyntx.org
  • Crossroads Community Services — "nutritious food, nutrition education, and life-changing resources" for children, families, and seniors in Dallas, Ellis, and Navarro counties. (214) 560-2511 (checked October 28, 2026). ccsdallas.org
  • Texas Department of Housing & Community Affairs (TDHCA) — does not help renters directly: "TDHCA assistance and funds go to local provider organizations, NOT individuals directly." Its Help for Texans page finds the local providers for your city or county; its Emergency Solutions Grants money goes to "private nonprofit organizations, cities, and counties" (checked October 28, 2026). tdhca.texas.gov/help-for-texans

The Texas Rent Relief Program has ended

The Texas Rent Relief Program (TRR) and the Texas Eviction Diversion Program (TEDP), which distributed federal rental funds during the pandemic, both closed in summer 2023. All funds are gone. If anyone tells you to "apply to Texas Rent Relief," that information is out of date. The City of Dallas's own rental assistance is not taking applications either, so use the organizations listed above and our Dallas rent help guide.

Utility assistance: LIHEAP and CEAP

In Texas, the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program is administered as the Comprehensive Energy Assistance Program (CEAP) through local Community Action Agencies. In Dallas County, Dallas County HHS says "The Comprehensive Energy Assistance Program (CEAP) is open," by appointment only ("No walk-ins allowed"), for households with income at or below 150% of the federal poverty guideline. Call (214) 819-1848 (checked October 21, 2026). Lowering your utility bill frees up cash for rent. See the County's CEAP page.

Tenant Rights in Texas

Texas has one of the more landlord-friendly legal frameworks in the country. Knowing the specific rules in Dallas can save your tenancy:

  • No source-of-income protection — and cities are preempted: Texas does not have a statewide law preventing landlords from refusing Section 8 vouchers. In 2015, Texas SB 267 prohibited cities from passing local source-of-income ordinances, so Dallas cannot adopt one either. Finding a landlord who already accepts vouchers is part of the work — DHA can provide a list of participating landlords
  • 3-day notice to vacate for nonpayment (Texas Property Code §24.005) — among the shortest in the country. Once you get one, act immediately. See how to avoid eviction
  • Security deposit return: the landlord must return your deposit (or written itemized deductions) within 30 days of move-out (TX Prop Code §92.103). Texas does not cap the deposit amount
  • Warranty of habitability and right to repair: Texas Property Code Chapter 92 requires landlords to make a diligent effort to repair conditions that materially affect health or safety, after written notice and a reasonable time. Don't withhold rent without legal advice
  • Notice to end month-to-month: one full rental period (typically 30 days), from either side
  • Retaliatory eviction is illegal: Texas Property Code §92.331 prohibits eviction or rent increases in retaliation for repair requests or tenant organizing within the prior 6 months
  • Self-help eviction is illegal: Your landlord cannot change locks, shut off utilities, or remove belongings to force you out. They must go through Justice of the Peace court (TX Prop Code §92.0081)
  • Fair housing: discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability is illegal under the federal Fair Housing Act and Texas Property Code Chapter 301. The City of Dallas Fair Housing Division takes local complaints at (214) 670-FAIR (3247); you have one year after an alleged violation to report it (checked October 28, 2026)

For help: the Texas Tenants' Union holds online Tenants' Rights Workshops on the 2nd or 3rd Monday at 6:30 p.m. ((214) 823-2733), and Legal Aid of NorthWest Texas gives free civil legal help to low-income people, including in eviction cases: Legal Aid Line 1-888-529-5277, landlord-tenant line 855-548-8457 (checked October 28, 2026). For state-level details, see our Texas housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Other Housing Programs in Dallas

  • Public housing: DHA says it owns 31 properties with about 5,000 units across North Texas. The public housing waitlist is separate from Section 8 — ask about both when you call 214-951-8300
  • LIHTC (Tax Credit): privately owned income-restricted apartments — Dallas has one of the largest LIHTC inventories in Texas. Search HUD's LIHTC database for properties in Dallas County. See how to find LIHTC housing
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): combines a voucher with VA case management. Dallas-area veterans are referred through the VA North Texas Health Care System. See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Rapid Rehousing & Permanent Supportive Housing — coordinated through Housing Forward's Coordinated Access System. Access it through an emergency shelter, street outreach, or the Homeless Crisis Helpline, 888-411-6802
  • International Rescue Committee (IRC) in Dallas — resettlement help "Meeting the basic needs for food, shelter, education services, health services" of newly arrived refugee families, plus extended case management and job placement, for refugees, asylees, and other immigrants. 6500 Greenville Ave, Suite 500, (214) 461-9781 (checked October 28, 2026). rescue.org
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator — for example, the Dallas County Home Loan Counseling Center, which says it is "a HUD-approved housing counseling agency" and helps with home buying and foreclosure: (214) 819-6060 (checked October 28, 2026)

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I apply for Section 8 in Dallas right now?

Yes. DHA, Housing Solutions for North Texas states on its own how-to-apply page: "You can submit a preliminary application at any time," and "DHA accepts pre-applications on an ongoing basis instead of limiting it to a few days." So there is no opening window to catch. Two things to know: DHA selects from the pool by lottery, so applying early does not move you up; and your application expires — "Once you apply, your application will stay on the waiting list until you are selected, or for up to 18 months," after which you may re-apply. Its Administrative Plan calls this a continuously open waiting list, in place since January 21, 2020. Apply at dhantx.com/applicants. (Checked October 15, 2026.) DHA's web pages list no general phone number; 214-951-8300 is the phone number on HUD's PHA contact list for DHA, and it is printed on DHA's own forms, such as its Change of Ownership and/or Management packet for landlords (PDF). (Checked October 15, 2026.)

Is Texas Rent Relief still available in Dallas?

No. The Texas Rent Relief Program (TRR) and the Texas Eviction Diversion Program (TEDP) both closed in summer 2023 — all federal pandemic funds are spent. As of October 16, 2026 the City of Dallas says its rental assistance is "Not accepting applications at this time." Current paths include St. Vincent de Paul North Texas (through its online Need Assistance form), Dallas County's Welfare Assistance program for residents with a disability ((214) 819-1800), and 211. For free eviction help, call the Dallas Eviction Advocacy Center at (469) 436-2704.

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Dallas?

The Bridge Homeless Recovery Center at 1818 Corsicana St ((214) 670-1100) is a downtown shelter and day center, open 24/7 to people experiencing homelessness who are 18 or older. Family Gateway serves families with children: call 1-888-411-6802, option 3. Austin Street Center serves women 18 and over and men 45 and over. Genesis Women's Shelter has a 24-hour domestic violence line at (214) 946-4357. For other homeless services, call Housing Forward's Homeless Crisis Helpline at 888-411-6802. (Checked October 28, 2026.)

Can a Dallas landlord legally refuse my Section 8 voucher?

Yes. Texas has no statewide source-of-income protection, and Texas SB 267 (2015) prohibits cities like Dallas from passing local ordinances to require landlords to accept vouchers. You'll need to find landlords who already participate in the program — DHA can provide a list of participating landlords.

How long is the eviction notice in Texas?

Texas Property Code §24.005 sets the notice to vacate for nonpayment of rent at just 3 days — one of the shortest in the country. Once you receive a notice, contact Legal Aid of NorthWest Texas or Texas Tenants Union immediately. Self-help eviction (landlord lockouts, shutting off utilities, removing belongings) is illegal under §92.0081 — the landlord must go through Justice of the Peace court.

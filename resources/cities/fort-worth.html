Section 8 in Fort Worth: FWHS Status (checked October 15, 2026)

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Fort Worth are run by Fort Worth Housing Solutions (FWHS), the former Fort Worth Housing Authority. From FWHS's own pages:

  • Housing Choice Vouchers: not accepting applications. FWHS: "FWHS is not currently accepting Wait List applications." FWHS keeps its voucher wait list "through a lottery system" and suggests contacting its affordable communities directly in the meantime (fwhs.org).
  • Project-Based Voucher window, only November 1–5, 2026. FWHS says the PBV waitlist for these properties "will open Sunday, November 1, 2026, at 8:00 AM, and will close on Thursday, November 5, 2026, at 5:00 PM": Hughes House III, 4912 East Rosedale Street (1- and 2-bedroom); Sedona Village Senior Living, 2800 Sedona Ranch Drive (2-bedroom); and Cowan Place, 5400 East Rosedale Street (1- and 2-bedroom). FWHS allows two persons per bedroom. Applications are taken only from November 1, 2026 at 8:00 AM to November 5, 2026 at 5:00 PM; after that, this window is closed, so check fwhs.org for the next opening (checked October 28, 2026). During the window, apply online through the FWHS RentCafe portal linked in FWHS's announcement; you need an account, and people with disabilities can call 817-333-3400 for help with the pre-application. If you have no computer, use a public library.
  • No traditional public housing. FWHS says it "has transitioned out of traditional public housing." Instead it owns mixed-income affordable communities and Rental Assistance Demonstration (RAD) units where tenants pay 30% of adjusted income; for RAD units, FWHS says to "contact the property directly to ask about the waitlist" (FWHS housing options).
  • Mainstream vouchers serve people ages 18 to 61 with disabilities, and FWHS homelessness programs require a referral from a partner agency.
  • Moving to Fort Worth with a voucher: FWHS portability: portability@fwhs.org or 817-333-3605.

Neighboring authorities (Arlington, Grand Prairie, the Tarrant County Housing Assistance Office) keep separate lists. See our step-by-step Section 8 guide and what to do when you find an open waiting list.

Rent and Utility Help in Fort Worth

  • City of Fort Worth Community Action Partners (CAP) is the Community Action Agency for all of Tarrant County. On October 15, 2026, CAP listed Emergency Rental Assistance as currently unavailable: "all funding for Emergency Rental Assistance has been exhausted," and CAP does "not know when or if additional funds will become available." Still available: utility assistance for electric, gas and propane bills, HVAC repair, case management, and rent, mortgage and utility help for Tarrant County veterans, active military and surviving spouses. Online applications open Fridays in October 2026 at 9 a.m. (October 16, 23 and 30) and close when capacity is reached. 817-392-2276 · fortworthtexas.gov/CAP
  • Tarrant County Emergency Solutions Grant (ESG): short-term eviction prevention for income-qualified renters, but only "within Tarrant County, outside of Arlington, Fort Worth and Grand Prairie." If you live in those areas and have a notice to vacate, apply with the County's Prevention Screening Form (tarrantcountytx.gov).
  • Partnership Home (the Tarrant County Continuum of Care) does housing crisis screening for people experiencing homelessness: partnershiphome.org/help
  • Catholic Charities Fort Worth, St. Vincent de Paul and the Salvation Army give one-time help when they have funds; 211 knows which have money this month

The pandemic-era Texas Rent Relief program is closed. See our emergency rental assistance guide and utility assistance programs.

Texas Eviction Rules in Fort Worth (2026)

Texas Property Code § 24.005, as amended by SB 38 effective January 1, 2026 (statutes.capitol.texas.gov):

  • At least three days' written notice to vacate before the landlord can file, "unless the parties have contracted for a shorter or longer notice period in a written lease." Read your lease.
  • If you were not late before the month of the notice and the case is only about unpaid rent, the notice must be a notice to pay rent or vacate, which gives you the chance to pay.
  • How notice can be delivered: by mail, inside the home in a conspicuous place, by hand to a tenant 16 or older, or by email or other electronic means only if you agreed to that in writing.
  • Only a court can evict you. Eviction suits are filed in Justice of the Peace court with a sworn petition. Lockouts, utility shut-offs and removing your belongings are illegal (Property Code § 92.0081).
  • Repairs and retaliation: landlords must make a diligent effort to repair conditions that materially affect health or safety after written notice (Property Code Chapter 92), and may not retaliate against a tenant for a repair request (§ 92.331). Don't withhold rent without legal advice.
  • Vouchers: Texas has no source-of-income protection and bars cities from adopting one, so a Fort Worth landlord may legally decline a voucher. Ask FWHS which landlords participate.

Free legal help: Legal Aid of NorthWest Texas, 600 East Weatherford St., Fort Worth. Landlord-tenant line 855-548-8457; Legal Aid Line 888-529-5277 (lanwt.org). See also how to avoid eviction and our Texas page.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Fort Worth Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

No. Fort Worth Housing Solutions says "FWHS is not currently accepting Wait List applications" for Housing Choice Vouchers (checked October 15, 2026). FWHS announced a Project-Based Voucher window only from November 1, 2026 at 8:00 AM to November 5, 2026 at 5:00 PM for Hughes House III, Sedona Village Senior Living and Cowan Place, with applications online through its RentCafe portal; after 5:00 PM on November 5, 2026, that window is closed (checked October 28, 2026). Watch fwhs.org or call 817-333-3400.

Does Fort Worth have public housing?

Not in the traditional sense. FWHS says it has transitioned out of traditional public housing. It owns mixed-income affordable communities and Rental Assistance Demonstration (RAD) units, where tenants pay 30 percent of adjusted income; for RAD units, FWHS says to contact the property directly about its waitlist. LIHTC apartments across Tarrant County are another option.

Where can I get rental assistance in Fort Worth right now?

Options are thin. On October 15, 2026, the City of Fort Worth's Community Action Partners said all funding for Emergency Rental Assistance has been exhausted, though utility help and veterans' rent, mortgage and utility help remain (817-392-2276). Tarrant County's ESG prevention program serves only renters outside Fort Worth, Arlington and Grand Prairie. Dial 211 for programs that have funds this month.

How much notice does a Texas landlord have to give before filing an eviction?

At least three days' written notice to vacate, unless your written lease sets a shorter or longer period (Texas Property Code 24.005, as amended by SB 38 from January 1, 2026). If you were not late before the month of the notice and the case is only about rent, the notice must be a notice to pay rent or vacate. Only a Justice of the Peace court can order an eviction; lockouts are illegal.

Can a Fort Worth landlord refuse Section 8?

Yes. Texas has no statewide source-of-income protection and prohibits cities like Fort Worth from adopting one, so landlords may legally decline vouchers. Ask FWHS for help finding participating landlords, and contact Legal Aid of NorthWest Texas at 855-548-8457 if a refusal seems to be about race, disability, family status or another protected class.

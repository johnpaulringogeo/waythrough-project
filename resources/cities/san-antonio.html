Affordable Housing in San Antonio, TX

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

San Antonio's housing system runs through two authorities — Opportunity Home San Antonio (formerly SAHA) for the city and the Housing Authority of Bexar County (HABC) for surrounding areas — and a single nationally-recognized shelter campus (Haven for Hope) that consolidated most homeless services. Here's what's currently operating. If you are behind on rent, our San Antonio emergency rent help guide explains the City's once-a-month rent application window, who qualifies, and the free Right to Counsel lawyers.

Section 8 in San Antonio: Opportunity Home vs. HABC

Apply to both authorities when their lists open — they cover different geographies.

Opportunity Home San Antonio (formerly SAHA)

  • HCV (Housing Choice Voucher) waitlist is closed. Opportunity Home says "Applications for the Housing Choice Voucher Program are accepted only when the waitlist is open. Openings will be advertised on our home page," and its home page announced no opening (checked November 3, 2026)
  • Wait time: Opportunity Home gives a wait of up to 4 years
  • Public Housing: unlike Section 8, Opportunity Home says you "can apply any time" for public housing and its other assisted housing online, but on November 3, 2026 its application portal listed only two open public housing lists, "Elderly/Disabled Housing" and "504 Accessible Housing." Its 2026–2027 Moving to Work Plan says the family public housing list has been closed since May 26, 2025. It lists a 2 to 4 year wait for public housing
  • Apply at: homesa.org · in person at 818 South Flores · Monday-Friday 8am-5pm

Our full guide to Opportunity Home: Opportunity Home San Antonio (formerly SAHA): waitlist status, contact and how to apply.

Housing Authority of Bexar County (HABC)

  • Serves Bexar County areas outside San Antonio city limits — Schertz, Selma, Live Oak, Universal City, Converse, Helotes, and unincorporated areas
  • Separate Section 8 waitlist from Opportunity Home. HABC's portal page says "the housing choice voucher program waiting list is currently closed" (checked November 3, 2026). habctx.org/applicant-portal

TDHCA — Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs

  • Statewide voucher program for areas without local PHAs, plus LIHTC allocations. Worth applying to as a third option: tdhca.texas.gov

Emergency Rental Assistance in San Antonio

Start here: our San Antonio emergency rent help guide covers the City's Rental Assistance Program step by step: when the portal opens, who qualifies, what to upload, what your landlord must send, and the free eviction lawyers (checked November 3, 2026).

  • City of San Antonio Rental Assistance Program (Neighborhood & Housing Services Department) — up to three months of help, with a $3,500 maximum for rent and $1,500 for utilities, for renters inside the city limits (Council Districts 1-10) at or below 80% of the Area Median Income who have an active Notice to Vacate or eviction citation. The landlord must agree to accept the money. Applications are accepted only on the second Wednesday of each month from 1 p.m. to 2 p.m.; in November 2026 the portal opens only on Thursday, November 12, from 1 p.m. to 2 p.m. After 2 p.m. on November 12 that window is closed, so check the City's Rental Assistance page for the next one. Status line 210-207-6459
  • Outside the city limits: the City's page for residents outside the city lists Bexar County Resources at 210-335-3666 and the Alamo Area Council of Governments' Alamo Service Connection at 210-477-3275
  • City of San Antonio Utility Assistance (Department of Human Services) — help with CPS Energy bills for households at or below 150% of the federal poverty guidelines, with priority for families with children under 17, people with a disability, and adults 60 and older. 210-207-8198
  • St. Vincent de Paul San Antonio — emergency assistance through parish conferences across the metro
  • Catholic Charities of the Archdiocese of San Antonio — emergency financial assistance through its Guadalupe Community Center and St. Stephen's CARE Center, plus immigration legal services. Guadalupe Community Center: (210) 226-6178
  • Society of St. Vincent de Paul Christian Service Center at 1421 W Commerce — walk-in emergency aid
  • YWCA San Antonio — Family Empowerment Program with case management and emergency support
  • 211 Texas — the front door for everything else. Dial 211 (or 877-541-7905) to be routed to whoever currently has rental assistance funds
  • Comal Independent Resource Center and Alamo Area Council of Governments serve surrounding counties

Tenant Rights in Texas — San Antonio Specifics

Texas has weaker tenant protections than most large states. The basics:

  • No state source-of-income protection — landlords in Texas can legally refuse Section 8 vouchers. San Antonio has no local ordinance overriding this. Opportunity Home maintains a list of voucher-friendly landlords — call (210) 477-6000 and ask
  • No rent control — no state or local cap on rent increases. Landlords can raise rent any amount at lease renewal with proper notice
  • Eviction notice for nonpayment: 3 days written before the landlord can file. Texas evictions move fast — read every notice and act the day you receive it
  • Court process: filed in the Bexar County Justice of the Peace court for your precinct. The trial must be held between the 10th and 21st day after the case is filed. Since January 1, 2026 (SB 38), if the landlord files a motion for summary disposition, you have only until the 4th day after you are served to file a written response. If you lose, you have 5 days to appeal, and the writ of possession cannot issue before the 6th day after the judgment
  • Right to Counsel: the City of San Antonio partners with Texas RioGrande Legal Aid (TRLA) to give free legal help to low-income households facing eviction. If you got a notice to appear in court or have a judgment against you, ask City staff for a direct referral or call TRLA's intake line at 956-996-8752 (righttocounsel@trla.org)
  • Security deposit return: 30 days from move-out, itemized. Bad-faith withholding triggers $100 + 3x the wrongful amount + attorney fees
  • Habitability: Texas Property Code §92.052+ requires landlords to address conditions materially affecting health and safety. Submit repair requests in writing and follow the statutory notice-and-cure process
  • No self-help eviction: landlords cannot change locks or shut off utilities — Texas Property Code §92.0081
  • Fair housing: federal Fair Housing Act applies. Local complaints can go through the City of San Antonio Department of Human Services Fair Housing Office

State-level details: Texas housing resources. To file a complaint: how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Other Affordable Housing Options in San Antonio

  • Opportunity Home Public Housing: mixed-finance and traditional public housing developments across the city. Apply through homesa.org
  • Project-Based Vouchers (PBV) at specific developments — often shorter waits than tenant-based Section 8
  • LIHTC (Tax Credit) properties: thousands of income-restricted units across Bexar County. Search HUD's LIHTC database or TDHCA's property directory. See how to find LIHTC housing
  • HUD-VASH for veterans: referrals through the Audie L. Murphy VA Hospital in San Antonio. See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Rapid Rehousing through Haven for Hope's network for households exiting homelessness
  • San Antonio Housing Trust — finances affordable housing developments throughout the city, increasing supply

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Opportunity Home San Antonio (SAHA) Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

No. Opportunity Home San Antonio (formerly SAHA) accepts Housing Choice Voucher applications only when the waitlist is open and advertises openings on its home page, which announced no opening as of November 3, 2026. For public housing, only the elderly/disabled and 504 accessible lists were open on its application portal; the family public housing list has been closed since May 26, 2025. Opportunity Home lists a public housing wait of 2 to 4 years. The Housing Authority of Bexar County (HABC) waitlist is also closed. Opportunity Home gives a voucher wait of up to 4 years. Call (210) 477-6000 or visit homesa.org to check status.

What is the difference between Opportunity Home and HABC?

Opportunity Home San Antonio (formerly SAHA) serves residents within San Antonio city limits. The Housing Authority of Bexar County (HABC) serves Bexar County areas outside the city — Schertz, Selma, Live Oak, Universal City, Converse, Helotes, and unincorporated areas. They run separate Section 8 waitlists. Apply to both when their lists open.

Where do I go for emergency shelter in San Antonio?

Haven for Hope at 1 Haven for Hope Way is the largest homeless services campus in Texas — a one-stop hub for shelter, meals, healthcare, behavioral health, and case management. Walk-in 24/7 through the Prospects Courtyard, or call 210-220-2100. SAMM (San Antonio Metropolitan Ministry) serves families. Salvation Army Hope Center serves men, women, and families. For DV survivors, call the Battered Women & Children's Shelter crisis line at 210-733-8810.

Where can I get emergency rental assistance in San Antonio?

The main door is the City of San Antonio's Rental Assistance Program: up to $3,500 for rent and $1,500 for utilities for renters inside the city limits at or below 80% of the Area Median Income who have an active Notice to Vacate or eviction citation. It takes applications only from 1 p.m. to 2 p.m. on the second Wednesday of each month; in November 2026 the window is Thursday, November 12, and after 2 p.m. that day it is closed until the next month's window. Call 210-207-6459 with questions. Outside the city limits, the City lists Bexar County Resources at 210-335-3666. Dial 211 Texas to find whichever community organization currently has open funds.

Does San Antonio have a Right to Counsel in eviction cases?

Yes. The City of San Antonio partners with Texas RioGrande Legal Aid (TRLA) on a Right to Counsel program for low-income households facing eviction. If you got a notice to appear in court or have a judgment against you, ask City staff for a direct referral or call TRLA's intake line at 956-996-8752, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Show up to your court date — default judgments lead to fast lockouts under Texas's eviction timeline, and since January 1, 2026, a summary disposition motion gives you only 4 days after you are served to respond.

Affordable Housing in Columbus, OH

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Columbus and Franklin County, not generic Section 8 advice. Two things stand out in Columbus right now: CMHA runs a continuous lottery pool rather than an open-and-close waiting list, so open or closed is the wrong question here, and the city has a local source-of-income protection ordinance that makes voucher refusal illegal. The named resources below are where to start.

Quick numbers to write down:
  • LSS 211 Central Ohio (formerly HandsOn Central Ohio): dial 211 (free; call, text or chat) for rent, utility and housing help in Franklin County
  • Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority (CMHA): (614) 421-6000 · 880 E 11th Ave · cmhanet.com
  • Community Shelter Board (CoC central intake): 614-274-7000 · csb.org
  • IMPACT Community Action: (614) 252-2799 · HEAP and Winter Crisis appointments

Emergency Help Tonight in Columbus

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or are facing an imminent eviction, these are the local resources to contact first:

  • YWCA Family Center: Columbus's main family shelter; intake for all families experiencing homelessness in Franklin County
  • Faith Mission (Lutheran Social Services): emergency shelter for men, and a separate location for women and families
  • Van Buren Center: emergency shelter for single adults, operated through the Community Shelter Board network
  • Star House: 24-hour drop-in center and emergency shelter for youth and young adults ages 14–24
  • Choices For Victims of Domestic Violence: DV crisis line and shelter: (614) 224-4663 (Columbus) or (800) 247-2336 (statewide). Bilingual advocates
  • Community Shelter Board (CSB) Coordinated Entry: single intake for all CoC-funded shelters in Franklin County. Call 614-274-7000 or 211
  • LSS 211 Central Ohio: free information line (call, text or web chat) for shelters, food, rent and utility assistance, and social services

For a full walkthrough of finding shelter the first night, see our emergency housing tonight guide.

Section 8 in Columbus: CMHA Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Columbus are administered by the Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority (CMHA), serving more than 15,000 low- and moderate-income households across Franklin County. Current status (checked November 4, 2026):

  • CMHA does not say open or closed. It runs a lottery pool. The only status wording on its own Housing Choice Voucher page is "Waitlist selections are currently in process. If selected, you will receive a notice via mail (and email if on file) with the next steps." Register online through CMHA's RentCafe portal at cmhanet.com/apply-housing-waitlist
  • Applying early buys you nothing, and your application expires at two years. CMHA's Administrative Plan: "Applications will be taken continuously or for specified periods of time - eligible applicants will have an equal chance of being drawn in the Lottery irrespective of the date and time of their application," and "Preliminary Applications will be retained in the Lottery Pool for two years. If they have not been drawn in the Wait List Lottery by that time, the applicant will be notified that the Preliminary Application has expired." Diary the date and re-register
  • CMHA cut its payment standard on July 1, 2026. In its own statement CMHA says it "is facing a projected funding shortfall within the federal Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) Program due to insufficient federal appropriations" and "will be reducing its payment standard from 110% of Fair Market Rent (FMR) to 100% of FMR." CMHA adds that "some voucher holders may be required to contribute a greater share of their income toward housing costs." The statement says nothing about pausing or closing intake
  • Public Housing through CMHA is a separate program with its own waitlist for CMHA-owned properties. Apply when you call
  • Other special programs: HUD-VASH for veterans, Mainstream vouchers for non-elderly people with disabilities, Family Self-Sufficiency (FSS), each with a separate referral process
  • Apply to neighboring authorities too: Fairfield Metropolitan Housing Authority, Delaware MHA, Licking MHA, and the Ohio Housing Finance Agency run separate lists with their own openings
  • Income limits: Columbus AMI is $109,000 for a family of four; Very Low Income (50% AMI) is $54,500. Many working families qualify, so check the limits before assuming you don't
  • Status check: call CMHA at (614) 421-6000 if you've already applied and need to verify your position on the list

For the national application process, see our step-by-step Section 8 guide and how to find your PHA.

Emergency Rental Assistance in Columbus (Named Programs)

If you're behind on rent or can't pay this month, these are the local organizations currently operating in Columbus. Funding shifts month to month, so always call to confirm current availability:

  • IMPACT Community Action: Franklin County's community action agency and HEAP provider. Its Housing Stability & Supportive Services page says: "Due to high demand, our wait list is currently paused" (checked November 4, 2026), so treat it as a utility door for now: HEAP and Winter Crisis appointments are posted daily at 10 a.m. for the next business day. 711 Southwood Ave., (614) 252-2799. impactca.org
  • Franklin County Prevention, Retention & Contingency (PRC): Jobs & Family Services Emergency Assistance for households with a minor child, a pregnant person or a non-custodial parent, at or below 175% of the federal poverty level, with rent no more than 75% of income and no Emergency Assistance in the last 12 months. The County's PRC plan says "Emergency Assistance cannot exceed $2,000," and rent qualifies when it is overdue or there is an eviction notice. PRC Inbound Center 614-233-2999; JFS staff also screen tenants at eviction court (checked November 4, 2026). franklincountyohio.gov
  • Rentful614: Franklin County's rental assistance hub. Helps tenants and landlords find current programs. rentful614.com
  • Columbus Urban League Housing Accelerator is not a rent fund now: CUL says "emergency housing funds have been discontinued," and its Housing Accelerator offers coaching and services for people carrying rental debt (checked November 4, 2026). cul.org: Housing Accelerator
  • City of Columbus Resilient Housing Initiative: the City's own homelessness-prevention program, run through seven community agencies, for extremely low-income Columbus residents facing an immediate housing crisis; not all participants get money. Reach it through the RHI Screening Line, 614-278-0080. On November 4, 2026 Rentful614 listed its status as "FROZEN." columbus.gov
  • Salvation Army of Central Ohio: seasonal rent and utility assistance
  • Catholic Social Services of Central Ohio: emergency financial assistance, food, immigration legal services
  • St. Vincent de Paul Society of Columbus: one-time emergency rental and utility help through local parish conferences

Federal pandemic-era ERA has ended

The federal pandemic Emergency Rental Assistance Program (ERAP) that distributed billions through Ohio counties has closed. Current Columbus paths are Franklin County PRC, the City's Resilient Housing Initiative when its screening line is open, 211, and the nonprofits above. Don't waste time on old 2021–2023 application portals.

Utility assistance: HEAP and PIPP

Ohio's Home Energy Assistance Program (HEAP) helps with winter heating bills (typically through May) and the Summer Crisis Program runs July–September. The Percentage of Income Payment Plan (PIPP) lets eligible Ohioans pay a percentage of household income toward their utility bill. Apply through IMPACT Community Action, Franklin County's provider, or call 211. The HEAP Winter Crisis Program runs November 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027 for households at or below 175% of the federal poverty guidelines facing disconnection; IMPACT posts appointments daily at 10 a.m. for the next business day (checked November 4, 2026). Lowering your utility bill frees up cash for rent.

Tenant Rights in Columbus & Ohio

Ohio's state framework is generally landlord-friendly, but Columbus has stronger local protections than the rest of Ohio:

  • Source-of-income protection in Columbus (Ordinance 0494-2021, July 2021): it is illegal in Columbus for a landlord to refuse to rent, discriminate in rental terms, or discourage a prospective tenant based on source of income, including Section 8 vouchers, child support, and other government assistance. A landlord who violates this commits a first-degree misdemeanor (up to 180 days in jail and a $1,000 fine). Report violations to the Columbus Urban League, the city's fair housing contractor. Note: outside Columbus, Ohio has no statewide source-of-income protection
  • 3-day notice to vacate for nonpayment (Ohio Revised Code §1923.04), among the shortest in the country. Act fast
  • 30-day notice for month-to-month termination from either party
  • Security deposit return: within 30 days, with an itemized list of any deductions (ORC §5321.16). Ohio does not cap deposit amounts
  • Warranty of habitability: ORC §5321.04 requires landlords to comply with building, health, and housing codes; keep common areas safe; provide working plumbing, heat, hot water, and structurally sound housing
  • Retaliatory eviction is illegal under ORC §5321.02 if the landlord acts in response to tenant complaints to code enforcement, joining a tenant association, or pursuing legal remedies
  • Self-help eviction is illegal: ORC §5321.15 prohibits landlords from locking you out, shutting off utilities, or removing belongings. They must use court eviction (forcible entry and detainer)
  • Pay to Stay (City of Columbus, Ordinance 2109-2023, City Code 4551.07): if you, or an agency on your behalf, tender all past-due rent with reasonable late fees (and court costs once a case is filed) before judgment and the landlord refuses, that tender is an affirmative defense in a nonpayment eviction. A separate ordinance (2108-2023) requires landlords to accept third-party payments for a tenant not otherwise in breach
  • Access to Legal Services (City of Columbus, Ordinance 3510-2024, City Code 4551.08): Columbus tenants with at least one child and income at or below 100% of the federal poverty guidelines are eligible for legal services in an eviction case filed in a Franklin County court, as funded by City Council; Legal Aid's Tenant Advocacy Project meets tenants at eviction court
  • Fair housing: discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, military status, ancestry, or disability is illegal under Ohio Revised Code Chapter 4112. Columbus adds source of income, sexual orientation, and gender identity as protected classes locally

For free legal help: Legal Aid of Southeast and Central Ohio (LASCO), whose Tenant Advocacy Project represents low-income tenants at eviction court, takes applications at 888-246-4420 (Columbus intake 9:30 a.m. to 1:30 p.m., Monday through Friday) or online at any hour; the Franklin County Municipal Court's Self Help Center (16th floor, (614) 645-7381, weekdays 9 a.m. to 3 p.m.) gives legal information and forms but not legal advice. For state-level details, see our Ohio housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Other Housing Programs in Columbus

  • Public housing: CMHA owns more than 2,000 public-housing units across Columbus. Application is separate from Section 8. Ask when you call (614) 421-6000
  • LIHTC (Tax Credit): privately owned income-restricted apartments. Columbus has a substantial LIHTC inventory growing each year. Search HUD's LIHTC database for properties in Franklin County. See how to find LIHTC housing
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): combines a voucher with VA case management. Columbus-area veterans are referred through the Chalmers P. Wylie VA Ambulatory Care Center. See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Rapid Rehousing & Permanent Supportive Housing: coordinated through the Community Shelter Board (CSB), Franklin County's CoC lead. Access via Coordinated Entry (call 614-274-7000 or 211)
  • Ohio Housing Finance Agency programs: down-payment assistance, homebuyer programs, and Ohio Heroes Mortgage for first responders, teachers, and military. ohiohome.org
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator. Homeport, Columbus Housing Partnership, and IMPACT offer counseling

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Columbus Section 8 waitlist open, and how does CMHA actually select people?

CMHA never says open or closed, and that is not evasion, open or closed is the wrong question in Columbus. The only status language on its own Housing Choice Voucher page is "Waitlist selections are currently in process. If selected, you will receive a notice via mail (and email if on file) with the next steps." The reason is structural: CMHA's Administrative Plan describes a continuous lottery pool, in which "Applications will be taken continuously or for specified periods of time - eligible applicants will have an equal chance of being drawn in the Lottery irrespective of the date and time of their application." Two consequences worth knowing: applying early gains you nothing, and "Preliminary Applications will be retained in the Lottery Pool for two years," after which they expire and you must re-register. Register online through CMHA's RentCafe portal, linked from cmhanet.com/apply-housing-waitlist. CMHA says to call (614) 421-6000 during business hours if you need help registering, and not to call "regarding your position or request to be placed on the waitlist." Note that CMHA is Columbus, Ohio. The Housing Authority of Columbus, Georgia is a different agency. (Checked September 9, 2026; registration details checked September 21, 2026.)

Can a Columbus landlord refuse my Section 8 voucher?

No, not in Columbus. Ordinance 0494-2021 (July 2021) makes source-of-income discrimination illegal in Columbus. Landlords cannot refuse to rent, discriminate in terms, or discourage applicants based on source of income, including Section 8 vouchers. Violations are first-degree misdemeanors with up to 180 days in jail and a $1,000 fine. Report violations to the Columbus Urban League, the city's fair housing contractor. Outside Columbus, Ohio has no statewide source-of-income protection.

Where can I get emergency rental assistance in Columbus?

Franklin County Prevention, Retention & Contingency (PRC) Emergency Assistance is the main door, but only for households with a minor child, a pregnant person or a non-custodial parent at or below 175% of the federal poverty level; the County's plan says it "cannot exceed $2,000" (PRC Inbound Center 614-233-2999), and Job and Family Services staff screen tenants at eviction court. The City's Resilient Housing Initiative (614-278-0080) is only for extremely low-income Columbus residents facing homelessness, and Rentful614.com listed it as "FROZEN" on November 4, 2026. IMPACT Community Action says its housing stability wait list "is currently paused," and the Columbus Urban League says "emergency housing funds have been discontinued." Dial 211 for what is open this month.

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Columbus?

Call 614-274-7000 (Community Shelter Board) or 211 to access Coordinated Entry. YWCA Family Center is the main family shelter. Faith Mission (Lutheran Social Services) shelters single men and separately shelters women with families. Van Buren Center serves single adults. Star House is a 24-hour youth shelter (ages 14–24). Choices for Victims of Domestic Violence: (614) 224-4663 crisis line.

How long is the eviction notice in Ohio?

Ohio Revised Code §1923.04 sets the notice to vacate for nonpayment at just 3 days, one of the shortest in the country. Once you receive a 3-day notice, contact Legal Aid of Southeast and Central Ohio at 888-246-4420 immediately, and apply to Franklin County PRC if you have a child at home. If you are summoned, go to the Franklin County Municipal Court hearing, where Legal Aid's Tenant Advocacy Project meets tenants before their cases. Self-help eviction (lockouts, utility shutoffs, removing belongings) is illegal under ORC §5321.15. The landlord must go through court.

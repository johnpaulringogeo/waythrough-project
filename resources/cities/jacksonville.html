Section 8 and Low-Income Housing in Jacksonville, FL

Waythrough Project · Updated September 2026

This page is about getting into subsidized or income-restricted housing in Jacksonville and Duval County: the Jacksonville Housing Authority (JHA) Section 8 and public housing lists, JHA's project-based voucher properties, and tax credit (LIHTC) apartments. Section 8 in Jacksonville: JHA Waitlist Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Jacksonville are administered by the Jacksonville Housing Authority (JHA), which manages 2,881 public housing units and roughly 7,868 Housing Choice Vouchers across Duval, Clay, St. Johns and Nassau counties. Current status:

  • The general HCV waitlist is closed and has been since May 31, 2023, with no scheduled reopening. JHA's site still read "All Waiting Lists(s) are officially closed effective Wednesday, May 31st, 2023" on November 18, 2026. The last opening ran February 8, 2021 to May 31, 2023, when JHA closed the waiting lists for all of its housing programs at 5:00 p.m.
  • Mainstream Voucher waitlist: closed. It opened alongside the general HCV list on February 8, 2021 and was closed in the same May 31, 2023 shutdown. These vouchers are for non-elderly people with disabilities. Watch for the next opening
  • Age 62+ and disability: these are preferences that move you up the main HCV list. JHA does not run them as separate waiting lists
  • How long the wait runs: JHA’s own public-housing FAQ gives 3–5 years for 1–2 bedrooms, 1–3 years for 3 bedrooms, 3–5 years for 4–5 bedrooms, and about 5 years for high-rise studios. Plan for the long haul
  • JHA preferences (move applicants up the list): Duval County residents, applicants age 62 or older, honorably discharged veterans, families with a disabled member, victims of domestic violence, and households displaced by government action or a condemned dwelling. For public housing, working families are selected 2:1 over non-working families. Document everything when you apply
  • Public housing through JHA is a separate program with its own waitlist, also closed in the May 31, 2023 shutdown. Individual lists have opened briefly since (see the next section)
  • Apply to neighboring authorities too: Housing Authority of Clay County, St. Johns County Housing Partnership, and the Florida Housing Finance Corporation run separate lists with their own openings
  • Status check: call JHA at (904) 630-3810 if you've already applied and need to verify your position on the list

For the national application process, see our step-by-step Section 8 guide and how to find your PHA.

Project-Based and Public Housing Apartments in Jacksonville

While JHA's main lists are closed, its Affordable Housing Now Available page lists project-based voucher (PBV) properties with 1 to 4 bedroom units, and you apply at the property, not through the closed list (checked November 18, 2026). JHA's steps: pick a unit from the list and call the complex; if the property manager has a unit and approves your application, the manager contacts JHA's housing counselors; once JHA confirms you are eligible, it issues a certificate. JHA warns that "Project Based Vouchers are NOT transferable," so if you move out, the help ends. The properties on JHA's list:

  • Brentwood Lake Apartments, 761 Village Center Drive N.: 904-353-1338 (2 and 3 bedrooms)
  • Caroline Arms, 6457 Ft. Caroline: 904-744-2966 (2, 3 and 4 bedrooms)
  • Cathedral Townhouse Apartments, 501 North Ocean Street: 904-798-5377
  • Oak Hammock Apartments, 500 Acme Street: 904-855-0730 (2 and 3 bedrooms)
  • Senior Citizen Village, 801 West 4th Street: 904-359-0907 (1 and 2 bedrooms, elderly and disabled)

Public housing: JHA owns and runs its public housing, from scattered duplexes to high-rises for seniors, with units on every side of town and in Baldwin and Jacksonville Beach, and rent "set for each family based on their anticipated annual adjusted income." The list is closed, but single lists have reopened briefly: JHA reopened the five-bedroom list in November 2023, then closed Centennial Towers, Hogan Creek and the five-bedroom lists on August 23, 2024. JHA's public housing communities list shows each property; check jaxha.org regularly for openings. Our JHA guide tracks each list.

LIHTC (Tax Credit) Apartments in Jacksonville

Tax credit apartments are privately owned buildings that keep rents below market for households under an income limit, most often 60% of area median income. There is no central waiting list and you do not need a voucher: you apply at each property's leasing office. Search HUD's LIHTC database for Duval County, then call the buildings and ask which have openings and what their income limits are. Our guide on how to find LIHTC housing walks through the search.

Other Housing Programs in Jacksonville

  • HUD-VASH (veterans): combines a voucher with VA case management. Jacksonville-area veterans referred through the North Florida/South Georgia Veterans Health System. See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Rapid Rehousing & Permanent Supportive Housing: coordinated through Changing Homelessness (the Continuum of Care lead agency). Access via Coordinated Entry (call 211)
  • Florida Housing Finance Corporation programs: includes the State Housing Initiatives Partnership (SHIP) administered by the City of Jacksonville for down-payment assistance and home repair
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator. Family Foundations and Jacksonville Urban League offer counseling

Rent Help and Eviction Help in Jacksonville

The short version: the City of Jacksonville Emergency Assistance Program, (904) 255-2469, helps with rent or utilities if you have a delinquent, eviction or shut-off notice. The Jacksonville Eviction Diversion Program can pay up to 3 months of back rent, but your landlord has to start it (jacksonvilleevictiondiversion.org). Jacksonville Area Legal Aid, (904) 356-8371, gives free help to tenants facing eviction. The pandemic-era ERAP program has closed and its funds are spent.

Other places to ask: St. Vincent de Paul Society of Jacksonville, one-time rent and utility help through local parish conferences; the Salvation Army of Jacksonville, eviction prevention and utility help; and the Mental Health Resource Center, housing help for people with serious mental illness. For power bills, LIHEAP in Duval County runs through LJD Jewish Family & Community Services and other partners; call 211.

Tenant Rights in Florida

Florida has a landlord-friendly legal framework. Knowing the specific rules in Jacksonville can save your tenancy:

  • No source-of-income protection: Florida does not have a statewide law preventing landlords from refusing Section 8 vouchers. Jacksonville has no local ordinance either. Finding a landlord who already accepts vouchers is part of the work. JHA maintains a list of participating landlords
  • No rent control allowed: Florida Statute 125.0103 prohibits cities and counties from passing local rent-control ordinances except in a declared housing emergency (which is very rare). Jacksonville landlords can raise rent freely at lease end
  • 3-day notice for nonpayment (Florida Statute §83.56): excludes Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. Among the shortest in the country. Act fast
  • Security deposit return: within 15 days with no deductions, or 30 days with itemized deductions sent by certified mail (FS §83.49). Florida does not cap deposit amounts
  • Warranty of habitability: Florida Statute §83.51 requires landlords to comply with building, housing, and health codes; provide working plumbing, heat in winter, hot water, and structural soundness
  • Notice to end month-to-month: 15 days from either side under FS §83.57
  • Retaliatory eviction is illegal under FS §83.64 if you've complained about code violations or organized other tenants within the prior year
  • Self-help eviction is illegal: changing locks, shutting off utilities, or removing belongings violates FS §83.67. The landlord can be liable for 3 months' rent plus damages
  • Fair housing: discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability is illegal under federal and Florida law (Florida Fair Housing Act). The Jacksonville Human Rights Commission handles local intakes

For free legal help: Jacksonville Area Legal Aid (JALA) and Three Rivers Legal Services serve low-income tenants in Duval County. For state-level details, see our Florida housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Jacksonville Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

No. The Jacksonville Housing Authority (JHA) general Housing Choice Voucher waitlist has been closed since May 31, 2023, with no reopening scheduled. The Mainstream Voucher list (for non-elderly people with disabilities) is also closed; it opened alongside the general list on February 8, 2021 and closed in the same May 31, 2023 closure. Age 62+ and disability are preferences on the main HCV list rather than separate waiting lists. JHA’s published public-housing waits run about 1 to 5 years depending on bedroom size. Call JHA at (904) 630-3810 to verify position or ask about other programs.

How do I find low-income apartments in Jacksonville?

JHA's Section 8 and public housing lists have been closed since May 31, 2023, so start with its project-based voucher properties, which you apply to at the complex: Brentwood Lake Apartments (904-353-1338), Caroline Arms (904-744-2966), Cathedral Townhouse Apartments (904-798-5377), Oak Hammock Apartments (904-855-0730) and Senior Citizen Village (904-359-0907), as listed on JHA's site on November 18, 2026. The help stays with the unit, so it ends if you move out. Then apply to tax credit (LIHTC) apartments: search HUD's database at huduser.gov/lihtc for Duval County and apply at each leasing office. For rent help, call the City's Emergency Assistance Program at (904) 255-2469; our Jacksonville emergency rent help guide has the steps.

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Jacksonville?

Sulzbacher Center downtown is Jacksonville's main emergency shelter and day center: (904) 359-0457. City Rescue Mission serves men, women, and families with addiction recovery programs. Family Promise specifically shelters families with children. Hubbard House has a 24-hour DV crisis line at (904) 354-3114. Call 211 to be routed through Changing Homelessness Coordinated Entry.

Can a Jacksonville landlord refuse Section 8?

Yes. Florida has no statewide source-of-income protection, and Jacksonville has no local ordinance. Landlords can legally refuse vouchers. Finding landlords who already accept Section 8 is part of the work. JHA maintains a list of participating landlords. Florida Statute 125.0103 also prohibits local rent control, so landlords can raise rent freely at lease end.

How long is the eviction notice in Florida?

Florida Statute §83.56 sets the notice for nonpayment at just 3 days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, one of the shortest in the country. Once you receive a 3-day notice, contact Jacksonville Area Legal Aid at (904) 356-8371 and the Eviction Diversion Program immediately. Self-help eviction (lockouts, utility shutoffs) is illegal under FS §83.67. The landlord can owe you 3 months' rent plus damages.

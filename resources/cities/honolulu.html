Affordable Housing in Honolulu, HI

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Honolulu and the rest of O‘ahu, not generic Section 8 advice. Three things to know up front: the City's Section 8 list is closed and the City says it is not expected to reopen for about three years, and the State's HPHA lists on O‘ahu are closed too; the State's Hawai‘i Relief Program, run on O‘ahu by Catholic Charities Hawai‘i, is taking applications from families with children who fell behind; and since February 5, 2026, a landlord must give 10 calendar days' notice for unpaid rent and send a copy to a free mediation center before filing an eviction. Everything here was checked on November 5, 2026.

Section 8 in Honolulu: DCS and HPHA Status and How to Apply

O‘ahu has two voucher agencies with separate lists. The City and County of Honolulu Department of Community Services (DCS) runs the City's Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher program, which it says has “about 4,100 units” (HUD code HI003). The State's Hawai‘i Public Housing Authority (HPHA) runs public housing across the state and its own voucher program, which “covers the island of Oahu only” (HUD codes HI001 and HI901). Current status (checked November 5, 2026):

  • City (DCS) Section 8 list: closed. DCS took online pre-applications from July 14, 2025 at 7:45 a.m. to July 18, 2025 at 4:30 p.m., then placed 3,000 applicants on the list by random selection. Its own words: “The Waitlist is now closed,” and “At this time, the wait list is not expected to reopen for approximately three years.” If you applied, check your result at waitlistcheck.com; DCS says ACTIVE means you were selected for the list and INACTIVE means you were not. Help: (808) 768-7077
  • HPHA Section 8 on O‘ahu: closed. HPHA's How to Apply page says: “Waiting List(s) are CLOSED. All waiting lists for this program are currently closed. Please check back later.”
  • HPHA federal public housing on O‘ahu: closed, in the same words. HPHA manages 5,406 federal public housing units and 864 State-funded units statewide. Its How to Apply page gives no separate status for State public housing, so call (808) 204-9042
  • HPHA Rent Supplement Program for Kūpuna: interest list open. HPHA's home page says “Interest List is Open!” The program pays up to $500 a month toward rent for elderly households who are homeless or at imminent risk, at or below 50% of area median income. Joining the interest list “does not guarantee rental assistance”
  • What you will pay: DCS says families “generally pay approximately 30% to 40% of their adjusted monthly income toward rent and utilities.” DCS's 2026 income limits start at $53,900 for one person and $77,000 for four
  • Offices: DCS Section 8, 842 Bethel Street, 1st Floor, Honolulu, HI 96813, cchs8@honolulu.gov. HPHA, PO Box 17907, Honolulu, HI 96817, (808) 204-9042

Emergency Rental Assistance in Honolulu (Named Programs)

Start here: our guide to emergency rent help in Honolulu walks through each door step by step: the Hawai‘i Relief Program, 211, free mediation, legal aid, utility help, and Hawai‘i's eviction deadlines.

The pandemic-era O‘ahu rent relief programs are over, and we found no open City and County of Honolulu rent fund on honolulu.gov on November 5, 2026. These are the doors that are open now:

  • Hawai‘i Relief Program (State Department of Human Services, through Catholic Charities Hawai‘i on O‘ahu): Catholic Charities says “Applications are now being accepted.” It covers “non-recurring short-term housing, utility, and automobile payments, not to exceed four (4) months” for families with a dependent child under 18, or a pregnant woman in the third trimester, who have an episode of financial need from a listed cause, such as an involuntary job loss or income cut, an emergency medical bill, a disaster, or losing public assistance. Housing payments can reach $6,000, for up to four months, and the income limit is about 300% of the federal poverty level ($113,850 a year for a family of four). Apply online or call (808) 521-4357, press 1, then 1 again. catholiccharitieshawaii.org (checked November 5, 2026)
  • Aloha United Way 211: dial 2-1-1 or text (877) 275-6569 for the current list of rent, deposit and utility programs on O‘ahu
  • Rent-to-Work (City DCS, WorkHawai‘i Division): a “Short-term rental subsidy for 12-24 months” with job help and case management for people who are homeless or at risk, with household income at or below 60% of area median income and who are “willing to work.” Ask through the program interest form on the DCS WorkHawai‘i page or call (808) 768-7762. DCS posts no open or closed status
  • Legal Aid Society of Hawai‘i: free legal help for low-income tenants. O‘ahu intake: (808) 536-4302, Monday to Friday, 9 to 11:30 a.m. and 1 to 3:30 p.m., or apply online. Its offices are not open for walk-in intake. legalaidhawaii.org
  • Mediation Center of the Pacific: the free, State-funded mediation center for O‘ahu under Act 278. Call (808) 521-6767 or email Landlordtenant@mediatehawaii.org within 10 days of a nonpayment notice

Utility assistance: H-HEAP and Hawaiian Electric

Hawai‘i's LIHEAP is called H-HEAP, and on O‘ahu the Honolulu Community Action Program (HCAP) takes applications. Energy Crisis Intervention is for a household with a final disconnection notice; HCAP says “Applications are processed year-round, although funding is limited,” and Hawaiian Electric's October 16, 2026 assistance list puts the maximum at $700. The once-a-year Energy Credit window ran June 1 to 30, 2026; HCAP says “Applications are closed for 2026,” so the next chance is the June window next year. HCAP: (808) 521-4531, or its Central District Office at (808) 488-6834. Hawaiian Electric offers interest-free payment plans over 4 or 6 months or an extension of up to 15 days, with late fees waived, and auto-enrolls residential customers who get an H-HEAP credit in its Tier Waiver Provision for 12 months. See Hawaiian Electric payment arrangements.

Tenant Rights in Honolulu & Hawai‘i

Hawai‘i's Residential Landlord-Tenant Code (HRS chapter 521) applies statewide. The key rules for O‘ahu renters:

  • 10 calendar days for unpaid rent, plus free mediation: since February 5, 2026, the landlord's notice must give “not less than ten calendar days after receipt” to pay, and a copy must go to a State-funded mediation center (HRS § 521-68, as amended by Act 278 of 2025). If mediation is scheduled within those 10 days and you take part, the landlord cannot file for possession until 20 calendar days after you got the notice. The mediation rules run through February 4, 2028; the 10-day notice stays after that
  • You do not have to move out by the date on the notice. The Hawai‘i Judiciary says “Eviction can only occur after a judge issues an order permitting the landlord to evict the tenant” and that landlords generally cannot change the locks or shut off utilities
  • Security deposits: no more than one month's rent (plus up to one month more for a pet the lease allows). The landlord must return it, or send written reasons for keeping part of it, within 14 days after the rental agreement ends, or lose the right to keep any of it (HRS § 521-44)
  • Rent increases: no rent control, but a month-to-month rent cannot go up without 45 days' written notice, and a late fee cannot exceed 8% of the rent due (HRS § 521-21)
  • Vouchers: since May 1, 2023, Act 310 (2022) makes it a discriminatory practice for a landlord to refuse a tenant because the tenant uses Section 8 or a permanent supportive housing program. Exemptions include owners of four or fewer units in the State and a case where the voucher is not approved within 21 days of a good-faith request for tenancy approval. A court can fine a landlord up to $2,000 for a first violation
  • No right to counsel: Honolulu has no guaranteed free eviction lawyer. Call the Legal Aid Society of Hawai‘i at (808) 536-4302 or Volunteer Legal Services Hawai‘i at (808) 528-7046. For general questions, the State's Landlord-Tenant Information Center is 1-844-808-DCCA (3222), weekday mornings

For state-level details, see our Hawai‘i housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint and our source-of-income protections guide.

Other Housing Programs in Honolulu

  • HPHA public housing: federal and State public housing on O‘ahu; the federal O‘ahu lists are closed. Rent is based on income. Call (808) 204-9042
  • LIHTC (tax credit) apartments: income-restricted, no voucher needed. Search HUD's LIHTC database for Honolulu County. See how to find LIHTC housing
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): a voucher plus VA case management, by referral. See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Coordinated entry: rapid rehousing and supportive housing on O‘ahu run through Partners in Care's Coordinated Entry System. partnersincareoahu.org
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Honolulu Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

No. As of November 5, 2026, the City and County of Honolulu's Department of Community Services says "The Waitlist is now closed" and "At this time, the wait list is not expected to reopen for approximately three years." Its last window ran from July 14 to July 18, 2025, and 3,000 applicants were placed on the list by random selection. If you applied, check your status at waitlistcheck.com or call (808) 768-7077. The State's HPHA says its O‘ahu voucher and federal public housing lists are also closed.

Where can I get emergency rent help in Honolulu?

The State's Hawai‘i Relief Program is the main open door. On O‘ahu, Catholic Charities Hawai‘i runs it and says applications are now being accepted. It is for families with a child under 18, or a woman in her third trimester, who fell behind because of a listed hardship such as losing a job or income. It can pay housing costs up to $6,000 for up to four months, with an income limit of about 300% of the federal poverty level. Call (808) 521-4357, press 1, then 1 again. For other programs, dial 211. We found no open City rent fund on November 5, 2026.

How much notice does a Honolulu landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

Ten calendar days. Since February 5, 2026, Hawai‘i law (HRS 521-68, as changed by Act 278 of 2025) says the notice must give at least ten calendar days after you receive it to pay, and the landlord must send a copy to a free, State-funded mediation center. On O‘ahu, that is the Mediation Center of the Pacific at (808) 521-6767. If mediation is scheduled within the 10 days and you take part, the landlord cannot file until 20 days after you got the notice. The mediation rules run through February 4, 2028.

Can I get a free lawyer for an eviction in Honolulu?

There is no right to counsel in Honolulu, but free legal aid exists. Call the Legal Aid Society of Hawai‘i at (808) 536-4302, Monday to Friday, 9 to 11:30 a.m. and 1 to 3:30 p.m., or apply online; its offices do not take walk-ins. Volunteer Legal Services Hawai‘i is at (808) 528-7046. Mediation under Act 278 is free and does not need a lawyer, but get legal help as soon as you receive a notice.

Can a Honolulu landlord refuse my Section 8 voucher?

Usually not. Since May 1, 2023, Hawai‘i's Act 310 makes it a discriminatory practice for a landlord to refuse a tenant, advertise against, or set different terms because the tenant uses Section 8 or a permanent supportive housing program. There are exemptions, including owners of four or fewer units in the State and cases where the voucher is not approved within 21 days of a good-faith request. A court can fine a landlord up to $2,000 for a first violation. Legal Aid can help you decide what to do.

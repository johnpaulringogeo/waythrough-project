Affordable Housing in St. Louis, MO

Waythrough Project · Updated October 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in the City of St. Louis and St. Louis County — not generic Section 8 advice. Two things to know: St. Louis City has a Right to Counsel program (HELP-STL) that provides free legal representation for tenants facing eviction, and SLHA's Elderly-Only waitlist stays open year-round even when other lists are closed. The City of St. Louis and St. Louis County have separate housing authorities. The named resources below are where to start.

Quick numbers to write down:
  • United Way 2-1-1 — dial 211 or 1-800-427-4626 for rent help and any housing emergency
  • St. Louis Housing Authority (SLHA, city): (314) 531-4770 · 3520 Page Blvd · slha.org
  • HELP-STL (Right to Counsel for eviction): (314) 657-1671 or (314) 657-1654 · HELP-STL page (stlouis-mo.gov)
  • ALIVE (DV 24-hr): (314) 993-2777

Emergency Help Tonight in St. Louis

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or are facing an imminent eviction, these are the local resources to contact first:

  • Peter & Paul Community Services — emergency shelter for men in South City, plus permanent supportive housing programs. ppcsinc.org
  • St. Patrick Center — Catholic Charities-affiliated provider of supportive housing, emergency assistance, and case management for adults experiencing homelessness
  • Gateway 180 / Gateway Homeless Services — emergency shelter for families with children in St. Louis
  • Sts. Joachim & Ann Care Service — emergency shelter and stabilization for families, plus rental assistance referrals
  • Salvation Army Midland Division — St. Louis — emergency shelter (Family Haven) and rental assistance at multiple corps
  • St. Louis Crisis Nursery — emergency short-term care for children of families in crisis
  • Humanitri — emergency and transitional housing for the working poor
  • ALIVE (Alternatives to Living in Violent Environments) — domestic violence shelter and 24-hour crisis line: (314) 993-2777. Bilingual advocates
  • St. Louis Housing Help Line — (314) 802-5444, listed by the City's Homeless Services Division (Gateway 180 lists the same number as its Housing Resource Hotline)
  • United Way 2-1-1 — dial 211 or 1-800-427-4626; run by United Way of Greater St. Louis for its service area and all of Missouri. Connects callers to shelters, rent and utility help, food, and social services

Section 8 in St. Louis: SLHA Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in St. Louis are administered by the St. Louis Housing Authority (SLHA) for the City of St. Louis and by separate authorities for the County and surrounding municipalities. Current status (checked October 28, 2026):

  • The SLHA Public Housing and Housing Choice Voucher waitlists are closed. SLHA says they "are currently closed" and that open waitlists will be announced by phone, on its website, and in local newspapers. Watch slha.org/find-a-home/apply-for-housing
  • The SLHA Elderly-Only waitlist remains OPEN year-round — for households where the head of household, co-head, or spouse is age 62 or older. If you qualify, apply at slha.org right now
  • Housing Authority of St. Louis County (County Housing) — separate authority covering municipalities outside the City of St. Louis. It says "we do not have an exact date when our waitlist will reopen" (checked October 28, 2026) and will post a reopening on its website 30 days ahead. Check status at countyhousing.org/wait-list-info
  • Project-Based Voucher (PBV) lists at specific properties may be open
  • Other special programs: HUD-VASH for veterans, Mainstream vouchers for non-elderly people with disabilities — separate referral processes
  • Apply to neighboring authorities too: Housing Authority of the City of St. Charles, Jefferson County, and the Missouri Housing Development Commission (MHDC, statewide) run separate lists
  • Status check: call SLHA at (314) 531-4770 if you've already applied and need to verify your position on the list

For the national application process, see our step-by-step Section 8 guide and how to find your PHA.

There is no open City or County rent fund right now (checked October 28, 2026). Rent help comes from nonprofits with limited money, so always call to confirm:

  • United Way 2-1-1 — dial 211 or 1-800-427-4626 (or text 1-800-427-4626, weekdays 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.) and ask for rent help in the City or the County. helpingpeople.org
  • HELP-STL (Housing Eviction Law Program / Right to Counsel) — the City of St. Louis's Right to Counsel for eviction. Connects City renters facing eviction with free legal representation from Legal Services of Eastern Missouri. Call the Department of Human Services at (314) 657-1671 or (314) 657-1654. The City's request-help page asks you to call (checked October 28, 2026)
  • Legal Services of Eastern Missouri (LSEM) — free civil legal help for low-income people in 21 eastern Missouri counties, including St. Louis City and St. Louis County. Intake: (314) 534-4200 or 1-800-444-0514, by phone Monday, Wednesday and Thursday, 9 a.m. to 1 p.m., or online. lsem.org
  • Guardian Angel Settlement Association — listed on the City's Housing Help page; its Emergency Assistance Program helps people "at risk for eviction, foreclosure/or utility shut-off." (314) 773-9027. gasastl.org
  • Society of St. Vincent de Paul, St. Louis — rent and utility help through local parish conferences; find yours with its Conference Finder Map or dial 211. svdpstlouis.org
  • St. Patrick Center SSVF (veterans) — for veteran households that are homeless or at risk of homelessness: 314-356-0657. St. Patrick Center says it does not currently have rental assistance for other households

Tenant Rights in St. Louis & Missouri

Missouri has a landlord-friendly framework with relatively few statutory tenant protections, but the City of St. Louis has built up local programs to compensate:

  • HELP-STL (Housing Eviction Law Program) — City of St. Louis Right to Counsel: the City funds free legal representation for tenants facing eviction or equivalent proceedings. Eligible City residents get an attorney through Legal Services of Eastern Missouri and partners. Call (314) 657-1671 or (314) 657-1654; the City's request page asks you to call and has no online application. This is one of the strongest tenant defenses available in Missouri
  • No statewide source-of-income protection: Missouri does not have a statewide law preventing landlords from refusing Section 8 vouchers. The City of St. Louis has had source-of-income language in its civil rights ordinance — coverage and enforcement vary; the Missouri state legislature has at times restricted local rules. Verify with the St. Louis Civil Rights Enforcement Agency before relying on this
  • Rent-and-possession process: Missouri's eviction process is fast. For unpaid rent, the landlord only has to demand the rent; no written notice period is required before filing (RSMo 535.020). The court date can be no more than 21 business days after the summons is issued, and you must be served at least 4 days before it (RSMo 535.030). Paying all the rent and court costs on the date of the money judgment stops the case (RSMo 535.160), and an order to put you out cannot issue until 10 days after a judgment (RSMo 534.350). City renters should contact HELP-STL immediately if they're served
  • Notice to end month-to-month: one month from either party
  • Security deposit cap: Missouri caps the deposit at 2 months' rent (RSMo §535.300). Must be returned within 30 days of move-out with itemized deductions
  • Warranty of habitability: recognized by Missouri case law; the City of St. Louis enforces habitability through Building Division code inspections
  • Limited statutory retaliation protections: Missouri's explicit anti-retaliation protections are narrower than many states. Federal Fair Housing Act protections still apply if the retaliation is for protected reporting
  • Self-help eviction is illegal: a landlord who removes or locks out a tenant without a court order, or cuts off electric, gas, water, or sewer service to force a tenant out, is guilty of forcible entry and detainer (RSMo 441.233) — the landlord must use court
  • Fair housing: discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability is illegal under federal law and the Missouri Human Rights Act. The City of St. Louis Civil Rights Enforcement Agency handles local intakes

For free legal help: Legal Services of Eastern Missouri (LSEM) through HELP-STL is the primary RtC provider for City of St. Louis residents. Mid-Missouri Legal Services and Legal Aid of Western Missouri serve other parts of the state. For state-level details, see our Missouri housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Other Housing Programs in St. Louis

  • Public housing: SLHA owns and manages public-housing communities across the City of St. Louis. Application is separate from Section 8 — site-specific waitlists may have different statuses
  • LIHTC (Tax Credit): St. Louis has substantial LIHTC inventory, with strong development by Mission St. Louis, Beyond Housing, and other CDCs. Search HUD's LIHTC database for properties in St. Louis City and County. See how to find LIHTC housing
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): combines a voucher with VA case management. St. Louis-area veterans are referred through the VA St. Louis Health Care System. See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Rapid Rehousing & Permanent Supportive Housing — coordinated through the St. Louis CoC. Access via 211 or Coordinated Entry
  • Missouri Housing Development Commission (MHDC) programs — homebuyer assistance, MO Mortgage Credit Certificate, and statewide rental development. mhdc.com
  • Affordable Housing Trust Fund (City of St. Louis) — local funding for affordable housing development; income-restricted apartments come online each year
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator — Beyond Housing and Justine Petersen cover the St. Louis area

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the St. Louis Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

Partially. The St. Louis Housing Authority (SLHA) says its Public Housing and Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) waiting lists "are currently closed." BUT the SLHA Elderly-Only waiting list "remains open throughout the year" — for households where the head, co-head, or spouse is 62 or older. The separate Housing Authority of St. Louis County says "we do not have an exact date when our waitlist will reopen" (both checked October 28, 2026). Apply at slha.org if you qualify for Elderly-Only, or call (314) 531-4770 to verify position.

What is HELP-STL?

HELP-STL is the City of St. Louis's Housing Eviction Law Program. The City says it is "also called HELP STL City or Right to Counsel" and that it "was created to provide access to legal representation for tenants facing eviction or equivalent proceedings." The City works with Legal Services of Eastern Missouri to offer the free legal services, and "To be eligible for the program, you need to be a renter living in the City of St. Louis." To request help, the City's page says: "Call the Department of Human Services at (314) 657-1671 or (314) 657-1654." There is no online application; the request page is stlouis-mo.gov/government/departments/human-services/help-stl/request-help-stl.cfm. (Checked October 28, 2026.) This is one of the strongest tenant defenses available in Missouri — act immediately if you're served.

Where can I get emergency rental assistance in St. Louis?

Not from a City or County fund right now. The City of St. Louis's last Emergency Rental Assistance round (ERA 3) closed to applications on December 16, 2024, and we found no open City or St. Louis County rent program on October 28, 2026. Rent help now comes from nonprofits with limited money: dial United Way 2-1-1 (211 or 1-800-427-4626), call the St. Louis Housing Help Line at (314) 802-5444, and try Guardian Angel Settlement Association at (314) 773-9027. Veterans can call St. Patrick Center's SSVF program at 314-356-0657. If you have court papers and live in the City, call HELP-STL at (314) 657-1671 the same day.

Can a St. Louis landlord refuse Section 8?

It depends on the specific property and current local enforcement. Missouri has no statewide source-of-income protection. The City of St. Louis has source-of-income language in its civil rights ordinance, but coverage and enforcement vary and the Missouri legislature has at times restricted local tenant rules. Verify with the St. Louis Civil Rights Enforcement Agency. Finding landlords who already accept vouchers is part of the work — SLHA can provide a list of participating landlords.

How fast can a landlord evict me in Missouri?

Fast. For unpaid rent, the landlord only has to demand the rent; Missouri requires no written notice period before filing a rent-and-possession case (RSMo 535.020). The court date can be no more than 21 business days after the summons is issued, and you must be served at least 4 days before it (RSMo 535.030). If the judge rules for the landlord, the order to put you out cannot issue until 10 days after the judgment (RSMo 534.350). If you live in the City of St. Louis, contact HELP-STL at (314) 657-1671 immediately — it provides free legal representation. Lockouts and utility shutoffs to force you out are illegal — the landlord must use court.

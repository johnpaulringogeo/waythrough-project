Affordable Housing in New Orleans, LA

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in New Orleans (Orleans Parish), not generic Section 8 advice. Three things to know, all checked November 5, 2026: the Housing Authority of New Orleans (HANO) voucher list is closed with no reopening date, and its portal shows no open lists; the City's pandemic rent and utility money is gone; and New Orleans has a Right to Counsel law, so tenants facing eviction can ask for a free lawyer through Southeast Louisiana Legal Services or the Louisiana Fair Housing Action Center. Louisiana evictions move fast: a 5-day notice to vacate, which many leases waive, and a court hearing as soon as the third day after you are served. The named resources below are where to start.

Emergency Help Tonight in New Orleans

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or are facing an imminent eviction, these are the local resources to contact first:

  • VIA LINK 211: free 24/7 line for shelter, food, rent and utility help in Orleans and nine nearby parishes, in over 150 languages. Dial 211 or 504-269-2673, or text your ZIP code to 898211
  • UNITY of Greater New Orleans: coordinates outreach and housing for people who are homeless; the City's Health Department lists 504-570-9812
  • Ozanam Inn and New Orleans Mission: emergency shelter, meals and showers for adults
  • The Salvation Army of Greater New Orleans: emergency shelter and services
  • Covenant House New Orleans: shelter for young people
  • New Orleans Family Justice Center: help and safety planning for survivors of domestic violence. You can also call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233

For a full walkthrough of finding shelter the first night, see our emergency housing tonight guide.

Section 8 in New Orleans: HANO Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers and public housing in New Orleans are run by the Housing Authority of New Orleans (HANO). Current status (checked November 5, 2026):

  • Section 8 is closed with no reopening date. HANO's voucher page says "The HCVP waiting list is closed" and "no future reopen date has been determined. Public notice will be given when the list reopens." HANO last took applications in November 2022 and says about 20,000 families are still waiting
  • No public housing or project-based list is open: HANO's applicant portal at hano.myhousing.com says "There are currently no waiting lists open." Each public housing community keeps its own list, and HANO announces openings by public notice, newspaper ads and its website
  • Neighboring parishes: Kenner's voucher and project-based lists are closed, Slidell's list is "closed until further notice," and in Jefferson Parish the housing authority says it "is no longer handling Section 8/HCVP vouchers" and sends questions to the parish at 504.342.4475
  • Already on HANO's list? Keep your My Housing account up to date; HANO says if you do not, you may lose your place. For public housing, call the community where you applied

Under HUD's programs your share of the rent is usually about 30% of your adjusted monthly income, and HANO's minimum rent is $50, with hardship exemptions. Utility assistance: LIHEAP, Entergy and the Sewerage & Water Board

In Orleans Parish, LIHEAP is run by Total Community Action: pick up an application packet from the box outside 1410 S. Norman C. Francis Parkway and return it inside; TCA says it "is not accepting any applications via e-mail or over the phone." Questions: 504-875-2660. The Louisiana Housing Corporation lists the seasons as "Heating: December 15 – March 31" and "Crisis: October 1 - September 30." Entergy New Orleans offers a deferred payment arrangement for customers "experiencing financial hardship" and payment extensions through myEntergy; its Power to Care fund for customers 60 and older or with disabilities is handled by the New Orleans Council on Aging at (504) 821-4121. Tenant Rights in New Orleans & Louisiana

Louisiana eviction law is fast, but New Orleans renters have one big protection most of the state does not: a right to a lawyer.

  • Right to Counsel (2022): the City Code's Tenant Eviction Assistance Program and Right to Counsel (Chapter 50, Article V, Ord. No. 29019 M.C.S., May 5, 2022) provides "legal counsel as a matter of right to residential tenants facing eviction proceedings within the City of New Orleans." It covers proceedings to evict or end "the tenancy or housing subsidy of a residential tenant living in New Orleans," and says a case counts as starting "when a tenant receives notice of eviction, termination of tenancy, or lease non-renewal." The Code text sets no income limit, but "Participation may be subject to the availability of funding." The court's site lists Southeast Louisiana Legal Services at (504) 529-1000 ext. 223 and the Louisiana Fair Housing Action Center at (504) 596-2100 or (877) 445-2100
  • 5-day notice to vacate (Code of Civil Procedure art. 4701), but a lease can waive it in writing, letting the landlord file right away. Read your lease
  • Fast hearing: the court sets the hearing "not earlier than the third day after service" (art. 4732). If you do not show up, the court rules for the landlord
  • 24 hours after judgment: if you do not leave within 24 hours, the court issues a warrant to the constable or marshal (art. 4733). An appeal stops the eviction only if you answered under oath with a defense and file the appeal and bond within 24 hours (art. 4735)
  • Which court: First City Court, 421 Loyola Ave., handles east bank evictions; Second City Court, 225 Morgan St., handles Algiers (ZIP codes 70114 and 70131)
  • Security deposits: no cap; returned within one month after you move out and give a forwarding address, with an itemized list of any deductions
  • Repairs: the landlord must keep the home in good repair (Civil Code arts. 2682 and 2691), and in some cases you may make a needed repair and deduct the cost (art. 2694)
  • No source-of-income protection: New Orleans' fair housing code (Chapter 86) does not list source of income, and neither does state law, so a landlord can turn down a voucher. Refusals based on race, disability, family status or another protected trait are still illegal
  • Self-help eviction is illegal: only the court's constable or marshal can put you out, under a court warrant

For state-level details, see our Louisiana housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the New Orleans Section 8 waiting list open in 2026?

No. As of November 5, 2026, the Housing Authority of New Orleans says "The HCVP waiting list is closed" and that "no future reopen date has been determined." HANO last took applications in November 2022. Its applicant portal, hano.myhousing.com, says "There are currently no waiting lists open," which includes public housing and project-based lists. HANO says it will give public notice and advertise in major media when a list reopens.

Does New Orleans have a right to counsel for tenants?

Yes. In 2022 the City Council passed a law, now in the City Code (Chapter 50, Article V), that provides legal counsel as a matter of right to residential tenants facing eviction in New Orleans, including cases that end a housing subsidy. Coverage starts when you get a notice of eviction, termination or non-renewal. The Code sets no income limit, but help depends on funding. Call Southeast Louisiana Legal Services at (504) 529-1000 or apply online at slls.org, or call the Louisiana Fair Housing Action Center at (504) 596-2100 or (877) 445-2100, or see lafairhousing.org.

Where can I get emergency rental assistance in New Orleans?

Not from the City right now. The Office of Community Development says its rent and utility funding "has now been depleted, and the program has ended," and Louisiana's pandemic rent program closed to applications on December 21, 2022. Dial VIA LINK 211 (or 504-269-2673) for any rent help that has money this month, and if you have an eviction notice, ask for a free Right to Counsel lawyer the same day. (Checked November 5, 2026.)

How fast is an eviction in Louisiana?

Very fast. The landlord must give a written notice to vacate of at least 5 days, but a lease can waive that notice. Then the court can hold the hearing as soon as the third day after you are served. If the landlord wins and you have not left within 24 hours, the court issues a warrant to the constable or marshal. To stop the eviction with an appeal, you must have answered under oath with a defense and file the appeal and bond within 24 hours of the judgment.

Can a New Orleans landlord refuse Section 8?

Yes. Neither Louisiana law nor the New Orleans fair housing code lists source of income as a protected class, so a landlord can legally turn down a voucher. Refusals based on race, disability, family status, or another trait protected by federal, state or City law are still illegal. HANO's landlord pages list how landlords can join the program, and VIA LINK 211 can help with housing searches.

Affordable Housing in Newark, NJ

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Newark and Essex County, not generic Section 8 advice. Three things to know: the Newark Housing Authority (NHA) says its Section 8 voucher list is closed, but most of its public housing lists are open online; Newark has its own free lawyers for tenants facing eviction, the Office of Tenant Legal Services, for households at or below 200% of the poverty level; and in New Jersey a landlord does not have to give notice before filing for unpaid rent, but you can end the case by paying everything owed by 4:30 p.m. on the trial date. Everything below was checked on November 12, 2026.

Emergency Help Tonight in Newark

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or could lose your home in the next few days, start with these:

  • New Jersey's homeless hotline: dial 2-1-1 or 877-652-1148. NJ 211 says it offers services 24/7, and Code Blue warming centers open when "temperatures drop below freezing and conditions pose a threat to unsheltered individuals." You can also text 898211. nj211.org: Code Blue
  • Essex County Division of Family Assistance and Benefits (formerly Welfare): call center (973) 395-8000, Monday to Friday 7:30 am to 4:00 pm; offices at 18 Rector Street and 465 Martin Luther King Jr. Blvd. in Newark. Ask about Emergency Assistance (see below). essexcountynj.org
  • Domestic violence: New Jersey's 24-hour statewide hotline, 1-800-572-SAFE (7233). In Essex County, the Family Justice Center is at 973-230-7229, Monday to Friday 8:30 am to 4:30 pm, and Access Family Services runs a 24-hour hotline at 862-444-3126.
  • Seeing someone outside who needs help? Newark's Office of Homeless Services says to "Text PATH HOME to 855-11" for an unhoused person who needs non-emergency help.
  • 911 if you are in danger right now.

Inside the City of Newark, public housing and Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers are run by the Newark Housing Authority (NHA), 500 Broad Street. New Jersey's Department of Community Affairs (DCA) also runs a statewide voucher program that Newark residents can apply to. Current status (checked November 12, 2026):

  • NHA voucher (Section 8) list: closed. NHA's Housing Choice Voucher page says: "The Housing Choice Voucher Program waiting list is currently closed." and "No new applications are being accepted at this time." No reopening date is posted. (A waitlist table on NHA's public housing page shows "Yes" next to the voucher program, which does not match. Go by the voucher page, and call HCV customer service at 973-273-6206 if you want it confirmed.)
  • NHA public housing: most lists are open. NHA says: "Applications are currently being accepted for all open low-income public housing waiting lists." Its table shows "Yes" for Pennington Court, John W. Hyatt Court, the Stephen Crane, Kretchmer, Seth Boyden and James Baxter senior buildings, Kretchmer Townhomes, and the scattered sites (Janice Cromer, Kemso, Claremont, Betty Shabazz, Clinton Ave Townhomes, Park Place and Redevelopment). Bradley Court shows "No." Four RAD sites (Stephen Crane Village, James C. White Manor, Riverside Villa and Wynona Lipman Gardens) show the date 2/10/2025 instead of yes or no, so ask about those.

  • Already applied? Check your status and update your contact information online, at the computer terminals in NHA's lobby at 500 Broad Street (8:30 am to 4:30 pm, Monday to Friday), or by calling (973) 273-6127 (9 am to noon Monday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday; 1 pm to 4 pm Tuesday).
  • NHA's office: 500 Broad Street, Newark, NJ 07102. Customer Service Center 973-273-6000; TTY (800) 601-1478; HCV customer service 973-273-6206 or hcvcustomerservice@newarkha.org.
  • New Jersey DCA vouchers: closed. DCA says its statewide program "is available to residents of all New Jersey counties," but its waitlist page says "THE WAITLIST IS NOW CLOSED." The last window ran January 17 to February 3, 2023. DCA's state-funded SRAP list is closed too: "The SRAP waiting list opening for pre-applications has closed" (that window ran January 13 to 31, 2025). Watch nj.gov/dca for the next opening, or call 609-292-4080.

Its Eviction Guide lists, for Essex: Essex-Newark Legal Services, (973) 624-4500; United Community Corporation, (973) 642-0181; and Volunteer Lawyers for Justice, (973) 943-4754.   • Essex County Emergency Assistance: the County says "WFNJ clients may receive Emergency Assistance (EA) in certain situations," including "temporary rental assistance or back rent or mortgage payments" and utility payments. If you get Work First New Jersey cash assistance, or think you qualify, call (973) 395-8000 or go to 18 Rector Street.
  • NJ 211: dial 2-1-1 or text 898211 and ask for rent and utility help in Newark. nj211.org
  • DCA's Office of Eviction Prevention answers general landlord-tenant questions at 609-376-0810 or evictionprevention@dca.nj.gov. You can check which DCA programs you may qualify for at dcaid.dca.nj.gov.
  • Utility help: LIHEAP and the Universal Service Fund (USF) take one application, and the BPU says "The LIHEAP application period runs from October 1, 2026, through June 30, 2027." New Jersey's Winter Termination Program protects eligible customers from shutoff November 15 through March 15. Details are in our Newark rent help guide

Tenant Rights in Newark & New Jersey

New Jersey's tenant protections are some of the strongest in the country. Here is what matters most in Newark:

  • Good cause only: under the Anti-Eviction Act (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.1), most landlords can only evict for one of the reasons the law lists, and cannot simply refuse to renew your lease
  • No notice needed for unpaid rent: DCA's Truth in Renting guide says a notice to quit "is required for all good cause evictions, except for an eviction for nonpayment of rent" (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.2), so the first paper you see may be a court summons. Do not ignore it
  • Court: Essex County Landlord/Tenant, Special Civil Part, Historic Courthouse, 470 Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Blvd., Newark, 973-776-9300 ext. 56897. The trial date "will be at least 21 days from date of service of the summons," and you must show up: "Written responses are not acceptable."
  • Paying to end the case: the courts say a nonpayment case "may be dismissed if the tenant pays to the court by 4:30 p.m. on the scheduled trial date" the rent owed plus court costs and other charges the lease allows
  • After a judgment: a warrant of removal cannot be issued until three business days after the judgment, and "Residential tenants have three business days to leave the property after they are served with a warrant of removal." Even after a lockout, the courts say the "Total amount shown on the Judgment of Possession must be paid any time up to three business days after an eviction"
  • Lockouts are illegal: only a court officer with a warrant of removal can put you out. A landlord who changes the locks or shuts off utilities can owe you damages (N.J.S.A. 2A:39-1). Call the police and a lawyer
  • Newark rent control: Newark's ordinance says "In no case shall the allowable rent increase exceed 4%," and no increase is allowed if the unit is not in substantial compliance or the landlord has not registered. Newark's Office of Rent Control, 920 Mayor Kenneth A. Gibson Blvd., Room 111, 973-733-3675, hears complaints, and you do not need a lawyer to file
  • Security deposits: no more than one and one-half months' rent (N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.2), returned with interest and an itemized list within 30 days; a landlord who wrongly keeps it can owe double (N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.1)
  • Grace period: seniors on Social Security and people on SSDI, SSI or Work First New Jersey get a five business day grace period with no late charge when rent is due on the first (N.J.S.A. 2A:42-6.1)
  • Vouchers: the New Jersey Law Against Discrimination bars refusing you because of your source of lawful income or rent payment, including a Section 8 voucher (N.J.S.A. 10:5-12). Report it to the NJ Division on Civil Rights, 1-833-653-2748
  • Right to counsel: Newark's Office of Tenant Legal Services gives free legal help to Newark tenant households "facing the threat of eviction" with gross income at or below 200% of the federal poverty level (with an exception for applicants 60 or older and infirm). 920 Mayor Kenneth A. Gibson Blvd., Room 110, 973-877-9424

For state-level details, see our New Jersey housing resources. If you face discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

  • NHA public housing: family sites, senior and disabled buildings, townhomes and scattered sites. Most lists are open (see above)

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Newark Section 8 waitlist open right now?

No. On November 12, 2026, the Newark Housing Authority said its Housing Choice Voucher waiting list is closed and that no new applications are being accepted. New Jersey DCA's statewide voucher list is closed too. Most NHA public housing lists are open, though, and you apply online through NHA's portal. See newarkha.org or call 973-273-6000.

How do I apply for NHA public housing?

NHA says applications must be submitted online through its Rent Café portal, unless you have an approved reasonable accommodation. The pre-application stays active for up to 18 months. To check your status or update your contact information, use the portal, the computer terminals in the lobby at 500 Broad Street, or call (973) 273-6127.

Is there emergency rent help in Newark?

Some. The State funds eviction-prevention partners in Essex County, including Essex-Newark Legal Services at (973) 624-4500, United Community Corporation at (973) 642-0181 and Volunteer Lawyers for Justice at (973) 943-4754. If you get Work First New Jersey, ask Essex County about Emergency Assistance at (973) 395-8000. Dial 211 for other help. The pandemic CVERAP is closed.

How much notice does a Newark landlord have to give?

For unpaid rent, none before filing: New Jersey does not require a notice to quit for nonpayment, so the first paper may be a court summons. The trial will be at least 21 days after you are served. After a judgment, the landlord must wait three business days for a warrant of removal, and you then have three business days to leave. You can still pay everything owed by 4:30 p.m. on the trial date to end the case.

Can I get a free lawyer for an eviction in Newark?

Yes, if you qualify. Newark's Office of Tenant Legal Services gives free legal help to Newark tenant households facing eviction with income at or below 200% of the federal poverty level; call 973-877-9424. Essex-Newark Legal Services at (973) 624-4500 and the LSNJLAW hotline at 1-888-576-5529 also help low-income tenants.

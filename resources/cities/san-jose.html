Affordable Housing & Renter Help in San José, CA

Waythrough Project · Updated October 2026

San José has its own renter rules on top of California law, and Santa Clara County runs Section 8 differently from most places. Checked on the official sites on October 15, 2026:

  • Section 8: the Santa Clara County Housing Authority (SCCHA) says its "online interest lists are open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week." Joining is the first step; vouchers go to people drawn at random.
  • Rent increases: in apartments covered by San José's Apartment Rent Ordinance, the limit is one 5% increase every 12 months.
  • Evictions: under the Tenant Protection Ordinance, landlords of most apartment buildings may end a tenancy only for one of 13 just causes. The City's Eviction Prevention Program answers questions at 408-975-4444.
  • Behind on rent: the City sends tenants to the Homelessness Prevention System at 408-516-5100.

Section 8 in San José: SCCHA's Interest Lists

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in San José are run by the Santa Clara County Housing Authority (SCCHA). SCCHA reports more than 19,179 households with a voucher and more than 37,000 people on its interest lists. How it works, in SCCHA's words (checked October 15, 2026, on SCCHA's applicant portal page):

  • Sign up any time. "Our new online interest lists are open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week." Signing up takes about three minutes online, and there are two interest lists you may join.
  • Stay active. "Logging in at least once a year tells us you are still interested in receiving a Housing Choice Voucher." If you never log in, you can drop off.
  • Random selection. "If rental assistance is available, applicants will be chosen randomly and added to the waiting list. Not everyone is guaranteed housing." SCCHA then contacts the people selected to check eligibility.
  • Other affordable options. SCCHA's new applicants page also lists its other rental assistance programs and affordable housing outside SCCHA.

Call SCCHA at (408) 275-8770 (TDD (408) 993-3041). Because the interest list is a lottery pool, also apply to income-restricted buildings directly (see below). Our waiting-list strategies guide explains how to stack applications.

San José Renter Protections: Rent Cap, Just Cause and Voucher Rules

The City's Rent Stabilization Program runs these ordinances and helps tenants with eviction questions, rent increase limits and disputes. Call 408-975-4480. Checked on sanjoseca.gov on October 15, 2026:

  • Apartment Rent Ordinance (ARO): covers apartments in buildings of three or more units that were built and occupied before September 7, 1979, about 38,000 apartments. "The maximum allowable rent increase is one 5% increase in a 12-month period." Increases after a voluntary move-out or a lawful eviction are exempt, and a landlord can petition to pass through some capital-improvement costs (capped at 3% of monthly rent) or for a fair-return increase. Single-family homes, duplexes, condos, townhomes and ADUs are not covered. To check a building, call 408-975-4470 (details).
  • Petitions: ARO tenants can file a petition over an invalid rent increase, health and safety (code) problems, or a cut in services. A hearing officer can reduce or disallow an increase or order a rent credit.
  • Tenant Protection Ordinance (TPO): covers most apartment buildings in San José. Landlords "may only terminate tenancies for 13 just causes" and must follow set procedures for eviction notices.
  • Housing Payment Equality Ordinance: bars discrimination against applicants who pay with a housing voucher or other rental assistance, so they must be considered like any other applicant.
  • Free Housing Mediation Program: voluntary, no-cost mediation for landlords and tenants, including nonpayment of rent, lease issues, nuisance and roommate disputes.
  • No retaliation: under the ARO, a landlord may not threaten eviction, harass you, cut services, raise rent or add new charges because you used your rights. Report it in writing to the Rent Stabilization Program.

California law also applies: AB 1482 limits increases on most other units at least 15 years old to 5% plus local inflation, or 10%, whichever is lower; SB 329 makes refusing a voucher illegal statewide; and lockouts or utility shut-offs are illegal (Civil Code § 789.3). See our California page for the statewide rules.

Behind on Rent or Facing Eviction in San José

  • Eviction Prevention Program (City of San José): staff are at San José City Hall, 200 E. Santa Clara Street, 12th floor, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. The City's advice: "Do not move out of your apartment without learning your rights, protections and options." 408-975-4444 · evictionhelp@sanjoseca.gov · sanjoseca.gov
  • Can't pay rent: the City says to contact the Homelessness Prevention System, which offers temporary financial assistance, legal support and case management: 408-516-5100 · preventhomelessness.org
  • Sacred Heart Community Service rental assistance for San José residents: (408) 709-2364 · sacredheartcs.org. Sacred Heart notes that ZIP codes 95050, 95051 and 95054 are now served by the Salvation Army in San José, 408-282-1165
  • If you are served with an eviction lawsuit (unlawful detainer): the City warns that "Tenants must file responses in court within ten business days." Free help filing a response: the Santa Clara County Superior Court Self Help Center, 201 N. First St., San José (Monday to Thursday 8:30 a.m. to 1:30 p.m., Friday 8:30 a.m. to noon), or by phone at (408) 882-2926
  • Free and low-cost legal help: Law Foundation of Silicon Valley (408) 280-2424; Bay Area Legal Aid (800) 551-5554; Santa Clara County Bar Association Lawyer Referral Service (669) 302-7803

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get on a Section 8 list in San José right now?

Yes, by joining SCCHA's interest lists. The Santa Clara County Housing Authority says its "online interest lists are open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week" (checked October 15, 2026). When rental assistance is available, applicants are chosen at random and added to the waiting list, and not everyone is guaranteed housing. Log in at least once a year to stay active. Call SCCHA at (408) 275-8770.

How much can my landlord raise the rent in San José?

If your apartment is covered by San José's Apartment Rent Ordinance (buildings of three or more units built and occupied before September 7, 1979), the limit is one 5% increase in a 12-month period, according to the City's Rent Stabilization Program. Most other units at least 15 years old fall under California's AB 1482 cap of 5% plus local inflation, or 10%, whichever is lower. Call 408-975-4470 to check whether your building is covered.

Can a San José landlord evict me without a reason?

Not in most apartment buildings. San José's Tenant Protection Ordinance lets landlords end a tenancy only for one of 13 just causes and requires set procedures for notices. If you get a notice, contact the City's Eviction Prevention Program at 408-975-4444 or evictionhelp@sanjoseca.gov before moving out. If you are sued, the City says your court response is due within ten business days.

Where can I get help paying rent in San José?

The City of San José tells tenants who cannot pay rent to contact the Homelessness Prevention System at 408-516-5100, which offers temporary financial assistance, legal support and case management. Sacred Heart Community Service also runs rental assistance at (408) 709-2364. The City's free Housing Mediation Program can help you and your landlord agree on a payment plan.

Can a San José landlord refuse my Section 8 voucher?

No. California's SB 329 makes refusing a voucher or other rental subsidy illegal statewide, and San José's Housing Payment Equality Ordinance requires landlords to consider voucher holders in an equal manner to other applicants. File a complaint with the California Civil Rights Department, or call the City's Rent Stabilization Program at 408-975-4480.

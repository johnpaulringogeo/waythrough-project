Affordable Housing in Bakersfield, CA

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page lists the programs, agencies, phone numbers and rules that apply in Bakersfield and Kern County. Three things to know first: the Housing Authority of the County of Kern says its Section 8 list is closed, but its project-based voucher lists have been open since May 1, 2026, one apartment complex at a time; the City of Bakersfield pays for a rental assistance program run by The Open Door Network, through June 30, 2027, for City residents; and in California a 3-day notice does not count weekends or court holidays, and you get 10 court days to answer an eviction lawsuit. Everything below was checked on November 25, 2026.

Quick numbers to write down:
  • 2-1-1 Kern County (CAPK): dial 211, 24 hours a day
  • Housing Authority of the County of Kern: (661) 631-8500 · kernha.org
  • Behind on rent? Open Door Network Housing Department: 661-716-1020 · Greater Bakersfield Legal Assistance: (661) 325-5943

Emergency Help Tonight in Bakersfield

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or could lose your home in the next few days, start with these:

  • 2-1-1 Kern County: dial 2-1-1. It is run by the Community Action Partnership of Kern (CAPK), answers 24 hours a day, and is the main way into Kern County's Coordinated Entry System for people who are homeless or about to be. capk.org
  • The Open Door Network: outreach center at 1921 19th Street, (661) 322-9199. For survivors of domestic violence, sexual violence, child abuse or trafficking, its 24-hour crisis hotline is 661-327-1091, with emergency shelter and transitional housing.
  • Men: The Mission at Kern County provides overnight shelter for men 18 and older, with meals, showers and clothing.
  • Families on CalWORKs: Kern County's Department of Human Services can pay for temporary shelter for up to 16 days in a 12-month period while you look for permanent housing. Call your Human Services Technician or visit any DHS office.
  • 911 if you are in danger right now.

For a full walkthrough of finding shelter the first night, see our emergency housing tonight guide.

Section 8 in Bakersfield: Housing Authority Status and How to Apply

In Bakersfield and the rest of Kern County, Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers, project-based vouchers and public housing are run by the Housing Authority of the County of Kern, 601 24th Street, Bakersfield, CA 93301. It says it meets the housing needs of nearly 20,000 low-income Kern County residents. Current status (checked November 25, 2026):

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) list: closed. The Housing Authority's voucher page says: "The Section 8 Waiting List is closed and applications are not available for this program." No reopening date is posted. People already on the list are picked at random and contacted by email.
  • Project-Based Voucher (PBV) lists: open. The Housing Authority says "The waiting list for the Project-Based Voucher (PBV) Program is now open as of May 1, 2026, at 10:00 AM (PST)." You apply for each apartment complex separately. The assistance stays with the apartment, so if you move out you may lose it. Sites include 4th Street Senior Apartments, 22nd Street Lofts, Adelante Vista, Park Place Senior Apartments, Residences at Old Town Kern, Residences at West Columbus, South Baker Apartments and Rio Vista in Bakersfield, plus sites in Delano, Arvin and McFarland.
  • Public housing list: closed. The Housing Authority says "The waiting list for the Low-Income Public Housing (LIPH) program is currently closed, and applications are not being accepted at this time." Its old public housing list moved to site-based lists under HUD's RAD program, and applicants kept their original date and time.
  • Units ready now: the Housing Authority also lists move-in-ready affordable apartments, including Pinewood Glen Retirement Community for people 55 and older ((661) 834-4222), Residences at East Hills and Westchester Senior Village (62 and older) in Bakersfield, and farmworker housing that requires farm labor income.
  • How to apply: online at kernhousing.com: choose Applicant Login, register, and pick "I want to Apply." You need Social Security numbers and dates of birth for everyone, a phone number and an email address. Kiosks are at the Housing Authority office, the Child Support office at 3701 N. Sillect Ave., and the court building at 1215 Truxtun, Room 300. For free translation, call 661-631-8500 ext. 4101.
  • Main office: 601 24th Street, Bakersfield, CA 93301. Phone (661) 631-8500. Disability accommodation requests for the online application can be made at the office or by phone during the whole application window.

Emergency Rental Assistance in Bakersfield (Named Programs)

Start here: our guide to emergency rent help in Bakersfield walks through each door: the City's rental assistance program, Kern County's CalWORKs help, GBLA, 211, CAPK utility help and California's eviction deadlines.

These are the doors that are open now:

  • City of Bakersfield Rental Assistance Program (The Open Door Network): the City says that in early 2025 it gave The Open Door Network a two-year agreement for "short-term rental assistance, rental arrears, rental deposits, move in assistance, housing search, and case management to qualifying households residing within the City of Bakersfield," and that "Services are currently available through June 30, 2027." Call 2-1-1 or The Open Door Network Housing Department at 661-716-1020 (checked November 25, 2026). City of Bakersfield Homeless Hub
  • CalWORKs families: Kern County DHS can pay up to two months of back rent to stop an eviction (Permanent Homeless Arrearages), help with move-in costs, and enroll families in the CalWORKs Housing Support Program. Ask your Human Services Technician or visit a DHS office. kcdhs.org
  • Free legal help: Greater Bakersfield Legal Assistance (GBLA), 615 California Ave., (661) 325-5943, Monday through Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m. Its housing team helps tenants before and during an eviction. The Kern County Superior Court's Landlord Tenant Assistance line is (661) 610-6299.
  • Utility help: CAPK's Energy Program pays electric, gas and propane bills once a year for Kern County households at or below 150% of the poverty guidelines. Details are in our Bakersfield rent help guide

Tenant Rights in Bakersfield & California

State law sets the main rules in Bakersfield. Here is what matters most:

  • Notice for unpaid rent: the landlord must give a written 3-day notice that states the amount due and who to pay. The three days do not count Saturdays, Sundays or court holidays (Code of Civil Procedure 1161)
  • 10 court days to answer: once you are served with an eviction lawsuit (unlawful detainer), you have 10 days, not counting weekends and court holidays, to file a response (Code of Civil Procedure 1167). The Kern court's self-help page still says 5 days; the law changed on January 1, 2025. If you miss the deadline, the landlord can win by default
  • Just cause and rent caps: after you have lived in the home for 12 months, a landlord covered by the Tenant Protection Act needs a just cause, stated in the notice, to end your tenancy (Civil Code 1946.2), and yearly rent increases are capped at 5 percent plus inflation, or 10 percent, whichever is lower (Civil Code 1947.12). Some homes, such as many single-family homes, are exempt
  • Lockouts and shutoffs are illegal: a landlord cannot change the locks, remove doors or windows, take your things, or cut utilities to force you out. The landlord can owe up to $100 a day, and at least $250, plus your damages (Civil Code 789.3)
  • Security deposits: most landlords may charge no more than one month's rent; a small landlord who owns no more than two rental properties with four units total may charge two months. The landlord has 21 days after you move out to return the deposit or send an itemized list (Civil Code 1950.5)
  • Vouchers are protected: California makes it illegal for a landlord to discriminate based on source of income, which includes Section 8 vouchers and HUD-VASH vouchers (Government Code 12955). Report a refusal to the California Civil Rights Department

Other Housing Programs in Bakersfield

  • Housing for people who are homeless: the Housing Authority runs sites such as 6th Street Apartments and 889 Oak Street with on-site case management. It says "To access these services and housing resources, call 2-1-1"
  • Farmworker housing: the Housing Authority manages year-round farmworker housing and Migrant Farm Labor Centers that open about six months a year for seasonal workers whose permanent home is at least 50 miles away
  • LIHTC (Tax Credit): search HUD's LIHTC database for Kern County properties. See how to find LIHTC housing
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): see how to apply for HUD-VASH. The California Veterans Assistance Foundation, 3509 Union Avenue, (661) 323-5637, offers transitional and permanent housing for veterans and accepts walk-ins

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Bakersfield Section 8 waitlist open right now?

No. On November 25, 2026, the Housing Authority of the County of Kern said its Section 8 waiting list is closed and applications are not available. Its project-based voucher lists have been open since May 1, 2026; you apply for each apartment complex separately at kernhousing.com. The public housing list is also closed. Call (661) 631-8500 with questions.

Is there emergency rent help in Bakersfield?

Yes, for City of Bakersfield residents. The City pays The Open Door Network to provide short-term rental assistance, back rent, deposits and case management, with services available through June 30, 2027. Call The Open Door Network Housing Department at 661-716-1020 or dial 211. CalWORKs families can also ask Kern County Human Services for help with up to two months of back rent.

How much notice does a Bakersfield landlord have to give?

For unpaid rent, three days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays or court holidays. The notice must state the amount due and who to pay. If the landlord then files an eviction lawsuit, you have 10 court days after you are served to file a response. If you have lived there 12 months, a covered landlord also needs a just cause stated in the notice.

Can I get a free lawyer for an eviction in Bakersfield?

You can ask. Greater Bakersfield Legal Assistance gives free civil legal help to low-income Kern County residents, and its housing team helps tenants before and during an eviction. Call (661) 325-5943, Monday through Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., or go to 615 California Ave. The court's Landlord Tenant Assistance line is (661) 610-6299.

Can a Bakersfield landlord refuse my Section 8 voucher?

No, not because it is a voucher. California law bans discrimination based on source of income, and it names Section 8 and HUD-VASH vouchers. A landlord can still check your rental history and whether you can pay your share. If you are turned away because of the voucher, file a complaint with the California Civil Rights Department.

Affordable Housing and Rent Help in New York City

Waythrough Project · Updated October 2026

If you live in New York City and you are behind on rent, facing eviction, or looking for a place you can afford, these are the doors that are actually open, checked on October 28, 2026. Start with the one that fits you today.

What to do today

  • Behind on rent: apply for HRA's One Shot Deal, a one-time Emergency Assistance grant for back rent, on ACCESS HRA or at a Benefit Access Center. You do not need a court case or to be on Cash Assistance. Then call 311 and ask for your local Homebase office. Our NYC emergency rent help guide has the documents, the steps, and the repayment rules.
  • Got court papers or a marshal's notice: you can get a free lawyer in every ZIP code, regardless of immigration status. Call 311 and ask for the Tenant Helpline, or Housing Court Answers at 718-557-1379. Answer a nonpayment petition within 10 days and do not miss your court date. How to get the lawyer.
  • Looking for housing: NYCHA's Section 8 list is closed, but NYCHA public housing takes applications online through ApplyNYCHA, and affordable lotteries are on NYC Housing Connect. See our NYCHA page.
  • No place to sleep tonight: families with children go to PATH, 151 East 151st Street, Bronx, open 24 hours. Single adults and adult families: see the intake list below, or dial 311.
Quick numbers to write down:
  • 311 — Tenant Helpline (free lawyer), your Homebase office, shelter intake, and the Commission on Human Rights
  • HRA InfoLine (One Shot Deal, CityFHEPS): 718-557-1399
  • Housing Court Answers: 718-557-1379 or 212-962-4795, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.
  • NYCHA Customer Contact Center: 718-707-7771 · nyc.gov/nycha

Emergency Help Tonight in New York City

NYC's Department of Homeless Services (DHS) runs shelter intake by household type. NYC311 lists these intake sites, all open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, including holidays (checked October 28, 2026):

  • Families with children, or pregnant women: PATH, 151 East 151st Street, Bronx. (718) 503-6400
  • Single men: Single Adult Men's Intake Center, 8 East 3rd Street (between Bowery and 2nd Avenue), Manhattan. The old intake center at 400-430 East 30th Street was relocated as of August 1, 2026
  • Single women: Franklin Women's Shelter, 1122 Franklin Avenue, Bronx, (929) 281-2330; or HELP Women's Center, 114 Snediker Avenue, Brooklyn, (718) 483-7700
  • Adult families (couples and other families without minor children): Adult Family Intake Center, 333 Bowery, Manhattan
  • Domestic violence: NYC's 24-hour hotline, 1-800-621-4673 (HOPE). If you are in immediate danger, call 911

Rent Help in New York City

  • One Shot Deal (HRA Emergency Assistance) — HRA calls these grants "one-time payments to help people who are experiencing homelessness or at risk of losing their housing." Apply on ACCESS HRA by starting a Cash Assistance application and choosing One Shot Deal. HRA says "NY State law requires most people to pay HRA back at least some of the money," though SSI recipients do not have to repay. Checks go to the landlord or Housing Court. Full steps in our NYC emergency rent help guide
  • Homebase — neighborhood prevention offices offering "Services to prevent eviction," "Emergency rental assistance," and "Short-term financial assistance" for low-income New Yorkers at imminent risk of entering shelter. Find yours on HRA's Homebase page or through 311, and call ahead for an appointment
  • CityFHEPS — the City's rental assistance voucher, which pays part of the rent anywhere in New York State for up to five years. If you still have housing, HRA says your household's gross income must be at or below 200% of the federal poverty level and you must meet one of four criteria (for example, a veteran at risk of homelessness, or facing eviction in court and someone in the household previously lived in a DHS shelter). You apply through Homebase; if you are in shelter, ask your case manager
  • 311 — dial 311 for the Homebase office that serves your ZIP code

Section 8 and Public Housing in NYC: NYCHA and HPD Status

Status checked October 28, 2026:

  • NYCHA Section 8 is closed. NYCHA says its Housing Choice Voucher waitlist "is currently closed to new applicants." On August 1, 2025, NYCHA "paused active outreach, including activities related to the application process" for the general waitlist; applicants already on it keep their place. NYCHA still accepts referrals for HUD-VASH (veterans) and Stability Vouchers through partner agencies. NYCHA also says "funding for the EHV program will end in late 2026"; Emergency Housing Vouchers are not open to new applicants. See our NYCHA contact & waiting-list guide
  • NYCHA public housing is taking applications. NYCHA says it is "home to roughly 1 in 15 New Yorkers across over 177,000 apartments within 335 housing developments." Apply online through ApplyNYCHA (NYCHA's apply page)
  • HPD Section 8 — the Department of Housing Preservation and Development runs a separate Section 8 program for families in certain HPD buildings and homeless households referred by DHS and HRA. HPD says: "Applications are not accepted by the general public."
  • NYC Housing Connect — the City's portal for affordable housing lotteries: housingconnect.nyc.gov

For the national application process, see our step-by-step Section 8 guide.

Eviction and Tenant Rights in New York City

  • Right to Counsel — HRA's Office of Civil Justice says tenants facing eviction in Housing Court or NYCHA administrative proceedings have access to free legal representation or advice, "free and available in every ZIP code, regardless of immigration status." Call 311 and ask for the Tenant Helpline, or say "I would like an attorney" at your first court date. It also covers a lockout without a court order. More on getting a lawyer
  • Housing Court basics — a landlord must make a written rent demand at least 14 days before starting a nonpayment case. If you are served with a nonpayment petition, the courts say you "must answer within 10 days"; the clerk then sets a date 3 to 8 days later. If you get a marshal's notice, go to court as soon as possible to ask a judge to stop or delay the eviction. Court information: 646-386-5750. The steps in detail
  • Good Cause Eviction — HPD says this "is a New York State law that went into effect on April 20, 2024" and covers some tenants in unregulated, market-rate homes. It does not cover a small landlord who owns 10 units or fewer, or homes renting for more than 245% of the Fair Market Rent. Call 311 and ask for the Tenant Helpline to check whether it covers you
  • Rent stabilization — the NYC Rent Guidelines Board set 0% for one-year and 0% for two-year leases commencing October 1, 2026 through September 30, 2027 (Apartment Order #58, adopted June 25, 2026)
  • Voucher discrimination — HRA says landlords and brokers "cannot refuse to rent to you because you are receiving CityFHEPS" and that this is unlawful source-of-income discrimination in New York City. Report it to the NYC Commission on Human Rights at 212-416-0197 or through 311. The separate state-law status for Section 8 is on our New York State page
  • Heat and hot water — HPD says heat season runs October 1 to May 31: at least 68°F from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. when it is below 55°F outside, and at least 62°F from 10 p.m. to 6 a.m. regardless of the outside temperature. Hot water must be at least 120°F all year. Report problems to 311

For statewide rules (notices, deposits, late fees), see our New York housing resources.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do first if I am behind on rent in New York City?

Apply for a One Shot Deal, HRA's one-time Emergency Assistance grant for back rent. Apply online on ACCESS HRA by starting a Cash Assistance application and choosing One Shot Deal, or at a Benefit Access Center. You do not need a court case or to be on Cash Assistance, and most people have to pay some of it back. Then call 311 and ask for your local Homebase office. HRA InfoLine: 718-557-1399. Checked October 28, 2026.

How do I get a free lawyer for Housing Court in NYC?

NYC's Right-to-Counsel program gives tenants facing eviction in Housing Court or NYCHA proceedings free legal help in every ZIP code, regardless of immigration status. Call 311 and ask for the Tenant Helpline, call Housing Court Answers at 718-557-1379 or 212-962-4795, or say "I would like an attorney" at your first court date. If you were served a nonpayment petition, answer within 10 days.

Is the NYCHA Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

No. NYCHA says its Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) waitlist "is currently closed to new applicants," and it paused active outreach for the general waitlist on August 1, 2025 (checked October 28, 2026). NYCHA still accepts referrals for HUD-VASH and Stability Vouchers through partner agencies. HPD's separate Section 8 program does not take applications from the general public. NYCHA public housing does take applications online through ApplyNYCHA.

What is CityFHEPS and who qualifies?

CityFHEPS is NYC's rental assistance voucher. HRA says it pays part of the rent anywhere in New York State for up to five years. If you still have housing, your household's gross income must be at or below 200% of the federal poverty level, and you must meet one of four criteria, such as a veteran at risk of homelessness, or facing eviction in court with someone in the household who previously lived in a DHS shelter. You apply through a Homebase office; people in shelter ask their case manager.

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in NYC?

Families with children go to PATH, 151 East 151st Street in the Bronx. Single men go to the intake center at 8 East 3rd Street in Manhattan, which replaced the East 30th Street site on August 1, 2026. Single women can go to Franklin Women's Shelter, 1122 Franklin Avenue in the Bronx, or HELP Women's Center, 114 Snediker Avenue in Brooklyn. Adult families go to 333 Bowery. NYC311 says these sites are open 24 hours, 7 days a week. Dial 311 for help.

How much can a rent-stabilized rent go up in NYC?

For leases starting October 1, 2026 through September 30, 2027, the NYC Rent Guidelines Board set 0% for one-year leases and 0% for two-year leases (Apartment Order #58, adopted June 25, 2026). If your landlord asks for more on a stabilized renewal, call 311 and ask for the Tenant Helpline.

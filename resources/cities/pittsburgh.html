Affordable Housing in Pittsburgh, PA

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Pittsburgh and Allegheny County, not generic Section 8 advice. Three things to know: the Housing Authority of the City of Pittsburgh (HACP) says its Section 8 voucher list is closed, but several of its public housing and project-based lists are open; the main rent-help program, run by ACTION-Housing, is for renters who already have an eviction case; and in Pennsylvania an eviction for unpaid rent starts with a 10-day notice to quit. Everything below was checked on November 5, 2026.

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or could lose your home in the next few days, start with these. Shelter beds and family shelter openings in Allegheny County are managed through one county line:

  • Allegheny Link — Allegheny County's own words: "Allegheny Link is the place to call for housing assistance in Allegheny County." Call 866-730-2368, Monday through Friday, 8:00 am to 5:00 pm, or go in person Monday through Friday, 8:00 am to 4:00 pm, to the Human Services Building, First Floor, One Smithfield Street, Pittsburgh, PA 15222. The County says the Link manages openings for family shelters. alleghenycounty.us — Allegheny Link
  • PA 211 Southwest — dial 2-1-1 or text your ZIP code to 898-211, 24 hours a day, every day, for shelter, rent, utility and food help. pa211.org
  • Women's Center & Shelter of Greater Pittsburgh — 24/7 domestic violence hotline 412-687-8005; text 412-744-8445 (9 am to 5 pm, Monday through Friday). wcspittsburgh.org
  • 911 if you are in danger right now.

Section 8 in Pittsburgh: HACP Status and How to Apply

Inside the City of Pittsburgh, Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers and public housing are run by the Housing Authority of the City of Pittsburgh (HACP), HUD code PA001. Current status (checked November 5, 2026):

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) list: closed. HACP's waiting-list page says: "The Housing Authority of the City of Pittsburgh Housing Choice Voucher Program Waiting List is now CLOSED." and "We cannot accept applications once the waiting list is closed." No reopening date is posted.
  • Some public housing lists are open. HACP's Apply for Housing page says "WAITING LISTS FOR THE FOLLOWING SITES ARE NOW OPEN" and lists Arlington Heights (2 bedrooms), Caliguiri, Carrick Regency, Finello, Gualtieri, Mazza and Morse Gardens (1 bedroom), Northview Heights (2 to 5 bedrooms), PA Bidwell and Pressley Street high rises (1 to 2 bedrooms) and Scattered Sites (2 to 5 bedrooms). Public housing applications are not online: email them to occupancy@hacp.org, fax 412-456-5182, or mail or drop them off at the One-Stop Shop, 412 Boulevard of the Allies.
  • Some project-based voucher lists are open. HACP says "The following PBV waiting lists are OPEN until further notice": First and Market (1 bedroom, 62+), Larimer Pointe (3 bedrooms), Legacy Apartments (1 bedroom, 62+), Manchester Commons (1 and 4 bedrooms), Sandstone Quarry (3 bedrooms) and The Reed Senior Housing (2 bedrooms, 55+). Apply online with a pre-application at hacp.myhousing.com. Several other PBV lists, and the Christopher Smith Terrace list, closed at 3 p.m. on October 16, 2026, so check before you count on any list.
  • HACP's office: 412 Boulevard of the Allies, Pittsburgh, PA 15219. Main line 412-456-5000; Housing Choice Voucher department 412-456-5090; TTY 412-201-5384. HACP's contact page lists hours of Monday to Friday, 8:00 am to 5:00 pm, but its homepage says the One-Stop Shop takes walk-ins Monday, Wednesday and Friday, with Tuesday and Thursday by appointment, so call first.
  • Already on a list? HACP says it "will no longer check your applicant status over the phone." Check online at the HACP Applicant Portal or call the automated line at 412-520-3848.
  • If you live outside city limits: the Allegheny County Housing Authority (ACHA), HUD code PA006, 301 Chartiers Avenue, McKees Rocks, (412) 355-8940, runs separate lists. Its page says "THE HCVP WAITING LIST IS NOW CLOSED." ACHA's February 2026 voucher lottery drew 6,791 applications and put the top 2,000 on the list (checked November 5, 2026).

Emergency Rental Assistance in Pittsburgh (Named Programs)

Start here: our guide to emergency rent help in Pittsburgh walks through each door step by step: ACTION-Housing's eviction program, Neighborhood Legal Services, 211, LIHEAP and Dollar Energy Fund, and Pennsylvania's eviction deadlines.

The honest picture: we found no open City of Pittsburgh or Allegheny County rent fund you can apply to before a court case. The main rent door needs an eviction case already filed:

  • Allegheny Housing Stabilization Collaborative (AHSC), run by ACTION-Housing — offers "Rental Assistance," "Landlord-Tenant Mediation," and "Legal Support." You may qualify if you "Have an active eviction case filed in the Magisterial District Courts or Allegheny County Court of Common Pleas" or "Have a completed Mediation Agreement with your landlord through Just Mediation Pittsburgh." Call the hotline at 412-248-0021 or email rentalassistance@actionhousing.org. ACTION-Housing says "Applying for services does not guarantee approval," and that its drop-in center "permanently closed" as of June 30, 2026 while "HSC Program operations are continuing as normal." The County's resource page lists it for renters inside the City of Pittsburgh with an eviction filed, so ask when you call (checked November 5, 2026). actionhousing.org
  • Just Mediation Pittsburgh — (412) 228-0730. Free landlord-tenant mediation before a case is filed. Your landlord has to take part, and the County notes it "does not provide rental assistance." A finished mediation agreement can make you eligible for AHSC
  • No eviction case yet? The County's January 2026 resource guide says that without a court filing "You are not eligible for specific funds to assist with your rent" at this time, and sends you to PA 211 Southwest. Dial 211 and ask about church and nonprofit help in your neighborhood
  • Utility help — Pennsylvania's LIHEAP season runs November 2, 2026 through April 23, 2027; apply on COMPASS or through the Allegheny County Assistance Office. Dollar Energy Fund says its 2026–2027 Hardship Program is open, "first come, first served basis while funding is available." Details are in our Pittsburgh rent help guide

The federal pandemic ERA has ended

The pandemic-era emergency rental assistance that ran through Allegheny County from 2021 to 2023 is over. Ignore old flyers and websites that still advertise it. Today's doors are AHSC (with an eviction case), 211, and the utility programs above.

Tenant Rights in Pittsburgh & Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania's rules come from the Landlord and Tenant Act of 1951. Here is what matters most in Pittsburgh:

  • Notice to quit: for unpaid rent, the notice must give you 10 days to leave; for the end of a lease of one year or less, 15 days; for a lease of more than one year, 30 days (68 P.S. 250.501). A written lease can shorten or waive this notice, so read yours
  • Hearing: eviction cases start at your Magisterial District Court. Court rules set the hearing "not less than seven or more than fifteen days from the date the complaint is filed" (Pa.R.Civ.P.M.D.J. 504). Go to the hearing
  • Paying to stop a rent eviction: in a case only about unpaid rent, you can stop the eviction at any time before it is carried out by paying the constable or sheriff the rent in arrears and the costs (68 P.S. 250.503(c))
  • Appeal: you have 10 days after the judgment to appeal to the Court of Common Pleas, and you must pay rent into court to stay in your home while it is pending. The Housing Court Help Desk, City-County Building, 414 Grant St., 1st Floor, (412) 350-4462, takes these filings
  • Free legal help: Neighborhood Legal Services, 928 Penn Avenue, takes requests at 1-866-761-6572 and online at nlsa.us. Pittsburgh's City Code (§ 237.06) sets aside part of the Stop the Violence Fund for eviction prevention for tenants at or below 80% of the area median income, and says "Free legal representation shall be offered to tenants who are facing eviction proceedings in court." The City posts no separate sign-up line for it; ask AHSC or Neighborhood Legal Services
  • Lockouts are illegal: only a constable or sheriff with a court order can remove you. A landlord may not change the locks or shut off utilities to force you out
  • Security deposits: no more than two months' rent in the first year and one month after that (68 P.S. 250.511a); returned with an itemized list within 30 days of move-out (68 P.S. 250.512)
  • Vouchers: the current list of protected classes in Pittsburgh's fair-housing code (City Code § 659.03) and in Allegheny County's human relations ordinance does not include source of income. If a landlord turns you down because of race, disability, family status or another protected trait, file a complaint with the Pittsburgh Commission on Human Relations or HUD

Other Housing Programs in Pittsburgh

  • HACP public housing: several site lists are open (see above). Each site keeps its own list, so you can apply to more than one
  • HACP Homeownership Program: for HACP public housing residents, voucher holders and some eligible families, for a home "within the city limits of Pittsburgh." HACP lists up to $7,000 for closing costs and a soft second mortgage of up to $52,000. Contact home.ownership@hacp.org
  • Family Self-Sufficiency: HACP's Resident Self-Sufficiency office, 2305 Bedford Avenue, 412-395-3950
  • LIHTC (Tax Credit): search HUD's LIHTC database for Allegheny County properties. See how to find LIHTC housing
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): a voucher plus VA case management. See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Rapid rehousing and supportive housing — through Allegheny County's homeless system. Start with the Allegheny Link, 866-730-2368
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Pittsburgh Section 8 waitlist open right now?

No. On November 5, 2026, the Housing Authority of the City of Pittsburgh (HACP) said its Housing Choice Voucher Program waiting list is closed and that it cannot accept applications while it is closed. Some HACP lists are open, though: public housing lists at sites such as Northview Heights, Arlington Heights and Scattered Sites, and project-based voucher lists at places such as Larimer Pointe and Sandstone Quarry. Check hacp.org or call 412-456-5000 before you apply. If you live outside the city, the Allegheny County Housing Authority's voucher list is also closed.

How do I apply for HACP public housing?

Fill out HACP's public housing application and email it to occupancy@hacp.org, fax it to 412-456-5182, or mail or drop it off at the One-Stop Shop, 412 Boulevard of the Allies. Choose sites whose lists HACP says are open. Project-based voucher lists use an online pre-application at hacp.myhousing.com instead. If you need a disability accommodation, HACP lists 412-456-5282.

Is there emergency rent help in Pittsburgh?

Mostly for people who already have an eviction case. ACTION-Housing's Allegheny Housing Stabilization Collaborative offers rent help, mediation and legal support if you have an active eviction case or a completed mediation agreement through Just Mediation Pittsburgh. Call 412-248-0021. Help depends on funding and is not guaranteed. If no case has been filed, dial 211, and call Just Mediation Pittsburgh at 412-228-0730 to try to settle things with your landlord.

How much notice does a Pittsburgh landlord have to give?

Under Pennsylvania's Landlord and Tenant Act, a notice to quit for unpaid rent must give you 10 days. To end a lease of one year or less, it is 15 days, and for a lease of more than one year, 30 days. A written lease can shorten or waive this notice. After that the landlord must file in Magisterial District Court and win; only a constable or sheriff can carry out an eviction.

Can I get a free lawyer for an eviction in Pittsburgh?

You can ask. Neighborhood Legal Services helps low-income tenants; request help at 1-866-761-6572 or at nlsa.us. Pittsburgh's City Code says Stop the Violence Fund money for tenants at or below 80% of the area median income should be used to offer free legal representation in eviction cases, and ACTION-Housing's program lists legal support for tenants with a filed case. Call as soon as you get a notice or court papers.

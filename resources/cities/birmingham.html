Affordable Housing in Birmingham, AL

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Birmingham and Jefferson County, Alabama — not generic Section 8 advice. Three things to know up front: the Housing Authority of the Birmingham District (HABD) does not post whether its Section 8 list is open or closed, so call its voucher line at (205) 974-4440 before you trust anyone who says it is; Jefferson County's emergency rent program has ended and we found no open City rent fund; and Alabama gives a tenant behind on rent 7 business days after the landlord's notice to pay. Alabama has no right to counsel and no ban on refusing vouchers, so free legal aid and 211 are where to start. Everything here was checked on November 5, 2026.

Quick numbers to write down:
  • 211 Connects Alabama — dial 211 or 888-421-1266, or text your ZIP code to 898-211, for rent, utility and shelter help in Jefferson County
  • Housing Authority of the Birmingham District (HABD): (205) 521-0774 — habd.org — Section 8 call center (205) 974-4440
  • Legal Services Alabama (free eviction help): 866-456-4995, Spanish 888-835-3505 — Birmingham office 205-328-3540
  • LIHEAP (Community Action Agency of Northeast Alabama): 855-287-1730 — One Roof (homeless help): 205-254-8833

Emergency Help Tonight in Birmingham

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or could lose your home in the next few days, start here:

  • 211 Connects Alabama (United Way of Central Alabama) — dial 2-1-1 or 888-421-1266 (toll-free). You can also text your ZIP code to 898-211. United Way says 211 can connect you to “Emergency shelter,” “Rental and mortgage assistance” and “Utility assistance.” If you are hearing-impaired, call 7-1-1 and ask for 211 Connects Alabama
  • One Roof — the agency that runs the homeless response system and coordinated entry for Jefferson, St. Clair and Shelby counties. Call 205-254-8833; office at 730 8th Avenue West, Suite 100, Birmingham, AL 35204. oneroofonline.org
  • City of Birmingham Community Service Hub — the City opened an in-person hub at The Salvation Army in North Birmingham, 2015 26th Ave. North, open Monday, Wednesday and Friday from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m., with “Connections to housing, food assistance, healthcare, transportation, employment, utility support, and more.” The City's search site, bham.findhelp.com, is open 24/7
  • Veterans — United Way's Priority Veteran program helps veterans and their families who are homeless or at immediate risk of losing their housing. Dial 211 to be connected

Section 8 in Birmingham: HABD Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Birmingham are run by the Housing Authority of the Birmingham District (HABD), which HUD lists as PHA AL001. HABD's vouchers can be used with private landlords “throughout HABD's jurisdiction in Birmingham and Jefferson County.” Current status (checked November 5, 2026):

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8): HABD posts no open or closed status. Its Section 8 page only says: “When a Housing Choice Voucher program waiting list opens, interested participants can get started by visiting HABD.org/portals to register and join the waiting list.” Its FAQ points voucher applicants to habdportals.org. For questions about applications or the waitlist, call the HCV call center at (205) 974-4440
  • Public housing (14 HABD developments, more than 4,000 units) — a separate paper application. HABD says “Interested public housing residents can apply using this form,” and its FAQ says to drop off the form at the George W. McCoy Building, 1301 25th Ave. N, or at any HABD community center during normal business hours. HABD publishes no open or closed status and no wait time
  • Site-based lists — HABD's portal has separate links for the “TAX CREDIT WAITING LIST - TUXEDO” and the “PBRA WAITING LIST - FREEDOM MANOR.” Neither says whether it is open, so call the site
  • Special programs by referral: HUD-VASH for homeless veterans registered with the VA (screening with Cortney Rankins, (205) 936-1365), the Moderate Rehabilitation SRO program through the Alabama Regional Medical Center ((205) 370-1422), and the MIMR project-based program at Metropolitan Gardens/Park Place for applicants referred by a mental health agency or doctor
  • Apply to neighboring authorities too: the Jefferson County Housing Authority (HUD code AL086), and the housing authorities of Bessemer, Fairfield, Tarrant and Leeds each keep their own lists
  • Main office: 1826 3rd Ave. S, Birmingham, AL 35233, (205) 521-0774. The voucher office is at 1301 25th Avenue North, Birmingham, AL 35204

Emergency Rental Assistance in Birmingham (Named Programs)

The honest picture first: the big pandemic rent programs are over, and we found no open City of Birmingham rent fund. These are the doors that exist now. Funding changes month to month, so call before you go:

  • Jefferson County's Emergency Rental Assistance Program has ended. The County's Community Services page says: “The ERAP programs provided assistance to eligible applicants for emergency rental and utility assistance. This program has ended.” (checked November 5, 2026). Its old application site no longer loads
  • City of Birmingham — we found no open City rent program on birminghamal.gov on November 5, 2026. The City's Department of Community Development, (205) 254-2309, runs HUD grants for home repair, homeownership and “Public service programs that assist low- to moderate-income residents.” The City's Community Service Hub and bham.findhelp.com can point you to nonprofits with funds
  • 211 Connects Alabama — dial 2-1-1, call 888-421-1266, or text your ZIP code to 898-211. Ask for rent help and utility help in Jefferson County and write down every name and number
  • Community Action Agency of Northeast Alabama — which ADECA, the state agency, named in 2022 to run LIHEAP and other community action programs in Jefferson County. Its Birmingham office is at 900 38th Street North (205-502-2108; appointments (256) 999-1166). It says families may apply for “rental assistance, food cards, fuel gift cards” as funding allows, and that “a full calendar of available resources will be listed on our agency's website starting November 1.” caaneal.org
  • Legal Services Alabama — free civil legal help for low-income Alabamians, including “Eviction.” Statewide intake: 866-456-4995 (English) or 888-835-3505 (Spanish), Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., or apply online. Birmingham office: 1820 7th Ave North, Suite 200, 205-328-3540. legalservicesalabama.org
  • Volunteer Lawyers Birmingham — volunteer lawyers for Jefferson County residents with income at or below 125% of the federal poverty level. Call 205-250-5198

Utility assistance: LIHEAP and Alabama Power

Alabama's LIHEAP is run by ADECA, which says it “does not schedule appointments and does not accept or process applications.” In Jefferson County you apply through the Community Action Agency of Northeast Alabama (call center 855-287-1730, or book online at caaneal.org). Household income must not exceed 150% of the federal poverty level. Alabama Power says LIHEAP “helps low-income households pay for heating and cooling costs from November through May.” Alabama Power also offers Project SHARE, for “low-income Alabamians who are age 60 or older and/or disabled,” through The Salvation Army (205-328-2420), and a monthly discount for customers on SSI or Medicaid for Low Income Families, who “may save $14.50 monthly” (call 1-800-257-2726). Tenant Rights in Birmingham & Alabama

Alabama's Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (Ala. Code Title 35, Chapter 9A) covers most Birmingham rentals. The key rules:

  • 7 business days for unpaid rent: the landlord's written notice must state the rent and late fees owed and give “not less than seven business days after receipt of the notice” to pay. If you pay in full within that time, the lease does not end (Ala. Code § 35-9A-421(b))
  • Limit on fixing a breach: no breach of the lease “may be cured by a tenant more than two times in any 12-month period” without the landlord's written consent, and some acts, such as illegal drugs or illegal firearm use on the property, cannot be cured at all (Ala. Code § 35-9A-421(d))
  • Only a court can evict: if a landlord locks you out or cuts off heat, water, electric or gas, you can get back in or end the lease and recover up to three months' rent or your actual damages, whichever is more, plus attorney's fees (Ala. Code § 35-9A-407)
  • Seven days after a judgment: the writ of possession is automatically stayed for 7 days, and you may appeal to circuit court within 7 days. To stay in the home during an appeal, you must pay the circuit clerk all rent due since the case was filed and keep paying rent as it comes due (Ala. Code § 35-9A-461)
  • Month-to-month: either side can end it with written notice at least 30 days before the rental date (Ala. Code § 35-9A-441)
  • Security deposits: capped at one month's rent, except extra for pets, changes to the unit or added liability risk. The landlord must refund it or mail an itemized list within 60 days after you move out, or pay you double the deposit (Ala. Code § 35-9A-201)
  • Landlords may refuse vouchers: Alabama has no law against turning down a tenant because of Section 8, and we found no Birmingham ordinance that bars it. Refusals based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, disability or family status are still illegal under the federal Fair Housing Act; report them to HUD at 1-800-669-9777
  • No right to counsel: Birmingham and Jefferson County do not guarantee a free lawyer in eviction cases. Call Legal Services Alabama the day you get court papers

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Birmingham Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

The Housing Authority of the Birmingham District (HABD) does not say. As of November 5, 2026, its Section 8 page gives no open or closed status and only explains what to do "When a Housing Choice Voucher program waiting list opens": register on its online portal at habdportals.org and join the list. Public housing uses a separate paper application that HABD says to drop off at 1301 25th Ave. N or any HABD community center. Call the voucher call center at (205) 974-4440 before you trust any site that claims a list is open.

Where can I get emergency rent help in Birmingham?

Not from a big public fund right now. Jefferson County says its Emergency Rental Assistance Program "has ended," and we found no open City of Birmingham rent program on November 5, 2026. Start with 211 Connects Alabama: dial 211 or 888-421-1266, or text your ZIP code to 898-211. The Community Action Agency of Northeast Alabama, which serves Jefferson County, says it may offer rental assistance as funding allows; its Birmingham office is at 900 38th Street North, 205-502-2108. If you have court papers, call Legal Services Alabama at 866-456-4995 the same day.

How much notice does a Birmingham landlord have to give for unpaid rent?

At least seven business days. Under Ala. Code 35-9A-421, the landlord's written notice must state the rent and late fees owed and give you not less than seven business days after you receive it to pay. If you pay in full within that time, the lease does not end. But the law says a breach cannot be cured more than two times in any 12-month period without the landlord's written consent. After the notice, the landlord must win in court before you can be removed.

Can I get a free lawyer for an eviction in Birmingham?

There is no right to counsel in Birmingham or Alabama, but free legal aid exists. Call Legal Services Alabama at 866-456-4995, or 888-835-3505 for Spanish, Monday to Friday from 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.; its Birmingham office is at 1820 7th Ave North, Suite 200, 205-328-3540. Volunteer Lawyers Birmingham serves Jefferson County residents at or below 125% of the federal poverty level at 205-250-5198. Call as soon as you get a notice or court papers, because Alabama's appeal deadline is only 7 days after a judgment.

Can a Birmingham landlord refuse my Section 8 voucher?

Yes. Alabama has no law against refusing a tenant because of a Housing Choice Voucher, and we found no Birmingham ordinance that bars it. Refusals based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, disability or family status are still illegal under the federal Fair Housing Act, and you can report them to HUD at 1-800-669-9777. HABD's voucher call center, (205) 974-4440, can answer questions about finding a unit.

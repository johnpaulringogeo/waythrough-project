Affordable Housing in Chicago, IL

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Chicago and Cook County — not generic Section 8 advice. CHA's Housing Choice Voucher waitlist is closed (CHA says it last opened in 2014), so the realistic short-term path is one-time help through the City's Rental Assistance Program (RAP), which is accepting applications, and the named shelters and legal aid services below. Chicago tenants also have the Midwest's strongest local tenant protections under the RLTO, plus Illinois statewide source-of-income protection enacted in 2023.

Section 8 in Chicago: CHA Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Chicago are administered by the Chicago Housing Authority (CHA), the second-largest public housing authority in the country. See our CHA contact & waiting-list guide for status and how to apply. Current status (checked November 3, 2026):

  • HCV (Section 8) waitlist: closed. CHA says: "CHA's Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) waitlist is closed at this time. When the list is opened, CHA will make public announcements and all households will have the opportunity to participate in a waitlist lottery." CHA's January 2023 presentation to the City Council's Housing and Real Estate Committee says the list last opened in 2014
  • Project-Based Voucher (PBV) waitlist: CHA operates rolling PBV waitlists for specific developments — apply property-by-property through applyonline.thecha.org
  • Public Housing waitlist: open. CHA says "Public Housing, Project-Based Voucher (PBV), and PBRA (Project-Based Rental Assistance) waitlists are open" — apply property by property at applyonline.thecha.org. CHA warns that most waitlists are years long, "often estimated at 10+"
  • Specialty referrals still accepted: HUD-VASH (veterans), Family Unification Program (FUP), Mainstream vouchers, Foster Youth to Independence vouchers
  • Eligibility: 30% AMI for prioritized populations, up to 80% AMI for most CHA programs. 2026 limits vary by household size — check CHA's online portal
  • Suburban Cook County: the Housing Authority of Cook County (HACC) serves suburban Cook County and runs separate waitlists. (708) 502-9100 · thehacc.org
  • Contact: CHA Client Center, 60 E. Van Buren Street, 312-742-8500; HCV Customer Call Center (current voucher holders) 312-935-2600 · thecha.org

For the national application process, see our step-by-step Section 8 guide.

Emergency Rental Assistance in Chicago (Named Programs)

  • City of Chicago Rental Assistance Program (RAP) — run by DFSS. Its page says "The Rental Assistance Program (RAP) application portal is open and accepting new applications" (checked November 3, 2026). For Chicago residents at or below 30% of area median income who are at risk of homelessness and have a 5-day notice or eviction notice; immigration status does not matter. Apply on the RAP portal or get help at any of six DFSS Community Service Centers, Monday–Friday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m. chicago.gov/RentalAssistance
  • City of Chicago Emergency Rental Assistance Program (ERAP) — closed. The Department of Housing says "The City's ERAP program is now closed" and sends renters to RAP. chicago.gov/ERAP
  • Illinois Court-Based Rental Assistance Program (CBRAP) — for tenants already in eviction court for nonpayment, at or below 80% of area median income: up to $10,000 of back rent, up to $700 in court costs, and up to two months of future rent. IHDA's page says the program "is Open" (checked November 3, 2026); call (866) 454-3571 to confirm. illinoishousinghelp.org/cbrap
  • All Chicago Emergency Financial Assistance — help with rent, deposits, and utility bills, focused on 15 Chicago neighborhoods with the highest eviction rates. All Chicago says to "Call 311 and ask for short-term assistance." allchicago.org
  • Illinois Homeless Prevention Program (IDHS) — rent, back rent, utilities, and deposits, up to six months' worth of rent, through local provider agencies for households at immediate risk of eviction
  • Early Resolution Program (Cook County Legal Aid for Housing and Debt) — free legal help, mediation, and referrals to rental assistance for renters facing eviction, regardless of income, language, or immigration status. (855) 956-5763, Monday–Friday, 9 a.m.–4:30 p.m. · cookcountylegalaid.org
  • Rentervention by the Law Center for Better Housing (LCBH) — free online legal help tool for Chicago renters · rentervention.com
  • The Resurrection Project — Pilsen/Little Village rental assistance and homeownership help with Spanish-language services. resurrectionproject.org
  • Catholic Charities, Salvation Army, St. Vincent de Paul — one-time rental and utility help through neighborhood offices
  • NHS Chicago (Neighborhood Housing Services) — Emergency Assistance Grants, homeowner-focused but referrals available
  • LIHEAP through CEDA (Community and Economic Development Association) — utility help for low-income households. CEDA says all income-eligible households can apply starting November 1, first come, first served "through August 13, 2027, or until funds are exhausted." (800) 571-2332
  • 211 Metro Chicago — screens and routes to whichever provider has funds open this month

Tenant Rights in Chicago (RLTO and Cook County RTLO)

Chicago has the strongest tenant protections in the Midwest, layered over Illinois state law:

  • Chicago Residential Landlord and Tenant Ordinance (RLTO), Municipal Code Chapter 5-12 — covers almost all rental units in Chicago. Notable rules:
  • Late fee cap: $10 for the first $500 of monthly rent, plus 5% of any rent above $500. Many landlords overcharge here — RLTO violations entitle you to refund plus damages
  • Notice to terminate or non-renew: 30 days if tenancy under 6 months, 60 days if 6 months to 3 years, 120 days if 3+ years (under the 2020 Chicago Fair Notice Ordinance)
  • Security deposit: separate bank account in IL, interest paid annually, returned within 45 days. Chicago RLTO penalties for violations are two times the deposit plus attorney's fees
  • Warranty of habitability: RLTO § 5-12-070 — heat, hot water, electricity, gas, plumbing, structural integrity all required. Tenant remedies include withholding (with caution), repair-and-deduct, and termination
  • Retaliation prohibited: RLTO § 5-12-150 prohibits eviction, rent increases, or service reductions in response to tenant complaints or organizing
  • Cook County Residential Tenant Landlord Ordinance (RTLO) — covers most rentals in unincorporated and many suburban Cook County areas (effective June 1, 2021). Similar to but slightly weaker than the Chicago RLTO
  • Just Cause for Eviction (Chicago COVID-era ordinance, still partially in effect): certain protections apply during declared emergencies
  • Source-of-income protection: the Chicago Human Rights Ordinance prohibits landlords from refusing Section 8 vouchers or other housing assistance. Cook County also has source-of-income protection. Illinois statewide source-of-income protection was enacted in 2023 and now applies to all of Illinois
  • Notice for nonpayment of rent: 5-day notice statewide (735 ILCS 5/9-209). Paying the full amount demanded within those 5 days defeats the eviction; a partial payment does not cancel the notice unless the landlord agrees in writing
  • Eviction process: filed in Cook County Circuit Court (Daley Center). After an Order for Possession, the Cook County Sheriff enforces it. The Sheriff says enforcement "MAY OCCUR AS SOON AS 24 HOURS AFTER AN ORDER HAS BEEN PLACED" with its evictions office, and it does not tell tenants the date
  • Self-help eviction is illegal: RLTO § 5-12-160 and state law impose damages of two months' rent or twice actual damages

For free legal help: the Early Resolution Program at (855) 956-5763 · Legal Aid Chicago at 312-341-1070 · Law Center for Better Housing / Rentervention · The Law Project of the Chicago Lawyers' Committee. For state-level details, see our Illinois housing resources.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Chicago CHA Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

No. The Chicago Housing Authority says its Housing Choice Voucher waitlist is closed, and that when it opens, CHA will make public announcements and hold a waitlist lottery. CHA's 2023 presentation to the City Council says the list last opened in 2014. CHA says its Public Housing, Project-Based Voucher (PBV), and PBRA waitlists are open; apply at applyonline.thecha.org. Specialty referrals for HUD-VASH (veterans) and FUP continue. Call CHA at 312-742-8500.

What rental assistance is available in Chicago right now?

The City's Rental Assistance Program (RAP) is accepting applications from Chicago residents at or below 30% of area median income who have a 5-day notice or eviction notice — apply at chicago.gov. The City's ERAP is closed. If you are already in eviction court, the state's Court-Based Rental Assistance Program can pay up to $10,000 of back rent; call the Early Resolution Program at (855) 956-5763. For All Chicago's emergency financial assistance, call 311 and ask for short-term assistance. LIHEAP through CEDA at (800) 571-2332 handles utilities. Dial 211 for routing.

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Chicago?

Pacific Garden Mission ((312) 922-9822) is Chicago's oldest 24/7 shelter for men, women, and children. Franciscan Outreach ((773) 894-6100) operates two of the city's largest shelters. Salvation Army Shield of Hope ((312) 986-3707) is the central interim housing intake. Catholic Charities ((312) 655-5500), La Casa Norte ((773) 248-1940, Spanish-language), and The Night Ministry ((773) 878-1208, youth) round out the system. Dial 311 from a Chicago phone for DFSS Homeless Outreach or 211 for routing.

Does Illinois protect Section 8 voucher holders from landlord discrimination?

Yes. Illinois enacted statewide source-of-income protection in 2023, prohibiting landlords anywhere in Illinois from refusing Section 8 vouchers or other lawful sources of income. The Chicago Human Rights Ordinance and Cook County had local protection before that. A landlord who refuses your voucher is violating state law. Report violations to the Illinois Department of Human Rights or call Legal Aid Chicago at 312-341-1070.

What is the Chicago RLTO and how does it protect me?

The Chicago Residential Landlord and Tenant Ordinance (RLTO), Municipal Code Chapter 5-12, covers almost all rental units in Chicago. Key protections: late fees capped at $10 for the first $500 of monthly rent plus 5% above $500; security deposits in separate bank accounts with annual interest, returned within 45 days, with penalties of twice the deposit for violations; warranty of habitability (RLTO § 5-12-070); anti-retaliation (RLTO § 5-12-150); 30/60/120-day notice for non-renewal or rent increases under the Fair Notice Ordinance. Cook County has a similar but slightly weaker RTLO for suburban areas. Many landlords overcharge on late fees — RLTO violations entitle tenants to refunds plus attorney's fees.

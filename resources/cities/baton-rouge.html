Affordable Housing in Baton Rouge, LA

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Baton Rouge and East Baton Rouge Parish, not generic Section 8 advice. Three things to know: the East Baton Rouge Parish Housing Authority (EBRPHA) says both its Section 8 and public housing lists are closed; the City-Parish Office of Social Services runs a one-time rent and mortgage program and LIHEAP, both only while money lasts; and a Louisiana eviction moves fast, with a 5-day notice your lease may waive and a hearing that can come three days after you are served. Everything below was checked on November 12, 2026.

Quick numbers to write down:

  • East Baton Rouge Parish Housing Authority (EBRPHA): (225) 923-8100 · 4731 North Blvd. · ebrpha.org

Emergency Help Tonight in Baton Rouge

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or could lose your home in the next few days, start with these:

  • Capital Area United Way 211: dial 2-1-1, free and confidential, 24 hours a day, seven days a week, or text your ZIP code to 898-211 (texting is answered Monday through Friday, 8:00 am to 5:00 pm). Ask for shelter, rent and utility help in East Baton Rouge Parish. cauw.org/211
  • Bishop Ott Shelter Program, St. Vincent de Paul of Baton Rouge: 1623 Convention St., Baton Rouge, LA 70802, (225) 383-7343, office hours Monday through Friday, 8:00 am to 4:00 pm. St. Vincent de Paul also lists the Bishop Ott Sweet Dreams Shelter at the same address, (225) 383-7341. Call to ask about a bed. svdpbr.net
  • National Domestic Violence Hotline: 1-800-799-7233, 24/7, if you are not safe at home.
  • 911 if you are in danger right now.

EBRPHA's own advice for someone who needs housing today is the same: "call 211 or contact a local partner like Catholic Charities of Baton Rouge or the Capital Area United Way." For a full walkthrough of finding shelter the first night, see our emergency housing tonight guide.

Section 8 in Baton Rouge: EBRPHA Status and How to Apply

In East Baton Rouge Parish, Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers and public housing are run by the East Baton Rouge Parish Housing Authority (EBRPHA), HUD code LA003. Current status (checked November 12, 2026):

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) list: closed. EBRPHA's Applicants page says: "The Housing Choice Voucher and Affordable Housing waitlists are currently closed." It adds that "EBRPHA only accepts HCV applications when the waitlist is open." No reopening date is posted.
  • Public housing list: closed. EBRPHA calls the apartment communities it owns and manages "affordable housing," and its homepage says "Our Affordable Housing and Housing Choice Voucher waitlists are currently closed." The communities include Ardenwood Village, Capital Square, Clarksdale, Kelly Terrace, Monte Sano Village, Scotland Villa, Sharlo Terrace, Turner Plaza, Wood Plaza and Zion Terrace. When applications open, EBRPHA says you can apply to more than one community, and to the voucher list too.
  • How you will hear about an opening: EBRPHA says it announces one with "a public notice in the local newspaper, and a posting on our website," with the exact dates and how to apply. Applications are date and time stamped, so apply early in the window.
  • Who qualifies: EBRPHA says most households at or below 50% of the area median income may qualify for a voucher, and that it gives priority to extremely low-income families, about 30% of the area median income.
  • EBRPHA's office: 4731 North Blvd., Baton Rouge, LA 70806. Main line (225) 923-8100; toll free (833) 923-8100; TDD (800) 545-1833. Hours: Monday through Friday, 7:30 am to 4:30 pm.
  • Already on a list? EBRPHA's "Check my status" button opens its applicant portal at portals.ebrpha.org, where it says you can see "your current position and any updates."

Emergency Rental Assistance in Baton Rouge (Named Programs)

Start here: our guide to emergency rent help in Baton Rouge walks through each door step by step: the Office of Social Services, Southeast Louisiana Legal Services' eviction desk, 211, LIHEAP, and Louisiana's eviction deadlines.

The honest picture: Baton Rouge has one public rent door, and it is small. It runs only while money lasts:

  • City-Parish Office of Social Services, Rent/Mortgage Assistance: the City-Parish says its FEMA Emergency Rent/Mortgage Assistance Program "Assists individuals and families facing eviction or foreclosure due to an unforeseen crisis," and that help is "limited to a one-time payment in a 12-month period contingent upon the availability of funds." Its Community Services Block Grant (CSBG) emergency help "may assist with utilities, rent/mortgage, and medication payments" for households under its income limits, about 125% of the federal poverty guidelines. Bring proof of income, picture ID, Social Security cards for everyone in the household and proof of residency. Office: 4523 Plank Road, Baton Rouge, LA 70805, 225-358-4561, Monday through Friday, 8 am to 5 pm; walk-in and drop-off Monday through Thursday, 8:30 am to 3:00 pm, with Mondays for seniors only (checked November 12, 2026). brla.gov: Rental Assistance
  • Eviction papers? Southeast Louisiana Legal Services (SLLS) runs an Eviction Desk at Baton Rouge City Court, 233 St. Louis St., Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday starting at 9:00 am. Call its Baton Rouge office at (225) 448-0080 the day you get a notice
  • Everything else: dial 211 and ask about church and nonprofit rent help in your ZIP code. St. Vincent de Paul of Baton Rouge's "Request Other Assistance" page sends you to a parish resource map to find who serves your address
  • Utility help: the Office of Social Services is East Baton Rouge Parish's LIHEAP agency. It takes online applications for regular utility help, and crisis help once a year if your service is threatened or off. The City-Parish lists a heating season of November 15 to March 15; the Louisiana Housing Corporation's statewide page lists December 15 to March 31. Call 225-358-4561 to ask what is being taken now. Details are in our Baton Rouge rent help guide

The federal pandemic ERA has ended

The pandemic-era emergency rental assistance that ran through East Baton Rouge Parish and the state from 2021 on is over. Ignore old flyers and websites that still advertise it. Today's doors are the Office of Social Services, 211, and the legal help above.

Tenant Rights in Baton Rouge & Louisiana

Louisiana's eviction rules are in the Code of Civil Procedure, articles 4701 and following. They are some of the fastest in the country. Here is what matters most in Baton Rouge:

  • Notice to vacate: the landlord must give written notice that allows "not less than five days from the date of its delivery" to move (art. 4701). But "A lessee may waive the notice requirements of this Article by written waiver contained in the lease," and many leases do. Read yours
  • Hearing: the landlord files a rule to show cause, and the court sets it "not earlier than the third day after service" (art. 4732). Inside city limits, evictions are heard at Baton Rouge City Court, 233 St. Louis St. Go to the hearing; if you do not answer or appear, the court rules for the landlord
  • 24 hours: if you do not leave "within twenty-four hours after its rendition," the court issues a warrant to the sheriff, constable or marshal (art. 4733). An appeal stops the eviction only if you answered under oath with a defense and file the appeal and bond within 24 hours of the judgment (art. 4735). That takes a lawyer
  • Lockouts: apart from a home the landlord reasonably believes was abandoned after notice (art. 4731(B)), possession is delivered by an officer under a court warrant (arts. 4733 and 4734). In a parish under a federal disaster declaration, leaving your home is not treated as abandonment for 30 days after the declaration, and a landlord who breaks that rule can owe you $500 or twice the monthly rent, whichever is more (art. 4731(C))
  • Security deposits: returned within one month after the lease ends, with an itemized statement for anything kept, due within one month or within 15 days after that (La. R.S. 9:3251)
  • Vouchers: Louisiana has no statewide law against refusing a Housing Choice Voucher, and we did not find a Baton Rouge ordinance that adds one. A landlord still cannot turn you down because of race, disability, family status or another federally protected trait; file with HUD

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Baton Rouge Section 8 waitlist open right now?

No. On November 12, 2026, the East Baton Rouge Parish Housing Authority (EBRPHA) said its Housing Choice Voucher and affordable housing (public housing) waitlists are both closed. No reopening date is posted. EBRPHA announces openings with a public notice in the local newspaper and a post on ebrpha.org, so watch both. Questions go to (225) 923-8100.

Is there emergency rent help in Baton Rouge?

Some, while funds last. The City-Parish Office of Social Services has a one-time rent or mortgage payment for people facing eviction or foreclosure after an unforeseen crisis, limited to once in 12 months, and CSBG emergency help that may cover rent or utilities. Call 225-358-4561 or go to 4523 Plank Road. Dial 211 for church and nonprofit help.

How much notice does a Baton Rouge landlord have to give?

Louisiana's Code of Civil Procedure requires a written notice to vacate that gives you at least five days. But your lease can waive that notice, and many leases do, so the landlord may be able to file in court right away. The hearing can be set as soon as the third day after you are served, and you can be ordered out 24 hours after a judgment.

Can I get a free lawyer for an eviction in Baton Rouge?

You can ask. Southeast Louisiana Legal Services helps low-income tenants with evictions. Call its Baton Rouge office at (225) 448-0080 or its statewide line at 1-844-244-7871, or apply at slls.org. It also runs an Eviction Desk at Baton Rouge City Court, 233 St. Louis St., on Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday starting at 9:00 am. Baton Rouge has no Right to Counsel law, so call early.

Can a Baton Rouge landlord refuse my Section 8 voucher?

In most cases, yes. Louisiana has no statewide law against refusing a voucher, and we did not find a Baton Rouge ordinance that adds one. A landlord still cannot refuse you because of race, color, national origin, religion, sex, disability or family status. If that happens, file a complaint with HUD.

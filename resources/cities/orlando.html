Affordable Housing in Orlando, FL

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Orlando and Orange County, Florida — not generic Section 8 advice. Three things to know, all checked November 5, 2026: the Orlando Housing Authority's Section 8 and public housing lists are closed, and so is Orange County's separate Section 8 list; the only open lists on OHA's portal are three Sanford project-based voucher lists; and the City's pandemic rent program is over, so rent help now runs through Orange County's Crisis Assistance Program and EPIC. Florida evictions move fast, with a 3-day notice and a 5-day deadline to answer. The named resources below are where to start.

Quick numbers to write down:
  • Heart of Florida United Way 211 — dial 211 or (407) 839-4357, or text your ZIP code to 898-211 (free, 24/7)
  • Orlando Housing Authority (OHA): 407.895.3300 — orlandohousing.org — Orange County Section 8: (407) 836-5150
  • Orange County Crisis Assistance Program (rent, utilities): 407-836-6500 — EPIC eviction prevention (families with children): 407-836-6514
  • Community Legal Services: 1-800-405-1417 — Legal Aid Society of the Orange County Bar Association: 407-841-8310

Emergency Help Tonight in Orlando

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or are facing an imminent eviction, these are the local resources to contact first:

  • Heart of Florida United Way 211 — free 24/7 line for shelter, food, rent and utility help. Dial 211 or (407) 839-4357, text your ZIP code to 898-211, or chat at hfuw.org
  • Coalition for the Homeless of Central Florida — emergency shelter for women, families and men in downtown Orlando
  • Orlando Union Rescue Mission — emergency shelter and programs for men, women and families
  • The Salvation Army Orlando Area Command — family shelter and emergency services
  • Harbor House of Central Florida — Orange County's domestic violence shelter and 24-hour hotline. You can also call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233
  • Homeless Services Network of Central Florida — runs the region's coordinated entry system for Orange, Osceola and Seminole counties; ask 211 how to get screened

For a full walkthrough of finding shelter the first night, see our emergency housing tonight guide.

Section 8 in Orlando: OHA and Orange County Status

Two agencies run Section 8 in the Orlando area: the Orlando Housing Authority (OHA), which serves the City of Orlando and also manages the Sanford Housing Authority, and Orange County's Housing and Community Development Division, which runs a separate voucher program. Current status (checked November 5, 2026):

  • OHA Section 8 is closed. OHA's Apply for Housing page says "OHA Section 8/Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) - CLOSED" and "OHA cannot predict when the waiting lists will reopen because it depends upon when current clients leave the programs." OHA says the voucher list closed in June 2021
  • OHA public housing is closed: "OHA Public Housing Waiting Lists - CLOSED." OHA says it owns and operates over 1,450 public housing units in fourteen complexes
  • Sanford project-based vouchers are open: OHA's page says "SHA Project Based Voucher (PBV) - OPEN," and its MyHousing portal listed Somerset Landings, Georgetown Square and Monroe Landings, each starting July 20, 2026, with no end date shown. The help stays with the apartment in Sanford. Because no closing date is posted, these lists can close without notice; if they are gone from oha.myhousing.com, they have closed
  • Orange County Section 8 is closed: the County's page says "The Housing Choice Voucher Program (Section 8) is not accepting applications at this time." Its last opening ran May 9 to 11, 2023, and picked 1,000 applicants by lottery. Call (407) 836-5150 or email housing@ocfl.net
  • Neighboring authorities are closed too: Seminole County, Osceola County and Winter Park (public housing only) all said their lists were closed on November 5, 2026
  • Status check: if you are on an OHA list, use the MyHousing portal; OHA says changes must be made in writing within 10 business days. Portal help: 407-894-1500

Under HUD's voucher and public housing programs, your share of the rent is usually about 30% of your adjusted monthly income. Emergency Rental Assistance in Orlando (Named Programs)

Start here: our guide to emergency rent help in Orlando walks through each door step by step: Orange County's Crisis Assistance Program and EPIC, Heart of Florida United Way 211, free legal aid, utility help and Florida's eviction deadlines.

If you're behind on rent or can't pay this month, these are the programs that exist in Orlando right now. Funding changes month to month, so always call first (checked November 5, 2026):

  • The City of Orlando's rent program has ended. The City's page is titled "All Rental Assistance Program Funds Distributed" and says "applications are no longer being processed. There are no funds available."
  • Orange County's pandemic program has ended too: the County says "The Orange County Emergency Rental Assistance Program (ERAP2) is closed" and points renters to the programs below
  • Orange County Crisis Assistance Program (CAP) — "one-time financial assistance for rent, mortgage, and/or utilities (electric, gas, or water only)" for Orange County residents of at least 90 days who had an unforeseen loss of income or rise in expenses in the past 30 to 60 days. Call 407-836-6500 to be screened; appointments are "first call, first-serve," Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to noon and 1:30 to 4 p.m. Help is limited to once in two years. ocfl.net Crisis Assistance Program
  • EPIC (Eviction Prevention in the Community) — Orange County's program for families with minor children who are two or more months behind or have a court eviction filed, and who are working. Call the EPIC message line at 407-836-6514 with your name, phone number and court case number; staff call back within two business days. Limited to twice in a lifetime. ocfl.net EPIC
  • Heart of Florida United Way 211 — referrals to whoever has rent money this month. Dial 211 or (407) 839-4357
  • Community Legal Services — free civil legal help, including evictions, for Orange County and 11 other Central Florida counties, generally for income up to 125% of the federal poverty guidelines. Helpline 1-800-405-1417, or apply online at legalaccessforall.org
  • Legal Aid Society of the Orange County Bar Association — handles "Landlord/tenant issues, foreclosure defense, and substandard housing conditions." Call 407-841-8310 (option 1) or walk in Monday to Thursday, 8:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m., at 100 E. Robinson St. legalaidocba.org

Utility assistance: LIHEAP and OUC Project CARE

In Orange County, LIHEAP is run by the County's Community Action Division: apply online at ocfl.net/LIHEAP or call (407) 836-7429. The County's 2026 flyer says income "cannot exceed 60% of the State Median Income," crisis help can reach "$1,000 in benefits per LIHEAP Fiscal Year (subject to funds availability)," and applications are taken "according to funding availability." OUC customers can apply for Project CARE, "one time assistance up to $500 per household in a 12-month period," through Heart of Florida United Way's online portal; HFUW says "limited applications are accepted each week" (checked November 5, 2026).

Tenant Rights in Orlando & Florida

Florida has a landlord-friendly legal framework, and since 2023 the state controls most landlord-tenant rules. Knowing the deadlines can save your tenancy:

  • 3-day notice for nonpayment (Florida Statutes section 83.56(3)) — the 3 days do not count Saturdays, Sundays, or legal holidays. Act fast
  • 5 days to answer an eviction summons, not counting weekends and legal holidays. If you raise any defense other than payment, you must also pay the rent owed into the court registry, or ask the court to decide the amount, in those same 5 days (section 83.60(2)). The Orange County Clerk handles evictions at the courthouse, 425 N. Orange Ave.
  • 24-hour notice before removal: after a judgment, the sheriff posts a writ of possession and can return after 24 hours; "Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays do not stay the 24-hour notice period" (section 83.62(1))
  • Notice to end month-to-month: at least 30 days (section 83.57(3), changed in 2023)
  • Security deposit return: within 15 days with no deductions, or a written claim by certified mail within 30 days (section 83.49). Florida does not cap deposit amounts
  • Repairs: section 83.51 requires landlords to follow building, housing and health codes and keep the roof, plumbing and structure in good repair
  • Retaliatory eviction is illegal under section 83.64 if you complained to a government agency or to the landlord about code violations
  • Self-help eviction is illegal: shutting off utilities or changing the locks violates section 83.67, and the landlord can owe actual damages or 3 months' rent, whichever is greater
  • No rent control: Florida Statute 125.0103 bars local rent control except in rare declared emergencies
  • Vouchers: Orange County's Fair Housing ordinance (Code section 22-52) bans refusing to rent because of a "lawful source of income," which the County defines to include Section 8 and VASH vouchers, and the County's tenant-rights notice applies it in unincorporated Orange County. The City of Orlando's code has no such rule. Florida's 2023 preemption law (section 83.425) does not mention source of income, and we found no court ruling on whether it overrides the County's rule
  • Orange County Office of Tenant Services — a clearinghouse for tenant rights questions at 407-836-RENT (407-836-7368). It says it does not provide emergency rental assistance

For state-level details, see our Florida housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Other Housing Programs in Orlando

  • Public housing: OHA runs public housing communities across Orlando, including Carver Park, Reeves Terrace, Lake Mann and Murchison Terrace. Its lists are closed; watch orlandohousing.org/apply-for-housing
  • LIHTC (tax credit) apartments: income-restricted apartments with their own leasing offices. Search HUD's LIHTC database for Orange County, and see how to find LIHTC housing
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): both OHA and Orange County administer VASH vouchers; veterans are referred through the Orlando VA. See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Florida Housing Finance Corporation — the state housing agency, which funds affordable rentals and down payment help. floridahousing.org
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Orlando Section 8 waiting list open in 2026?

No. As of November 5, 2026, the Orlando Housing Authority lists its Section 8 and public housing waiting lists as CLOSED and says it cannot predict when they will reopen. Orange County's separate Section 8 program says it is "not accepting applications at this time." The only open lists on OHA's portal, oha.myhousing.com, are three Sanford Housing Authority project-based voucher lists (Somerset Landings, Georgetown Square and Monroe Landings) that opened July 20, 2026 with no closing date posted, so they can close at any time.

Where can I get emergency rental assistance in Orlando?

Not from the City: its rental assistance page says "There are no funds available," and Orange County says its ERAP2 program is closed. What exists now is Orange County's Crisis Assistance Program, which can make a one-time payment toward rent or utilities for residents of at least 90 days after an unforeseen emergency (call 407-836-6500 to be screened), and EPIC, for working families with minor children who are two or more months behind or have a court eviction (call 407-836-6514). Dial 211 for other help. (Checked November 5, 2026.)

Can I get a free eviction lawyer in Orlando?

Florida and Orlando have no right to counsel in eviction cases, but you can ask for free legal aid. Community Legal Services takes calls at 1-800-405-1417 and online at legalaccessforall.org, generally for income up to 125% of the federal poverty guidelines. The Legal Aid Society of the Orange County Bar Association handles landlord-tenant cases at 407-841-8310. Neither can promise to take every case, so call the day you get papers.

How fast is an eviction in Florida?

Very fast. The notice to pay rent is 3 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays, or legal holidays. After you are served with an eviction summons, you have 5 days, again not counting weekends or legal holidays, to file a written answer, and if you raise any defense other than payment you must also pay the rent into the court registry in that time. If the landlord wins, the sheriff can remove you 24 hours after posting the writ, even over a weekend.

Can an Orlando landlord refuse Section 8?

It depends on where the home is, and it is not settled. Orange County's Fair Housing ordinance bans refusing a renter because of a lawful source of income, including Section 8 and VASH vouchers, and the County applies it in unincorporated Orange County. The City of Orlando's code has no such rule. Florida's 2023 preemption law (section 83.425) does not mention source of income, and we found no court ruling on whether it overrides the County's rule. Refusals based on race, disability, family status or another protected trait are illegal everywhere.

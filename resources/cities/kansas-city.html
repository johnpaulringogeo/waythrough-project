Affordable Housing in Kansas City, MO

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Kansas City, Missouri and Jackson County, not generic Section 8 advice. Two things to know: HAKC does not post whether any of its waiting lists is open or closed (it takes one online application for all its voucher lists and one for all its public housing lists), and Kansas City funds a Right to Counsel eviction defense program. Kansas City's 2024 ban on source-of-income (voucher) discrimination can no longer be enforced: a Missouri law in effect since August 28, 2025 bars cities from enforcing such rules, so a landlord can legally turn down a voucher. Note: KC straddles the Missouri/Kansas border. This page covers the Missouri side. The named resources below are where to start.

Quick numbers to write down:

  • Salvation Army Kansas City: emergency shelter and corps locations across the metro
  • Hillcrest Transitional Housing: supportive housing and case management for individuals and families
  • Sheffield Place: emergency and transitional housing for women and children
  • Hope Faith Ministries: downtown day shelter with meals, showers, and case management
  • Rose Brooks Center: DV shelter and 24-hour crisis line: (816) 861-6100. Bilingual advocates
  • Newhouse: additional DV shelter and counseling: (816) 471-5800
  • ShelterKC: emergency shelter for men, with addiction recovery and faith-based programs
  • Greater Kansas City CoC Coordinated Entry: central intake operated through the City of KC and partner agencies. Call 211 to start
  • 211 Missouri: free 24/7 information line for shelters, food, financial assistance, and social services

Section 8 in Kansas City: HAKC Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Kansas City, Missouri are administered by the Housing Authority of Kansas City, Missouri (HAKC), HUD code MO002. Current status (checked November 3, 2026):

  • HAKC does not publish an open-or-closed status for any of its lists. Nothing on hakc.org states whether the Housing Choice Voucher, Project-Based Voucher or Public Housing waiting lists are open or closed. What its Apply Online page does say is that one voucher application, on the Bob.ai platform, covers every voucher list: "You submit one application and will be automatically added to 19 waitlists." A second application adds you to all 11 public housing lists. Call HAKC at 816.968.4100 if you are unsure. Do not assume either way (checked November 3, 2026)
  • What HAKC does publish is wait times. Its how-to-apply page says "The average wait for voucher assistance is 3 years," while its tenant-selection FAQ says "The waiting time for Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) program averages 1-3 years" and, for public housing, "The average waiting time is 6 to 18 months." It also notes: "You may choose to sign up for the Public Housing waiting list, Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) waiting list, or both lists"
  • HAKC's office: 3822 Summit St., Kansas City, Missouri 64111, Monday to Friday 8:30 am to 4:30 pm (HAKC notes "Summer Hours May Vary")
  • Other special programs: HUD-VASH for veterans, Mainstream vouchers for non-elderly people with disabilities (separate referral processes)
  • If you live on the Kansas side (KCK / Wyandotte County), that's a separate agency. The Kansas City, Kansas Housing Authority ((913) 281-3300) said on November 3, 2026: "Currently KCKHA is not accepting applications for the HCV/Section 8 Program"; its public housing wait list takes applications online at kckha.org. Johnson County, Kansas has its own Johnson County Housing Authority
  • Apply to neighboring authorities too: the Housing Authority of the City of Independence and the Lee's Summit Housing Authority run separate lists. HUD lists no separate housing authority for Jackson, Clay or Platte County; HAKC itself serves Jackson County
  • Status check: call HAKC at (816) 968-4100 if you've already applied and need to verify your position on the list

Emergency Rental Assistance in Kansas City (Named Programs)

Start here: our guide to emergency rent help in Kansas City walks through each door step by step: Right to Counsel, United Way 211, the City's rent-help providers, Metro Lutheran Ministry's monthly application, LIHEAP, and Missouri's eviction deadlines.

If you're behind on rent or can't pay this month, these are the local programs currently operating in Kansas City. Funding shifts month to month, so always call to confirm current availability:

  • City of Kansas City Assistance Providers for Tenants: the City maintains a directory of partner agencies providing rental assistance, rent arrears support, and eviction prevention. kcmo.gov/tenant-resources/assistance-providers-for-tenants
  • Right to Counsel (City of Kansas City): free lawyers for tenants inside Kansas City, Missouri limits whose landlord has filed an eviction in court. The City says the program "officially started on June 1, 2022"; it comes from a 2021 City ordinance (No. 211067), and the lawyers come from The Heartland Center for Jobs and Freedom, Legal Aid of Western Missouri and the UMKC Fellows program. Apply at (816) 474-5112 or online through the program's Eviction Defense application. Legal Aid says new applications open Mondays at 8 a.m., only a limited number are accepted each week, and "If you have a trial scheduled within four business days, your application will be rejected" (checked November 4, 2026). lawmo.org/right-to-counsel
  • Community Assistance Council: (816) 763-3277. The City lists it for homeless prevention and rent/utility help, but its own site said on November 4, 2026: "Emergency Assistance Applications Are Currently Closed"
  • Guadalupe Center: (816) 421-1015. The City lists it for rapid rehousing, homeless prevention, rental assistance and rent arrears
  • Metro Lutheran Ministry: (816) 931-0027. Rent and utility help for renters in Kansas City, Missouri (limited in Independence; Northland office for Clay and Platte). Its application is online only and opens monthly on the 15th from 9:00 to 10:00 a.m.; the first 30 eligible households are processed, once every 12 months (checked November 4, 2026). mlmkc.org
  • Greater KC Housing Information Center: (816) 931-0443. The City lists it for rapid rehousing, homeless prevention, rent/utility assistance and arrears
  • Catholic Charities of Kansas City–St. Joseph: emergency financial assistance, food, immigration legal services. Spanish-language services available
  • St. Vincent de Paul Society of Kansas City: one-time emergency rental and utility help through local parish conferences
  • Salvation Army Kansas City Metro: eviction prevention and utility assistance
  • KC Regional Housing Alliance: directory of current rental assistance programs across the metro. kcregionalhousingalliance.org/rental-assistance
  • KC Tenants: tenant organizing and advocacy group; not a direct cash provider but coordinates rights education, eviction court accompaniment, and policy work. kctenants.org

The federal pandemic ERA has ended

The City of KC's pandemic-era $14.8 million Rent Relief program and other federal pandemic ERA funds distributed through Missouri's SAFHR have closed. Current paths are the City's tenant assistance directory, Metro Lutheran Ministry's monthly application, and the named nonprofits above. Don't waste time on closed SAFHR portals from 2021–2023.

Utility assistance: LIHEAP

Missouri's LIHEAP has two components: Energy Assistance (a one-time payment toward heating or cooling, October to May) and the Energy Crisis Intervention Program (winter crisis help November to May, based on funding). In Jackson, Clay and Platte counties, Mid America Assistance Coalition (MAAC) "processes all LIHEAP applications." Download the form at maacliheap.org and mail or email it, or apply online through the Missouri Department of Social Services. Questions: 816-768-8900 (checked November 4, 2026).

Tenant Rights in Kansas City & Missouri

Missouri has a landlord-friendly framework with limited statewide tenant protections. Kansas City has added some local protections, but state law now limits what the City can enforce:

  • KC Tenants Bill of Rights (KCTBOR, 2019): the City Council adopted a Tenants Bill of Rights (Ordinance No. 190934) in December 2019. It lists rights tenants have under federal, state and City law, such as minimum health and safety standards for rentals, freedom from discrimination on federally protected grounds, and protection from retaliation. Kansas City's source-of-income protection came later, in a separate 2024 ordinance (next item)
  • Right to Counsel: City-funded free lawyers for tenants inside Kansas City, Missouri limits who have been sued for eviction; Legal Aid's page lists no income limit. This is one of the strongest tenant defenses in Missouri. Apply at (816) 474-5112 the day you're served: applications open Mondays at 8 a.m., and Legal Aid rejects them when trial is within four business days
  • Source-of-income protection is no longer enforceable: in January 2024 the City Council passed Ordinance No. 231019, which made source of income, including a Housing Choice Voucher, a protected trait in housing. But Missouri House Bill 595, signed July 14, 2025, amended RSMo 441.043 so that, since August 28, 2025, no county or city may "enact, maintain, or enforce" a rule that stops landlords from refusing a tenant whose rent money includes "funding from a federal or other housing assistance program." So in 2026 a Kansas City landlord can legally refuse a voucher. Federal fair-housing law still applies: if you are refused because of race, disability, family status or another federally protected trait, file a complaint with HUD
  • Demand for rent process: Missouri's eviction process is fast. For unpaid rent the landlord only has to demand the rent; no written notice period is required before filing (RSMo 535.020). The court date can be no more than 21 business days after the summons is issued, and you must be served at least 4 days before it (RSMo 535.030). Jackson County hears these cases on its Landlord/Tenant Docket. Apply for Right to Counsel immediately if you're served
  • Notice to end month-to-month: one month from either party
  • Security deposit cap: Missouri caps the deposit at 2 months' rent (RSMo §535.300). Must be returned within 30 days of move-out with itemized deductions
  • Warranty of habitability: recognized by Missouri case law; KC's Healthy Homes Department enforces habitability through code inspections
  • Limited statewide retaliation protections: Missouri's explicit anti-retaliation protections are narrower than many states. KCTBOR adds local anti-retaliation
  • Self-help eviction is illegal: Missouri prohibits lockouts and utility shutoffs to force a tenant out. The landlord must use court
  • Fair housing: discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability is illegal under federal law and the Missouri Human Rights Act. The KC Human Relations Department handles local intakes

For free legal help: Legal Aid of Western Missouri at lawmo.org is one of the City's three Right to Counsel providers; apply for the program at (816) 474-5112. KC Tenants at kctenants.org provides eviction court accompaniment and tenant rights training. For state-level details, see our Missouri housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint. For cross-state comparison, see our St. Louis page (different programs, same state law).

Other Housing Programs in Kansas City

  • Public housing: HAKC owns and manages public-housing communities across Kansas City, MO. HAKC does not post whether its public housing lists are open or closed; one application covers all 11 properties (checked November 4, 2026)
  • LIHTC (Tax Credit): Kansas City has substantial LIHTC inventory. Search HUD's LIHTC database for properties in Jackson County (and Clay/Platte for KC suburbs). See how to find LIHTC housing
  • KC Housing Trust Fund: city-funded affordable housing development. New income-restricted apartments come online each year
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): combines a voucher with VA case management. Kansas City-area veterans are referred through the Kansas City VA Medical Center. See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Rapid Rehousing & Permanent Supportive Housing: coordinated through the Greater Kansas City CoC. Access via 211
  • Missouri Housing Development Commission (MHDC) programs: homebuyer assistance, MO Mortgage Credit Certificate, and statewide rental development. mhdc.com
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator: Community Housing of Wyandotte County (for the Kansas side) and Westside Housing Organization cover the KC metro

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Kansas City Section 8 waitlist open right now?

The honest answer is that the Housing Authority of Kansas City does not say. No page on hakc.org states whether the Housing Choice Voucher, Project-Based Voucher or Public Housing waiting lists are open or closed, so anyone telling you one of them is open is not getting that from HAKC. What HAKC's Apply Online page at hakc.org/apply-online does show is one common application for all its voucher programs ("You submit one application and will be automatically added to 19 waitlists") and one for its 11 public housing properties. If you are unsure, call HAKC at 816.968.4100. What HAKC does publish is how long the wait runs: "The average wait for voucher assistance is 3 years" on its how-to-apply page, and on its tenant-selection FAQ, "The waiting time for Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) program averages 1-3 years" with public housing averaging "6 to 18 months." You can join both: "You may choose to sign up for the Public Housing waiting list, Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) waiting list, or both lists." HAKC's office is 3822 Summit St., Kansas City, Missouri 64111, Monday to Friday 8:30 am to 4:30 pm. Note that KC straddles two states: the Kansas (KCK) side is a separate jurisdiction, and the Kansas City, Kansas Housing Authority said it is not accepting Section 8 applications. (Checked November 3, 2026.)

What is the KC Tenants Bill of Rights?

The City Council adopted a Tenants Bill of Rights (KCTBOR, Ordinance No. 190934) in December 2019. It lists rights tenants have under federal, state and City law, such as minimum health and safety standards, freedom from discrimination on federally protected grounds, and protection from retaliation. Source-of-income protection came later: Ordinance No. 231019, passed in January 2024, banned voucher discrimination, but Missouri House Bill 595 amended RSMo 441.043 to bar cities from enforcing such rules starting August 28, 2025, so that ban cannot be enforced today. Free eviction lawyers come from the City's Right to Counsel program (Ordinance No. 211067, 2021), with lawyers from The Heartland Center for Jobs and Freedom, Legal Aid of Western Missouri and UMKC.

Does Kansas City have Right to Counsel for evictions?

Yes. The City says its Right to Counsel program officially started on June 1, 2022, under a 2021 City ordinance (No. 211067). It gives free lawyers to tenants living inside Kansas City, Missouri city limits (including the parts in Jackson, Clay, Platte and Cass counties) whose landlord has filed an eviction in court; Legal Aid's page lists no income limit. The lawyers come from The Heartland Center for Jobs and Freedom, Legal Aid of Western Missouri and UMKC. Apply by calling (816) 474-5112 or online. Legal Aid says new applications open at 8 a.m. on Monday, only a limited number are accepted each week, and an application is rejected if your trial is within four business days, so apply the day you are served. More at lawmo.org. (Checked November 4, 2026.)

Where can I get emergency rental assistance in Kansas City?

There is no open City rent fund; the City's pandemic-era Rent Relief program and Missouri's SAFHR have both ended. The City of KC lists its rent-help providers at kcmo.gov/city-hall/housing/tenant-resources/assistance-providers-for-tenants, including Metro Lutheran Ministry, Guadalupe Center, the Greater KC Housing Information Center and Save, Inc. Metro Lutheran Ministry's online application opens on the 15th of each month from 9 to 10 a.m. for the first 30 eligible households. Community Assistance Council said on November 4, 2026 that its emergency assistance applications are currently closed. Dial 211 for what is open this month.

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Kansas City?

reStart, Inc. operates Kansas City's largest emergency shelter system for adults, youth, and families. City Union Mission shelters men, with a separate Family Center for women and children. Hillcrest Transitional Housing, Sheffield Place (women & children), and Hope Faith Ministries also operate emergency programs. Rose Brooks Center has a 24-hour DV crisis line at (816) 861-6100. Call 211 Missouri to route through Greater Kansas City CoC Coordinated Entry.

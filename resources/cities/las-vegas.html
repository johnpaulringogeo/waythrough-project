Affordable Housing in Las Vegas, NV

Waythrough Project · Updated October 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Las Vegas and Clark County — not generic Section 8 advice. Two things to know up front: SNRHA's Section 8 (Housing Choice Voucher) list is closed, and Clark County's CHAP rent program has run out of money. For the rent help that still exists, see our Las Vegas emergency rent help guide. The named resources below are where to start.

Emergency Help Tonight in Las Vegas

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or are facing an imminent eviction, these are the local resources to contact first:

  • Catholic Charities of Southern Nevada — Men's Crisis Intervention Shelter — large emergency shelter for men at 1501 Las Vegas Blvd North. (702) 383-1818. catholiccharities.com
  • The Shade Tree — emergency shelter for women, children, and pets fleeing crisis or homelessness. (702) 385-0072. theshadetree.org
  • HELP of Southern Nevada — emergency assistance, rapid rehousing, and shelter referrals. helpsonv.org
  • Salvation Army Las Vegas — emergency shelter for men, women, and families at the Owens Campus and other locations
  • US VETS — Las Vegas — emergency and transitional housing specifically for veterans experiencing homelessness
  • WestCare Nevada — Community Triage Center for behavioral health crises; emergency stabilization and shelter referrals
  • Safe Nest — domestic violence shelter and 24-hour crisis line: (702) 646-4981. Bilingual advocates
  • Las Vegas Rescue Mission — emergency shelter and addiction recovery for men and women
  • Southern Nevada Continuum of Care — Coordinated Entry — central intake operated by Clark County Social Service. Call 211 to start
  • Nevada 211 — dial 211 or 1-866-535-5654, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 9 p.m., for shelters, food, financial assistance, and social services

Section 8 in Las Vegas: SNRHA Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Las Vegas are administered by the Southern Nevada Regional Housing Authority (SNRHA), the combined PHA for Las Vegas, North Las Vegas, and Clark County. Current status (checked October 28, 2026):

  • The tenant-based Housing Choice Voucher list is closed. SNRHA's own how-to-apply page carries the status line "Waitlist is Closed," and adds: "SNRHA accepts pre-applications only when a wait list becomes open. But don't worry you will be notified well ahead of time so that you can be well prepared to submit your pre-application online when the time comes"
  • Public housing and RAD pages show "Waitlist is Open." SNRHA's public housing and RAD application pages carry the status line "Waitlist is Open," and its RentCafe applicant portal says "We Are Currently Accepting Applications For Available Waiting Lists." The portal header lists which lists are open, so check it before you apply. SNRHA's affordable housing page shows "Waitlist is Closed"
  • The 2026 PBV waitlist is open October 26 to November 16, 2026. SNRHA's homepage reads "PBV Waitlist is Now OPEN!!!" and its public notice takes online entries only, from "Monday October 26, 2026 at 8 a.m." to "Monday, November 16, 2026 at 5 p.m.," for six properties: Buffalo Cactus Senior, James Down Towers, Pearson Pines Senior and Robert Gordon Plaza (seniors 62 and older, 1 or 2 bedrooms), Hampton Court in Henderson (family, 1 to 3 bedrooms) and Vera Johnson Manor B (family, 2 and 3 bedrooms). Apply on SNRHA's RentCafe portal; free computer help is at 340 N. 11th Street, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., Monday through Thursday (checked October 28, 2026)
  • The January 2026 opening is over. SNRHA's public notice opened PBV, Public Housing and LIHTC lists "Monday, January 26, 2026 at 8 a.m." and closed them "Monday February 9, 2026 at 5 p.m"
  • The Visions Park PBV list. SNRHA's June 2026 notice: the Blind Center of Nevada and SNRHA opened a Project Based Voucher waitlist for Visions Park Apartments "on Thursday, June 4th, 2026 at 8 a.m." Applications are taken Monday through Friday at The Blind Center of Nevada, 1001 N. Bruce Street, Las Vegas, or by calling (800) 466-7722 extension 301. No closing date was published
  • SNRHA is not an emergency housing source — for tonight, use the shelter resources above
  • Eligibility: SNRHA says applicants "are required to be income qualified prior to becoming a resident." Income limits follow HUD definitions for the Las Vegas-Henderson-Paradise Metro area
  • RAD-converted properties are SNRHA communities placed through the Project-Based Voucher program — apply by property when a list is open
  • Veterans: SNRHA's Applicant Overview says "VETERANS can apply to any AFFORDABLE, RAD/PBV, OR PUBLIC HOUSING community anytime," online through RentCafe or in person at 340 N. 11th Street, Monday through Thursday, 7 a.m. to 6 p.m.
  • Other special programs: HUD-VASH for veterans, Mainstream vouchers for non-elderly people with disabilities — separate referral processes
  • Status check: call SNRHA at (702) 477-3100

Emergency Rental Assistance in Las Vegas (Named Programs)

Clark County's big rent program is gone, so this list is shorter than it used to be. For the door-by-door version, with what to bring and who qualifies, see our Las Vegas emergency rent help guide. Checked October 28, 2026:

  • CHAP / Eviction Prevention and Fixed Income Rent Assistance — out of money. Clark County's CHAP site says "all remaining funds for the Eviction Prevention Program have been fully exhausted. As a result, no new or pending applications can be processed." The exception is tenants referred through the Eviction Diversion Program
  • Eviction Diversion Program (EDP) — Clark County says it "covers seniors age 62 years or older and those receiving SSI benefits due to a disability" who face eviction for nonpayment and file a tenant answer within the notice period, in the Las Vegas, Henderson, and North Las Vegas Justice Courts. Enrollment is automatic; Clark County Social Service may pay back rent, and Legal Aid Center of Southern Nevada provides a free lawyer. Questions: 702-671-3322
  • Clark County Social Service — Financial Assistance — up to $400 a month for a one-person household plus $135 a month for each additional member, and generally "only eligible for assistance once every twelve months." Apply in person at one of four offices, by fax at 702-455-6260, or by mail to 1600 Pinto Lane. 702-455-4270. clarkcountynv.gov
  • HopeLink of Southern Nevada (Henderson) — rent, utility, and deposit help for a limited number of households each month, 702-566-0576. Its online application said "We have reached the maximum number of entries for this application cycle" on October 28, 2026. link2hope.org
  • Economic Opportunity Board of Clark County — one month of rent and/or utilities through a monthly online portal that closes when the month's money runs out; 702-445-7105. Legal Aid Center reported a temporary funding hold in February 2026, so call first
  • Nevada 211 — dial 211 or 1-866-535-5654, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 9 p.m., for whatever else is open this month
  • Legal Aid Center of Southern Nevada — free legal help for low-income tenants facing eviction, (702) 386-1070. It also runs the Civil Law Self-Help Center on the first floor of the Regional Justice Center, 200 Lewis Ave. lacsn.org

CHAP has ended

CHAP began as Clark County's COVID-19 rent and utility program and, in the County's words, "transitioned to Eviction Prevention and Fixed Income Rental Assistance in December of 2023." That money is now exhausted. Some nonprofit pages and old flyers still say CHAP is taking applications; they are out of date. The City of Las Vegas's pandemic-era RAFT rent program is not a current door either: its old web address no longer works.

Utility assistance: Energy Assistance Program

Nevada's Energy Assistance Program (EAP) is run by the Nevada Division of Welfare and Supportive Services. Apply online or on a paper application (English or Spanish) with proof of all household income for at least the last 30 days, ID for the head of household, and your most recent heating or cooling bills. dwss.nv.gov

Tenant Rights in Las Vegas & Nevada

The rules that matter most for Las Vegas renters (statutes checked October 28, 2026):

  • 7-day notice for nonpayment (NRS 40.253). You have until the close of business on the seventh judicial day after service — weekends and legal holidays do not count — to pay or file a tenant's affidavit/answer with the justice court. If you do not answer, you can be evicted without a hearing. After an eviction order is posted, the sheriff or constable can remove you 24 to 36 hours later
  • Paying late rent: after a 7-day notice, a landlord may not refuse your rent just because you have not also paid collection fees, attorney's fees, other non-rent costs, a reasonable late fee, or a security deposit (NRS 40.253)
  • No-cause terminations: at least 30 days' notice for month-to-month and other periodic tenancies, and at least 7 days for week-to-week tenancies (NRS 40.251)
  • Security deposit cap: Nevada caps the deposit at 3 months' rent (NRS 118A.242). It must be returned within 30 days of move-out with itemized deductions
  • Warranty of habitability: NRS 118A.290 requires landlords to keep the unit in habitable condition, including working plumbing, heat, hot water, and electrical. NRS 118A.355 lays out tenant remedies for failure to repair
  • Retaliation is illegal under NRS 118A.510 — for example, ending or not renewing a tenancy or raising rent because a tenant complained in good faith to a code agency or joined a tenants' union
  • Lockouts and shutoffs are illegal: NRS 118A.480 says a landlord may not take back the unit — including by cutting off essential services — except through a court action, your surrender, or abandonment. NRS 118A.390 lets a tenant who is locked out or has services cut off file for expedited relief within 5 judicial days and recover damages, including up to $2,500
  • Fair housing and Section 8: Nevada's fair housing statute (NRS 118.100) covers race, religious creed, color, national origin, disability, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, ancestry, familial status, and sex. Source of income is not on that list, so a "no Section 8" policy is not by itself a violation of that statute

For free legal help: Legal Aid Center of Southern Nevada at (702) 386-1070 and its Civil Law Self-Help Center in the Regional Justice Center. For state-level details, see our Nevada housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Other Housing Programs in Las Vegas

  • Public housing: SNRHA owns public-housing communities across Las Vegas and Clark County. Application is separate from Section 8; SNRHA's public housing page shows "Waitlist is Open" (checked October 28, 2026) — check the RentCafe portal header for which lists are open
  • LIHTC (Tax Credit): Las Vegas has substantial LIHTC inventory. SNRHA's affordable housing page shows "Waitlist is Closed" (checked October 28, 2026). Search HUD's LIHTC database for properties in Clark County. See how to find LIHTC housing
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): combines a voucher with VA case management. Las Vegas-area veterans are referred through the VA Southern Nevada Healthcare System. See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Veterans at risk of losing housing: U.S.VETS Las Vegas runs Supportive Services for Veteran Families (SSVF), 525 East Bonanza Road; SSVF Program Coordinator 702-947-4471
  • Rapid Rehousing & Permanent Supportive Housing — coordinated through the Southern Nevada CoC. Access via 211 or Coordinated Entry
  • Nevada Housing Division — statewide homebuyer programs and funding for affordable rental development; it does not take rent applications from tenants. housing.nv.gov
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator — Financial Guidance Center and Chicanos Por La Causa cover the Las Vegas area

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Las Vegas Section 8 waitlist open right now?

No. SNRHA's Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) page shows the status line "Waitlist is Closed" and says "SNRHA accepts pre-applications only when a wait list becomes open. But don't worry you will be notified well ahead of time" (checked October 28, 2026). Other lists are different: SNRHA's public housing and RAD application pages show "Waitlist is Open," and its RentCafe applicant portal says it is "Currently Accepting Applications For Available Waiting Lists," so check the portal header for which lists are open. The January 2026 PBV, Public Housing and LIHTC window closed February 9, 2026. SNRHA's June 2026 notice opened a PBV list for Visions Park Apartments with the Blind Center of Nevada, applied for at 1001 N. Bruce Street or (800) 466-7722 extension 301. SNRHA's 2026 PBV waitlist takes online entries from October 26 to November 16, 2026, for six properties named in its public notice. Watch snvrha.org for the next notice and call (702) 477-3100 to check an existing application.

Can a Las Vegas landlord refuse my Section 8 voucher?

Nevada's fair housing statute, NRS 118.100, protects renters from discrimination because of race, religious creed, color, national origin, disability, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, ancestry, familial status or sex. Source of income and housing vouchers are not on that list (checked October 28, 2026), so a "no Section 8" policy is not by itself a violation of that statute. If you think a voucher refusal is cover for one of the listed reasons, get help from Legal Aid Center of Southern Nevada at (702) 386-1070.

Where can I get emergency rental assistance in Las Vegas?

Less than before. Clark County's big rent program, CHAP (later Eviction Prevention and Fixed Income Rent Assistance), is out of money: its site says "no new or pending applications can be processed" (checked October 28, 2026). What is left: the Eviction Diversion Program for tenants 62 or older or on SSI due to a disability who answer a nonpayment eviction notice (questions: 702-671-3322); Clark County Social Service's financial assistance, up to $400 a month for one person plus $135 for each additional member, generally once every twelve months (702-455-4270); and HopeLink (702-566-0576) and the Economic Opportunity Board (702-445-7105) when they have funding. Dial 211 for others.

How long is the eviction notice in Nevada?

Under NRS 40.253, a 7-day notice to pay rent or quit gives you until the close of business on the seventh judicial day after service; weekends and legal holidays do not count. Within that time, pay or file a tenant's affidavit/answer with the justice court; the Civil Law Self-Help Center in the Regional Justice Center has the forms. If you do not answer, you can be evicted without a hearing. After an eviction order is posted, the sheriff or constable can remove you 24 to 36 hours later. Lockouts and utility shutoffs outside the court process are illegal under NRS 118A.480 and NRS 118A.390.

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Las Vegas?

Catholic Charities of Southern Nevada Men's Crisis Intervention Shelter at 1501 Las Vegas Blvd North ((702) 383-1818) is the main downtown men's shelter. The Shade Tree ((702) 385-0072) is the largest women, children, and pets shelter. HELP of Southern Nevada handles rapid rehousing. Salvation Army Las Vegas operates Owens Campus shelter. Safe Nest has a 24-hour DV crisis line at (702) 646-4981. Call 211 Nevada to route through Coordinated Entry.

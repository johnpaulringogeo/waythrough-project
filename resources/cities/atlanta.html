Section 8 and Low-Income Housing in Atlanta, GA

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page is about getting into subsidized or income-restricted housing in Atlanta and Fulton County: Atlanta Housing's Section 8 list, its HomeFlex, mixed-income and AH-owned communities, and tax credit (LIHTC) apartments. Atlanta is a tough renter market in a landlord-friendly state: Georgia has no source-of-income protection and no statewide rent control. Atlanta Housing's voucher list has been closed since 2017, so the property-by-property lists are where the openings are. If you are behind on rent or facing an eviction right now, start with the guide linked just below.

Section 8 in Atlanta: AHA Waitlist Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Atlanta are administered by Atlanta Housing (AHA), the rebranded Atlanta Housing Authority. Current status (checked October 15, 2026):

  • The Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) waitlist is closed as of October 15, 2026. AH’s FAQ says: “No, the Housing Choice Voucher Program is not accepting applications at this time.” The most recent opening closed on March 27, 2017, according to AH’s own Housing Choice overview, and AH’s FY2026 Moving to Work report still shows it as closed, with 20,874 households on it. There is no notice of when it will reopen. Watch atlantahousing.org
  • Which site-specific lists are open is not something AHA publishes on its public pages. AHA says only: "To get a Housing Choice voucher, you must register for a chance to get on the waiting list. You can only register when the waiting list is open," and that when it opens the list it posts a notice on its website and advertises in the legal section of the Atlanta Journal-Constitution. For property-based assistance, AH’s Housing Programs page tells applicants to “contact each community’s property management office to find out if the waiting list is open”; the open-or-closed list AH links from that page is dated March 2018, so treat it as history. Call the community or AH at (404) 892-4700. Do not rely on a count of open lists from anywhere else (checked October 15, 2026)
  • RAD-converted and HomeFlex properties work like project-based vouchers: you apply at each property (see the next section)
  • Emergency Housing Vouchers (EHV) are closed to new applicants. HUD stopped all new EHV admissions nationwide in early September 2025 (Notice PIH 2025-07, reaffirmed by Notice PIH 2026-20 on August 10, 2026). If you already hold an EHV you keep your assistance. HUD has funded those households through calendar year 2026, so stay in contact with your case manager
  • Apply to neighboring authorities too: Housing Authority of Fulton County (separate from Atlanta), the Housing Authority of DeKalb County, the Marietta and Decatur housing authorities, and the Georgia Department of Community Affairs (DCA, statewide) run separate lists
  • Status check: call AHA at (404) 892-4700 if you've already applied and need to verify your position on the list

HomeFlex, Mixed-Income and AH-Owned Apartments in Atlanta

Atlanta Housing has redeveloped former public housing sites into mixed-income communities with private developers, and with the voucher list closed, its property lists are the way in. AH runs three kinds:

  • HomeFlex (formerly Project Based Rental Assistance): the subsidy stays with the apartment, so you live at that property
  • Mixed communities (formerly AH Sponsored Communities), built on the sites of former public housing
  • AH-owned communities, including senior high-rises and small family communities

AH says "Each property maintains a site-based waiting list," and that applicants "must first contact each community's property management office to find out if the waiting list is open." AH does not publish a current open-list roster: the open-or-closed list it links is dated March 2018, and its FY2026 Moving to Work report counts some site lists as open and others as closed as of June 30, 2026 without naming them. So the work is calling properties. AH's own contact lists are the place to start: the HomeFlex property listing (dated July 10, 2026) and the AH-owned property information (dated January 1, 2025). They give names and contacts but no waiting-list status, so ask each office whether its list is open. Our Atlanta Housing guide has more on how these lists work.

LIHTC (Tax Credit) Apartments in Atlanta

Tax credit apartments are privately owned buildings that keep rents below market for households under an income limit, most often 60% of area median income. There is no central waiting list and you do not need a voucher: you apply at each property's leasing office. Search HUD's LIHTC database for Fulton County (and DeKalb, if you can live east of the city), then call the buildings and ask which have openings and what their income limits are. Our guide on how to find LIHTC housing walks through the search.

Other Housing Programs in Atlanta

  • Atlanta Housing Opportunity Bond Program: city-funded financing for affordable housing development; new income-restricted apartments come online each year
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): combines a voucher with VA case management. Atlanta-area veterans are referred through the Atlanta VA Health Care System (Decatur). See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Rapid Rehousing & Permanent Supportive Housing: coordinated through Partners for HOME (Atlanta CoC). Access via 211 or Gateway Center intake
  • Georgia DCA programs: homebuyer programs, statewide Housing Choice Vouchers for non-AHA areas. dca.georgia.gov
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator. Resources for Residents and Communities (RRC) and ANDP cover the Atlanta metro

Rent Help and Eviction Help in Atlanta

The short version: ARCHI's free shared application at archicollaborative.org sends one rent, mortgage or utility request to a group of Atlanta nonprofits, and it is tied into the City of Atlanta's Housing Help Center, housinghelpcenter.com or (404) 492-7640. Atlanta Legal Aid (404-524-5811) gives free help to tenants facing eviction in Fulton, DeKalb, Cobb, Clayton and Gwinnett. The pandemic-era rent programs are over.

Other places to ask: HOPE Atlanta, a nonprofit offering rental assistance, eviction prevention and case management across the metro, 404-817-7070 (hopeatlanta.org); St. Vincent de Paul Georgia, one-time rent and utility help through local parish conferences; the Salvation Army of Metro Atlanta, eviction prevention and utility help at corps across the metro; and Georgia Legal Services Program for tenants outside the immediate Atlanta metro.

Tenant Rights in Georgia

Georgia has one of the more landlord-friendly legal frameworks in the country. Knowing the specific rules can save your tenancy:

  • No source-of-income protection, and Georgia preempts local protections: Georgia does not have a statewide law preventing landlords from refusing Section 8 vouchers. Atlanta has no local ordinance either. Finding a landlord who already accepts vouchers is part of the work. Atlanta Housing can provide a list of participating landlords
  • No state rent control allowed: O.C.G.A. § 44-7-19 preempts local rent control. Atlanta cannot adopt a rent-control ordinance, so landlords can raise rent freely at lease end
  • Demand for rent / dispossessory process: Georgia's eviction process is fast. For leases entered into or renewed on or after July 1, 2024, the Safe at Home Act (HB 404) requires the landlord to first give a notice to vacate or pay all past-due rent and charges within three business days, posted in a sealed envelope on the door (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50). If it is not paid, the landlord files a dispossessory affidavit in Magistrate Court. Tenants have 7 days to answer once served. Act immediately if you're served
  • Notice to end month-to-month: 30 days from tenant, 60 days from landlord (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-7)
  • Security deposit return: within 30 days of move-out with itemized deductions (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-34). Since the Safe at Home Act, a landlord may not charge a security deposit of more than two months' rent (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-30.1)
  • Warranty of habitability: O.C.G.A. § 44-7-13 requires the landlord to keep the premises in repair, and since the Safe at Home Act every lease is deemed to include a provision that the premises is fit for human habitation. Document everything in writing and contact Atlanta Legal Aid if you have habitability concerns
  • Limited retaliation protections: Georgia provides narrower explicit anti-retaliation protections than many states. Federal Fair Housing Act protections still apply if the retaliation is for protected reporting
  • Self-help eviction is illegal: Georgia case law and statute prohibit landlords from locking you out, shutting off utilities, or removing belongings. They must use Magistrate Court
  • Fair housing: discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability is illegal under federal law and the Georgia Fair Housing Law. The Georgia Commission on Equal Opportunity and HUD handle complaints. Atlanta and Fulton County also have local fair-housing offices

For free legal help: Atlanta Legal Aid Society at 404-524-5811 and Georgia Legal Services Program represent low-income tenants. For state-level details, see our Georgia housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Atlanta Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

The general Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) waitlist is closed; the most recent opening closed on March 27, 2017. For everything else, Atlanta Housing does not publish an open-list roster on its public pages, so we will not print one. AHA says: "To get a Housing Choice voucher, you must register for a chance to get on the waiting list. You can only register when the waiting list is open," and that when it opens one it posts a notice on its website and advertises in the legal section of the Atlanta Journal-Constitution. For property-based assistance, AHA tells applicants to contact each community's property management office to find out if its waiting list is open. Contact the community you want, or call AHA at (404) 892-4700. (Checked October 15, 2026.)

How do I find low-income apartments in Atlanta?

Three routes. Atlanta Housing's HomeFlex, mixed-income and AH-owned communities each keep their own waiting list, and AH tells applicants to contact each community's property management office to find out if its list is open; AH's HomeFlex and AH-owned property listings give the contacts. Tax credit (LIHTC) apartments are privately owned buildings with income limits; search HUD's database at huduser.gov/lihtc for Fulton County and apply at each leasing office. And apply to neighboring authorities such as Fulton County, DeKalb County, Marietta and Decatur, which keep separate lists. For rent help, start with ARCHI's shared application or the City's Housing Help Center at (404) 492-7640; our Atlanta emergency rent help guide has the steps.

Can an Atlanta landlord refuse Section 8?

Yes. Georgia has no statewide source-of-income protection, and Georgia law (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-19) also preempts local rent control. Atlanta cannot pass an ordinance requiring landlords to accept vouchers. Finding a landlord who already participates in the program is part of the work. Atlanta Housing maintains a list of participating landlords. Georgia generally has one of the more landlord-friendly legal frameworks in the country.

How fast can a landlord evict me in Georgia?

Fast. For leases entered into or renewed on or after July 1, 2024, the landlord must first give a notice to vacate or pay within three business days (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50). If it is not paid, the landlord can file a dispossessory affidavit in Magistrate Court. Once you're served, you have just 7 days to answer (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50 et seq.). If you don't answer, the landlord wins by default. Contact Atlanta Legal Aid at 404-524-5811 immediately if you're served. Self-help eviction (lockouts, utility shutoffs) is illegal. The landlord must use court.

Can I still get an Emergency Housing Voucher (EHV)?

No. The federal Emergency Housing Voucher (EHV) program stopped admitting new families nationwide in early September 2025, under HUD Notice PIH 2025-07, and HUD reaffirmed that in Notice PIH 2026-20 on August 10, 2026: there are no new admissions to the EHV program. This is a nationwide rule, not an Atlanta decision, so no housing authority can add you to it. If you already hold an EHV you keep your assistance. HUD has funded those households through calendar year 2026, so work with your case manager on next steps. Note: EHV is different from regular Section 8, which is still open to applications when a waiting list opens.

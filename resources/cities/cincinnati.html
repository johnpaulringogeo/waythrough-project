Affordable Housing in Cincinnati, OH

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Cincinnati and Hamilton County, not generic Section 8 advice. Three things to know: the Cincinnati Metropolitan Housing Authority (CMHA) says its Section 8 voucher list is closed, but its public housing takes preliminary applications at any time; the City funds an Access to Counsel program that pairs rent help with a lawyer for eligible renters facing eviction; and Cincinnati's Pay to Stay law lets you defend a nonpayment case by paying what you owe by the hearing. Everything below was checked on November 5, 2026.

Emergency Help Tonight in Cincinnati

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or could lose your home in the next few days, start with these:

  • Central Access Point (CAP): Hamilton County's shelter line. Call 513-381-SAFE (7233), Monday to Friday 9:00 am to 5:00 pm and Saturday and Sunday 9:00 am to 1:00 pm (closed 12:00 to 12:45 pm on weekdays). Strategies to End Homelessness notes: "we do not provide rental assistance or services beyond shelter placement." strategiestoendhomelessness.org
  • United Way 211: dial 2-1-1, 513-721-7900 or 1-800-233-4357, "24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year," for rent, utility, food and shelter help. uwgc211.org
  • YWCA Greater Cincinnati: 24-hour domestic violence hotline 513-872-9259; text 513-436-3606. Serves Hamilton, Clermont, Brown and Adams counties. ywcacincinnati.org
  • 911 if you are in danger right now.

Section 8 in Cincinnati: CMHA Status and How to Apply

In Cincinnati and the rest of Hamilton County, Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers and public housing are run by the Cincinnati Metropolitan Housing Authority (CMHA), HUD code OH004. This is a different agency from the Cuyahoga (Cleveland) and Columbus housing authorities that also go by CMHA. Current status (checked November 5, 2026):

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) list: closed. CMHA's waiting-list page says: "The waiting list for CMHA's Housing Choice Voucher Program is currently closed." People picked in CMHA's 2026 lottery were told by email; CMHA says it "will begin to pull from the 2026 HCV Wait List after the current wait list is exhausted." Its estimate of the wait: "6 months to 3 years."
  • Public housing: you can apply now. CMHA says: "You can submit a preliminary application at any time. Once you apply, your application will stay on the waiting list until you are selected." Register on CMHA's applicant portal at myinfo.cintimha.com. CMHA counts "over 3,700 units" across Hamilton County, from efficiencies to six bedrooms. CMHA says you can only open the lists that are "featured or highlighted" in the portal.
  • Project-based vouchers: some apartment communities have their own project-based voucher lists, which CMHA says you join through Rent Cafe.
  • CMHA's offices: main office 1627 Western Ave, Cincinnati, OH 45214, (513) 721-4580. Housing Choice Voucher customer service: (513) 977-5800, 1635 Western Ave, Monday to Friday 8:00 am to 4:30 pm; email hcvhelp@cintimha.com.
  • Already on the voucher list? Call 513-977-5800. CMHA says staff "will not be able to provide you with a position number but will confirm if you're active on the list." Tell CMHA about any address change in writing, and keep the email address you applied with, because CMHA contacts applicants by email.
  • Apply nearby too: the Butler, Clermont and Warren metropolitan housing authorities run their own lists in Ohio, and across the river the Housing Authority of Covington and the Housing Authority of Newport serve Northern Kentucky (HUD's Ohio and Kentucky contact lists).

Emergency Rental Assistance in Cincinnati (Named Programs)

Start here: our guide to emergency rent help in Cincinnati walks through each door step by step: the City's Access to Counsel program, Legal Aid, 211, the Court Help Center, Winter Crisis utility help, and Ohio's eviction deadlines.

We found no open Hamilton County rent fund you can apply to on your own. These are the doors that exist:

  • City of Cincinnati Access to Counsel: a City-funded eviction program that, in United Way's words, "provides rent assistance and legal counsel to families facing the loss of their homes," with Legal Aid and United Way as partners. Under City Code Chapter 868 it covers Cincinnati renters, including CMHA tenants, whose income is at or below 60% of the area median, who face an eviction case or got a 3-day notice in the last 60 days, and who have not had Access to Counsel rent help in the last 12 months. The code says help depends on the money the City sets aside and may be prioritized when funds run short. To ask, call Legal Aid at 513-241-9400 and ask for housing intake, or dial 211
  • Legal Aid Society of Southwest Ohio: 513-241-9400 or 1-800-582-2682, intake Monday to Friday, 8:45 am to 4:30 pm, for households under 200% of the federal poverty guidelines. A Hamilton County page says "Tenants who have court papers should call our intake line as soon as they are served." lascinti.org
  • Court Help Center: Hamilton County Courthouse, 1000 Main Street, Room 113, 513-946-5650. Its eviction guide offers "a free 40 minute appointment with a lawyer," who cannot represent you in court. cincyhelpcenter.org
  • United Way 211: dial 211 or 513-721-7900 and ask for rent help in your ZIP code; churches and nonprofits change what they offer month to month
  • Hamilton County Job & Family Services PRC: Prevention, Retention and Contingency help for families with a child under 18 who are working or have a job offer. It "is not a cash payment and not ongoing support," and the current page does not list rent among its uses, so ask before you count on it. Apply online; 513-946-1000

The federal pandemic ERA has ended

The pandemic-era emergency rental assistance that ran through Hamilton County is over. Ignore old flyers and websites that still advertise it, including dated COVID-era City notices.

Utility assistance: HEAP and Winter Crisis

Ohio's energy programs moved to the Ohio Department of Job and Family Services on April 6, 2026. The HEAP Winter Crisis Program "runs from Nov. 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027" for households at or below 175% of the federal poverty guidelines facing a shutoff or low fuel. In Hamilton County, book a Winter Crisis appointment with the Community Action Agency at (513) 685-4478 (option 1 for an appointment, option 2 for same day; phone lines open Monday to Thursday 9 to 11 am). Regular HEAP is applied for online through the Energy Assistance Portal on OHID; the state warns of "a 12-week processing time." Duke Energy's Share the Light Fund, run by The Salvation Army, gives one-time help to customers with a past-due balance who are not on PIPP Plus: 513-762-5636.

Tenant Rights in Cincinnati & Ohio

Ohio's landlord-tenant law is Revised Code chapters 5321 and 1923. Cincinnati adds some local rules on top:

  • 3-day notice: before filing an eviction, the landlord must give you notice to leave "three or more days before beginning the action" (ORC 1923.04). It does not mean you must be out in three days; it means the landlord may file
  • Court date: the summons must be served "at least seven days before the day set for trial" (ORC 1923.06). Cincinnati evictions are heard in Hamilton County Municipal Court
  • Cincinnati's Pay to Stay law: in a nonpayment case, paying all past-due rent, reasonable late fees, court costs and attorney fees of no more than $125, no later than the eviction hearing, "shall constitute an affirmative defense" (City Code 871-9(c)). A voucher from a social service or government agency that guarantees the payment counts
  • Late fee cap: in Cincinnati a late fee may not be more than $50 or 5% of the monthly rent, whichever is greater, and cannot be charged on the part of the rent a subsidy provider is responsible for (City Code 871-9(a)(10)-(11))
  • Lockouts and utility shutoffs are illegal: a landlord who shuts off utilities or locks you out to get you to leave is liable for your damages and attorney's fees (ORC 5321.15), and Cincinnati lets you recover at least $1,000 (City Code 871-10)
  • Notice to end month-to-month: at least 30 days before the rent due date (ORC 5321.17)
  • Security deposits: Ohio has no cap; the deposit must be returned with an itemized list within 30 days, and amounts over $50 or one month's rent earn 5% interest if you stay six months or more (ORC 5321.16)
  • Vouchers: Ohio has no statewide source-of-income protection, and the protected classes in Cincinnati's anti-discrimination code (Chapter 914) do not include source of income. Refusals based on race, disability, family status or another protected trait are still illegal; report them to HUD

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Cincinnati Section 8 waitlist open right now?

No. On November 5, 2026, the Cincinnati Metropolitan Housing Authority (CMHA) said the waiting list for its Housing Choice Voucher Program is currently closed. People picked in its 2026 lottery were told by email, and CMHA says it will pull from that list after the current one runs out. Public housing is different: CMHA says you can submit a preliminary application at any time. See cintimha.com or call (513) 721-4580.

How do I apply for CMHA public housing?

Create an account on CMHA's applicant portal, choose Apply to Waitlist, and pick from the lists that are highlighted as open. CMHA says your application stays on the waiting list until you are selected. Keep your email address working and tell CMHA in writing if your address changes. For voucher questions, call (513) 977-5800.

Is there emergency rent help in Cincinnati?

There is no open county rent fund you can apply to on your own. The main door is the City's Access to Counsel program, which pairs rent help with a lawyer for Cincinnati renters at or below 60% of the area median income who face an eviction case or got a 3-day notice in the last 60 days. Help depends on City funding. Call Legal Aid at 513-241-9400 and ask for housing intake, and dial 211 for other rent help.

Can I stop a Cincinnati eviction by paying what I owe?

In a nonpayment case inside the City of Cincinnati, often yes. Cincinnati's Pay to Stay law says paying all past-due rent, reasonable late fees, court costs and up to $125 in attorney fees, no later than the eviction hearing, is a defense. A voucher from a social service or government agency that guarantees payment also counts. Bring proof of payment to court. Ohio law has no statewide rule like this.

How fast is an eviction in Ohio?

Fast. The landlord must give a 3-day notice to leave before filing, and the summons must be served at least seven days before the hearing. If the landlord wins, the court can issue an order to put you out, and the officer must carry it out within ten days of getting it. The Court Help Center says the writ comes after 7 or fewer days. Call Legal Aid at 513-241-9400 or visit cincyhelpcenter.org as soon as you get a notice.

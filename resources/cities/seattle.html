Affordable Housing in Seattle, WA

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Seattle and King County — not generic Section 8 advice. Seattle has some of the strongest renter protections in the country: a Just Cause Eviction Ordinance that allows only 16 specific reasons to end a tenancy, a Right to Counsel program providing free attorneys in eviction cases, and Washington's statewide source-of-income protection making voucher refusal illegal. The named resources below are where to start.

Quick numbers to write down:
  • 211 (Crisis Connections) — dial 211 or 877-211-9274 Mon–Fri 9 AM–5 PM; rent help referrals are by phone only (free)
  • Seattle Housing Authority (SHA), voucher list: 206-239-1674 · seattlehousing.org
  • Keep King County Housed (rent help; apply through United Way of King County): uwkc.org/renthelp
  • Housing Justice Project (free eviction attorneys): via King County Bar Association · kcba.org

Emergency Help Tonight in Seattle

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or are facing an imminent eviction, these are the local resources to contact first:

  • Downtown Emergency Service Center (DESC) — Seattle's largest single-adult shelter system. Multiple shelters across downtown including Morrison and Lyon. desc.org
  • Mary's Place — emergency shelter and rapid rehousing specifically for families with children. Crisis call line: 206-621-8474. marysplaceseattle.org
  • Seattle Union Gospel Mission — emergency shelter for men, including addiction recovery and faith-based programs
  • Compass Housing Alliance — day shelter, supportive housing, and Compass Center hygiene services downtown
  • Solid Ground — multiple shelter programs and rapid rehousing for families. solid-ground.org
  • New Beginnings — domestic violence shelter and 24-hour crisis line: 206-522-9472. Bilingual advocates
  • Salvation Army Seattle — emergency shelter and meals at multiple locations
  • King County Regional Homelessness Authority (KCRHA) — Coordinated Entry for All — central intake for housing assistance across King County. Call 211
  • 211 (Crisis Connections) — dial 211 or 877-211-9274, Mon–Fri 9 AM–5 PM (closed holidays). Connects to help with food, housing, bills, and other basic needs; rent and eviction-prevention referrals are by phone only

Section 8 in Seattle: SHA Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Seattle are administered by the Seattle Housing Authority (SHA), separate from the King County Housing Authority (KCHA) which serves the suburbs. Current status (checked November 3, 2026):

  • SHA accepts Housing Choice Voucher applications at any time. SHA's own waitlist page says "The opportunity to apply for a chance to receive a voucher is ongoing. Families and individuals may apply at any time," and its FAQ adds "You can apply to be on the SHA voucher list anytime" and "There is no ranking on the SHA voucher list and no limit to when applications are accepted or the number of applicants on the list." SHA moved to this continuous, lottery-style intake on January 17, 2024, replacing the old open-and-close windows — so there is no opening date to wait for, and applying early buys you nothing. To stay on the list, SHA says "you must also check in once every month using Save My Spot" — online at savemyspot.seattlehousing.org or by phone at 206-256-7000 (toll-free 833-366-5925); SHA removes applicants who do not check in (checked November 3, 2026)
  • SHA Housing (Low-Income Public Housing and Seattle Senior Housing) takes applications for up to two properties in each program, but SHA says "All properties have a wait time" and "Usually wait time is no shorter than two years, and often longer." These lists also require the monthly Save My Spot check-in. Average waits by building and bedroom size are on SHA's property list; SHA Housing admissions: 206-239-1737 (checked November 3, 2026)
  • King County Housing Authority (KCHA) serves King County "not including Seattle or Renton," so Bellevue, Kent, Federal Way and the other suburbs. Its lists are closed: KCHA says "All waitlists are currently closed and we are not accepting any new applications" (checked November 3, 2026). Main line 206-574-1100 · kcha.org
  • Project-Based Voucher (PBV) lists at specific Seattle properties may be open even when the general HCV list is closed
  • Other special programs: HUD-VASH for veterans, Mainstream vouchers for non-elderly people with disabilities — separate referral processes
  • Apply to neighboring authorities too: the Renton Housing Authority (Renton is served by neither SHA nor KCHA), Tacoma, Everett, and the Washington State Housing Finance Commission all run separate programs
  • Questions after you apply: call SHA's voucher list line at 206-239-1674. SHA says "There is no ranking on the SHA voucher list," so there is no position to look up; keep your contact details current in the SHA Portal and check in every month

Emergency Rental Assistance in Seattle (Named Programs)

If you're behind on rent or can't pay this month, these are the local programs currently operating in Seattle. Funding shifts month to month — always call to confirm current availability:

  • Keep King County Housed — King County's rent-assistance and eviction-prevention program for renters with past-due rent or an active eviction notice, run with United Way of King County. King County says applicants "are placed on a waitlist" and are "randomly selected from the highest tier available" (checked November 3, 2026). You must owe back rent at your current King County address, have household income at or below 80% of AMI, and have a landlord who agrees to participate. United Way says it can never pay "more than $10,000 of rental assistance per household," and a household can be selected only once every two years. Fill out the Client Intake Form at uwkc.org/renthelp
  • City of Seattle — the City does not take rent-help applications itself right now. Its Renting in Seattle page says "Trouble Paying Rent - call 2-1-1 for referrals for rent assistance." In June 2026 the Human Services Department announced about $8 million in 2027 funding for rental assistance and eviction prevention, with an online application portal planned for next year; there is no public application yet (checked November 3, 2026). seattle.gov/rentinginseattle
  • YWCA Housing Stability Project of King County — grants for tenants in danger of eviction and homeless families. ywcaworks.org/programs/housing-stability-project-king-county
  • Housing Justice Project (HJP) — free legal help for low-income tenants who have an eviction summons or a filed eviction case in King County Superior Court, run by the King County Bar Association. Walk-in clinics at the King County Courthouse (516 Third Ave, Room W314, Mon–Fri 10 AM–noon) and the Regional Justice Center in Kent. To ask for a court-appointed lawyer under Washington's right to counsel (RCW 59.18.640), call the Eviction Defense Screening Line at 1-855-657-8387. kcba.org
  • Solid Ground — Tenant Services — tenant counseling on Washington landlord-tenant law (voice message line: 206-694-6767). Solid Ground says its Tenant Services "does NOT provide rental assistance"
  • Catholic Community Services of Western Washington — emergency financial assistance, food, immigration legal services. Spanish-language services available
  • St. Vincent de Paul of Seattle/King County — one-time emergency rental and utility help through local parish conferences
  • Salvation Army Northwest Division — eviction prevention and utility assistance
  • Wellspring Family Services — case management and emergency funding for families

For the step-by-step version — how Keep King County Housed's random waitlist works, utility help, the free-lawyer screening line, and what stops an eviction — see our Seattle emergency rent help guide.

The federal pandemic ERA has ended

The federal pandemic Emergency Rental Assistance Program funds distributed through Washington State and King County have closed. Current paths are Keep King County Housed, 211, and YWCA for rent help, and the Eviction Defense Screening Line and HJP for legal help. Don't waste time on old 2021–2023 application portals.

Utility assistance: LIHEAP

Washington's LIHEAP is administered by community action agencies. In Seattle, Byrd Barr Place (Central District) runs LIHEAP and PSE HELP; it says its Energy Assistance Program "is officially now open and will remain open until August 2027 or until funding is exhausted" (checked November 3, 2026), so after August 2027, or sooner if the money runs out, check its page for the next round. Call 206-812-4940. Hopelink serves East and North King County. Seattle City Light and Seattle Public Utilities customers can also apply for the City's Utility Discount Program (60% off electricity, 50% off water, sewer, and garbage) and emergency help with past-due bills: 206-684-0268.

Tenant Rights in Seattle & Washington

Washington has some of the strongest tenant protections in the country, and Seattle layers additional local rules on top:

  • Source-of-income protection is the law statewide: RCW 59.18.255 makes it illegal in Washington for a landlord to refuse a Section 8 voucher or other government rental subsidy. If a Seattle landlord tells you they "don't take Section 8," that's a state violation. File at the Washington State Human Rights Commission or with the Seattle Office for Civil Rights
  • Seattle Just Cause Eviction Ordinance: a landlord must have one of 16 specific causes to end your tenancy or decline to renew your lease. Examples include nonpayment after notice, lease violations, owner move-in, and substantial renovation. Generic "end of lease" is NOT a just cause in Seattle. seattle.gov/rentinginseattle
  • Statewide just cause eviction (RCW 59.18.650, effective May 2021): Washington extended just cause protections to most renters statewide, layered on top of Seattle's existing local rules
  • 14-day pay-or-vacate for nonpayment (RCW 59.18.057). The 14-day notice replaced the old 3-day notice in 2019 (Laws of 2019, ch. 356), and the required form must tell you about your right to a lawyer and the Eviction Defense Screening Line (1-855-657-8387)
  • Rent increases and notice: Washington caps rent increases at 7% plus CPI or 10%, whichever is less, in any 12-month period (9.683% for 2026), bars any increase in the first 12 months of a tenancy, and requires at least 90 days' written notice statewide. Seattle requires 180 days' advance written notice for any housing cost increase. A tenant ending a month-to-month tenancy gives 20 days' notice
  • Security deposit return: within 30 days of move-out with itemized deductions (RCW 59.18.280). Washington does not cap deposit amounts, but Seattle's Move-In Fees Ordinance limits non-refundable fees and allows tenants to pay deposits in installments
  • Warranty of habitability: RCW 59.18.060 requires landlords to maintain dwellings in habitable condition, comply with codes, and make repairs after written notice. Tenants have repair-and-deduct rights up to certain limits
  • Retaliatory eviction is illegal under RCW 59.18.240 within 90 days of tenant complaints or organizing activity
  • Self-help eviction is illegal: RCW 59.18.290 prohibits lockouts, utility shutoffs, and removal of belongings, with double the actual damages plus attorneys' fees
  • Mediation: Washington's Eviction Resolution Pilot Program (RCW 59.18.660), which required an offer of mediation before most nonpayment filings, expired July 1, 2023. Free or low-cost mediation is still available at dispute resolution centers, and the 14-day notice form must say so
  • Right to counsel (statewide): RCW 59.18.640 says that, subject to funding, the court must appoint a lawyer for an indigent tenant in an eviction case — people on benefits such as TANF, SSI, Medicaid, or food stamps, or with after-tax income at or below 200% of the federal poverty level. Ask through the Eviction Defense Screening Line at 1-855-657-8387. In King County, the Housing Justice Project (HJP) also runs courthouse clinics
  • Fair housing: Washington Law Against Discrimination protects more classes than federal law — including source of income, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity, ancestry, and military status. Seattle adds additional local protections

For free legal help: the Eviction Defense Screening Line at 1-855-657-8387 (court-appointed lawyers in eviction cases), the Housing Justice Project clinics, the Northwest Justice Project CLEAR system for other legal problems (King County residents reach it through 211; outside King County, 1-888-201-1014), and Tenants Union of Washington. For state-level details, see our Washington housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Other Housing Programs in Seattle

  • Public housing: SHA owns properties across Seattle including Yesler Terrace (currently being redeveloped), High Point, and NewHolly. Application is separate from Section 8
  • LIHTC (Tax Credit): Seattle has one of the larger LIHTC inventories in the Pacific Northwest. Search HUD's LIHTC database for properties in King County. See how to find LIHTC housing
  • Seattle Mandatory Housing Affordability (MHA) — new market-rate developments must include affordable units or pay into a fund. New affordable inventory comes online steadily
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): combines a voucher with VA case management. Seattle-area veterans are referred through the VA Puget Sound Health Care System. See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Rapid Rehousing & Permanent Supportive Housing — coordinated through the King County Regional Homelessness Authority. Access via 211 or Coordinated Entry for All
  • Washington State Housing Finance Commission programs — Home Advantage homebuyer assistance, down-payment programs, and rental development. wshfc.org
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator — Solid Ground and Neighborhood House cover Seattle

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I apply for Section 8 in Seattle right now?

Yes. The Seattle Housing Authority (SHA) accepts Housing Choice Voucher applications on an ongoing basis: its own waitlist page says "The opportunity to apply for a chance to receive a voucher is ongoing. Families and individuals may apply at any time," and its FAQ says "You can apply to be on the SHA voucher list anytime." SHA switched to this continuous intake on January 17, 2024, so there is no opening window to catch — but there is also no advantage to applying early, because SHA states there is no ranking on the list and selection is by lottery. Apply at seattlehousing.org, and call SHA's voucher list line at 206-239-1674 with questions. To stay on the list, you must check in once every month with Save My Spot, online or by phone at 206-256-7000; SHA removes applicants who do not. SHA also runs separate Public Housing waitlists at specific properties, and King County Housing Authority (KCHA) serves the suburbs on its own lists. (Checked November 3, 2026.)

What is Seattle's Just Cause Eviction Ordinance?

Seattle's Just Cause Eviction Ordinance limits a landlord to one of 16 specific causes to end your tenancy or decline to renew your lease — examples include nonpayment after notice, lease violations, owner move-in, and substantial renovation. Generic "end of lease" is NOT a just cause in Seattle. Washington also has statewide just cause protections under RCW 59.18.650 effective May 2021. Together these make Seattle one of the strongest just-cause jurisdictions in the U.S.

Can a Seattle landlord refuse my Section 8 voucher?

No. RCW 59.18.255 makes it illegal in Washington for a landlord to refuse a Section 8 voucher or other government rental subsidy. If a Seattle landlord says "no Section 8," that's a state violation. File a complaint with the Washington State Human Rights Commission or the Seattle Office for Civil Rights. The Tenants Union of Washington and Housing Justice Project can help.

Where can I get emergency rental assistance in Seattle?

Keep King County Housed is King County's rent-assistance and eviction-prevention program for renters with past-due rent or an active eviction notice, and you apply through United Way of King County at uwkc.org/renthelp. King County says "households who apply for Keep King County Housed support are placed on a waitlist" and are "randomly selected from the highest tier available on the waitlist," and United Way warns "Due to limited funding, not everyone who applies will be selected." United Way says it can pay up to $10,000 per household, and your household income must be at or below 80% of the area median income. (Checked November 3, 2026.) The City of Seattle does not take rent-help applications itself right now; its Renting in Seattle page says to call 2-1-1 for rent assistance referrals. YWCA Housing Stability Project and Wellspring Family Services round out the support network.

Does Seattle have Right to Counsel for evictions?

Yes, through state law. RCW 59.18.640 says that, subject to the money set aside for it, the court must appoint a lawyer for an indigent tenant in an eviction case: someone who receives benefits such as TANF, SSI, Medicaid, or food stamps, or whose income after taxes is 200% of the federal poverty level or less. Call the Eviction Defense Screening Line at 1-855-657-8387, weekdays 8:30 a.m. to 7 p.m. If you have an eviction hearing in King County, the Housing Justice Project, run by the King County Bar Association (kcba.org), has walk-in clinics at the King County Courthouse in Seattle and the Regional Justice Center in Kent.

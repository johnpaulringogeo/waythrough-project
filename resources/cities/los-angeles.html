Section 8, Low-Income Housing & Rent Help in Los Angeles

Waythrough Project · Updated October 2026

If you rent in Los Angeles and need cheaper housing or help with rent, here is where things stand, checked on the official sites on October 15 and 16, 2026:

  • HACLA Section 8 (Housing Choice Voucher): closed. HACLA's own words: "The Section 8 Waiting List is currently closed." (checked October 16, 2026). The last application window was an online lottery from October 17 to October 30, 2022.
  • HACLA public housing: open, except 1-bedroom units. HACLA's FAQ says "The Public Housing Waiting List is open" and "HACLA is not accepting 1-bedroom applications for Public Housing at this time." (checked October 15, 2026). You apply online through RentCafe for one citywide list.
  • LA County (LACDA): closed. Its Section 8 list is closed to public registration, and its public housing site-based lists stopped taking applications on September 16, 2026 (checked October 16, 2026).
  • Behind on rent or facing eviction: call or text 2-1-1 (LA County's 24/7 line), and call Stay Housed LA at 1-888-694-0040 for free eviction legal help.
  • City of LA renters: most rentals now need a legal reason to evict, RSO rent increases are capped at 3% through June 30, 2027, and a landlord cannot evict for nonpayment unless you owe more than one month's Fair Market Rent.

Section 8 and Public Housing in Los Angeles: HACLA and LACDA Status

Two housing authorities run Section 8 in the region. Which one you apply to depends on where you live. Status below was checked on the authorities' own sites on October 16, 2026 (HACLA's public housing FAQ on October 15, 2026).

  • Housing Authority of the City of Los Angeles (HACLA) covers the City of LA. Its Section 8 page says: "The Section 8 Waiting List is currently closed." (checked October 16, 2026). The last window was an online lottery from October 17 to October 30, 2022. There is nothing to apply for until HACLA announces an opening on hacla.org. HACLA's Customer Contact Center is (833) 422-5248. See our HACLA guide for how its past openings worked.
  • HACLA public housing is a separate program with one citywide waiting list, and it is open except for 1-bedroom units. HACLA's FAQ says "The Public Housing Waiting List is open" and "Please note HACLA is not accepting 1-bedroom applications for Public Housing at this time." (checked October 15, 2026). HACLA says placement "is based on date/time of the application and family eligibility status," and that "to apply for the Public Housing Program online you must register for a RentCafe account with a valid email address." You can also request a paper application at application.help@hacla.org or (833) 422-5248. HACLA gives preference to working households, students, veterans and active military, and people who are disabled or 62 and older. Applicants get up to three unit offers, and refusing all three removes you from the list. Details on HACLA's public housing applicants page.
  • Los Angeles County Development Authority (LACDA) covers unincorporated LA County and many smaller cities, and both of its main lists are closed (checked October 16, 2026). For Section 8, LACDA says "the waiting list is closed for public registration"; it only takes referrals of homeless families from the Coordinated Entry System and partner agencies, which you reach through 2-1-1. For public housing, LACDA's How To Apply page reads "WAITING LISTS FOR ELDERLY AND GENERAL OCCUPANCY PUBLIC HOUSING SITES ARE CLOSED" and "The waiting list application period closed on September 16, 2026, at 5:00 p.m." If you are already on a LACDA list, keep your information current in its online portal; LACDA says it cannot give a date when anyone will be served. LACDA main line: (626) 262-4511; waiting-list status: (626) 262-4510, extension 11205.
  • Other LA-area authorities run their own lists: Long Beach (see our Long Beach page), Pasadena, Burbank, Glendale, Compton, Inglewood and Pomona. Applying to several at once is allowed.
  • Specialty vouchers (HUD-VASH for veterans, Emergency Housing Vouchers, Family Unification) come by referral from partner agencies, not public sign-up. Veterans start with the VA; everyone else starts with 2-1-1.

Rent Help and Eviction Help in Los Angeles

LA County keeps a single list of its prevention programs at lacounty.gov/prevention. The County's advice: "If you are facing eviction today or have nowhere to sleep tonight, call or text 2-1-1." The programs that matter most for renters, as the County describes them on October 15, 2026:

  • Stay Housed LA — a County and City partnership that gives free legal services, tenant workshops and connections to rental assistance to LA County renters who received an eviction notice or fear eviction. 1-888-694-0040 · stayhousedla.org. City of LA tenants served with an eviction lawsuit may qualify for the City's Right to Counsel program through Stay Housed LA.
  • LAHSA Homeless Prevention — short-term financial help, landlord mediation and legal help for households at risk of homelessness, generally at or below 50% of area median income (separate programs for families with children and for adults and youth). Reached through 2-1-1.
  • General Relief and CalWORKs Diversion (DPSS) — General Relief is cash aid for adults without other support and can help prevent eviction; CalWORKs Diversion is a one-time payment for eligible families with a specific crisis such as an eviction notice. DPSS: 1-866-613-3777.
  • DMH Housing Assistance Program — deposits, rental help and eviction prevention for tenants with a serious mental illness who receive DMH services. 1-800-854-7771.
  • LADWP Low Income Home Energy Assistance — help with power and gas bills for City of LA households, including shut-off risk. 1-866-675-6623.
  • Unincorporated LA County tenants — the County's Department of Consumer & Business Affairs runs the County rent stabilization program, a tenant hotline and free landlord-tenant mediation: 1-800-593-8222.

The LA County Emergency Rent Relief Program is not taking applications

The County's 2026 Emergency Rent Relief round paid up to six months of past-due rent (maximum $15,000 per unit) straight to landlords, and both tenant and landlord had to complete their parts. Its application window ran February 9 to March 11, 2026, and has closed. The pandemic-era City and County ERAP programs are also over. Watch lacounty.gov/prevention for any new round.

City of LA Renter Protections (housing.lacity.gov)

The LA Housing Department (LAHD) enforces these rules for rentals inside the City of Los Angeles. Checked on LAHD's renter protections page on October 15, 2026:

  • Rent Stabilization Ordinance (RSO): covers most rental units in buildings built on or before October 1, 1978. The allowed annual increase is 3% from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027, once every 12 months. From February 2, 2026 the formula is 90% of CPI with a 1% floor and 4% ceiling, with no extra percentage for utilities. Check whether your unit is RSO by entering your address at zimas.lacity.org (Housing tab) or calling LAHD.
  • Just Cause Ordinance (JCO), effective January 27, 2023: covers most City of LA rentals that are not under the RSO, including single-family homes and condos. You are protected once your first lease ends or after 6 months, whichever comes first. The landlord needs a legal reason to evict, and no-fault evictions (owner move-in, demolition and similar) require relocation assistance.
  • Nonpayment threshold: since March 27, 2023, a City of LA landlord may not evict for unpaid rent unless the amount owed is higher than HUD's Fair Market Rent for your unit size (the FY 2027 figures apply from October 1, 2026).
  • Eviction notices are filed with LAHD: at-fault termination notices must be filed with LAHD within three business days of being served, and no-fault evictions must be filed with relocation payments. You can check whether a notice on your unit was filed.
  • Right to Counsel notice: since August 20, 2025, landlords must give tenants a Notice of Right to Counsel at the start of a tenancy and attach it to any eviction notice and to any notice ending a rental subsidy such as Section 8.
  • Tenant Anti-Harassment Ordinance: applies to all City of LA rentals and bars harassment such as cutting services, withholding repairs or refusing to accept rent.
  • Stove and refrigerator: under California AB 628, from January 1, 2026 landlords must provide working stoves and refrigerators for new, amended or renewed leases. LAHD inspectors enforce it.

To report a problem or ask whether a notice is legal, call the LAHD hotline at (866) 557-7368.

California Rules That Also Protect LA Renters

  • AB 1482 rent cap for most non-RSO units at least 15 years old: no more than 5% plus local inflation, or 10%, whichever is lower, per year.
  • Section 8 vouchers must be considered: California's fair housing law (Gov. Code § 12955) bars landlords from refusing an applicant because of a voucher or other lawful source of income. Report refusals to the California Civil Rights Department.
  • 3-day notice to pay or quit (Code of Civil Procedure § 1161) does not count weekends or court holidays. If you are sued, the court papers set a short deadline to respond; get help from Stay Housed LA right away.
  • Self-help eviction is illegal (Civil Code § 789.3): no lockouts, utility shut-offs or removing your things.

Emergency Help Tonight in Los Angeles

Start with 2-1-1 (call or text, 24/7). LAHSA's Coordinated Entry System Access Centers are the front door to shelter and housing programs, and LA-HOP connects people living outside to outreach teams. Other Low-Income Housing Options in Los Angeles

  • LIHTC (tax credit) apartments: privately owned, income-restricted rents, and you apply to each building directly. Search the HUD LIHTC database or read how to find LIHTC housing.
  • HACLA non-public-housing vacancies are listed from the HACLA home page under Key Resources.
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): run with the VA Greater Los Angeles Healthcare System. See how to apply for HUD-VASH.
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Los Angeles (HACLA) Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

No. HACLA's Section 8 page says: "The Section 8 Waiting List is currently closed." (checked October 16, 2026). The last window was an online lottery from October 17 to October 30, 2022, and there is no way to sign up until HACLA announces an opening on hacla.org. HACLA public housing is a separate list, and HACLA's FAQ says it is open except for 1-bedroom applications (checked October 15, 2026), so apply there through RentCafe, and apply to LIHTC buildings directly. HACLA's Customer Contact Center is (833) 422-5248.

How do I apply for low-income public housing in Los Angeles?

HACLA runs one citywide public housing waiting list, and its FAQ says the list is open but HACLA is "not accepting 1-bedroom applications for Public Housing at this time" (checked October 15, 2026). HACLA says that to apply online you must register for a RentCafe account with a valid email address; you get a Registration Code to keep for later updates. You can also request a paper application at application.help@hacla.org or (833) 422-5248. Placement is by application date and time plus preferences, and you get up to three unit offers.

Is the LA County (LACDA) Section 8 or public housing list open?

No. LACDA says its Section 8 "waiting list is closed for public registration"; it only takes referrals of homeless families from the Coordinated Entry System and partner agencies, reached through 2-1-1. Its public housing site-based waiting lists took applications from August 2026 until "September 16, 2026, at 5:00 p.m." and are now closed (checked October 16, 2026). LACDA's main line is (626) 262-4511.

Where can I get help paying rent in Los Angeles?

Call or text 2-1-1, LA County's 24/7 line, to be screened for prevention programs such as LAHSA Homeless Prevention. LA County lists all of its programs at lacounty.gov/prevention. For an eviction notice, call Stay Housed LA at 1-888-694-0040 for free legal help and connections to rental assistance. The County's 2026 Emergency Rent Relief round closed on March 11, 2026.

How much can my landlord raise the rent in Los Angeles?

For a City of LA unit under the Rent Stabilization Ordinance (most units in buildings built on or before October 1, 1978), the allowed increase is 3% from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027, once every 12 months, according to LAHD at housing.lacity.gov. Most other units at least 15 years old fall under California's AB 1482 cap of 5% plus local inflation, or 10%, whichever is lower.

Can I be evicted in Los Angeles for owing a small amount of rent?

Not inside the City of Los Angeles. Since March 27, 2023, a landlord may not evict for nonpayment unless you owe more than HUD's Fair Market Rent for your unit size, according to LAHD. You still owe the rent. Call the LAHD hotline at (866) 557-7368 to check a notice, and Stay Housed LA at 1-888-694-0040 for free legal help.

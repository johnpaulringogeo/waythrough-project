Affordable Housing in Lexington, KY

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Lexington and Fayette County, not generic Section 8 advice. Three things to know: the Lexington Housing Authority (LHA) says its Section 8 voucher list is closed, but its senior apartments and Centre Meadows lists are open; the City of Lexington runs emergency financial assistance for rent and utilities by appointment, while funds last; and Lexington follows Kentucky's landlord-tenant act, so an eviction for unpaid rent starts with a 7-day notice and you get only 7 days to appeal a judgment. Everything below was checked on November 12, 2026.

Emergency Help Tonight in Lexington

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or could lose your home in the next few days, start with these:

  • United Way of the Bluegrass 211: dial 2-1-1, text your ZIP code to 898211, or search uwbg211.org. Ask for shelter, rent and utility help in Fayette County.
  • The Hope Center Emergency Shelter: 360 West Loudon Avenue, 859-252-7881. The Hope Center says the shelter "operates 24 hours a day, 365 days a year and houses adult men experiencing homelessness," up to 200 men a night, with more room in winter. hopectr.org
  • Greenhouse17: 24-hour crisis hotline 800-544-2022 for domestic violence, with emergency shelter. greenhouse17.org
  • Families: Community Action Council's Emergency Family Housing program gives "short-term emergency shelter and supportive services for two-parent and group families in Lexington-Fayette County who are experiencing homelessness." Call Community Action Council at 859-233-4600.
  • Office of Homelessness Prevention and Intervention (City of Lexington): 101 E. Vine St., (859) 258-3070, Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.
  • 911 if you are in danger right now.

Section 8 in Lexington: LHA Status and How to Apply

In Lexington and Fayette County, Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers and public housing are run by the Lexington-Fayette Urban County Housing Authority, which calls itself LHA or the Lexington Housing Authority, HUD code KY004. Current status (checked November 12, 2026):

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) list: closed. LHA's voucher page says: "The LHA's HCV waiting list is currently closed." No reopening date is posted.
  • Senior apartments: open. LHA says its "wait list is open for ONE (1) bedroom units at the Ballard and Connie Griffith Apartments for elderly persons (62 years of age or older)," at 650 Tower Plaza. All utilities are included. Call (859) 281-5053.
  • Centre Meadows, two bedrooms: open. A project-based voucher site at 1301, 1317 and 1333 Centre Parkway. It is a self-sufficiency site: the head of household or spouse must show work of at least 20 hours a week, with exceptions for elderly and disabled applicants. Online applications only; for help, call 859-281-5087.
  • Centre Meadows, three bedrooms: a dated window. LHA's notice says it will take applications "starting 12:00 a.m., Monday, October 12, 2026 through 11:59 p.m. Friday, November 13, 2026." LHA's applicant portal shows a later end date, December 13, 2026. Apply as early as you can and do not count on the later date; if the window has passed, check lexha.org for the next one.
  • Other public housing lists: LHA's public housing page mentions a Northeast list that was to open June 21, 2026, but its portal does not show it as open now. Call (859) 281-5060 to ask.
  • How to apply: online only, at lexha.myhousing.com. LHA says to submit only one application and to report any change of address, income or family size within 10 business days, in writing.
  • LHA's office: HUD lists 300 West New Circle Road, Lexington, KY 40505. Main line (859) 281-5060, Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. LHA sees people by appointment and uses a doorbell and intercom at its offices.

Emergency Rental Assistance in Lexington (Named Programs)

Start here: our guide to emergency rent help in Lexington walks through each door step by step: the city's emergency financial assistance, Legal Aid of the Bluegrass, 211, LIHEAP, and Kentucky's eviction deadlines.

The honest picture: Lexington has a real city rent door, but it has rules and it runs out of money:

  • Emergency financial assistance, City of Lexington Community & Resident Services: the City says this help "is available to prevent eviction or utility service disconnection at your current residence," for rent, mortgage or utility payments, and that "Eligible clients will receive the appropriated financial assistance if funds are available." Its screening questions: you have lived in Fayette County for a year or more; Community & Resident Services has not helped you with rent or utilities in the last three years; and for rent, you have "a seven-day notice, or eviction notice" or are 30 days or more behind. Call (859) 300-5300 for an appointment. Office: 498 Georgetown St., Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. (checked November 12, 2026). lexingtonky.gov
  • The City's Housing Advocate: (859) 280-8425, 200 E. Main St. The City says the Housing Advocate helps if you "Want to know what to expect if you're facing eviction" or need "help finding a landlord who will accept a Housing Choice Voucher"
  • Free legal help: Legal Aid of the Bluegrass, 300 E. Main Street, Suite 210, takes intake calls at 859-431-8200, Monday through Thursday, 10 a.m. to 3 p.m., and Friday, 10 a.m. to 1 p.m., or online at lablaw.org
  • Utility help: Community Action Council runs LIHEAP for Fayette County. The Fall Subsidy takes applications November 9 through December 18, 2026, or until funds run out; book at 859-300-6960. Details are in our Lexington rent help guide

The federal pandemic ERA has ended

Lexington's pandemic-era Housing Stabilization Program is over. Community Action Council, which ran it with the City, says the federal funds "have now been exhausted." The state's Team Kentucky Eviction Diversion Program never covered Fayette County, and it "stopped accepting applications on Tuesday, April 1, 2025." Ignore old flyers that still advertise either one.

Tenant Rights in Lexington & Kentucky

Lexington-Fayette adopted Kentucky's Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA, KRS 383.505 to 383.715) by Ordinance No. 98-84 in 1984, so the full act applies here. Here is what matters most:

  • Notice for unpaid rent: the landlord must give written notice and 7 days to pay before ending the lease (KRS 383.660(2)). For other lease violations, the notice must give at least 14 days, and fixing the problem in time keeps the lease (KRS 383.660(1)). A month-to-month tenancy takes 30 days' written notice to end (KRS 383.695)
  • Court: eviction (forcible detainer) cases are filed in Fayette District Court at the Robert F. Stephens District Courthouse, 150 N. Limestone. The court clerk's page says that after a 7-day notice a landlord "can file on the 8th day." The warrant must give you "at least three (3) days' notice" of the trial (KRS 383.210). Go
  • Only 7 days after judgment: you can appeal "within seven (7) days next after the finding," but you must deposit the rent owed since the case began with the circuit court clerk and keep paying rent into court (KRS 383.255). If no appeal is filed by the seventh day, the court issues a warrant of restitution on request (KRS 383.245). In Fayette County, the clerk's page says the landlord requests that warrant between the 8th and 30th day after the judgment and schedules the sheriff or a constable
  • Lockouts and shutoffs are illegal: if a landlord locks you out or cuts off heat, running water, hot water, electric, gas or a similar service, you can get back in or end the lease and recover up to three months' rent and attorney's fees (KRS 383.655)
  • Security deposits: the landlord must keep your deposit in a separate bank account, tell you where it is, and give you a written list of existing damage before you move in and again at move-out. A landlord who skips those steps cannot keep any of the deposit (KRS 383.580)
  • Vouchers: Kentucky's House Bill 18 (2024) bars any city or urban-county government from adopting or enforcing a rule that stops a landlord from refusing a voucher, so a Lexington landlord can legally say no. Refusals based on race, disability, family status or another federally protected trait are still illegal; report them to HUD

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Lexington Section 8 waitlist open right now?

No. On November 12, 2026, the Lexington Housing Authority (LHA) said its Housing Choice Voucher waiting list is currently closed. Some LHA lists are open: one-bedroom senior apartments at Ballard and Connie Griffith for people 62 and older, and two-bedroom units at Centre Meadows, a project-based voucher site with a work requirement. Apply online through lexha.org or call (859) 281-5060.

Is there emergency rent help in Lexington?

Yes, by appointment and while funds last. The City of Lexington's Community and Resident Services offers emergency financial assistance for rent, mortgage or utilities to prevent eviction or disconnection. You must have lived in Fayette County at least a year, not have had its help with rent or utilities in the last three years, and have a seven-day notice, an eviction notice, or rent 30 days past due. Call (859) 300-5300.

How much notice does a Lexington landlord have to give?

For unpaid rent, seven days. Lexington-Fayette has adopted Kentucky's Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, which requires written notice that the lease will end if you do not pay within 7 days. For other lease violations the notice must give at least 14 days, and a month-to-month tenancy takes 30 days' written notice to end.

Can I get a free lawyer for an eviction in Lexington?

You can ask. Legal Aid of the Bluegrass helps low-income tenants facing eviction, including advice, negotiation with landlords and representation in court. Call intake at 859-431-8200, Monday through Thursday 10 a.m. to 3 p.m. and Friday 10 a.m. to 1 p.m., or apply online at lablaw.org. If your court date is three business days away or less, call instead of applying online.

How long do I have after an eviction judgment in Lexington?

Seven days. Under Kentucky law you can appeal within 7 days after the judgment, but you must deposit the rent owed since the case began with the circuit court clerk and keep paying rent into court. If you do not appeal, the landlord can ask for a warrant and schedule the sheriff or a constable to put you out. Call Legal Aid of the Bluegrass the day of the judgment.

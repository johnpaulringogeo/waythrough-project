Affordable Housing in Sacramento, CA

Waythrough Project · Updated October 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Sacramento and Sacramento County — not generic Section 8 advice. SHRA's tenant-based Section 8 waiting list is closed, and the pandemic-era SERA rent program has ended, so the realistic short-term paths are 2-1-1 and the Coordinated Access System, CalWORKs Homeless Assistance for families, SSVF for veterans, and free legal help. Sacramento renters also have California's Tenant Protection Act and, inside city limits, the City's Tenant Protection Program.

Quick numbers to write down:
  • 211 Sacramento — dial 211 or (916) 498-1000 (free, 24/7); the way into the Coordinated Access System
  • SHRA: (916) 440-1390 · shra.org
  • Legal Services of Northern California (Sacramento): (916) 551-2150 · lsnc.net
  • Sacramento Renters Helpline: (916) 389-7877

Emergency Help Tonight in Sacramento

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or are facing an imminent eviction, these are the local resources to contact first:

  • 211 Sacramento — free, "24 hours a day, seven days a week," with help in other languages. Dial 211, (916) 498-1000, or toll-free 1-844-546-1464. Sacramento County sends people seeking shelter here, and families seeking shelter are told to call 211
  • Coordinated Access System (CAS) — run by Sacramento Steps Forward with the City and County. In its words, a person experiencing or at risk of homelessness "will call 2-1-1, work with a street outreach team, or visit a CAS-enrolled provider to start the process." It includes homelessness diversion, shelter, and housing referrals, and those with the highest risk go first. sacramentostepsforward.org
  • Loaves & Fishes — services for people experiencing homelessness at 1351 North C St. (916) 446-0874 · sacloaves.org
  • Saint John's Program for Real Change — "a safe place for women and children to break free from the cycles of homelessness and poverty." (916) 453-1482
  • WEAVE — domestic violence and sexual assault services, "24/7 survivor-centered services every day of the year." Support line (916) 920-2952
  • Volunteers of America Northern California & Northern Nevada (VOA) — shelter and housing programs, including SSVF for veterans. Main office (916) 265-3400

For a full walkthrough of finding shelter the first night, see our emergency housing tonight guide.

Section 8 in Sacramento: SHRA Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in the Sacramento region are administered by the Sacramento Housing and Redevelopment Agency (SHRA), which acts as both the Housing Authority of the City of Sacramento and the Housing Authority of the County of Sacramento. Current status (checked October 28, 2026):

  • The Section 8 voucher list is closed. SHRA's waiting list portal says: "Tenant-Based Housing Choice Voucher Program waiting list (formerly known as the Section 8 program) is currently closed."
  • Some other lists were open, mostly for larger units. When we checked, project-based voucher lists for the City and County (3, 4, and 5 bedrooms), Phoenix Park (4 bedrooms), and Mirasol Village (3 to 5 bedrooms), and public housing lists for City Housing (4 bedrooms), County Housing (4 and 5 bedrooms), and Walnut Grove (3 bedrooms), were taking applications. The Foster Youth to Independence voucher list is "Open for Referral Only." Check sacwaitlist.com before you rely on any of these
  • Apply only on sacwaitlist.com. SHRA says "All Sacramento Housing and Redevelopment Agency waiting lists are ONLY posted on Sacwaitlist.com," that flyers listing "www.govassistance.org" are "NOT LEGITIMATE," and that "There is never a fee to apply"
  • If you already applied: SHRA says it is "unable to provide you with your exact position on the waiting list." Keep your contact information current on sacwaitlist.com so you do not miss a notice
  • No emergency housing: "SHRA does not provide immediate emergency housing or hotel vouchers." Call 211 instead
  • Contact: SHRA customer service (916) 440-1390, 630 I Street (by appointment only) · shra.org
  • Other Sacramento-area authorities: Roseville Housing Authority and Yolo County Housing run separate lists. Apply to more than one authority to improve your odds

Our full guide to SHRA: Sacramento Housing and Redevelopment Agency (SHRA): waitlist status, contact and how to apply. For the national application process, see our step-by-step Section 8 guide.

Emergency Rental Assistance in Sacramento (Named Programs)

Start here: our guide to emergency rent help in Sacramento walks through each door step by step. In short (checked October 28, 2026):

  • 211 Sacramento and the Coordinated Access System — dial 211 or (916) 498-1000 and say you are about to lose your housing. CAS includes homelessness diversion and prevention help, but "Because resources are limited, not everyone is referred right away"
  • CalWORKs Homeless Assistance (families) — for "eligible CalWORKs recipients or apparently eligible CalWORKs applicants, who are homeless or at risk of homelessness." It can pay "up to two months of rent arrearages" to prevent an eviction. Apply at the Sacramento County Department of Human Assistance, 1-800-560-0976, with form CW-42
  • Volunteers of America SSVF (veterans) — Supportive Services for Veteran Families, funded by the VA, offers case management and rental assistance to very low-income veteran families. (916) 228-3153 · 10636 Schirra Ave, Mather
  • Sacramento Renters Helpline — free tenant counseling and "tenant-landlord dispute resolution" for Sacramento County and the cities of Sacramento, Elk Grove, Rancho Cordova, Folsom, and Citrus Heights. (916) 389-7877, Monday through Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m.

The Sacramento Emergency Rental Assistance (SERA) program is closed

SHRA says: "As of December 11th, 2024, all rental assistance funds have been disbursed and the SERA program is closed." Its waiting list closed December 15, 2023. We found no City, County, or SHRA rent fund that replaced it. Don't waste time on old SERA links or flyers.

Utility help: SMUD and PG&E

Most of Sacramento gets electricity from SMUD and natural gas from PG&E, and a lower utility bill frees up money for rent. PG&E's discounts apply to your PG&E bill, so if SMUD supplies your electricity, that is your gas bill (checked October 28, 2026):

  • SMUD Energy Assistance Program Rate (EAPR) — SMUD says EAPR "provides a monthly discount on a qualified customer's energy bill." The monthly discount is up to $105 if your household income is at or below 50% of the federal poverty level, $42 from 50% to 100%, $20 from 100% to 150%, and $10 from 150% to 200%. To qualify, monthly household income must be no more than $3,607 for 1 or 2 people, $4,553 for 3, or $5,500 for 4 (effective February 1, 2026). Apply online at smud.org/EAPRapp (in Spanish: smud.org/EAPRappES), through My Account, or by mail; SMUD says "it may take us 2 to 4 weeks" to process. Questions: 1-888-742-7683 · smud.org
  • SMUD Medical Equipment Discount — "a $15 per month discount" for customers who rely on qualifying medical equipment, such as a dialysis cycler, electric wheelchair, oxygen concentrator, or ventilator (CPAP and BiPAP machines do not count). A health professional must sign the form. 1-888-742-7683 · smud.org
  • PG&E CARE (California Alternate Rates for Energy) — PG&E says CARE "provides a monthly discount of 20% or more on gas and 35% or more on electricity." You qualify by income or by enrollment in a qualifying public assistance program. The income limits, valid through May 31, 2027, are $43,280 a year for 1 or 2 people, $54,640 for 3, and $66,000 for 4. Apply online from PG&E's CARE page. Questions: 1-866-743-5832
  • PG&E FERA — an 18% discount on electric rates only. PG&E says "FERA does not offer a discount on gas rates," so it does not help a household whose electricity comes from SMUD. pge.com — FERA
  • PG&E REACH — "an energy credit (applied directly to your PG&E bill) up to $800 towards the past due portion of the bill." Your income must be at or below 200% of the federal poverty level, you must have received "either a 15-day or a 48-hour disconnection notice," you must be the main customer on the account, and you cannot have received REACH earlier in the same calendar year; customers in PG&E's Arrearage Management Plan are not eligible. "Funding is provided on a first-come, first-served basis and is subject to availability." Apply online through Dollar Energy Fund at myappconnect.com/pge. PG&E: 1-877-660-6789 · pge.com — REACH
  • HEAP (federal energy aid) through Community Resource Project — "HEAP provides one payment, typically ranging from $300-$600 every 12 months," as a credit on your utility bill. Processing "can take 8-12 weeks," and you still have to pay your bill meanwhile. For low-income households in Sacramento, Sutter, and Yuba counties, with priority for households with a high energy burden or a member who is over 60, disabled, or under 5. Apply online or at 250 Harris Avenue or 3821 41st Ave in Sacramento. (916) 567-5200, Monday to Friday, 7:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. · communityresourceproject.org

Tenant Rights in California (Sacramento)

Sacramento tenants are covered by:

  • Tenant Protection Act (AB 1482, Civil Code 1946.2 and 1947.12) — for covered units, yearly rent increases are capped at "5 percent plus the percentage change in the cost of living, or 10 percent, whichever is lower." After a tenant has "continuously and lawfully occupied" the home for 12 months, the owner needs a just cause, stated in the written notice, to end the tenancy. SB 567 (operative April 1, 2024) tightened the rules for owner move-in and remodel evictions; it did not remove the 12-month requirement. Some housing is exempt, including many single-family homes owned by individuals and units built in the last 15 years
  • City of Sacramento Tenant Protection Program (Tenant Protection and Relief Act, City Code Chapter 5.156) — protects "most City of Sacramento residential tenants renting in multifamily rental units (e.g., duplexes, apartments)." Rent increases cannot exceed "5 percent plus the change in the CPI," and "Effective July 1, 2026, the maximum rate increase is 8.6%." Rent can go up only once every 12 months. Tenants who have lived in the unit more than 12 months are protected from eviction without just cause and can file a Petition for Pre-Eviction Hearing. Contact 916-808-8121 or tpp@cityofsacramento.org · cityofsacramento.gov — Tenant Protection Program
  • 3-day notice to pay rent or quit (Code of Civil Procedure 1161) — the landlord must give "three days' notice, excluding Saturdays and Sundays and other judicial holidays," in writing, stating the amount due and who to pay
  • Unlawful detainer (eviction lawsuit) — since January 1, 2025 (AB 2347), a tenant has 10 days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and judicial holidays, to file a response after being served (Code of Civil Procedure 1167). Miss it and the landlord can get a default judgment. Sacramento County eviction cases are heard at the Carol Miller Justice Center, 301 Bicentennial Circle
  • Security deposit (Civil Code 1950.5) — since AB 12 took effect July 1, 2024, most landlords may not collect more than one month's rent as a deposit, with limited exceptions. The deposit must be returned within 21 days of move-out with an itemized statement
  • Source-of-income protection (Gov Code 12955) — California prohibits landlords from refusing Section 8 vouchers or other government rental assistance. This is a statewide protection that applies in Sacramento
  • Retaliation (Civil Code 1942.5) — landlords cannot retaliate against tenants for organizing or asserting their rights
  • Self-help eviction is illegal (Civil Code 789.3) — a landlord cannot shut off utilities, change the locks, or remove doors or windows to force a tenant out
  • Fair housing: the California Fair Employment and Housing Act protects beyond federal categories, including source of income, sexual orientation, gender identity, age, and ancestry

For free legal help: Legal Services of Northern California, Sacramento office, (916) 551-2150 — general intake Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday from 8:30 a.m. "or until full." The Superior Court's free Advisory Clinic at the Carol Miller Justice Center, Room 300, helps tenants without a lawyer on a walk-in basis Monday through Thursday, or call 916-875-7846. For state-level details, see our California housing resources.

Other Housing Programs in Sacramento

  • Public housing: SHRA owns and manages public-housing communities across Sacramento. Waiting lists are posted only on sacwaitlist.com
  • LIHTC (Tax Credit): Sacramento has substantial LIHTC inventory. Search HUD's LIHTC database for Sacramento County properties
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): Sacramento veterans are referred through the VA Northern California Health Care System (Mather VA)
  • Rapid Rehousing & Permanent Supportive Housing — referrals through the Coordinated Access System (dial 211)
  • CalHFA programs — California Housing Finance Agency first-time homebuyer and down-payment assistance. calhfa.ca.gov
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Sacramento SHRA Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

No. SHRA's waiting list portal says the "Tenant-Based Housing Choice Voucher Program waiting list (formerly known as the Section 8 program) is currently closed." When we checked, some project-based voucher lists and public housing lists (City Housing, County Housing, and Walnut Grove) were open, but only for some unit sizes, mostly 3 to 5 bedrooms. SHRA posts its waiting lists only on sacwaitlist.com and says there is never a fee to apply. Call SHRA at (916) 440-1390 with questions. (Checked October 28, 2026.)

What rental assistance is available in Sacramento right now?

The pandemic-era SERA program is closed; SHRA says all of its funds were disbursed as of December 11, 2024. What is left: dial 211 or (916) 498-1000 to be screened through the Coordinated Access System, which includes homelessness diversion and prevention help; families on CalWORKs, or who appear eligible, can ask the County Department of Human Assistance (1-800-560-0976) for CalWORKs Homeless Assistance, which can pay up to two months of back rent; and veterans can call Volunteers of America's SSVF program at (916) 228-3153.

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Sacramento?

Dial 211 or (916) 498-1000. 211 Sacramento answers 24/7 and is the main way into the Coordinated Access System, which makes shelter referrals; Sacramento County also tells families seeking shelter to call 211. Survivors of domestic violence can call WEAVE's support line at (916) 920-2952.

Does California protect Section 8 voucher holders in Sacramento?

Yes. California Government Code § 12955 prohibits landlords statewide from refusing Section 8 vouchers or other lawful sources of income. This applies in Sacramento. A landlord who refuses your voucher is violating state fair housing law. Report violations to the California Civil Rights Department or call Legal Services of Northern California at (916) 551-2150.

What is AB 1482 and how does it apply in Sacramento?

AB 1482, the Tenant Protection Act, caps yearly rent increases on covered units at 5 percent plus the change in the cost of living, or 10 percent, whichever is lower. After a tenant has lived in the home for 12 months, the landlord needs a just cause to end the tenancy; SB 567 (2024) tightened the owner move-in and remodel rules but kept the 12-month requirement. Inside city limits, the City of Sacramento's Tenant Protection Program covers most multifamily rentals: the maximum increase is 8.6% from July 1, 2026, and tenants who have lived there more than 12 months can petition for a pre-eviction hearing. Call 916-808-8121.

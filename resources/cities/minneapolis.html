Affordable Housing in Minneapolis, MN

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Minneapolis and Hennepin County, not generic Section 8 advice. Minneapolis stands out for layered tenant protections: a 30-day pre-eviction notice rule, a right to a lawyer in eviction cases for low-income renters, and state protection for renters who use rental assistance. The named resources below are where to start.

Quick numbers to write down:
  • 211 (United Way of Minnesota): dial 211 (free, 24/7) for any housing emergency in Hennepin County
  • Minneapolis Public Housing Authority (MPHA): 612-342-1400 · 1001 N Washington Ave · mphaonline.org · waitlist email hcvwaitinglist@mplspha.org
  • Hennepin County Adult Shelter Connect: 612-248-2350
  • Tubman Center (DV 24-hr): (612) 825-0000

Emergency Help Tonight in Minneapolis

If you need a safe place to sleep tonight or are facing an imminent eviction, these are the local resources to contact first:

  • Hennepin County Adult Shelter Connect: single intake line for adult shelter beds in Hennepin County: 612-248-2350. Operates the coordinated entry system
  • St. Stephen's Human Services: operates several shelters and outreach programs across Minneapolis, including drop-in centers
  • Catholic Charities of St. Paul and Minneapolis, Higher Ground Minneapolis: large emergency shelter and pay-for-stay options downtown. Also operates Exodus Residence and other facilities
  • People Serving People: Minneapolis's largest family shelter, with case management and child-focused services
  • Salvation Army Harbor Light Center: emergency shelter for adults, including addiction recovery programs
  • Simpson Housing Services: emergency shelter and supportive housing operator with multiple programs
  • Tubman: domestic violence shelter and 24-hour crisis line: (612) 825-0000. Bilingual advocates and culturally-specific services
  • Mary's Place: emergency shelter for families experiencing homelessness
  • 211 Minnesota: free 24/7 information line for shelters, food, financial assistance, and social services. Multilingual

For a full walkthrough of finding shelter the first night, see our emergency housing tonight guide.

Section 8 in Minneapolis: MPHA Status and How to Apply

Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers in Minneapolis are administered by the Minneapolis Public Housing Authority (MPHA), HUD code MN002, which manages over 7,000 vouchers. Current status (checked November 3, 2026):

  • The HCV waitlist is closed. MPHA: "The Housing Choice Voucher waitlist is currently closed for new applications," with no reopening date posted. Watch mphaonline.org/housing-choice-vouchers/tenant-based-vouchers for announcements. MPHA's separate, state-funded Bring It Home voucher list took applications only October 13–15, 2026 and is now closed; applicants were to see their status in RentCafe at portal.mphaonline.org by November 1, 2026
  • Eligibility: 50% or below Area Median Income (AMI) for Hennepin County
  • If you're already on the waitlist: complete the Section 8 HCV Waitlist Update Form and email hcvwaitinglist@mplspha.org, fax to 612-335-4427, or mail to MPHA HCV Waiting List Update, 1001 Washington Ave No. Suite 310 Minneapolis, MN 55401
  • Public Housing through MPHA is a separate program with its own lists. As of November 3, 2026, MPHA's portal lists its high-rise studio and one-bedroom lists for Near Elderly (50–61), Elderly (62+) and Disabled applicants as "Open"; near-elderly and disabled applicants pre-apply online, and seniors apply in person at 1001 Washington Ave. N. Family housing (2–5 bedrooms) is "Closed"
  • Project-Based Voucher (PBV) units: MPHA says its PBV program gives owners "access to a list of potential residents through MPHA's Housing Choice Voucher waiting list," so do not count on a PBV building having its own open list; ask the building
  • Other special programs: HUD-VASH for veterans, Mainstream vouchers for non-elderly people with disabilities, each with a separate referral process
  • Apply to neighboring authorities too: Metro HRA (Metropolitan Council; suburban Anoka, Carver, Hennepin and Ramsey counties, not Minneapolis. Its voucher page said "The waitlist is currently closed" on November 3, 2026), the St. Paul PHA, and suburban HRAs such as Bloomington, Richfield and Plymouth run separate lists
  • Status check: MPHA's Tenant-Based Vouchers page gives 612-335-4404 (have the head of household's Social Security number and date of birth ready), or use the email/address above. HousingLink also maintains a current Open Housing Authority Wait List directory at housinglink.org

Our full guide to MPHA: Minneapolis Public Housing Authority (MPHA): waitlist status, contact and how to apply. For the national application process, see our step-by-step Section 8 guide and how to find your PHA.

Emergency Rental Assistance in Minneapolis (Named Programs)

If you're behind on rent or can't pay this month, these are the local programs currently operating in Minneapolis. Funding shifts month to month, so always call to confirm current availability:

  • City of Minneapolis Emergency Rental Assistance: the City says "Minneapolis residents at risk of eviction can get help paying rent and utilities." Eligibility: rental unit in Minneapolis, household income at or below 30% AMI (counting every adult), and the 30-day pre-eviction notice from your landlord. Apply through a City partner such as Tenant Resource Connections (612-767-9737); the City cannot take applications itself (checked November 5, 2026). Our Minneapolis emergency rent help guide lists every partner. minneapolismn.gov
  • RentHelp Hennepin: Hennepin County's rent help portal, for renters with a pre-eviction notice or a court filing; "Funds are not guaranteed." Call or text 211 for help applying, or call the Tenant Resource Connection at 612-767-9737 (checked November 5, 2026). hennepincounty.gov
  • Hennepin County Emergency Assistance: emergency help for a "financial crisis that poses a direct threat to their physical health or safety," such as damage deposits and utility shut-off prevention. Apply at MNbenefits or call 612-596-1300 for help applying. hennepincounty.gov
  • Lutheran Social Service of Minnesota, Rental Assistance & Eviction Protection: combines rental help with case management and tenant counseling. lssmn.org
  • Catholic Charities of St. Paul and Minneapolis: emergency financial assistance, food, immigration legal services, and case management. Spanish, Somali, and other language services
  • Salvation Army Northern Division: eviction prevention assistance and utility help
  • St. Vincent de Paul of Greater Minneapolis: one-time emergency rental and utility help through local parish conferences
  • Mid-Minnesota Legal Aid Housing Unit: free legal help for low-income tenants facing eviction. mylegalaid.org
  • HOME Line: free tenant hotline and counseling for Minnesota renters. Call 612-728-5767 (Twin Cities) or 866-866-3546 (greater MN). homelinemn.org

The federal pandemic RentHelpMN has ended

Minnesota's RentHelpMN program from the pandemic era has closed. The current paths are the new 2026 City of Minneapolis ERA, Rent Help Hennepin, and the named nonprofits above. Don't waste time on old 2021–2023 application portals.

Utility assistance: EAP

Minnesota's LIHEAP is called the Energy Assistance Program (EAP). It is administered locally by community action agencies; in Hennepin County, by Community Action Partnership of Hennepin County (CAP-HC). For winter 2026–2027, Commerce says the deadline to apply is May 31, 2027. Apply through CAP-HC or call 211.

Tenant Rights in Minneapolis & Minnesota

Minnesota has been strengthening tenant protections, and Minneapolis adds local layers on top of state law:

  • Source-of-income protection is state law: the Minnesota Human Rights Act protects "status with regard to public assistance," which includes "being a tenant receiving federal, state, or local subsidies, including rental assistance or rent supplements" (Minn. Stat. § 363A.03, subd. 47), so a landlord cannot turn you away because you use a Section 8 voucher or other rental subsidy. Minneapolis had its own local ordinance going back to 2017. File a discrimination complaint with the Minnesota Department of Human Rights at mn.gov/mdhr
  • Minneapolis 30-day pre-eviction notice: the City says that for unpaid rent "The property owner must send the notice at least 30 days before they start the eviction process," listing the fees due, the timeline for paying and who you can pay (checked November 5, 2026). This is longer than the state's 14-day pre-filing notice and gives tenants time to apply for emergency rental assistance
  • 14-day notice before filing for nonpayment under state law (Minn. Stat. § 504B.321, subd. 1a): the landlord must give written notice of the total due and that an eviction can be filed if you do not pay or move out within 14 days; this is the state minimum under Minneapolis's 30-day rule. Separately, you can stop a nonpayment eviction by paying the rent owed with interest, costs and a $5 attorney fee before you are removed (§ 504B.291)
  • Notice to end month-to-month: one full rental period from either side (Minn. Stat. § 504B.135)
  • Security deposit: must be returned with interest within 21 days of move-out (Minn. Stat. § 504B.178). Minnesota does not cap deposit amounts but deposits must accrue interest
  • Warranty of habitability: Minn. Stat. § 504B.161 requires landlords to maintain fit-and-habitable premises, comply with health and safety codes, and make repairs after written notice. Tenants have rent-escrow remedies
  • Retaliatory eviction is illegal under Minn. Stat. § 504B.285 within 90 days of good-faith tenant complaints or organizing activity
  • Self-help eviction is illegal: Minn. Stat. § 504B.225 makes it a misdemeanor for a landlord to intentionally lock out or remove a tenant, or to cut off electric, heat, gas or water to force a tenant out
  • Eviction sealing/expungement: Minnesota law allows tenants to petition for expungement of eviction filings in many circumstances, especially nonpayment cases where the tenant paid or settled. Mid-Minnesota Legal Aid and HOME Line can help
  • Fair housing: Minnesota Human Rights Act protects more classes than federal law, including source of income, marital status, public assistance status, sexual orientation, gender identity, and creed

For free legal help: Mid-Minnesota Legal Aid, HOME Line (tenant hotline 612-728-5767), and Volunteer Lawyers Network. For state-level details, see our Minnesota housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Other Housing Programs in Minneapolis

  • Public housing: MPHA owns about 6,200 units of public housing in Minneapolis, including the Cedar-Riverside high-rises and many scattered-site homes. Application is separate from Section 8
  • LIHTC (Tax Credit): privately owned income-restricted apartments. Minneapolis has substantial LIHTC inventory. Search HUD's LIHTC database for properties in Hennepin County. See how to find LIHTC housing
  • HousingLink: Minnesota's go-to subsidized-housing search portal listing open waitlists, vacant units, and program information. housinglink.org
  • Minneapolis Inclusionary Zoning Policy: new market-rate developments must include affordable units; the city's portal lists current openings
  • HUD-VASH (veterans): combines a voucher with VA case management. Minneapolis-area veterans are referred through the Minneapolis VA Health Care System. See how to apply for HUD-VASH
  • Rapid Rehousing & Permanent Supportive Housing: coordinated through the Hennepin County CoC. Access via Adult Shelter Connect (612-248-2350) or 211
  • Minnesota Housing (state) programs: Start Up homebuyer assistance, Step Up move-up loans, and statewide rental development financing. mnhousing.gov
  • HUD-approved housing counseling: find a counselor through the HUD counselor locator. Model Cities and Twin Cities Habitat for Humanity offer counseling

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Minneapolis Section 8 waitlist open in 2026?

No. As of November 3, 2026, the Minneapolis Public Housing Authority (MPHA) says "The Housing Choice Voucher waitlist is currently closed for new applications," and it posts no reopening date. MPHA's separate state-funded Bring It Home voucher list took applications only October 13 to 15, 2026 and is now closed. What is open: MPHA's high-rise public housing lists for people 50 and older and for people with disabilities, at portal.mphaonline.org. If you're already on the Section 8 list, update your contact info via hcvwaitinglist@mplspha.org or by fax (612-335-4427), or check your status at 612-335-4404. HousingLink at housinglink.org maintains a directory of open Minnesota housing authority waitlists.

Where can I get emergency rental assistance in Minneapolis?

The City of Minneapolis Emergency Rental Assistance helps Minneapolis renters with household income at or below 30% of the area median income who have their landlord's 30-day pre-eviction notice. You apply through a City partner such as Tenant Resource Connections at 612-767-9737; the City cannot take applications itself. RentHelp Hennepin takes applications from renters with a pre-eviction notice or a court filing, and funds are not guaranteed. For free legal advice, call HOME Line at 612-728-5767.

What is Minneapolis's 30-day pre-eviction notice?

For unpaid rent, the City of Minneapolis says the property owner must send a notice at least 30 days before starting the eviction process, listing the fees due, the timeline for paying and who you can pay. Minnesota law separately requires a written notice at least 14 days before filing. With the notice in hand, you can apply to the City's Emergency Rental Assistance through a partner group and to RentHelp Hennepin. If you did not get the notice, the City says to get legal advice from HOME Line or Mid-Minnesota Legal Aid.

Can a Minneapolis landlord refuse my Section 8 voucher?

No. The Minnesota Human Rights Act protects "status with regard to public assistance," which includes tenants receiving federal, state, or local rental subsidies (Minn. Stat. § 363A.03, subd. 47), so it is illegal anywhere in Minnesota for a landlord to refuse a tenant because of a Section 8 voucher or other rental subsidy. Minneapolis had a local ordinance going back to 2017. File a discrimination complaint with the Minnesota Department of Human Rights at mn.gov/mdhr or contact HOME Line for help.

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Minneapolis?

Call Hennepin County Adult Shelter Connect at 612-248-2350. That's the single intake line for adult shelter beds in Hennepin County. People Serving People is the largest family shelter. Catholic Charities operates Higher Ground Minneapolis and other shelters. Salvation Army Harbor Light, Simpson Housing, and St. Stephen's Human Services also run shelters. Tubman has a 24-hour DV crisis line at (612) 825-0000.

Affordable Housing in Salt Lake City, UT

Waythrough Project · Updated November 2026

This page collects the specific programs, agencies, phone numbers, and rules that apply in Salt Lake City and Salt Lake County, Utah, not generic Section 8 advice. Three things to know: Salt Lake City has two separate housing authorities (HASLC for the city, Housing Connect for the county) that share one Section 8 waiting list, which is closed; no City, County or state rent fund is open right now; and Utah law protects voucher holders from source-of-income discrimination. Utah evictions are fast: 3 business days on a rent notice and 3 business days to answer in court, so act the day you get a notice. The named resources below are where to start.

Section 8 in Salt Lake City: HASLC Status and How to Apply

Salt Lake City has two housing authorities, and they are separate agencies. The Housing Authority of Salt Lake City (HASLC), HUD code UT004, serves the city. Housing Connect (formerly the Housing Authority of the County of Salt Lake), HUD code UT003, serves Salt Lake County. They share one waiting list for regular Section 8 vouchers. Current status (checked November 5, 2026):

  • The shared Section 8 list is closed. HASLC says "HASLC and Housing Connect (Salt Lake County Housing Authority) have a combined tenant-based HCV program waitlist." That list, the "Section 8 Waiting List – Salt Lake County Consolidated," sits on AffordableHousing.com with the Mainstream and Non-Elderly Disabled lists, and HASLC's waitlists page says: "All waiting lists managed on AffordableHousing.com are currently closed to new applicants"
  • How often it opens: HASLC says the voucher list "is often closed and only opens every several years because of funding limits." When it opens, one application puts you on the list for both agencies
  • Project-based and public housing lists: HASLC runs a separate Applicant Portal for project-based voucher buildings (The Aster, Atkinson Stacks/Sunrise Metro, Capitol Homes, Lake Street, Rendon Terrace, SPARK, Taylor Springs and Wasatch Manor) and for its two public housing buildings, Phillips Plaza and Romney Plaza. Those two are "for senior and/or disabled households only." The portal says "Register here if you would like to be added to the project-based voucher waiting list," but HASLC does not post an open-or-closed status for these lists. Call to confirm
  • Housing Connect's own lists are closed: Housing Connect lists its voucher, project-based, RAD and public housing programs and says "All of these programs' waiting lists are currently closed and are not accepting applications." Office: 3595 South Main Street, South Salt Lake, (801) 284-4400
  • HASLC's office: 1776 S. West Temple, Salt Lake City, UT 84115, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Phone 801-487-2161, TTY 801-487-3361
  • Special programs: HUD-VASH vouchers come by referral from the VA Salt Lake City Health Care System, not from a waiting list. Family Unification vouchers come by referral through DCFS
  • Already on a list? HASLC says to "check your status and, if applicable, update your contact information on AffordableHousing.com" and warns that "Failure to keep your information current may result in being removed from the waiting list"

The honest picture: no City, County or state rent fund is open right now (checked November 5, 2026). These are the doors that are left. Always call to confirm:

  • Utah Community Action (UCA) Case Management & Housing: rent or deposit help for a short-term crisis. UCA says "Clients must have a current lease in place or be able to sign a lease," and it pays the landlord directly. Apply online or call (801) 359-2444, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. UCA says appointments are about two weeks out. utahca.org
  • UCA Landlord-Tenant Mediation: for tenants who got a 3-day pay-or-vacate notice: (801) 214-3109 or mediation@utahca.org. UCA does not give legal representation
  • Salt Lake City Tenant Resource Center: run with the Community Development Corporation of Utah at 501 East 1700 South, 801-893-3779, Monday to Thursday 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. and Friday 9 a.m. to noon. Its Relocation Assistance for Tenants helps renters forced to move by repairs, demolition or changes to income limits. It is not a back-rent fund. slc.gov/can/renters
  • Salt Lake City Housing Stability Division: 801-535-7712, 451 S. State St. #445. Its pages list no rent fund for tenants
  • Salt Lake County: says it "does not provide direct affordable housing services" and refers renters to the Tenant Resource Center, the two housing authorities, 211 and Utah Community Action. Its 2024 eviction-prevention program ran January 1 to December 31, 2024 and has ended

The pandemic rent programs have ended

Utah Rent Relief and Salt Lake County's COVID-era rent program closed on February 5, 2023. Ignore old flyers and links that send you to them.

Utility assistance: HEAT, Rocky Mountain Power and Enbridge Gas

Utah's HEAT program (LIHEAP) helps households at or below 150% of the federal poverty level. For 2026–27, households with someone 60 or older, someone with a disability, or a child under 6 can apply from October 1; everyone else from November 1. Both run to September 30, 2027 "or when federal LIHEAP funds are exhausted." In Salt Lake County, Utah Community Action takes HEAT applications: call (801) 359-2444 or apply through myCase. If HEAT approves you, Rocky Mountain Power's HELP program can add a monthly bill credit, and Enbridge Gas adds a one-time $86 credit (checked November 5, 2026).

Tenant Rights in Salt Lake City & Utah

Utah law moves fast and favors landlords in court, but it does protect voucher holders. Salt Lake City's renter pages rely on state law and add no local tenant ordinance beyond rental licensing:

  • Statewide source-of-income protection: Utah's Fair Housing Act lists "source of income" as a protected class (Utah Code 57-21-5) and defines it to include "being a tenant receiving federal, state, or local subsidies, including rental assistance or rent supplements" (57-21-2). Some small owners are exempt (57-21-3). Complaints go to the Utah Antidiscrimination & Labor Division, 801-530-6800
  • 3 business days for unpaid rent: a tenant is in unlawful detainer only if a written pay-or-vacate notice "has remained uncomplied with for a period of three business days after service" (Utah Code 78B-6-802(1)(c))
  • 3 business days to answer: the summons gives you "three business days from the date of service" to respond (78B-6-807(3)(a)). If you answer, either side can ask for a hearing within 10 business days (78B-6-810(2))
  • Losing is expensive: the court "shall enter the judgment against the defendant for the rent and for three times the amount of the damages assessed" and awards attorney fees to the winner (78B-6-811)
  • Notice to end month-to-month: at least 15 calendar days before the end of the rental period (78B-6-802(1)(b))
  • Security deposit: no cap; the landlord must return it with a written list of deductions within 30 days of move-out (Utah Code 57-17-3)
  • Rent control is prohibited statewide (Utah Code 57-20-1)
  • Rental licensing: Salt Lake City requires a business license for residential rentals, and you can ask the City for an inspection if your home is unsafe

For free legal help: Utah Legal Services (801-328-8891, intake 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. weekdays) handles evictions for low-income tenants. For state-level details, see our Utah housing resources. If you experience discrimination, see how to file a housing discrimination complaint.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Salt Lake City Section 8 waitlist open right now?

No. The Housing Authority of Salt Lake City (HASLC) and Housing Connect share one tenant-based voucher list, and HASLC says every list it runs on its outside application site is "currently closed to new applicants." That covers the Salt Lake County Consolidated Section 8 list and the Mainstream and Non-Elderly Disabled lists. Housing Connect says its waiting lists "are currently closed and are not accepting applications." HASLC says the voucher list "only opens every several years because of funding limits." HASLC's project-based and public housing lists are on a separate Applicant Portal with no posted open-or-closed status, so call HASLC at 801-487-2161 to ask. Watch haslcutah.org and housingconnect.org for the next opening. (Checked November 5, 2026.)

Can a Salt Lake City landlord refuse my Section 8 voucher?

Usually not. Utah's Fair Housing Act lists source of income as a protected class (Utah Code 57-21-5), and it defines source of income to include rental assistance or rent supplements (57-21-2). That covers a Housing Choice Voucher. Some small owners are exempt, such as an owner who rents out a single-family home without a real estate agent and owns fewer than four (57-21-3). If you are refused, file a complaint with the Utah Antidiscrimination and Labor Division at 801-530-6800.

Where can I get emergency rental assistance in Salt Lake City?

No City, County or state rent fund is open right now. Utah Rent Relief closed on February 5, 2023, and Salt Lake County's 2024 eviction-prevention program ended on December 31, 2024. The main door left is Utah Community Action's case management, which can help with rent or a deposit after a short-term crisis if you have a lease or can sign one. Call (801) 359-2444, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., or apply online at utahca.org. If you got a 3-day notice, also call UCA's mediation line at (801) 214-3109. Dial 211 for anything else open this month. (Checked November 5, 2026.)

Where can I get emergency shelter tonight in Salt Lake City?

Call the Homeless Hotline at 801-990-9999. Salt Lake City says to call it if you or someone you know is experiencing or at risk of homelessness. The resource centers are the Gail Miller Resource Center for men and women (242 Paramount Ave), the Geraldine E. King Women's Resource Center (131 E 700 S) and the Pamela Atkinson Men's Resource Center (3380 S 1000 W, South Salt Lake). YWCA Utah shelters domestic violence survivors and their families at (801) 537-8604.

How fast is an eviction in Utah?

Very fast. For unpaid rent, the landlord's written notice gives you 3 business days to pay or move out (Utah Code 78B-6-802). If you are then served with court papers, you have 3 business days to answer (78B-6-807). If you answer, either side can ask for a hearing within 10 business days. If you do not answer or do not show up, the court will usually order you out. After an order of restitution, you have 3 calendar days to leave (78B-6-812). If you lose, the court must award the rent plus three times the damages, and the landlord's attorney fees (78B-6-811). Call Utah Legal Services at 801-328-8891 the day you get a notice.

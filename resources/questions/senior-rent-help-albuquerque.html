Reader Question

What rent help is there for a senior on Social Security in Albuquerque?

Asked October 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“I'm a senior living on Social Security in Albuquerque, and health problems keep me from working. What programs can help me with rent, other than apartments in bad areas? I only need help with rent.”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

Ongoing rent help usually comes from a Section 8 voucher, which you can use for a house, duplex or apartment in a neighborhood you choose, if the landlord accepts it. The Albuquerque Housing Authority is taking Section 8 pre-applications online only through November 15, 2026, so apply now.

Act before November 15, 2026

The Albuquerque Housing Authority (AHA) is taking Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher pre-applications online only, October 16 through November 15, 2026 (we checked AHA's How to Apply page on November 4, 2026). There are no paper applications or walk-ins, and AHA does not charge to apply. AHA picks applicants by lottery, so applying on the first day doesn't put you ahead, but you must apply before the window closes. See our AHA page for how the lottery works.

After November 15, 2026, the Section 8 window is closed. On the same AHA page, use "Be Notified When You Can Apply" to hear about the next window, and turn to the other options below.

Why a voucher fits what you asked

A voucher isn't tied to one apartment complex. You can rent a house, duplex, townhome or apartment anywhere the landlord accepts it, as long as the unit passes inspection and the rent fits AHA's limits. When you get a voucher, AHA must explain the advantages of lower-poverty areas and give you a list of landlords that includes those areas (24 CFR 982.301). You'd usually pay about 30% of your adjusted income. Households whose head or spouse is 62 or older, or has a disability, get an extra yearly deduction, plus a deduction for medical costs above 10% of income (24 CFR 5.611). That can lower your share.

Other ways to get rent help

  1. Project-based vouchers. AHA lists its Project Based Voucher program as open until further notice. The help comes with specific buildings, and some are for older adults.
  2. Section 202 senior housing. HUD's Section 202 program funds rental housing for very low-income households with someone 62 or older, with rent based on income. Ask each property about its waitlist.
  3. Tax credit (LIHTC) apartments. Albuquerque has several senior-only tax credit properties with lower rents.
  4. The Area Agency on Aging. The City of Albuquerque's Senior Information Line, 505-764-6400, connects older adults in Bernalillo County to senior services.
  5. Short-term crisis help. If you fall behind, the City's Emergency Rental & Utility Assistance Program may help once. It isn't ongoing rent help. See our Albuquerque rent help guide.

What to write

If your health affects what you need in a home or how you apply, you can ask for a reasonable accommodation, such as a ground-floor unit or extra time, at any step:

"Because of my medical condition, I'm asking for a reasonable accommodation: [what you need]. I can provide a letter from my doctor."

If you miss the window

Apply to senior properties and project-based units now, sign up for AHA's notices, and call 211 New Mexico for other programs. Keep your phone number and email current with every list you join, so you don't miss a letter.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

Reader Question

Where can I find private landlords who accept housing subsidies?

Asked September 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“Where can I find private landlords who accept housing subsidies like Section 8?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

Start with your housing authority: federal rules require it to give voucher holders a list of landlords or other resources that can help. Then look at tax credit (LIHTC) apartments, which can't turn you down just for having a voucher, and check whether your state or city bans source-of-income discrimination.

What the rules say

When a housing authority gives you a voucher, it must also give you an information packet. Under 24 CFR 982.301(b)(11), that packet has to include a list of landlords who may be willing to rent to you, or other resources such as websites and groups that help with the search. The list must cover areas outside high-poverty neighborhoods. If someone in your household has a disability, the housing authority must also share the accessible units it knows about (24 CFR 982.301(b)(12)).

No federal law makes every private landlord take vouchers. But two rules help:

  • Tax credit (LIHTC) apartments. Owners who get Low-Income Housing Tax Credits must agree not to refuse a tenant just because the tenant has a Section 8 voucher (26 U.S.C. 42(h)(6)(B)(iv)). They can still screen you in other ways, like rental history.
  • Source-of-income laws. Many states and cities make it illegal to turn someone down because they pay with a voucher. Where those laws apply, a "no Section 8" ad is not allowed. These laws vary a lot, so check your state's rules.

What to do now

  1. Ask your housing authority for its landlord list and any search website it uses. Ask if it has a housing search helper or a mobility program.
  2. Search tax credit apartments. Our LIHTC guide shows how to find them by city. Call each one and ask about openings and waitlists.
  3. Use rental listing sites, then call. Many let you filter for "accepts vouchers." Call before you visit and ask directly.
  4. Try smaller landlords. Owners with a few units are often more flexible. Remind them the housing authority pays its share of the rent straight to them every month.
  5. Ask 211 and local housing counselors for landlords who already rent to voucher holders.
  6. Keep a search log: every place you contact, the date, and what they said. If your voucher is close to expiring, this log helps when you ask for an extension.

What to say when you call

"Hi, I have a Housing Choice Voucher for a [number]-bedroom unit. Do you accept vouchers, and do you have anything open? The housing authority pays its part of the rent directly to you each month."

If a landlord says no because of the voucher

Write down the name, the date and exactly what they said, and save the ad. If your state or city has a source-of-income law, you can file a complaint with your state or local fair housing agency. If the refusal seemed tied to race, disability, having children or another protected trait, you can also file with HUD under the Fair Housing Act. See how to file a discrimination complaint. And keep going: one landlord's no is not the end of your search.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

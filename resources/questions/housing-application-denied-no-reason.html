Reader Question

My housing application was denied, but it didn't say why. What can I do?

Asked September 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“My application said I didn't qualify for anything. What's the reason?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

You have a right to know. The housing authority must give you written notice with a brief reason for the denial and tell you how to ask for an informal review (Section 8) or an informal hearing (public housing). Ask for the reason and your file in writing, and request the review before the deadline.

What the rule says

  • Section 8 (Housing Choice Voucher): The housing authority must give you prompt notice of a denial, a brief statement of the reasons, and an explanation of how to ask for an informal review (24 CFR 982.554(a)). The review must be done by someone who didn't make or approve the original decision, and you can give your objections in writing or in person.
  • Public housing: The housing authority must promptly tell you the basis for finding you ineligible and, if you ask, give you a chance for an informal hearing (24 CFR 960.208(a)).
  • Privately owned HUD buildings (like project-based Section 8): HUD's handbook says the rejection notice must be in writing, state the specific reasons, and give you 14 days to respond in writing or ask for a meeting (HUD Handbook 4350.3, paragraph 4-9).

A notice that only says "you didn't qualify" doesn't meet these rules.

Common reasons for a denial

  • Income over the limit for your household size
  • Missing documents or an incomplete application
  • Not answering a letter or missing an appointment
  • Background screening, or money owed to a housing authority
  • Citizenship or immigration status rules
  • Not fitting any program that's open right now. Online systems that screen you for several programs at once sometimes say "you don't qualify for any" when, for example, the only open list is for seniors.

What to do now

  1. Find out who denied you: a housing authority's voucher list, public housing, or a specific building.
  2. Write and ask for the specific reason and a copy of your file, including any background or credit report they used.
  3. In the same letter, ask for an informal review or hearing. Don't wait for the reason to arrive first, because the deadline keeps running.
  4. Gather papers that answer the reason: pay stubs, benefit letters, ID, or proof that a record is wrong or not yours.
  5. Go to the review. You can bring a friend, an advocate or a lawyer.

What to write

"My application [number] was denied, but the notice did not give a reason. Please send me the specific reason in writing and a copy of my file, including any screening reports you used. I also request an informal review [or hearing] of this decision."

If they say no

If the notice had no reason or no deadline, point that out in writing, because the rules require both. If the review goes against you, you should get a written final decision with reasons. If you think you were denied because of a disability, race, or another protected trait, you can file a fair housing complaint. Free legal aid can help you decide what to do. Keep applying to other open lists while you wait.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

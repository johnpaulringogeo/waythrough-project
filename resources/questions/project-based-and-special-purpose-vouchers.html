Reader Question

How do project-based vouchers and special purpose vouchers work?

Asked November 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“How do project-based vouchers and special purpose vouchers work?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

A project-based voucher is tied to one apartment instead of to you; you usually pay about 30% of your adjusted income, and after a year you can usually ask for a voucher that moves with you. Special purpose vouchers are Section 8 vouchers set aside for certain groups, like homeless veterans, families and youth involved with foster care, and adults with disabilities, and most come through a partner agency's referral.

Project-based vouchers (PBV)

A housing authority (PHA) signs a contract with a building owner to help certain units. When you move into one of those units, you get help with rent much like regular Section 8: you usually pay about 30% of your adjusted income, and the subsidy covers the rest.

  • Where you apply: often on a separate waiting list from the regular voucher list, sometimes one for each building. These lists can move faster.
  • If you move out: the help stays with the unit. But after one year you can ask the PHA for a voucher that moves with you, and if none is available you get priority for the next one (24 CFR 983.261).
  • Not the same as HUD “project-based Section 8” buildings, where a private owner has a contract directly with HUD and you apply at the building's office.

Special purpose vouchers

These are Housing Choice Vouchers that Congress set aside for certain groups. Once you have one, it works much like a regular voucher. What's different is how you get one.

  • HUD-VASH is for veterans experiencing homelessness. The VA decides who is referred and gives you a case manager. Start at a VA medical center or call the National Call Center for Homeless Veterans at 1-877-424-3838.
  • Family Unification Program (FUP) helps families when not having adequate housing is a main reason a child may go into foster care, or can't come home from it. It also helps youth ages 18 to 24 who have left foster care (or will leave within 90 days) and who were homeless or at risk of homelessness at age 16 or older. The public child welfare agency makes the referral. Youth vouchers are usually limited to 36 months, with up to 24 more months in some cases; family vouchers have no time limit.
  • Mainstream vouchers are for households that include a person with a disability who is 18 to 61. HUD aims them at people leaving institutions, at risk of going into one, or homeless or at risk. Many PHAs fill them from their regular list, so ask whether yours has them.
  • Emergency Housing Vouchers (EHV) are ending. PHAs stopped giving new ones to families in 2025. Families who were leased up with an EHV on September 30, 2026 are being moved to regular vouchers by December 31, 2026, without going back on a waiting list or being rescreened (HUD Notice PIH 2026-25). If you have an EHV, answer any letters from your PHA quickly.

Some PHAs also have other set-aside vouchers, such as Foster Youth to Independence (FYI) or vouchers for non-elderly people with disabilities. Ask your PHA what it has.

What to do now

  1. Ask your PHA: “Do you have project-based units or special purpose vouchers, and how do I get on those lists?”
  2. If you're a veteran without stable housing, contact the VA first. If you're a young adult who was in foster care, or a parent working with child welfare, ask your caseworker about FUP or FYI.
  3. If someone in your household has a disability and is under 62, ask about Mainstream vouchers and whether a local agency makes referrals.
  4. Apply to every list you qualify for. Being on one list doesn't hurt your place on another.

What to write

“Please tell me whether you have project-based voucher units or special purpose vouchers (HUD-VASH, FUP, FYI or Mainstream), whether any of those lists are open, and how I can apply or be referred.”

If they say no

Not every PHA has every program. If yours doesn't, ask which nearby PHAs do, and call 211 to find the local VA, child welfare or disability agencies that make referrals.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

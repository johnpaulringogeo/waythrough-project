Reader Question

Can I send my housing appeal by email, or does it have to be mailed?

Asked June 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“Can I send my appeal by email, or does it have to go by mail?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

It depends on your housing authority. Your denial or termination letter should say how and by when to ask for a review or hearing. If email is allowed, use it, and also send a copy by certified mail or hand-deliver one so you can prove the date.

What the rule says

Federal rules require the housing authority to tell you, in writing, why it made its decision and how to ask for a review. If you applied for a Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) and were denied, the notice must say you can request an informal review and explain how to get one (24 CFR 982.554). If you already have a voucher and the housing authority wants to end it, the notice must give the reasons and the deadline to ask for an informal hearing (24 CFR 982.555).

The federal rules don't say whether your request must be mailed, emailed or handed in. Each housing authority sets its own steps in a written policy called the Administrative Plan (for public housing, it's usually called the Admissions and Continued Occupancy Policy). So the answer is in your letter or in that policy.

What to do now

  1. Find the deadline. Look at your letter for the due date and how to send your request. Deadlines are often short, sometimes only 10 to 14 days.
  2. If the letter doesn't say how, call the housing authority and ask, "Can I send my request by email? To which address?" Write down the person's name, the date and what they said.
  3. Send it the way the letter says. If email is allowed, send it and ask for a reply confirming they got it.
  4. Back it up. Also send a copy by certified mail with a return receipt, or hand-deliver it and ask for a date-stamped copy. That way you have proof even if an email gets lost.
  5. Keep copies of everything: the letter, your request, the sent email and the mail receipt.

What to write

"I am requesting an informal review [or hearing] of the decision in your letter dated [date] about [my application / my voucher]. My application or client number is [number]. Please confirm you received this request and tell me the date of the review. I also ask to see my file before the review."

Keep it short. You don't have to argue your whole case in the request. You can explain at the review, bring papers, and bring someone to help you.

If they won't take email or the deadline passed

  • If they won't take email, mail or hand-deliver your request right away. Don't wait for them to answer your email.
  • If the deadline already passed, send your request anyway. Explain why it's late (for example, the letter came late or you were in the hospital) and ask them to accept it. Some housing authorities accept late requests for good cause, so it's worth asking.
  • If a disability made it hard to meet the deadline or to use the mail, you can ask for more time or another way to send it as a reasonable accommodation.
  • If they still refuse, contact free legal aid quickly. Legal aid can sometimes get a review reopened.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

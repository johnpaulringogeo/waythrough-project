Reader Question

How can the sheriff lock me out when I never got notice from the court?

Asked November 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 8, 2026

The question

“How can the sheriff come and lock me out of my home when I never got anything from the court saying I was being evicted?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

In almost every state, a sheriff can only lock you out with a court order, and that order comes after a landlord wins an eviction case. So there is most likely a case on file. Court papers are often posted on the door or mailed, and people miss them. Today, get copies from the court clerk and ask legal aid about setting aside the judgment and stopping the writ.

Why the sheriff is involved

In almost every state, a sheriff, marshal or constable can only carry out a lockout with a court order. That order usually comes after the landlord wins an eviction case, and it goes by different names: writ of possession, writ of restitution, writ of execution. So if the sheriff is coming, there is most likely a case on file with your name on it, even if you never saw a single paper.

This happens more than people think. Many states let the court papers be posted on your door, mailed, or both, instead of handed to you. Papers blow away, get thrown out with the junk mail, or go to an old address. If you don't answer or show up by the deadline, the landlord usually gets a default judgment, which means they win because you weren't there. Then they ask the court for the writ and hand it to the sheriff.

In California, for example, the sheriff posts a Notice to Vacate that gives you 5 days to move before the lockout. Other states give more time or less. Wherever you live, the window is short, so start today.

What to do today

  1. Call or go to the court clerk. Find the court that handles evictions in your county (it may be called landlord-tenant, housing, district or civil court). Give your name and address and ask whether there is an eviction case. Ask for copies of the summons and complaint, the proof of service (the form that says how and when you were "served"), the judgment and the writ. Read the proof of service closely. If it says someone handed you papers on a day you weren't home, or it lists the wrong address, write that down.
  2. Call legal aid and say: "I never got notice of the eviction case." If you weren't served the way your state requires, a lawyer can ask the judge to set aside (cancel) the default judgment and to stay the writ, which means stop or delay it while the judge looks at the case. Many courts have self-help centers with forms for this, usually called a motion to set aside default judgment and a motion to stay the writ. The names differ by state. Asking to set aside the judgment doesn't stop the lockout by itself, so ask for both. In California you only get one chance at a set aside, so get help with it if you possibly can.
  3. If the lockout hasn't happened yet, bring the notice the sheriff posted when you go to the clerk or to legal aid. It shows the lockout date and usually the case number, so everyone can see how much time is left.
  4. If you're already locked out, call the sheriff's office or the landlord and ask how long you have to get your belongings and how to set up a time. Each state sets its own rule, and in some it's only a few days. Ask in writing too (a text or email works) so you have a record.
  5. If you have Section 8, tell the housing authority in writing today. Federal rules require you to give the housing authority a copy of any eviction notice from the owner (24 CFR 982.551(g)), and to tell it before you move out of the unit. An eviction can put your voucher at risk. Let your caseworker hear it from you first, and say that you're asking the court to reopen the case.

If there was no sheriff and no court order

If the landlord changed the locks, took the door off or put your things out on their own, with no sheriff and no court paper, that's a different problem. It's called an illegal or "self-help" lockout, and it's against the law in nearly every state. Call the police non-emergency line, say you were locked out without a court order, then call legal aid. Two other readers went through this: one was locked out for not being on the lease, the other after reporting mold.

What to say to the clerk

"The sheriff says I'm being locked out of [address], but I never got any court papers. Is there an eviction case under my name or this address? I need copies of the summons, the proof of service, the judgment and the writ."

If you need a place to stay tonight

Call or text 211, say you're being evicted or were just locked out, and ask for a place to stay. Then call the clerk as soon as the court opens. The papers you get there are what legal aid will ask to see first.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

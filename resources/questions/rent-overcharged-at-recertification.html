Reader Question

My rent was set too high at recertification. Does my landlord have to pay me back?

Asked October 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“At my recertification, my rent was calculated too high. I paid the higher amount for two months before it was corrected, and now my landlord won't credit my account for the overpayment. Is that legal?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

Usually not. When a rent mistake is corrected back to when it started, you are generally owed what you overpaid, as a refund or a credit. In Section 8, a landlord must return any rent you paid above your share, and in HUD-assisted buildings the owner must reimburse overpayments caused by its own error. Ask in writing, and bring in the housing authority or HUD if the landlord won't.

What the rules say

Who owes you depends on your program.

  • Section 8 voucher: The housing authority figures your share of the rent. The landlord may not accept more than that and "must immediately return any excess rent payment to the tenant" (24 CFR 982.451(b)(4)(ii)). Ask the housing authority whether it made the correction retroactive to the start date, and whether it paid the landlord its larger share for those months. If it did, the landlord was paid twice for the same rent.
  • Project-based Section 8 and other HUD Multifamily buildings (where the owner does your recertification): HUD's Occupancy Handbook says that when the owner finds it over-counted your income, it must correct the past certifications, tell you in writing how much you overpaid, and let you choose a full, immediate refund or a credit toward future rent (HUD Handbook 4350.3, paragraph 8-24). When a review finds the error, the owner must reimburse your overpayment (paragraph 8-25).
  • Public housing: The housing authority is your landlord. If it won't fix your balance, use its grievance procedure.

What to do now

  1. Gather proof: the old and corrected rent notices, receipts or bank records for the months you overpaid, and your rent ledger.
  2. Do the math: (what you paid minus the corrected rent) times the number of months.
  3. Write the landlord asking for a refund or a credit by a set date, like 14 days.
  4. Send a copy to your housing authority caseworker (voucher) or ask the manager about HUD's correction process (HUD building).
  5. Keep paying your corrected rent on time. Don't hold back rent to make up the difference without first talking to legal aid and checking your state's rules. Withholding rent can lead to an eviction case.

What to write

"My rent was corrected from $[old amount] to $[new amount], effective [date]. I paid the higher amount for [number] months, an overpayment of $[total]. Please refund this amount or credit it to my account within 14 days and send me an updated ledger. Under [24 CFR 982.451(b)(4) / HUD Handbook 4350.3, paragraph 8-24], overpaid rent must be returned to the tenant."

If they say no

  • Voucher: Ask the housing authority to enforce its contract with the owner. If the housing authority itself won't make the correction retroactive, you can ask for an informal hearing on how your rent was figured (24 CFR 982.555(a)(1)(i)).
  • HUD building: Call HUD's Multifamily Housing Complaint Line at 1-800-685-8470 (weekdays, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Eastern).
  • You may also be able to go to small claims court for the money. Free legal aid can tell you whether that makes sense in your state.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

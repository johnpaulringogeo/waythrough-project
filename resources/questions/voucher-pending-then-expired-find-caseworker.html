Reader Question

My voucher said “pending,” then “expired.” How do I find my caseworker, and what about the broken elevator?

Asked November 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 6, 2026

The question

“My housing authority's website said my voucher was pending, and about an hour later it said expired. Why? I have a disability, and I live on a high floor in a building where the elevators often stop working, and I can't get down the stairs. I also don't know who my caseworker is. How do I find out?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

Ask the housing authority in writing for your voucher's real status. If a request to approve a unit was under review, the voucher clock must pause until you get a written answer (24 CFR 982.303(c)). Because of your disability, you can also ask for more time as a reasonable accommodation (24 CFR 982.303(b)(2)). Your caseworker's name is usually on your letters or in the online portal, or the main office can tell you.

Why it may say “pending,” then “expired”

We can't see your file, so we can't say for sure. Usually “pending” means something was being reviewed. That might be a Request for Tenancy Approval (the RFTA, the form you and a landlord fill out for a unit), or a request for more time. “Expired” means the voucher's search time ran out. Websites can also show the wrong status for a while, so don't rely on the screen. Get the answer in writing.

What the rule says

  • The clock should stop during a review. The housing authority (PHA) “must provide for suspension” of the voucher term from the day you turn in a request for tenancy approval until it tells you in writing whether the request is approved or denied (24 CFR 982.303(c)). If your voucher “expired” while a request was pending, ask them to add those days back.
  • A disability can get you more time. If you need more search time because of a disability and ask for it as a reasonable accommodation, the PHA “must extend the voucher term up to the term reasonably required for that purpose” (24 CFR 982.303(b)(2)). Ask right away, even if the voucher already shows as expired.
  • An ordinary extension is up to the PHA. PHAs don't have to offer an informal review when they turn down a regular extension (24 CFR 982.554(c)(4)). A denied reasonable accommodation is different: it is a civil rights issue, and you can push back.

How to find your caseworker

Many housing authorities call this person a housing specialist, case manager or advisor. Some don't assign one at all and use a call center instead.

  1. Look at the letters you've gotten from the PHA: your voucher, your briefing packet, and any appointment or recertification notice. The caseworker's name and phone number are often at the bottom.
  2. Log in to the PHA's online portal, if it has one. Some list your caseworker's direct phone and email there. You may need your client number or Social Security number.
  3. Call the PHA's main number and ask: “Who is my caseworker, and what is the best way to reach them?” Write down the name, the date and who you spoke with.

Our guide on how to find your Section 8 caseworker has more ways to get through, including what to do if nobody answers.

The elevator

  • Tell the landlord in writing each time the elevator is out, and ask for a repair date.
  • Ask for a reasonable accommodation. Because you have a disability, you can ask for faster repairs, a move to a lower-floor unit when one opens up, or help with things like mail while the elevator is down (24 CFR 100.204).
  • If you rent with a voucher, ask the PHA for a special inspection. Under HUD's inspection standard, an elevator that doesn't work fails inspection.
  • Report it to your city. Call 311 or the building or code department. An elevator that keeps breaking down can be a code problem.
  • If you're ever stuck and it's an emergency, call 911. Ask the building manager how residents who can't use stairs get out during a fire or power outage.

What to write

“My name is [name], client number [number]. On [date] my voucher showed as pending, and then as expired the same day. I have a disability. I am asking for: (1) written confirmation of my voucher's status and expiration date; (2) if a Request for Tenancy Approval was pending, the days it was under review added back to my voucher (24 CFR 982.303(c)); and (3) an extension of my voucher as a reasonable accommodation for my disability (24 CFR 982.303(b)(2)). Please also tell me the name and phone number of my caseworker.”

Send it by email or through the portal, keep a copy, and write down the date you sent it.

If they say no

Ask for the answer and the reason in writing. Ask for the name of the PHA's reasonable accommodation or Section 504 coordinator, and ask for an informal hearing or review anyway. If they won't answer at all, call HUD's Public and Indian Housing customer service line at 800-955-2232. Free legal aid can help too; call 211 to find it.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

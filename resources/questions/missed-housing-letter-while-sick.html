Reader Question

I was too sick to answer a housing letter. Where do I stand now?

Asked July 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“I applied for housing a while ago and got a letter back, I think saying I was approved, but I was very sick at the time and couldn't respond. I don't remember exactly what it said or when. Where do I stand now?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

You may still have options. Write to the housing authority and ask for your current status. If you were removed from a waiting list because a disability or serious illness kept you from responding, you can ask to be put back as a reasonable accommodation, and for Section 8 waiting lists the rules say the housing authority must reinstate you.

What the rule says

A housing authority can remove people from its waiting list who don't answer its letters, but only under rules written in its policy (24 CFR 982.204(c)(1)). There is an important exception. If someone in your family has a disability and that disability is why you didn't respond, the housing authority must put you back in your old place on the Section 8 waiting list (24 CFR 982.204(c)(2)).

A serious illness can count as a disability under fair housing law when it greatly limits what you can do, like getting out of bed, reading mail or making calls. You don't have to share your diagnosis. A short note from a doctor or other provider saying you were not able to respond during that time is usually enough.

If the letter was an approval and you were given a voucher, it may have expired. Ask anyway. When a person with a disability needs more time to use a voucher, the housing authority must extend it as a reasonable accommodation for as long as is reasonably needed (24 CFR 982.303(b)(2)). If it already expired, ask whether they can reissue it for the same reason. Public housing and privately owned subsidized buildings also have to make reasonable accommodations under Section 504 and the Fair Housing Act.

What to do now

  1. Figure out who sent the letter. Was it a housing authority or a specific building? Check old mail, email and texts. If you applied online, log in to the applicant portal.
  2. Write and ask for your status. Give your full name, date of birth, about when you applied, any application number, and your current address and phone.
  3. Ask to be reinstated as a reasonable accommodation if you were removed or an offer expired while you were sick.
  4. Get a provider's note saying you were seriously ill and unable to respond during those dates.
  5. Send it in a way you can prove, such as email plus certified mail, and keep copies.
  6. Follow up if you don't hear back in about two weeks. Ask for a supervisor or the reasonable accommodation coordinator if you need to.
  7. Update your contact information so the next letter reaches you.

What to write

"I applied for housing around [month and year] (application number [number], if known). I received a letter around [month] but was seriously ill and could not respond. Please tell me my current status. If my application was closed or an offer expired, I ask that you reinstate me to my former place as a reasonable accommodation for my disability. A note from my provider is attached."

If they say no

  • Ask for the decision and the reason in writing. Taking you off a Section 8 waiting list counts as denying assistance, so you can ask for an informal review (24 CFR 982.552(a)(2) and 982.554). Watch the deadline in that letter.
  • If they deny your accommodation request, ask why in writing and whether a different accommodation would work.
  • You can file a fair housing complaint with HUD within one year, or get help from free legal aid.
  • In the meantime, apply to other open waiting lists so you aren't counting on just one.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

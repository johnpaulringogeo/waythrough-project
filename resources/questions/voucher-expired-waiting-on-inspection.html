Reader Question

My voucher expired while I was waiting on an inspection that never happened. Can I get it back?

Asked July 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“I waited years on the list, found a unit, and it failed inspection, so I got an extension while the property manager made the repairs. The reinspection was scheduled but never happened, and now my caseworker says my voucher expired and I have to get back on the waiting list. How can I lose my voucher over something that was out of my hands?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

You may still have time left. HUD rules say the voucher clock must stop from the day you turn in the Request for Tenancy Approval until the housing authority tells you in writing whether the unit is approved or denied (24 CFR 982.303(c)). If that pause wasn't counted, ask in writing for your expiration date to be recalculated and your voucher reinstated.

What the rule says

A voucher comes with a search term of at least 60 days (24 CFR 982.303(a)). But the clock doesn't run the whole time. The housing authority (PHA) must suspend, or pause, the term from the date you submit a Request for Tenancy Approval (the RFTA, the form you and the landlord fill out for a unit) until the date the PHA tells you in writing that the unit is approved or denied (24 CFR 982.303(c)).

In a case like yours, the days after the RFTA went in, while the unit waited for repairs and a reinspection that never took place, usually should not count against you. The exception is if the PHA sent you a written denial of that unit. If it did, the clock started again on that date, so ask to see that letter.

HUD also expects inspections to happen quickly. A smaller PHA (up to 1,250 vouchers) must inspect within 15 days of the RFTA. A larger one must inspect within a reasonable time, and within 15 days when it can (24 CFR 982.305(b)(2)). That 15-day clock pauses while the unit isn't ready to be inspected.

What to do now

  1. Write down your dates: when the voucher was issued, when the RFTA was turned in, the first inspection, any extension letter, and the reinspection that was missed.
  2. Ask the PHA for a copy of your file, including any written notice approving or denying the unit.
  3. Do the math. Count the days the voucher ran before the RFTA went in. If that is less than your full term (with any extension), you should still have time left.
  4. Send a written request to your caseworker and their supervisor. Ask them to recalculate the expiration date, reinstate the voucher, and reschedule the inspection or let you submit a new unit.
  5. At the same time, ask in writing for an informal review or hearing, and for the decision in writing. Deadlines to ask are short, often 10 to 14 days.
  6. Ask the property manager if the unit is still available, and get a short note confirming the reinspection date that was set and missed.

What to write

“My Request for Tenancy Approval was submitted on [date], and I never received a written approval or denial of the unit. Under 24 CFR 982.303(c), my voucher term was suspended during that time. Please recalculate my expiration date, reinstate my voucher, and reschedule the inspection. If you deny this, please give me the reason in writing and an informal review.”

If they say no

Contact free legal aid. A short letter from a lawyer that points to 24 CFR 982.303(c) often gets results. If you or someone in your household has a disability that played a part in the delay, you can also ask for more time as a reasonable accommodation. If you can't get the voucher back, ask whether the PHA's Administrative Plan lets you return to the waiting list with your original date or a preference, instead of starting over. And stay on any other waiting lists you're on.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

Reader Question

Can someone live with me and pay rent without being added to my voucher?

Asked July 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“Someone lives with me, but they don't want to be added to my voucher. They just want to pay their share of the rent and utilities. Is that allowed?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

No, usually not. Under Section 8 rules, everyone who lives in your unit has to be approved by the housing authority as part of your household (24 CFR 982.551(h)), and taking rent from someone who isn't approved can count as subletting. The person can be added, stay only as a short-term guest, or live somewhere else.

What the rule says

When you get a voucher, you agree to certain family obligations (24 CFR 982.551(h)):

  • The housing authority (PHA) must approve everyone who lives in your unit. You tell the PHA about a birth, adoption or court-awarded custody, and you must ask before adding anyone else.
  • No one else may live there, except a foster child or a live-in aide the PHA approved.
  • You may not sublease or rent out the unit. Taking rent from someone who lives there but isn't on the voucher can look exactly like that.

Paying a share of rent and bills doesn't make it OK. If anything, it's the clearest sign that the person lives there. If the PHA finds someone living in your unit who wasn't approved, it can end your assistance, and it may say you owe back subsidy you got because that person's income wasn't counted.

What about guests?

A guest is someone staying temporarily with your permission (24 CFR 5.100). Guests are fine. HUD doesn't set one national limit on how long a guest can stay. Your PHA's Administrative Plan and your lease set it, often a few weeks a year. Someone who gets mail at your address, keeps their belongings there or helps pay rent is usually treated as living there, not visiting.

Your choices

  1. Add them. Ask the PHA in writing. The PHA will screen the person, often with a background check, and count their income. Your share of the rent may go up, but the PHA usually still pays part. Your landlord may also need to approve them under the lease, and a bigger household can sometimes mean a bigger voucher size.
  2. Keep them a guest. Only if they really live somewhere else and stay within your PHA's guest limit.
  3. They move out. If they won't be added, they need their own place.
  4. A live-in aide. If they live there only to give care someone in your household needs because of a disability, ask the PHA to approve them as a live-in aide (24 CFR 982.316). An approved aide isn't part of the family on the voucher, and their income doesn't count toward your rent.

People often don't want to be added because of privacy or worries about their record. If that's the worry, they can ask the PHA what it checks before deciding.

What to write

“I would like to add [name] to my household. They have been staying with me since [date] [or: would like to move in on date]. Please send me the forms and tell me what you need from them.”

If they say no

A PHA can refuse to add someone, for example because of certain criminal history. Ask for the reason in writing. If the PHA won't add the person, they can't live with you while you have the voucher. If you think the refusal is wrong, or it's about a person you need for disability care, contact free legal aid.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

Reader Question

My TBRA rental assistance ended early and I'm about to lose my apartment. What can I do?

Asked September 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“My tenant-based rental assistance (TBRA) ended earlier than I expected, and my case manager didn't try to help me get it back. I have a signed lease for the whole year and couldn't find a job in time, and now I'm about to lose my apartment. I don't want to be homeless again.”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

Ask the program in writing why it ended and how to request a review, and deal with the rent right away. TBRA is short-term help, usually paid for by HUD's HOME program and run by a city, county, state or partner agency (24 CFR 92.209), so the program's written policies and your rental assistance contract decide when it can end.

What TBRA is

TBRA usually means HOME tenant-based rental assistance. A city, county or state gets HOME money from HUD and uses some of it to help people pay rent, often people leaving homelessness. It can run the program itself or hire a housing authority or nonprofit to run it (24 CFR 92.209(k)). It works like a small, short-term voucher:

  • The program signs a rental assistance contract with your landlord and you. Each contract can last up to 24 months, and it can be renewed only if HOME money is available (24 CFR 92.209(e)).
  • Each program must follow written selection policies (24 CFR 92.209(c)). Ask for them, along with any rules on ending assistance.
  • Some programs require you to take part in a self-sufficiency program, such as job training. Stopping that program is not a reason to end your assistance, though the program can refuse to renew it (24 CFR 92.209(c)(2)(iii)).

That last point may matter for you. If your help was cut before the contract's end date because you didn't find a job, ask whether that was allowed.

What to do now

  1. Find your papers: the TBRA rental assistance contract, any letter saying it ended, and your lease. Check the contract's end date.
  2. Write to the program, meaning the agency named on the contract, not only your case manager. Ask why the assistance ended, which policy they used, and how to request a review or appeal. Ask for a copy of the written TBRA policies.
  3. Ask if the assistance can be renewed or if you can reapply, and ask about other help they run, such as rapid rehousing.
  4. Talk to your landlord now. Your lease still makes you responsible for the rent. Explain what happened and ask for a payment plan while you sort it out. Get any agreement in writing.
  5. Apply for emergency rental assistance. Call 211 and your local community action agency. Many programs pay the landlord directly.
  6. If you get any eviction notice, call free legal aid that day. Don't ignore court papers, and don't move out before you get advice.
  7. If your income dropped, apply for benefits like SNAP and Medicaid, and get on Section 8 and public housing waiting lists.

What to write

“My TBRA assistance ended on [date], but my rental assistance contract runs until [date]. Please tell me in writing why it ended and which program policy you used, send me a copy of your TBRA policies, and tell me how to request a review. I am asking that my assistance continue while the review is pending.”

If they say no

Ask for the decision in writing, and take it, your contract and the policies to legal aid. If you think the decision broke the program's own rules or a fair housing law, you can also contact the city or county office that runs its HOME program, or HUD's local field office. Review rules differ from program to program, so get the process in writing.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

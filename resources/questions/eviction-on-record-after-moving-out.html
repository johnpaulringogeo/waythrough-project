Reader Question

We moved out by the agreed date, but the eviction is still on our record. What can I do?

Asked June 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“During my eviction case, the landlord's attorney asked when we could be out. We moved out on the date we agreed to, but the eviction is still on our record. What do I do?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

You may be able to fix it. Get the court file and your agreement: if it called for the case to be dismissed once you moved out, you can ask the court to dismiss it, and in many states to seal it. Tenant screening companies must report the case accurately, and you can dispute errors under the Fair Credit Reporting Act.

Why it's still there

An eviction case shows up in court records as soon as it is filed, even if you settle and move out on time. What matters is how the case ended. To a landlord, a "dismissal" looks very different from a "judgment for possession" or a money judgment. Many move-out agreements say the case will be dismissed after you leave, but someone has to file the dismissal, and sometimes no one does.

What to do now

  1. Get a copy of your agreement and the court docket (the list of filings in the case). Ask the court clerk or check the court's website.
  2. Look at how the case ended: dismissed, judgment, or still open.
  3. If the agreement said the case would be dismissed and it wasn't, ask the landlord's attorney in writing to file the dismissal. If they don't, you can ask the court yourself, with a motion to dismiss or to enforce the agreement. Legal aid or the court's self-help center can help with the form.
  4. Ask about sealing. Some states let you ask a court to seal or hide an eviction record, especially when the case was dismissed or settled, and a few do it automatically. The rules vary a lot; check your state's page.
  5. Get your tenant screening reports. If a landlord turns you down because of a report, they must tell you which company made it, and you can get a free copy if you ask within 60 days.

Dispute wrong information

Tenant screening companies are covered by the Fair Credit Reporting Act. If a report says you were evicted when the case was dismissed, leaves out that you settled, lists the wrong amount, or shows someone else's case, dispute it in writing. The company must investigate, usually within 30 days (up to 45 if you send more information during that time), and correct or delete anything it can't verify (15 U.S.C. 1681i). Civil cases, including evictions, generally can't be reported once they are more than seven years old (15 U.S.C. 1681c). If the company hasn't fixed it when the 30 days are up, you can file a complaint with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB).

What to write

"Your report lists an eviction case against me, [case number]. That case [was dismissed on (date) / ended in an agreement, and I moved out on the agreed date]. A copy of the court record is attached. Please correct or delete this entry and send me the updated report."

When you apply for a new place

Bring the agreement, the dismissal if you have it, and proof that you moved out on time. A short note explaining that you left by agreement can help. If you have a voucher or are applying for subsidized housing, ask the housing authority or property to look at the court record itself, not just the screening score.

If they say no

If the court won't dismiss or seal the case, ask legal aid whether your state has another option. If a screening company won't fix a wrong entry, file a CFPB complaint and keep copies of everything you send.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

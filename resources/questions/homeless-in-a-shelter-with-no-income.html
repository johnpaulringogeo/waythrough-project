Reader Question

How can a parent in a shelter with no income get a home?

Asked October 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“I'm a parent with a young child, and we're staying in a shelter. I have no income, just a little savings. The shelter is only temporary, and I need a real home for us. What can I do?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

Having no income does not disqualify you from Section 8 or public housing. Ask your shelter's case manager to connect you to coordinated entry for rapid rehousing, apply to every housing authority with an open list and tell them you're homeless, and apply for benefits that can bring in some income.

What the rules say

  • No income is not a barrier. Section 8 and public housing have income limits, not income minimums. With no income, you'd usually pay the housing authority's minimum rent, which it sets anywhere from $0 to $50 a month. If you can't pay even that, you can ask for a hardship exemption (24 CFR 5.630). A small amount of savings won't keep you out.
  • Homeless families can move up the list. Housing authorities may set local preferences (24 CFR 982.207), and many give one to people who are homeless. Ask each one if it does.
  • Coordinated entry is the front door. Every area that gets HUD homeless funding must run a coordinated assessment system (24 CFR 578.7(a)(8)). It decides who gets rapid rehousing, transitional housing and supportive housing.

What to do now

  1. Talk to your shelter case manager. Ask: "Have I been assessed through coordinated entry? What housing am I matched to, and what's my next step?" Ask about rapid rehousing, which can pay a deposit and part of the rent for a while.
  2. Apply for Section 8 and public housing at every housing authority near you with an open list. On each application, say you're homeless and living in a shelter. Ask the shelter for a letter that proves it.
  3. Apply for benefits now: SNAP for food, WIC for children under 5, Medicaid, and TANF cash assistance. TANF can give you some income and may help with child care so you can work or train.
  4. Ask about Early Head Start. Children who are homeless qualify for Head Start and Early Head Start (45 CFR 1302.12(c)). It gives your child care and learning while you look for work and housing.
  5. Protect your savings. Keep them for a deposit, an application fee or moving costs when you find a place.
  6. Keep your papers together: photo IDs, Social Security cards, birth certificates and the shelter letter. Housing applications move faster when you have them ready.

What to say

"I'm a parent living in a shelter with my child, and I have no income right now. I'd like to apply for housing. Do you have a preference for families who are homeless, and what documents do you need from me?"

If the lists are closed

Ask your case manager about every program in your area, not just Section 8: rapid rehousing, transitional housing for families, and family shelters that allow longer stays. Check for open lists often, since some open for only a few days. Ask to be told when your coordinated entry status changes, and keep your phone number current with every program.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

Reader Question

Where can I get help with first and last month's rent to move in?

Asked August 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“I need help paying my first and last month's rent so I can move in. Where can I get it?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

Help with move-in costs is harder to find than help with back rent, but it exists. If you're homeless or about to be, rapid rehousing and homelessness prevention programs can pay a security deposit and last month's rent. Otherwise, try 211, local charities, and your housing authority if you have a voucher.

Who pays move-in costs

Most emergency rent programs pay rent you already owe, not the cost of moving into a new place. The biggest exception is for people who are homeless or at risk of becoming homeless. Programs paid for by HUD's Emergency Solutions Grants can cover a security deposit of up to two months' rent, the last month's rent, rental application fees, utility deposits and moving costs (24 CFR 576.105(a)). They can also help with rent for a while. These programs are usually called rapid rehousing or homelessness prevention, and you often reach them through your area's coordinated entry system.

Each program sets its own rules about income and who qualifies, and money can run out. Apply to more than one.

What to do now

  1. Call 211 and say you need help with a deposit and first month's rent to move in. Ask about rapid rehousing, homelessness prevention and any local deposit help.
  2. If you're homeless or about to lose your housing, ask 211 or a shelter how to get assessed through coordinated entry. That's the front door to most deposit help.
  3. If you have a voucher, ask your housing authority whether it offers security deposit assistance or works with a group that does. Some do.
  4. Contact local charities such as Catholic Charities, the Salvation Army, St. Vincent de Paul and nearby churches. Many give one-time help toward a deposit.
  5. Ask your state's cash assistance (TANF) office about one-time emergency payments, especially if you have children.
  6. Ask the landlord to split the deposit into a few monthly payments. Get any agreement in writing before you pay.
  7. Know your state's limits. Some states cap how much a landlord can collect up front. Check your state page.

What to say

"I've found a place I can afford, but I need help with the security deposit and the first and last month's rent to move in. Do you have a program for move-in costs, or can you refer me to one?"

If you can't find help

Ask each program when it expects new funding, and call back then. Look for places with lower move-in costs, such as tax credit apartments or landlords who ask only for the first month and a deposit. Get receipts for every payment. And be careful with anyone who wants a "holding fee" before you've seen the unit in person or signed a lease; that's a common scam.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

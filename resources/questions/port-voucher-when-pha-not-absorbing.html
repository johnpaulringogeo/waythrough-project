Reader Question

Can I still port my voucher if the housing authorities where I want to move aren't absorbing?

Asked October 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“I have a Section 8 voucher in a cold state and want to move somewhere warmer in the South, because the cold is hard on my disability. The housing authorities I called there say they'll accept me but aren't absorbing vouchers this year. Am I stuck where I am, or are there other options?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

Usually you're not stuck. A receiving housing authority that isn't absorbing can still take you by billing your current housing authority, which keeps paying (24 CFR 982.355). Your current housing authority can say no only if it truly doesn't have the money for the move, and it has to report that to HUD.

What the rule says

When you port, the housing authority (PHA) where you move, called the receiving PHA, has two choices. It can absorb you, using one of its own vouchers. Or it can bill your current PHA, called the initial PHA, which keeps paying for your assistance while the new PHA runs your case (24 CFR 982.355(d) and (e)).

“Not absorbing” only means the receiving PHA won't use its own vouchers. It doesn't mean it can turn you away. HUD's rule says a receiving PHA “cannot refuse to assist incoming portable families” (24 CFR 982.355(b)). It has to bill instead.

So the real question is your current PHA. If billing would cost it more, because rents are higher where you're going, it may deny the move only if it doesn't have enough funding to keep assisting families. It must tell its HUD field office in writing within 10 business days of deciding to deny moves for that reason (24 CFR 982.354(e)(1) and 982.355(c)(5)). A move to a lower-cost area usually doesn't raise that issue at all.

One more thing: the new PHA uses its own rules for bedroom size (24 CFR 982.355(c)(12)), so your voucher size could change.

What to do now

  1. Pick the area you want and find the PHA that serves it. If more than one PHA covers the area, you get to choose (24 CFR 982.355(b)).
  2. Ask that PHA one question: “If I port in, will you bill my housing authority?” The answer should be yes. Ask for it by email.
  3. Give your current PHA a written portability request that names the area. The rule says your PHA must contact the receiving PHA before approving the move. Ask it to tell you in writing whether it approves.
  4. If your PHA says it can't afford the move, ask for that in writing, including the date it notified HUD.
  5. Because the cold affects your disability, also ask for the move as a reasonable accommodation, with a short letter from your doctor. That can help if your PHA limits moves.
  6. Don't give notice to your landlord until you have the approval and a voucher in hand. The receiving PHA's voucher must run at least 30 days past your old voucher's expiration date (24 CFR 982.355(c)(13)).

What to write

“I request to port my voucher to [county, state]. The housing authority there has told me it will bill rather than absorb. Please contact them as required by 24 CFR 982.355 and approve my move. Because of my disability, I am also asking for this move as a reasonable accommodation; a letter from my doctor is attached.”

If they say no

Ask for the denial and the reason in writing, and ask for an informal hearing. If the denial is about funding, ask when the PHA expects to approve moves again, and ask to be on its list. You can also contact the HUD field office over your current PHA, or free legal aid, to ask whether the denial follows the rules. Funding can change during the year, so ask again in a few months.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

Reader Question

My Section 8 landlord charges a late fee every month, but I pay on time. Is that allowed?

Asked August 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“My Section 8 landlord charges me a late fee every month, even though I don't pay late. Is that allowed?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

No, not if your share is paid on time. A late fee is allowed only if your lease includes one, your own share of the rent was actually late, and the amount is reasonable under your state's law. You can't be charged a late fee because the housing authority paid its part late.

What the rule says

  • It has to be in the lease. A landlord can charge only the fees your lease lists. If the lease doesn't mention late fees, there shouldn't be any.
  • Your share has to be late. With a Housing Choice Voucher, you owe only your part of the rent. The tenancy addendum in the HAP contract (form HUD-52641, Part C) says you are not responsible for the part the housing authority pays, and that a late payment by the housing authority is not a lease violation. If the housing authority pays late, the contract lets the owner ask the housing authority for a late penalty instead (Part B). It shouldn't land on you.
  • It has to be reasonable. State law controls how big a late fee can be and when it can start. Some states cap late fees or require a grace period. Check your state's page.
  • HUD apartment buildings have their own limits. If you live in a HUD project-based building rather than using a voucher, HUD's handbook (4350.3, paragraph 6-23) allows a late fee only after a grace period: the rent must be received by the 5th, the fee can be up to $5 on the 6th, then $1 a day. The owner can't evict you for unpaid late fees. Some senior and disability buildings (Section 202 and 811 PRAC) can't charge late fees at all.

Why this happens

Monthly late fees when you pay on time often come from a bookkeeping problem: your payment gets applied to an old charge, the landlord thinks your share went up after a recertification, or the housing authority's part arrives after the due date. Each one can make the ledger show you "behind" when you aren't.

What to do now

  1. Gather proof of each payment: money order stubs, bank records, receipts or app screenshots, with dates.
  2. Find your latest rent notice from the housing authority. It shows your share. Compare it with what the landlord bills you.
  3. Ask the landlord in writing for a full ledger that shows every charge and every payment, with dates.
  4. Write back about each wrong fee and ask for it to be removed.
  5. Send a copy to your caseworker. The housing authority checks that owners follow the HAP contract.
  6. Keep paying your share on time while you dispute the fees.

What to write

"I have paid my share of the rent on time each month (proof attached), but I am being charged a late fee every month. Please send me a full ledger and remove the late fees from [months]. Under my HAP contract, I am not responsible for the housing authority's share. I am sending a copy to my caseworker."

If they say no

If the fees continue, ask your caseworker to step in, and contact legal aid. Unpaid fees can pile up into a balance the landlord later says you owe, so don't ignore them. If the landlord threatens eviction over late fees, get legal help right away.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

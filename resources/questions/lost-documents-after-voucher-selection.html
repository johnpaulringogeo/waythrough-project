Reader Question

I was selected for a voucher but lost my documents in a move. Who do I contact?

Asked October 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“I was picked for a housing voucher this summer, then moved to another state with my kids and lost all of our documents in the move. Family members are trying to get our records for me. Can I ask for more time or appeal, and who should I even contact?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

Contact the housing authority that selected you, in writing, as soon as you can. Explain the move and the lost documents, ask what they still need, and ask for more time to get it. Moving to another state can change where you're allowed to use the voucher, so ask about that before you sign a lease.

Who to contact

Your contact is still the housing authority (PHA) that picked you from its list, not a PHA in your new state. It decides whether you're eligible and issues the voucher. Look at the letter, email or text that said you were selected; it usually names a caseworker or intake office. If you can't find it, call the PHA's main number and give your full name, date of birth and the last four digits of your Social Security number so they can find your file.

What the rules say

  • Documents. You have to give the PHA the information it needs to decide eligibility (24 CFR 982.551(b)). Most PHAs give a deadline and can give more time when you're actively replacing papers. Ask how long.
  • Appeals. If the PHA denies you, it must tell you why in writing and offer an informal review (24 CFR 982.554). There's nothing to appeal until there's a denial. If you already got a denial letter, follow its deadline, often 10 to 14 days.
  • Moving states. If you lived in that PHA's area when you applied, you can usually take the voucher to another area by porting. If you didn't, the PHA can require you to rent in its own area for the first 12 months (24 CFR 982.353(c)). Ask which applies to you.

What to do now

  1. Write to the PHA today. Give your new address, phone and email so you don't miss a letter.
  2. Explain that your documents were lost and list what you've ordered and when.
  3. Ask for a list of what they still need, the deadline, and more time if you need it. Ask whether they'll accept copies or receipts for now, and whether your interview or briefing can be done by phone or video.
  4. Replace documents:
    • Birth certificates: from the vital records office in the state where each person was born. Most take online or mail orders.
    • Social Security cards: free from the Social Security Administration, often online. The PHA may only need the numbers.
    • Income: a benefit letter from Social Security or your state benefits office, a child support printout, or pay stubs.
  5. Ask the PHA whether you can port to your new state, and what you'd need to do.

What to write

“I was selected from your waiting list in [month]. I have moved to [state] and lost my family's documents during the move. I have ordered replacements. My new address and phone number are below. Please tell me what you still need, give me more time to provide it, and tell me whether I can use my voucher in my new state.”

If they say no

If they deny your application, ask for an informal review in writing by the deadline in the letter, and attach proof that you've ordered the documents. Free legal aid can help you get ready. If someone in your family has a disability that made replacing documents harder, ask for more time as a reasonable accommodation.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

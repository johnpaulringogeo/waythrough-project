Reader Question

Can I switch my project-based voucher to a regular Section 8 voucher?

Asked September 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“I live in an apartment with a project-based voucher. Can I switch it to a regular Section 8 voucher that I can take with me?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

Usually yes, once you've had project-based assistance for one year. Ask the housing authority for a tenant-based voucher before you give your landlord notice; if none is available right away, it must put you first in line for the next one (24 CFR 983.261).

What the rule says

A project-based voucher (PBV) stays with the apartment, not with you. But HUD gives PBV families a right to move with continued help (24 CFR 983.261):

  • After one year of PBV assistance, you may end your lease and move.
  • The housing authority (PHA) must offer you a tenant-based voucher, or other comparable help that moves with you.
  • You must contact the PHA and ask for it before you give your landlord notice.
  • If no voucher is available right then, the PHA must give you priority for the next one.
  • If you move out before one year, you give up this right. There's an exception for moves needed because of domestic violence, dating violence, sexual assault or stalking.

Priority doesn't always mean right away. At some PHAs few vouchers open up, so the wait can be months. You keep your PBV help in your current unit while you wait.

Check what kind of help you have

This rule covers project-based vouchers from a housing authority. Some buildings instead have “project-based Section 8” contracts directly between HUD and the owner, where you deal only with the property's management office. Families in those buildings don't have this right, except in buildings converted under HUD's Rental Assistance Demonstration (RAD), which has its own “choice mobility” rule. Ask the office: “Is my help a PBV from a housing authority, a HUD project-based contract, or a RAD conversion?”

What to do now

  1. Check your move-in date. Make sure you've had PBV help for at least 12 months.
  2. Write to the PHA, not just the property manager, and ask for a tenant-based voucher under 24 CFR 983.261.
  3. Ask how the PHA tracks families waiting to move, where you are on that list, and about how long it may take. HUD requires the PHA to explain this in its Administrative Plan.
  4. Don't give your landlord notice until you have the voucher in hand.
  5. When the voucher comes, the normal search time applies, so have your documents ready and start a list of landlords who take vouchers.

What to write

“I have received project-based voucher assistance at [address] since [month and year], which is more than one year. Under 24 CFR 983.261, I request a tenant-based voucher so I can move with continued assistance. If none is available now, please give me priority for the next one and tell me in writing where I stand.”

If they say no

Ask for the reason in writing, and ask for the part of the PHA's Administrative Plan that covers PBV families who want to move. If you've had a full year of PBV help and are told you can never move with a voucher, contact free legal aid, because that doesn't match the rule. If the PHA offers “comparable” tenant-based help instead of a voucher, ask how long it lasts and how it compares before you accept.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

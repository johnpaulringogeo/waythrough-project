Reader Question

Where can I stay while I wait to find out about Section 8?

Asked September 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“I need a place to stay until I find out whether I can get Section 8. Where can I go?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

Section 8 waitlists often take months or years, so you'll need a plan for now. Call 211 for shelter, ask to be assessed through your area's coordinated entry system for rapid rehousing or transitional housing, and keep applying to other affordable housing while you wait.

Why you need a plan for now

Section 8 almost never helps right away. Many lists are closed or use a lottery, and waits of a year or more are common. Housing authorities can set local preferences that move some applicants up the list (24 CFR 982.207), and many have one for people who are homeless. So tell every housing authority your situation, but plan as if the wait will be long.

What to do tonight and this week

  1. Call 211 for shelters near you. Ask about family, women's, youth or veterans' shelters if one fits you. HUD also lists local help at hud.gov/findshelter.
  2. Ask to be assessed through coordinated entry. Every area that gets HUD homeless funding must run a coordinated assessment system (24 CFR 578.7(a)(8)). It's the front door to rapid rehousing, transitional housing and supportive housing. Shelters and 211 can tell you how to get in.
  3. If you're fleeing violence, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233 for help finding a safe place.
  4. If you stay with friends or family, ask them to check their lease first. If they live in public housing or use a voucher, a long-term guest can put their housing at risk.
  5. Keep your contact information current with every housing authority you've applied to. Use a phone number and email you'll keep, or a mailing address at a shelter or a trusted friend's home. A missed letter can get you dropped from a list.

Apply to more than Section 8

  • Public housing through your housing authority. It's a separate list.
  • Project-based voucher buildings, where the help is tied to the apartment.
  • Tax credit (LIHTC) apartments, with lower rents and their own waitlists.
  • Other housing authorities nearby with open lists. You can be on more than one.

What to say when you call

"I don't have a stable place to stay, and I'm waiting to hear about Section 8. Can you help me find shelter, and how do I get assessed for rapid rehousing or other housing programs?"

If shelters are full

Ask to be put on any waitlist and call back each morning, since beds open up overnight. Ask about warming or cooling centers, motel voucher programs and day centers where you can shower, eat and get mail. Keep a simple record of where you stay each night; it can help you prove you're homeless when you apply for programs and preferences.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

Questions Readers Asked Us, Answered

Updated November 4, 2026

These are real questions people sent us through our Ask a Question form. We rewrote each one so no one can be identified, then answered it in plain language with links to the rules and to our guides. For general questions, see our community answers and FAQ.

Keeping and using your voucher

My housing authority won't extend my voucher, even as a disability accommodation. What can I do?

Usually you can still get more time. When a disability is the reason you need longer to find a place, HUD's rule says the housing authority must extend your voucher as long as reasonably needed (24 CFR 982.303(b)(2)), even if its usual policy is "no more extensions." Put the request in writing before the voucher expires, and if they still say no, file a fair housing complaint.

My voucher expired while I was waiting on an inspection that never happened. Can I get it back?

You may still have time left. HUD rules say the voucher clock must stop from the day you turn in the Request for Tenancy Approval until the housing authority tells you in writing whether the unit is approved or denied (24 CFR 982.303(c)). If that pause wasn't counted, ask in writing for your expiration date to be recalculated and your voucher reinstated.

My voucher expired while I was waiting on ID documents. Can I get it back?

Maybe. No federal rule makes a housing authority reinstate an expired voucher, but many will reopen or reissue one when you can show the delay wasn't your fault. Ask in writing right away, include proof of the dates, and if a disability was part of the delay, ask for the extra time as a reasonable accommodation.

Can I still port my voucher if the housing authorities where I want to move aren't absorbing?

Usually you're not stuck. A receiving housing authority that isn't absorbing can still take you by billing your current housing authority, which keeps paying (24 CFR 982.355). Your current housing authority can say no only if it truly doesn't have the money for the move, and it has to report that to HUD.

I was selected for a voucher but lost my documents in a move. Who do I contact?

Contact the housing authority that selected you, in writing, as soon as you can. Explain the move and the lost documents, ask what they still need, and ask for more time to get it. Moving to another state can change where you're allowed to use the voucher, so ask about that before you sign a lease.

Can I switch my project-based voucher to a regular Section 8 voucher?

Usually yes, once you've had project-based assistance for one year. Ask the housing authority for a tenant-based voucher before you give your landlord notice; if none is available right away, it must put you first in line for the next one (24 CFR 983.261).

How do project-based vouchers and special purpose vouchers work?

A project-based voucher is tied to one apartment instead of to you; you usually pay about 30% of your adjusted income, and after a year you can usually ask for a voucher that moves with you. Special purpose vouchers are Section 8 vouchers set aside for certain groups, like homeless veterans, families and youth involved with foster care, and adults with disabilities, and most come through a partner agency's referral.

Can someone live with me and pay rent without being added to my voucher?

No, usually not. Under Section 8 rules, everyone who lives in your unit has to be approved by the housing authority as part of your household (24 CFR 982.551(h)), and taking rent from someone who isn't approved can count as subletting. The person can be added, stay only as a short-term guest, or live somewhere else.

Applying, waitlists and denials

Can I send my housing appeal by email, or does it have to be mailed?

It depends on your housing authority. Your denial or termination letter should say how and by when to ask for a review or hearing. If email is allowed, use it, and also send a copy by certified mail or hand-deliver one so you can prove the date.

I was too sick to answer a housing letter. Where do I stand now?

You may still have options. Write to the housing authority and ask for your current status. If you were removed from a waiting list because a disability or serious illness kept you from responding, you can ask to be put back as a reasonable accommodation, and for Section 8 waiting lists the rules say the housing authority must reinstate you.

My housing application was denied, but it didn't say why. What can I do?

You have a right to know. The housing authority must give you written notice with a brief reason for the denial and tell you how to ask for an informal review (Section 8) or an informal hearing (public housing). Ask for the reason and your file in writing, and request the review before the deadline.

I'm a trafficking survivor and was told I'm not eligible for Section 8. Why?

You don't need a job or any income for Section 8, and being homeless doesn't disqualify you. Ask the housing authority for the exact reason in writing and request an informal review. VAWA protections can apply if you also lived through sexual assault, stalking or domestic violence, and trafficking advocates can help you find housing.

I'm leaving domestic violence and was dropped from the Section 8 waitlist. What can I do?

If you are in danger now, call 911. For safety planning and shelter, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233 or text START to 88788. Then ask the housing authority in writing why you were removed, ask to be put back with your original date, and ask about preferences for survivors.

Rent, income and charges

Do I get both the disability deduction and the $480 dependent deduction?

There's no automatic standard deduction. Your household gets the elderly or disabled family deduction ($400 a year under the rules most housing authorities use now) once, plus a separate dependent deduction ($480 a year now) only for each member who counts as a dependent. These are federal amounts, so your state doesn't change them.

Will taking trust distributions affect my subsidized housing?

Possibly, but it depends on how the payments count. Under HUD's updated (HOTMA) rules, payments of principal from an irrevocable trust you don't control aren't counted as income, while payments of what the trust earned are. Ask your housing provider in writing how it would count the distributions before you take them.

My rent was set too high at recertification. Does my landlord have to pay me back?

Usually not. When a rent mistake is corrected back to when it started, you are generally owed what you overpaid, as a refund or a credit. In Section 8, a landlord must return any rent you paid above your share, and in HUD-assisted buildings the owner must reimburse overpayments caused by its own error. Ask in writing, and bring in the housing authority or HUD if the landlord won't.

How can my landlord bill me for utilities when the whole building is on one meter?

It depends on your state's law, your lease and Section 8 rules. Some states let landlords split one meter's bill among tenants by a formula (often called ratio billing), and some limit or ban it. On Section 8, a lease change that moves utilities onto you has to go through your housing authority, and you should get a utility allowance for utilities you pay.

My TBRA rental assistance ended early and I'm about to lose my apartment. What can I do?

Ask the program in writing why it ended and how to request a review, and deal with the rent right away. TBRA is short-term help, usually paid for by HUD's HOME program and run by a city, county, state or partner agency (24 CFR 92.209), so the program's written policies and your rental assistance contract decide when it can end.

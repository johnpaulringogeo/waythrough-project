Reader Question

My landlord locked me out but kept collecting Section 8 payments. What can I do?

Asked October 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 4, 2026

The question

“My landlord gave me two months to move out so they could sell, but then locked me out early. They kept taking the housing authority's payments, even though I'd already filled out my move packet. I've been homeless for months. What can I do?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

Tell your housing authority in writing the date you were locked out. Housing assistance can be paid only while you live in the unit, so the housing authority can stop payments and recover what the owner shouldn't have kept. Ask about using your voucher to move, contact legal aid about the lockout, and report suspected fraud to HUD's Inspector General.

What the rule says

  • The lockout. A voucher landlord may evict only by going to court (24 CFR 982.310(f)), and almost every state says the same. Even if you agreed to a move-out date, locking you out early without a court order is usually illegal. Wanting to sell doesn't change that.
  • The payments. Housing assistance may be paid only "while the family is residing in the unit" (24 CFR 982.311). The HAP contract says the housing authority may not pay the owner "for any month after the month when the family moves out" (form HUD-52641, Part B). Money kept after that is an overpayment the housing authority can recover, and it can also end its contract with the owner (24 CFR 982.453). Knowingly taking payments for a unit you no longer live in can be fraud.
  • Your voucher. A voucher issued for a move lasts at least 60 days, and the housing authority can extend it under its policy. The clock pauses while a request for tenancy approval is under review (24 CFR 982.303). If you or a family member has a disability, you can ask for an extension as a reasonable accommodation.

What to do now

  1. Write to your housing authority with the date of the lockout and the last day you lived there. Ask them to stop payments to the owner as of that month and recover any overpayment. Ask for written confirmation.
  2. In the same letter, ask about your voucher: is it active, when does it expire, and can it be extended because you were locked out and are homeless? If it already expired, ask in writing to have it reinstated and explain why.
  3. Ask for help finding a unit: landlord lists, a housing search worker, or a referral to homeless services.
  4. If the housing authority doesn't answer, call HUD's Public and Indian Housing customer service line at 800-955-2232.
  5. Report suspected fraud to the HUD OIG Hotline at 1-800-347-3735. Include dates, the address and copies of your move packet.
  6. Contact legal aid about the illegal lockout. In many states you can recover money damages, and possibly your belongings and deposit.
  7. For shelter tonight, call 211.

What to write

"On [date] my landlord locked me out of [address], and I have not lived there since. Please stop housing assistance payments for that unit, recover any payments made after I left, and tell me in writing the status and expiration date of my voucher. I am asking for an extension because I was locked out and am now homeless."

If they say no

If the housing authority says your voucher expired or your assistance ended, ask in writing for an informal hearing right away, and bring proof of the lockout and of your move packet. If you miss the deadline in the letter, ask anyway and explain why. Keep copies of everything you send.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

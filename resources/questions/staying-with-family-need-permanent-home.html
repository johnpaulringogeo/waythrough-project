Reader Question

We're staying with family after leaving a shelter. How do we find a permanent home?

Asked November 2026 · Answered by Matt · Updated November 8, 2026

The question

“We left the shelter and we're staying with my spouse's family for now, with our toddler. My spouse can't work because of a disability. I don't want us to end up on the street. How do we find a permanent home?”

Sent through our Ask a Question form. We removed names, places and other details so no one can be identified.

The short answer

Staying with relatives doesn't shut you out of help. Call 211 or your area's coordinated entry and ask about homelessness prevention, get on every open Section 8 and public housing list, apply for disability benefits for your spouse, and enroll your toddler in Early Head Start, which counts children staying with others as homeless.

Where you stand

Moving in with family doesn't shut you out of help, even if it feels that way. But programs sort people by where they sleep, so it's worth knowing which box you're in.

  • For HUD homeless programs, a family staying with relatives usually isn't counted as literally homeless. That means rapid rehousing, which is mostly for people in shelters or on the street, may not be open to you right now (24 CFR 576.104).
  • You may still count as "at risk of homelessness." HUD's definition includes a household with very low income, no other resources to fall back on, that is living in someone else's home because of economic hardship (24 CFR 576.2). Families in that spot can get homelessness prevention help, which pays for things like short-term rent and help moving into a place of your own (24 CFR 576.103). How much money there is depends on your area.
  • If your relatives tell you to leave, things change. A family that will lose its place to stay within 14 days, with no other place lined up and no money or people to fall back on, counts as homeless under HUD's definition (24 CFR 576.2).
  • Your toddler is covered by a wider rule. Under the federal McKinney-Vento definition, children "sharing the housing of other persons due to loss of housing, economic hardship, or a similar reason" are homeless (42 U.S.C. 11434a(2)). That matters for child care, below.

What to do this week

  1. Call 211 and ask for coordinated entry, the front door to your area's homeless and housing programs. Tell them you left a shelter, you're staying with relatives for now, you have a toddler and a spouse with a disability, and you have nowhere to go if this ends. Ask to be assessed, and ask by name for homelessness prevention. If your relatives tell you to leave, call back the same day and say so, because that changes what you can get.
  2. Get on every open Section 8 and public housing list you can reach, including housing authorities in the next county over. List your spouse's disability on every application. Some housing authorities give a preference to families with a member who has a disability, and some give one to homeless families, so ask each one which preferences it uses. Write down every confirmation number. Keep your phone number and mailing address current with each one, because a letter that goes to the wrong place can get you dropped.
  3. Apply for SSI or SSDI for your spouse if they don't already get disability benefits. A steady monthly check makes it easier to qualify for an apartment and to keep it, and with a voucher your rent is based on your income, so you'd pay only a share. Approval can take months, so start now. Our SSI and SSDI guide goes step by step.
  4. Ask about Early Head Start. It's free child care and early learning for children under 3 (45 CFR 1302.12(b)(1)). A child who is homeless is eligible no matter what the family earns (45 CFR 1302.12(c)(1)(iii)), and Head Start uses the McKinney-Vento definition, which covers children staying with others after losing their housing (45 CFR 1305.2). Tell the program you left a shelter and are staying with relatives. With your child in care, you have more hours to work, look at apartments and get to appointments. The center locator at headstart.gov lists programs near you.
  5. Pick up the basics if you haven't yet: SNAP for food, WIC for your toddler (children under 5 qualify), and TANF cash assistance for families with children. Your county social services office handles all three. TANF puts some cash in your hand each month, and SNAP and WIC free up money you can save for a deposit.

What to say when you call

"We left a shelter and we're staying with relatives for now, with our toddler. My spouse has a disability and can't work. If this ends, we have nowhere to go. Can I be assessed through coordinated entry, and do you have homelessness prevention help for a family like ours?"

Protect the place you have now

While you wait, look after the arrangement with your family. If your relatives rent, and especially if they live in public housing or have a voucher, ask them to check their lease, since a long-term guest can put their housing at risk. Chip in what you can for food or bills, and keep a short note of when you moved in and why. If they do ask you to leave, try to get it in writing, even a text, and call 211 that same day.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ by state and by housing authority. For your own situation, talk to your housing authority, a free legal aid office, or call 211.

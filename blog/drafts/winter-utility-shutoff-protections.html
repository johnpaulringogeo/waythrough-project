Can They Shut Off Your Heat in Winter? 2026-27 Rules

Matt · November 25, 2026 · 11 min read

It depends on your state. There is no single national rule. Some states, like Wisconsin, stop heat shutoffs for nonpayment all winter. Others protect only some households, such as seniors or families with low incomes, or only stop shutoffs on freezing days. And no winter rule wipes out your bill. It keeps growing, and many protections only last while you stay on a payment plan.

The fastest way to keep your heat on is to do two things before the shutoff date: call your utility and ask for a payment plan, and apply for LIHEAP, the federal program that helps pay heating bills. There is LIHEAP money for this winter. The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) released $3.6 billion in LIHEAP money on October 9, 2026. To find your local office, call the National Energy Assistance Referral line at 1-866-674-6327.

Can a Utility Shut Off Your Heat in the Winter?

Yes, in many cases, unless your state's rules stop it. Each state's utility commission sets the rules for the gas and electric companies it oversees. Those rules usually work in one of three ways:

  • A winter ban. No heat shutoffs for nonpayment for a set period, often from November or December to March or April.
  • Protection for some households. Seniors, people with disabilities, or households with low incomes cannot be shut off, often only if they sign up and make payments.
  • Cold-day rules. No shutoffs on days when the forecast is at or below freezing.

One catch: city-owned utilities and electric co-ops are often not covered by state commission rules. If that is who sends your bill, call them and ask what winter rules they follow. Delivered fuels like propane, oil and wood are often not covered either. Minnesota's rule, for example, does not cover them.

Winter Shutoff Rules by State (2026-27)

Here are the rules in 10 states, from each state's utility commission. These apply to the companies each commission regulates. If your utility will not follow them, call the commission number listed.

  • Georgia: From November 15 to March 15, utilities the Public Service Commission regulates, such as Georgia Power, cannot shut off service on days when the local low is forecast below 32°F. You must agree in writing to pay the past-due balance in equal payments after March 15. PSC: (800) 282-5813.
  • Illinois: From December 1 to March 31, LIHEAP customers, Percentage of Income Payment Plan (PIPP) customers, people who heat with electricity, and military members and veterans cannot be shut off for nonpayment. Other customers who heat with gas or electricity can only be shut off if they turn down a payment plan with a down payment of no more than 10%. No shutoffs when the forecast is 32°F or colder. Illinois Commerce Commission: 1-800-524-0795.
  • Michigan: From November 1 to March 31, seniors 65 and older are protected. Households at or below 150% of the poverty level, or who get MDHHS cash help, SSI, SNAP or Medicaid, can join the Winter Protection Plan. You must show you applied for heating help and pay at least 7% of your yearly bill each month. Michigan Public Service Commission: 800-292-9555.
  • Minnesota: The Cold Weather Rule runs October 1 to April 30. If your household income is at or below 50% of the state median, a public utility cannot shut off your heat, and must turn it back on, if you set up and keep a payment plan. It cannot make you pay more than 10% of your household income toward your heating bills. Everyone else has a right to a payment plan based on their finances. Minnesota PUC: 1-800-657-3782.
  • New York: From November 1 to April 15, utilities must make special efforts to find out if shutting off your heat would hurt someone's health or safety, and must try to reach an adult in your home before a shutoff. Extra protections apply if you and all the adults living with you are 62 or older, blind or disabled. Department of Public Service helpline: 1-800-342-3377.
  • North Carolina: From November 1 to March 31, a regulated utility cannot shut you off without the Utilities Commission's approval if someone in your home is 65 or older or disabled, you cannot pay, and your county social services office certifies you as eligible for energy assistance. This does not cover city-owned utilities or electric co-ops. Public Staff Consumer Services: 866-380-9816.
  • Ohio: From October 12, 2026 to April 16, 2027, the Special Reconnect Order lets you keep or restore gas or electric service by paying no more than $175, plus a reconnection fee of up to $36. You can use it once per winter, with no income limit. If your income is at or below 175% of the poverty level, the HEAP Winter Crisis Program can help pay the $175. PUCO: (800) 686-7826.
  • Pennsylvania: From December 1 to March 31, the utility needs the Public Utility Commission's approval to shut you off if your income is at or below 250% of the poverty level (150% for Philadelphia Gas Works customers). If you are shut off in winter, the utility must reconnect you within 24 hours after you pay or make an arrangement. PUC: 1-800-692-7380.
  • Texas: There is no winter-long ban. Your retail electric provider cannot disconnect you for nonpayment during an extreme weather emergency, and must offer a deferred payment plan if you ask. It also cannot disconnect you if an energy assistance agency tells it, by the due date on your notice, that it is helping pay your bill. Co-ops and city-owned utilities are not covered. Public Utility Commission: 1-888-782-8477.
  • Wisconsin: From November 1 to April 15, utilities cannot shut off service for nonpayment when it is used to heat your home. If you are already shut off, the utility does not have to reconnect you until you make a payment arrangement. Public Service Commission: 1-800-225-7729.

What If Your State Is Not on This List?

Every state has its own rules, and some have no winter rule at all. Look at your shutoff notice first. It often explains your rights and lists a phone number for help. Then call your state's public utility commission (some states call it the public service commission). Ask three questions: Is there a winter shutoff rule? Does it cover my utility? What do I have to do to qualify?

Many states also delay a shutoff if someone in your home has a serious illness. Pennsylvania, Georgia, Michigan and Texas all have medical protections. You usually need a note from a doctor, and the delay is short, so use the time to set up a plan.

LIHEAP 2026-27: How to Get Help With Your Heating Bill

LIHEAP (the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program) is federal money that states use to help pay heating and cooling bills, stop shutoffs, and repair or replace heating equipment. HHS released $3.6 billion for this fiscal year on October 9, 2026. That was 90% of the money available under the short-term funding law Congress passed, so more may come later.

Who qualifies: Each state sets its own income limit. Federal law lets states help households with incomes up to 150% of the federal poverty level or 60% of the state median income, whichever is higher. A state cannot turn you away for income alone if you are under 110% of the poverty level. States can also qualify you if someone in your home gets SNAP, SSI or TANF.

When to apply: Each state sets its own dates, and money can run out. In North Carolina, for example, households with someone 60 or older or disabled can apply December 1 to 31, and everyone else from January 1 to March 31 or until funds run out. Apply as early as your state allows.

If you have a shutoff notice: Ask for crisis help. Federal law says LIHEAP crisis programs must give some help that resolves the crisis within 48 hours after an eligible household applies, or within 18 hours if the situation is life-threatening.

How to apply: Call 1-866-674-6327 or visit energyhelp.us to find your local office. You can also find your state's LIHEAP office on the HHS state contact map. HHS says LIHEAP does not charge a fee and does not give direct grants to individuals. If you get a message offering you a LIHEAP grant or asking for a fee, call the HHS fraud hotline at 1-800-447-8477.

Got a Shutoff Notice? What to Do Today

  1. Read the notice. Write down the shutoff date, the amount you must pay to stop it, and any phone numbers listed.
  2. Call the utility before that date. Ask for a payment plan, and ask whether you qualify for a winter protection program or a low-income discount. Write down who you talked to and what they said.
  3. Apply for LIHEAP crisis help the same day, and tell the office your shutoff date. Ask the office to tell your utility that help is coming.
  4. Call 211 for local charities and emergency funds while you wait.
  5. If someone in your home is seriously ill, ask your doctor for a medical certificate and send it to the utility right away.
  6. If the utility will not work with you, call your state utility commission and file a complaint.

If You Have Section 8: A Shutoff Can Put Your Voucher at Risk

If the gas or electric account is in your name, keeping it on is one of your duties as a voucher holder. HUD's rules say your family is responsible when your home fails housing quality standards because you did not pay a utility you are supposed to pay. Your housing authority (PHA) will give you a deadline to fix it: 24 hours if it is life-threatening, or 30 days for other problems unless the PHA gives you more time. If you do not fix it, the PHA can end your assistance.

So act fast, and keep copies of your payment plan and your LIHEAP application to show your caseworker. Learn more about housing quality standards and how voucher termination works.

Some good news for voucher holders:

  • LIHEAP will not raise your rent. Federal law says LIHEAP help does not count as income or resources for any federal or state program.
  • Your utility allowance does not disqualify you from LIHEAP. If you pay some or all of your heating costs, the state cannot deny you automatically. It may give you a smaller benefit because of your utility allowance. Read how utility allowances work.
  • You can ask for a higher utility allowance if you have a disability. If someone in your home needs extra heat or power because of a disability, HUD's rules say the PHA must approve a higher allowance when it is needed as a reasonable accommodation. See how to request a reasonable accommodation.

If heat is included in your rent and the landlord's utility gets shut off, that is the landlord's problem to fix. Call your PHA. A unit with no heat can fail inspection, and the owner must fix life-threatening problems within 24 hours. Read what to do when your landlord won't fix things.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a law against shutting off heat in the winter?

There is no single national law. Each state sets its own rules for the utilities it regulates. Some states, like Wisconsin, stop heat shutoffs for nonpayment for the whole winter. Others protect only some households, like seniors or families with low incomes, or only stop shutoffs on freezing days. City-owned utilities and electric co-ops often follow their own rules.

Do I still have to pay my bill during a winter shutoff ban?

Yes. A winter rule only delays a shutoff. Your bill keeps growing, and in many states the protection depends on making payments under a payment plan. When the winter period ends, the utility can shut off service for any unpaid balance.

How fast can LIHEAP help if I have a shutoff notice?

Federal law says LIHEAP crisis programs must give some help that resolves the crisis within 48 hours after an eligible household applies, or within 18 hours if the situation is life-threatening. Apply as soon as you get a notice and tell the office you have a shutoff date.

Will LIHEAP help raise my Section 8 rent?

No. Federal law says LIHEAP help does not count as income or resources for any purpose under any federal or state law. Your housing authority cannot count it when it figures your rent.

Can I lose my Section 8 voucher if my utilities get shut off?

It can put your voucher at risk. If a utility you are supposed to pay is shut off, HUD's rules treat it as a family-caused failure of housing quality standards. Your housing authority gives you a deadline to fix it and can end your assistance if you do not. Act fast and keep proof of your payment plan and your LIHEAP application.

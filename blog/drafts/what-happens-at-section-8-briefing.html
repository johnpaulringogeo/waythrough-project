Your Section 8 Briefing: What to Expect and What to Bring

Matt · October 21, 2026 · 6 min read

A Section 8 briefing is the meeting where your housing authority (PHA) explains how the voucher program works and gets you ready to receive your voucher. HUD says you must attend a briefing before a voucher is issued. Plan on spending a few hours, bring everything your letter lists, and do not leave without three numbers: your voucher's expiration date, your voucher size, and your payment standard.

If you cannot make the date, call the PHA before the briefing and ask to reschedule. Each PHA sets its own rules for missed appointments, and a no-show can put your voucher at risk. If a disability makes attending hard, you can ask for a reasonable accommodation, like a different time or format. The PHA is required to tell you how to ask.

What Happens at a Section 8 Briefing

Federal rules (24 CFR 982.301) say the briefing must cover:

  • How the voucher program works
  • What you are responsible for, and what the landlord is responsible for
  • Where you can rent, including outside the PHA's area
  • How portability works (moving your voucher to another city or state)
  • The benefits of looking in neighborhoods that are not high-poverty areas

You should also get an information packet. It must include your voucher term and how to ask for an extension, how the PHA figures your payment standard and your share of the rent, the Request for Tenancy Approval (RFTA) form you give to a landlord, the HUD tenancy addendum that goes in every voucher lease, a list of landlords or other search resources, your family obligations, fair housing information, and how to ask for a hearing if the PHA takes action against you.

Keep this packet in one folder. You will use it all through your search.

What to Bring to Your Briefing

Your briefing letter is the final word, so bring exactly what it lists. If the letter is unclear, call and ask. It also helps to bring:

  • Your briefing letter and a photo ID
  • A notebook and pen, or your phone's notes app
  • A calendar, so you can mark your voucher deadline before you leave
  • Any changes since you applied: a new baby, someone moving in or out, a new job, or lost income

That last one matters. You must tell the PHA about changes in your household, and who lives with you affects your voucher size. It is much easier to fix now than after you sign a lease. Our document checklist lists the papers PHAs ask for most often.

The Three Numbers to Write Down

1. Your voucher's expiration date. Your voucher must give you at least 60 days to find a place, and HUD says most PHAs give 60 to 120 days. The date is printed on the voucher. The clock stops while the PHA reviews a Request for Tenancy Approval you turned in, and starts again if that unit is denied. PHAs can grant extensions under their own policy, and they must extend the time if you need it as a reasonable accommodation for a disability.

2. Your voucher size. This is the number of bedrooms the PHA will pay for, based on its subsidy standards and who is in your household. You can rent a bigger or smaller place, but the PHA uses the payment standard for your voucher size or for the unit's size, whichever is lower.

3. Your payment standard. This is the most the PHA will use to figure its share of the rent for your voucher size. If the rent plus utilities is above the payment standard, you pay the difference. At move-in, there is a limit: if the unit costs more than the payment standard, your share cannot be more than 40% of your adjusted monthly income. Read our payment standards guide to see how this works.

Questions to Ask Before You Leave

  • What is the payment standard for my voucher size, and what utility allowance applies?
  • How do I turn in the Request for Tenancy Approval: email, online portal, or in person?
  • How do I ask for an extension, and what proof of my search do you want?
  • How soon are inspections usually scheduled after I turn in an RFTA?
  • Can I move to another city right away, or does the 12-month rule apply to me?
  • Do you have a landlord list, a search helper, or landlord incentives?
  • Who is my caseworker, and what is the best way to reach them?

Write down your caseworker's name, direct phone number, and email. If you have trouble reaching your PHA later, our guide on how to deal with your housing authority can help.

After the Briefing: Your First Week

Start your search the same day. The most common way people lose a voucher is running out of time. Keep a simple log of every landlord you contact: the date, the address, and what they said. That log is your proof if you need an extension, and your evidence if a landlord turns you down because of your voucher.

When a landlord says yes, give them the RFTA and turn it in fast. The PHA will check that the rent is reasonable and inspect the unit before it starts paying. Our posts on what happens after you get your voucher and finding an apartment with a voucher walk through the next steps.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Section 8 briefing mandatory?

Yes. HUD says that once you are selected from the waiting list, you must attend a voucher briefing before a voucher is issued. If a disability makes it hard to attend, you can ask for a reasonable accommodation, such as a different format or time.

What happens if I miss my Section 8 briefing?

Call your housing authority right away and ask to reschedule. Each PHA sets its own rules for missed appointments in its Administrative Plan, and missing a briefing can put your voucher at risk. If the PHA denies you, it must tell you why in writing and offer an informal review. Our guide on appealing a housing denial explains how.

How long do I have to find a place after the briefing?

At least 60 days, and HUD says most PHAs give 60 to 120 days. The exact date is on your voucher. The clock pauses while the PHA reviews a Request for Tenancy Approval, and you can ask for an extension. See what happens if your voucher expires.

Can I use my voucher in another city?

Usually, yes. This is called portability, and the PHA must explain it at your briefing. If neither the head of household nor spouse lived in the PHA's area when you applied, the PHA can require you to rent in its area for the first 12 months. Read moving with a Section 8 voucher for the steps.

How to Report Unsafe Housing Conditions (and When HUD Can Help)

Matt · November 11, 2026 · 7 min read

Who you report unsafe housing to depends on the kind of housing you live in. For most private rentals, call your city or county code enforcement (building inspection) office or the health department. If you have a Section 8 voucher or live in public housing, call your housing authority (PHA). If you live in a HUD-assisted apartment complex, call HUD's Multifamily Housing Complaint Line at 1-800-685-8470. Each one can inspect and push the landlord to make repairs.

If it is an emergency, like a gas smell, sparking wires, or no heat in freezing weather, call 911 or your gas or electric company first. For everything else, HUD's own advice is to tell your landlord or manager about the problem first, in writing. Then use the right agency below.

Who to Call, Based on Where You Live

  • Private rental, no rent help: your city or county code enforcement or building inspection office. For mold, pests, sewage, or lead, also try the local health department. Many cities take these reports through 311.
  • Section 8 voucher: your PHA, which must inspect when you report a problem. You can also call code enforcement.
  • Public housing: put in a work order with your PHA in writing. If it is not fixed, HUD's PIH Information Resource Center at (800) 955-2232 can point you to your local HUD office.
  • HUD-assisted apartment complex (for example, project-based Section 8 or Section 202 senior housing): tell management in writing, then call HUD's Multifamily Housing Complaint Line at 1-800-685-8470 (TTY 1-800-432-2209).

Not sure which one you are in? Look at your lease or ask your property manager whether the building has a HUD contract. If you pay about 30% of your income for rent and do not have a voucher, you may be in public housing or a HUD-assisted building.

What Counts as Unsafe Housing

Local codes and HUD's inspection standards cover the basics every home needs, including:

  • Heat, hot and cold running water, and working toilets and drains
  • Safe electrical wiring, with no exposed or sparking wires
  • Roofs, walls, floors, and stairs that are sound, with no big leaks
  • Working smoke alarms, and carbon monoxide alarms where required
  • Doors and windows that lock
  • No serious mold, rodent, or bug infestations
  • No peeling or chipping paint in homes built before 1978, which may contain lead

Our post on Housing Quality Standards covers what voucher units must meet.

Step 1: Write It Down and Take Photos

Before you report, build a record. Take dated photos and videos. Write down when the problem started and every time you asked for a fix. Send your landlord a short written repair request and keep a copy. Our repair request letter template and letter generator make this quick. If the problem is making anyone sick, ask your doctor to note it in your records.

Step 2: If You Have a Voucher, Call Your Housing Authority

This is the strongest tool voucher holders have. When you report a problem, federal rules (24 CFR 982.405 and 982.404) give the PHA firm deadlines:

  • Life-threatening problems: the PHA must inspect and notify the landlord within 24 hours. The landlord then has 24 hours to fix it.
  • Other problems: the PHA must inspect and notify the landlord within 15 days. The landlord then has 30 days, unless the PHA approves more time.
  • If the landlord does not fix it: the PHA stops its share of the rent. The landlord cannot end your tenancy because of that. You can also choose to move, and the PHA must give you your voucher.
  • If it is still not fixed after 60 days: the PHA ends its contract with the landlord. It must give you your voucher at least 30 days before that and at least 90 days to find a new place. It may help with moving costs or a security deposit.

These rules apply to HAP contracts signed or renewed since June 6, 2024. If the damage was caused by someone in your household or a guest, beyond normal wear and tear, you may be responsible for it instead. For how inspections work, see how Section 8 inspections work.

Step 3: If You Rent Privately, Call Code Enforcement

Search your city or county name plus "code enforcement" or "housing inspection," or call 311 if your city has it. An inspector can visit, write up violations, and give the landlord a deadline, with fines if they do not comply. HUD's complaint lines are for HUD-assisted housing, so for a regular private rental, the local inspector is the one with the power to act.

One exception: if the problem is discrimination, such as a landlord refusing repairs because of your race, disability, or because you have kids, you can file with HUD for most housing. Call 1-800-669-9777 or report it online.

Step 4: If You Live in a HUD-Assisted Complex

HUD's Multifamily Housing Complaint Line takes reports about poor maintenance, dangers to health and safety, mismanagement, and fraud at HUD-insured and HUD-assisted properties. It is staffed Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Eastern. Before you call, have your name, the complex name, your address and unit number, the property manager's name, and a short description of the problem and when you reported it.

Protect Yourself From Retaliation

Most states have laws against landlords punishing tenants for reporting code violations, such as by raising rent, cutting services, or filing an eviction. Keep a timeline of when you reported the problem and what the landlord did after. If you get any eviction notice, contact legal aid right away. If you have a voucher, you are required to give your PHA a copy of the notice promptly. Find free help through the Legal Services Corporation's legal aid finder.

Do not stop paying rent on your own. Some states allow rent withholding or paying rent into escrow, but only if you follow strict steps. Our post your landlord won't fix things? Here's what to do explains those options.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I report my landlord to HUD?

If you live in a HUD-assisted apartment complex, yes: call HUD's Multifamily Housing Complaint Line at 1-800-685-8470. If you have a Section 8 voucher or live in public housing, HUD tells you to contact your local housing authority. For an ordinary private rental, repair problems go to your city or county code enforcement or health department. Discrimination can be reported to HUD for most housing at 1-800-669-9777.

How fast does the housing authority have to inspect after I complain?

If you have a voucher and report a life-threatening problem, the PHA must inspect and notify the landlord within 24 hours. For other problems, it has 15 days. After that, the landlord has 24 hours to fix life-threatening problems and 30 days for others, unless the PHA approves more time.

Can I stop paying rent if my landlord will not make repairs?

Not without following your state's exact rules, or you could be evicted. Some states allow rent withholding or paying rent into escrow, but the steps are strict. If you have a voucher, the housing authority may stop its share of the rent, but you should keep paying your share unless a lawyer tells you otherwise.

Can my landlord evict me for reporting problems?

Most states have laws against retaliation for reporting code violations. If you have a voucher, the landlord also cannot end your tenancy because the housing authority withheld or stopped its payments over failed repairs. Keep records of when you reported and what the landlord did next, and contact legal aid if you get a notice.

Where to Report

HUD-assisted apartment complexes: Multifamily Housing Complaint Line, 1-800-685-8470 (TTY 1-800-432-2209). HUD complaint line page.

Vouchers and public housing: your housing authority. Find it in our PHA directory, or call HUD's PIH Information Resource Center at (800) 955-2232.

Housing discrimination: HUD, 1-800-669-9777 or hud.gov/reporthousingdiscrimination.

Private rentals: your city or county code enforcement office or health department.

Is Section 8 Taxable? What Voucher Holders Need to Know at Tax Time

Matt · November 18, 2026 · 5 min read

No. The rent your housing authority (PHA) pays to your landlord is not taxable income, and you do not report it on your tax return. IRS Publication 525 says not to include need-based government benefit payments in your income, and Section 8 is need-based help paid on your behalf. A utility reimbursement check from your PHA is part of that same housing help.

Tax time still matters on Section 8 in two ways. First, you may be owed money through credits like the Earned Income Tax Credit, even if you do not owe any tax. Second, the income on your tax return should match what you told your PHA. And here is good news: your tax refund will not raise your rent.

Will My Tax Refund Raise My Section 8 Rent?

No. HUD's income rules say federal and state tax refunds, and refundable credits like the Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC), are not counted as income when you get them. HUD's rules also say a federal tax refund or refundable credit does not count as an asset for 12 months after you receive it.

So you can save your refund for a car repair, a security deposit, or an emergency fund without it being held against you at your next recertification. What does count is the wages or other income you earned during the year. Our list of what counts as income on Section 8 goes through the details.

Tax Credits You Might Be Missing

Many low-income workers are owed money they never claim because they do not file. You often need to file a return to get these credits, even if your income is low enough that you are not required to file. For tax year 2026, the returns you file in early 2027, the IRS amounts are:

  • Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC): up to $664 with no qualifying children, $4,427 with one child, $7,316 with two, and $8,231 with three or more. You need income from work, and the credit phases out as income rises. Check if you qualify with the IRS EITC page.
  • Child Tax Credit: up to $2,200 per qualifying child, and up to $1,700 of that can be paid to you as a refund even if you owe no tax. See the IRS Child Tax Credit page.
  • State credits: some states have their own earned income credit or a renter's credit. Check your state tax agency's website.

Make Sure Your Tax Return Matches What Your PHA Knows

Your PHA figures your rent from your household income. You must give it the information it asks for, and it can ask for your tax return, especially if you are self-employed. If your return shows income you never reported, the PHA can find that you were underpaying rent. You may then have to pay the difference back, and owing a PHA money can put your voucher at risk.

This comes up most with side income: gig driving, delivery apps, selling online, cleaning, or hair braiding. For Section 8, what counts is your net self-employment income, after business expenses. Report income the way your PHA's rules require, and keep records of what you earn and spend. Read how self-employment income works on Section 8 and what happens when your income goes up.

Where to Get Free Tax Help

  • VITA (Volunteer Income Tax Assistance): free tax prep from IRS-certified volunteers for people who generally make about $70,244 or less, people with disabilities, and people who speak limited English. Find a site with the IRS VITA/TCE locator or call 800-906-9887.
  • TCE (Tax Counseling for the Elderly): free help focused on people 60 and older. Most TCE sites are run by AARP Foundation Tax-Aide, 888-227-7669.
  • IRS Free File: free online tax software for people with adjusted gross income of $89,000 or less, according to the IRS. The limit can change each tax season, so check IRS Free File before you start.

Be careful with paid preparers who charge high fees or offer "refund advance" loans. Free programs can claim every credit you qualify for.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to report Section 8 on my taxes?

No. The rent your housing authority pays to your landlord is need-based government help, and IRS Publication 525 says not to include need-based government benefit payments in your income. You do not list it anywhere on your return.

Will my tax refund raise my Section 8 rent?

No. HUD's income rules do not count federal or state tax refunds or refundable credits, like the Earned Income Tax Credit, as income when you get them. A federal refund or refundable credit also does not count as an asset for 12 months after you receive it.

Does my housing authority need my tax return?

It can ask for it. You must give the PHA the information it needs to figure your income, and many PHAs ask for tax returns, especially if you are self-employed. Make sure the income on your return matches what you reported to the PHA.

Can I deduct my rent on my federal taxes?

No. There is no federal tax deduction for rent. Some states offer a renter's credit or rebate, so check your state tax agency's website.

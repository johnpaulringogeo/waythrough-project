What Is an Eviction Diversion Program? How to Find One

Matt · December 9, 2026 · 7 min read

An eviction diversion program helps a landlord and tenant settle a rent problem before it ends in an eviction order. Depending on where you live, it may give you a mediator, time to make a payment plan, free legal help, or money to pay back rent. Most programs are run by a city, a county, or a local court, so the rules and the way in are different in every place.

To find out if there is one near you, look at your eviction papers, then call the court clerk and your local legal aid office. Ask: "Is there an eviction diversion or mediation program for my case, and how do I get in?" Do this right away. Many programs have deadlines, and some only help before the landlord files in court.

How Does Eviction Diversion Work?

Each program is different, but most follow the same idea. Instead of going straight to a judge, the landlord and tenant get a chance to work things out. The City of Philadelphia describes its program as one that helps landlords and tenants reach an agreement that works for both of them without going to court.

A program may offer some or all of these:

  • Mediation: a neutral person helps you and your landlord agree on a plan.
  • A payment plan: time to catch up on what you owe while you stay in your home.
  • Rent money: some programs pay part of your back rent straight to the landlord.
  • Legal help: a free lawyer or legal aid worker to explain your rights.

Diversion does not erase what you owe. It gives you a way to pay it back or settle it without an eviction order on your record.

Three Real Programs, Three Different Doors

These three programs show how much the way in can vary. Check the official program for the latest rules before you act.

  • Philadelphia: the landlord must go first. Philadelphia Municipal Court requires landlords to take part in the City's Eviction Diversion Program before they file an eviction. They must wait 30 days before filing if the problem is not solved, or 90 days after the last month covered if the landlord got rent money through the program's Targeted Financial Assistance. Learn more in our Philadelphia emergency rent guide.
  • Jacksonville: the landlord signs you up. The City of Jacksonville's Eviction Diversion Program can pay up to three months of back rent for Duval County renters. Your household must include a child under 18, a senior over 60, a veteran, or an adult with a disability, and meet other rules. Tenants cannot apply on their own: your landlord has to send in a consent form first. See our Jacksonville emergency rent guide.
  • Las Vegas area: you get in through court. Clark County's Eviction Diversion Program, in the Las Vegas, Henderson and North Las Vegas justice courts, covers seniors 62 and older and people who get SSI because of a disability. To qualify, you must be facing eviction for not paying rent and file a tenant answer within the notice period. See our Las Vegas emergency rent guide.

Some well-known programs have closed. The Texas Eviction Diversion Program ended on June 30, 2023, so if a website tells you to apply for it, that information is out of date.

Who Qualifies for Eviction Diversion?

Every program sets its own rules. Common ones include:

  • The eviction is for unpaid rent, not for other lease problems.
  • You live in the city or county the program serves.
  • Your income is below a limit, or someone in your home is a child, a senior, a veteran, or a person with a disability.
  • You can show you can afford the rent going forward once you catch up.
  • Your landlord agrees to take part, in programs that are not required by law.

Gather these papers so you are ready: a photo ID, your lease, any notices from your landlord, proof of income such as pay stubs or a benefits letter, and a record of what you have paid.

How to Find an Eviction Diversion Program Near You

  1. Read every paper you got. A notice or court summons often lists diversion, mediation, or rent help programs, plus the deadline to respond.
  2. Call the court clerk. The court that handles evictions in your county (often called housing court, justice court, or municipal court) can tell you if there is a program and how to get in.
  3. Call legal aid. Free lawyers know the local programs. Use the Legal Services Corporation's legal aid finder, or see our guide to free legal help for tenants.
  4. Call 211. Ask for eviction diversion, mediation and emergency rent help in your county.
  5. Check our city guides. We have step-by-step rent and eviction help pages for Nashville, Dallas, Austin, El Paso, Oklahoma City, Albuquerque and more. Our free legal help guide lists every city we cover.

If You Already Have a Court Date

Do not skip court, even if you are in a diversion program or have made a deal with your landlord. If you do not show up, the judge can rule against you. Answer the case by the deadline on your papers, bring proof of any agreement or payments, and tell the judge you are working with a diversion program or applying for rent help.

Before you sign any agreement, read it carefully and ask what happens if you miss a payment. Ask whether it includes a judgment against you, because a judgment can show up when you apply for apartments later. If you are not sure, ask legal aid to look at it first. If You Have Section 8 or Live in HUD Housing

An eviction can cost you more than your home if you have a voucher. HUD's rules say your housing authority (PHA) must end your assistance if you are evicted from your assisted home for a serious lease violation. They also say you must give your PHA a copy of any eviction notice from your landlord right away.

So send your PHA the notice, tell your caseworker you are trying to settle, and ask whether your rent share should be lowered if your income dropped. Settling the case through diversion, without an eviction judgment, can help protect your voucher. Read HUD eviction rules for subsidized housing and how voucher termination works.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does eviction diversion mean?

Eviction diversion means steering a rent dispute away from an eviction judgment. A diversion program helps the landlord and tenant reach an agreement, often with a mediator, a payment plan, or money for back rent, so the case is settled instead of ending in an eviction order.

Can I sign up for eviction diversion myself?

It depends on the program. Some programs start with the landlord, like Philadelphia's, where landlords must apply before filing, and Jacksonville's, where the landlord sends in a consent form. Court-based programs, like the one in the Las Vegas area, usually start when you file your answer to the eviction case. Read your court papers and call the court clerk or legal aid to find out how to get in.

Does eviction diversion stop the eviction?

It can, if you and your landlord reach an agreement and you keep it. It does not erase what you owe. If the agreement falls apart or you miss a payment, the landlord may be able to go back to court. Keep going to every court date until the case is officially closed.

Is there an eviction diversion program in Texas?

Not statewide anymore. The Texas Eviction Diversion Program ended on June 30, 2023. Some cities and counties have their own rent help, so call 211 and your local justice court, and check our Dallas, Austin and El Paso guides.

Can You Use Section 8 for Assisted Living or Senior Housing?

Matt · November 4, 2026 · 7 min read

Sometimes, but only for the rent. A Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher can be used in senior apartments and independent living buildings like any other rental. It cannot be used in nursing homes, board and care homes, or places that provide continual medical or nursing care. For assisted living, HUD rules say the voucher cannot pay for meals or care services, and your lease cannot require you to buy them. So a voucher only works where the rent is separate from the care, and the housing authority (PHA) approves the unit.

If you or a parent needs daily help and a voucher alone will not cover it, the usual answer is to pair housing help with care help: Medicaid in-home care, PACE, or HUD senior housing. The best first call is the free Eldercare Locator at 1-800-677-1116, which connects you to your local Area Agency on Aging.

Where Seniors Can Use a Section 8 Voucher

  • Regular apartments and houses. Any rental where the landlord accepts the voucher, the rent is reasonable, and the unit passes inspection.
  • Senior apartments and 55+ or 62+ communities. These work the same way. Many have elevators, grab bars, and community rooms.
  • Independent living buildings. These can work if the voucher pays only toward rent and any meals or activities are optional.
  • Congregate housing and group homes (if your PHA allows them). Congregate housing gives you a private unit with shared meals from a central kitchen. A group home is a state-licensed home for up to 12 people who are elderly or have disabilities and do not need continual medical or nursing care. PHAs do not have to allow these "special housing types," but they must allow one if you need it as a reasonable accommodation for a disability.

Your share of the rent is based on your income, about 30% of your adjusted income in most cases. Our guide on how your rent is calculated explains the math.

Why Assisted Living Is Different

Assisted living bundles a place to live with help like meals, bathing, dressing, and medication reminders. Two federal rules get in the way:

  • Vouchers cannot be used in nursing homes, board and care homes, or facilities that provide continual psychiatric, medical, or nursing services (24 CFR 982.352).
  • The cost of meals or supportive services cannot be included in the rent, and the lease cannot require you to pay for them. Not paying for services cannot be a reason to end your tenancy (24 CFR 982.510).

That means a voucher can only work at an assisted living community that rents the apartment on its own, under a regular lease, with services billed separately. Before you sign anything, ask the facility: "Can I lease the apartment by itself, without being required to buy a services package?" Then ask your PHA whether it will approve the unit. The PHA makes the final call.

Voucher Rules That Help Older Renters

A live-in aide. If you are elderly or have a disability, you can ask the PHA to approve a live-in aide who provides care. The PHA must approve one if you need it as a reasonable accommodation. The aide's income does not count toward your rent, and the aide is counted when the PHA sets your voucher size, which can mean an extra bedroom.

Bigger deductions for elderly and disabled households. Your rent is based on adjusted income, after deductions. Under HUD's updated income rules, which most PHAs must use for rent changes starting January 1, 2027, households where the head, spouse, or co-head is 62 or older or has a disability get a $575 deduction in 2027, up from $400 under the old rules.

Medical expenses. These households can also deduct unreimbursed medical costs above a set share of income. Under the updated rules, that share is 10% of annual income, up from 3%. If you already got this deduction as of January 1, 2024, HUD's rules phase in the change and offer hardship relief, so ask your PHA. Either way, save every receipt: prescriptions, copays, insurance premiums, medical supplies, and transportation to appointments.

Other Programs for Seniors Who Need More Support

Section 202 senior housing. HUD's Section 202 program funds apartments for very low-income households where at least one person is 62 or older. Rent is usually about 30% of adjusted income, and many buildings have a service coordinator who connects residents to help with things like cleaning, cooking, and rides. You apply directly at the property. You do not need a voucher, and you cannot use one there because the building already has its own subsidy. Find properties with HUD's Resource Locator by choosing elderly and special needs housing.

PACE. The Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly covers medical care and support services so people can stay in the community. According to Medicare, you must be at least 55, live in a PACE service area, need a nursing-home level of care as certified by your state, and be able to live safely in the community with PACE's help. PACE is only offered in some states. See Medicare's PACE page.

Medicaid home and community-based services. Most states use Medicaid to pay for some care at home or in assisted living, often through programs called waivers. Rules and waiting lists vary by state. Your Area Agency on Aging or state Medicaid office can tell you what is offered. Our guide on how to apply for Medicaid can help you start.

Ask for Reasonable Accommodations

If a disability is part of the picture, you have the right to ask your PHA for changes to its rules so the program works for you. Examples include more time to search, a higher payment standard so you can rent an accessible unit, an extra bedroom for medical equipment or an aide, or approval of a special housing type. Use our reasonable accommodation request template and read your rights to reasonable accommodations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Section 8 pay for assisted living?

Only the rent, and only in some places. HUD rules do not let the voucher pay for meals or care services, and the lease cannot require you to buy them. If a facility separates the rent from the services and the housing authority approves the unit, the voucher can help with the rent. Medicaid or other programs would need to cover the care.

Can I use Section 8 in a senior apartment or 55+ community?

Yes. A senior apartment works like any other rental. The landlord has to agree to take the voucher, the rent has to be reasonable, and the unit has to pass the housing authority's inspection.

Can I use Section 8 in a nursing home?

No. Federal rules list nursing homes, board and care homes, and facilities that provide continual medical or nursing care as housing a voucher cannot pay for.

Can a caregiver live with me if I have Section 8?

Yes, if the housing authority approves them as a live-in aide. The PHA must approve one if you need it as a reasonable accommodation for a disability. The aide's income does not count toward your rent, and the aide is counted when the PHA decides your voucher size.

Is Section 202 the same as Section 8?

No. Section 202 is HUD housing built for very low-income people age 62 and older. The help is tied to the building, so you apply at the property and do not need a voucher. You cannot use a voucher in a Section 202 unit.

Does Section 8 Check Your Credit? What PHAs and Landlords Look At

Matt · October 28, 2026 · 6 min read

No. Your credit score is not part of HUD's eligibility rules for a Section 8 voucher. The housing authority (PHA) looks at your income, who is in your household, citizenship or eligible immigration status, certain criminal history, and whether you owe money to a housing authority. But once you have a voucher, the landlord does its own screening. HUD's rules say choosing a tenant is the owner's job, so a landlord can check your credit, and a low score is a common reason voucher holders get turned down.

So bad credit will not keep you off the waiting list, but it can make your apartment search harder. Here is what each side really checks, what to do if a landlord says no, and how to get approved anyway.

What the Housing Authority Checks

  • Income. Your household income must be under the limit for your area. See our Section 8 income limits guide.
  • Citizenship or eligible immigration status. At least one household member must qualify.
  • Certain criminal history. PHAs must deny anyone on a lifetime sex offender registry and anyone convicted of making meth in federally assisted housing. They may deny for recent drug-related or violent criminal activity, using their own written policy. Read criminal records and housing for details.
  • Money owed to a housing authority. A PHA may deny you if you currently owe rent or other money to any PHA, or if a PHA ended your assistance before.
  • Assets. Under HUD's updated income rules, which most PHAs must use starting January 1, 2027, families with more than $109,797 in net assets, or who own a home they could live in, generally cannot get assistance. A few exceptions apply.

Credit is not on that list. A PHA can choose to screen for "suitability for tenancy" if its written Administrative Plan says so, but federal rules put tenant screening on the landlord.

What Landlords Check

Landlords can screen voucher holders much like any other renter. HUD's rules say an owner may look at your history of:

  • Paying rent and utility bills
  • Taking care of a home
  • Respecting neighbors' right to peace and quiet
  • Drug-related or other criminal activity that threatens others
  • Following the rules of a lease

Many landlords buy a tenant screening report to check this. According to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, these reports can include your credit report, eviction records, rental history, criminal history, and a "risk score." The PHA must also give the landlord your current and past addresses and the names of your current and past landlords, if it has them.

Some states and cities have source-of-income laws that make it illegal to refuse a voucher, and some limit how landlords can screen voucher holders. Check the rules where you are looking.

If a Landlord Turns You Down Because of Credit

If a landlord uses a credit or screening report to deny you, the CFPB says they must tell you. That notice must include the screening company's contact information, your right to a free copy of the report if you ask within 60 days, and your right to dispute anything wrong.

  1. Ask for the free copy of the report. Errors are common, like another person's debt or an eviction case that was dismissed.
  2. Dispute anything wrong with the company that made the report.
  3. Ask the landlord if they would reconsider with more information, like a reference letter or proof the problem is fixed.
  4. If you think the real reason was your voucher, and your state or city bans that, see what to do when a landlord rejects your voucher.

How to Get Approved With Bad Credit

  • Explain how the voucher works. The PHA pays its share of the rent straight to the landlord. Your share is based on your income, so it is set at an amount you can afford.
  • Lead with your rental history. If you paid rent on time and had no evictions, say so up front and bring proof, like receipts or a letter from a past landlord.
  • Bring references. Letters from past landlords, employers, or caseworkers help.
  • Write a short note about your credit. If medical bills, a job loss, or a divorce caused the damage, a few honest sentences can help a landlord look past the number.
  • Look where screening is more flexible. Smaller landlords may look at your whole story. Tax credit (LIHTC) apartments often rent to voucher holders. See how to find LIHTC apartments.
  • Ask your PHA for help. Many PHAs keep landlord lists or have staff who help with your search.

If an old eviction is the real problem, read renting with an eviction on your record.

Fixing Your Credit While You Search

Get your free credit reports at AnnualCreditReport.com or by calling 1-877-322-8228. The FTC says the three credit bureaus have permanently let you check each report for free once a week. Dispute any errors you find, pay bills on time from now on, and work out payment plans for old debts if you can.

Once you have a voucher, look into the Family Self-Sufficiency (FSS) program. HUD's FSS rules list financial coaching, including reviewing and improving credit scores, as a service the program can connect you to. Our guide to rebuilding credit for housing has a step-by-step plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Section 8 run a credit check?

Your credit score is not one of HUD's eligibility rules for a Housing Choice Voucher. HUD's rules make tenant screening the landlord's job, so the landlord, not the housing authority, is the one that usually checks your credit.

What credit score do I need for Section 8?

There is no minimum credit score to qualify for a voucher. Each landlord sets its own screening rules, so the score you need to rent a specific apartment depends on that landlord.

Can a landlord turn me down for bad credit if I have a voucher?

In most places, yes, as long as they use the same standards for every applicant. Some states and cities have source-of-income laws that limit how landlords screen voucher holders. If a landlord denies you because of a credit or screening report, they must tell you, and you can get a free copy of that report if you ask within 60 days.

Will owing money to a housing authority stop me from getting Section 8?

It can. A PHA may deny you if you currently owe rent or other money to any housing authority. Paying the debt or setting up a repayment agreement is usually the way forward. See what to do if you owe a PHA money.

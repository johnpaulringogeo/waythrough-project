Moving to a New Place With Your Section 8 Voucher

Matt · May 27, 2026 · 7 min read

Section 8 vouchers are portable: you can move to any area in the country that has a housing authority. To transfer, give your current PHA written notice, request portability paperwork, and they'll coordinate with the receiving PHA. The process typically takes 2-4 weeks. One limit: if you didn't live in your PHA's area when you applied, it can require you to rent there for your first 12 months before you port.

Yes, You Can Move

The program is called the Housing Choice Voucher for a reason. Your voucher belongs to you, and it isn't tied to one apartment or one city. You can use it anywhere in the United States where a PHA runs the program.

Moving with it is called portability, and it's a federal right. There are timing rules and paperwork, but your PHA usually can't refuse to let you port. It can say no in a few cases, such as when it doesn't have enough funding, or when it has grounds to deny or end your assistance, like money you owe it (24 CFR 982.354(e)). HUD spells out the rule in its own words on HUD's Housing Choice Voucher portability page.

The One-Year Rule

If neither you nor your spouse lived in the area of the PHA that gave you the voucher when you applied, that PHA can require you to rent in its own area for your first 12 months on the program before you port to a different area (24 CFR 982.353(c)). If you already lived there when you applied, this rule doesn't apply to you. Either way, you can only move when your current lease allows.

There are exceptions. The PHA can choose to let you move sooner, and the 12-month limit never applies if someone in your household is a victim of domestic violence, dating violence, sexual assault or stalking and needs to move to stay safe.

How to Transfer: Step by Step

Step 1: Notify your current PHA. Tell them in writing that you want to move and port your voucher. Do it before your current lease expires, or on whatever schedule your lease sets for ending it. Our voucher portability request letter gives you wording you can fill in and send.

Step 2: PHA issues portability paperwork. Your current PHA (the "initial PHA") contacts the receiving PHA and sends over your voucher packet, which has your income verification, family composition, and voucher details.

Step 3: The receiving PHA contacts you. They'll schedule a briefing, issue you a new voucher with their payment standard, and give you the local rules for your housing search.

Step 4: Search for housing. This works the same way your first search did. The difference is that the receiving PHA's payment standard applies now, and it could be higher or lower than where you live today.

Step 5: Inspection and lease-up. Once you find a unit, the receiving PHA inspects it and processes your lease-up. Your new rent portion may change based on the local payment standard.

Billing vs. Absorbing

When you port your voucher, the receiving PHA has two options:

Billing: The receiving PHA administers your voucher but bills the cost back to your original PHA. Your original PHA's payment standard and rules may still apply.

Absorbing: The receiving PHA takes full responsibility for your voucher. It counts against their allocation and uses their payment standard. This is usually better for you if you're moving to a higher-cost area.

You don't get a say in which one they pick. Most PHAs absorb when they have vouchers available and bill when they don't.

What Changes When You Move

Your rent portion gets recalculated using the new area's payment standard and Fair Market Rent. A move to a cheaper area could lower what you pay, and a move to an expensive one could raise it.

You'll also be dealing with a new PHA, and local policies vary. Your new PHA might handle inspections, briefings, or paperwork differently from the one you're leaving.

And you'll need a landlord in the new area who accepts vouchers. Source of income discrimination protections vary by state, so check what the law is where you're going.

Tips for a Smooth Move

Start early. Give yourself at least 60-90 days before you need to be in the new location. The portability paperwork takes time, and you still have to find a unit after that.

Look up the new area's payment standard. If it's a lot lower than where you are now, you may end up paying more out of pocket. Check the receiving PHA's website or call them. Look them up in our housing authority directory, or in HUD's PHA contact directory if your area isn't covered there yet.

Keep copies of everything. Your current PHA sends documents to the receiving PHA, and things get lost between offices. Keep your own copies of all voucher paperwork, income verification, and letters.

Don't break your current lease early. Time the move to your lease end date. Breaking a lease can cause problems with your voucher.

Update your address right away. Once you've moved, give your new address to the PHA, Social Security, SNAP, Medicaid, and the school district if you have kids enrolled.
